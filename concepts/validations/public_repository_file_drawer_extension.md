# Public repository file-drawer extension

## Summary

Public repository file-drawer extension is the proposed open-science extension in which all studies, successful or not, are deposited in a public repository to reduce selective reporting of whole studies.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: study, repository record, successful result, null result, unpublished attempt, file-drawer item, or disclosure policy.
- Typical representation: all-studies repository, registered study log, null-result archive, or study-level publication-bias audit.
- Validation target: address whole-study selection that remains invisible even when published studies disclose analytic choices.
- Empirical signature: unsuccessful, null, or exploratory studies are visible outside the published article stream.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [researcher-degrees disclosure checklist](../methods/researcher_degrees_disclosure_checklist.md) from within-study flexibility to whole-study file-drawer bias.
- Connects [selective reporting and publication bias](../mechanisms/publication_bias_selective_reporting.md), [false-positive literature persistence](../mechanisms/false_positive_literature_persistence.md), and [open science interventions](open_science_interventions.md).
- Complements [preregistration and registered reports](preregistration_registered_reports.md), which can create prospective study records.
- Provides a long-term infrastructure proposal rather than a substitute for analytic-decision disclosure.

## Operationalization

- Require or incentivize study registration before or during data collection.
- Deposit completed studies regardless of whether the results are significant or publishable.
- Include enough methods detail, variables, conditions, exclusions, and analysis plans to interpret outcomes.
- Classify records so readers can locate null and failed studies.
- Pair repositories with journal policies that require disclosure of researcher degrees of freedom.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Simmons et al. (2011) notes that their disclosure solution does not reveal all degrees of freedom, especially the selective reporting of only experiments that work.
- The paper discusses a public repository for all studies, whether successful or not, as a possible long-term extension.
- Simmons et al. identify practical challenges, including enforcement, understandable descriptions, and proper classification.
- They argue that such repositories still need disclosure policies because study results cannot be interpreted unless researcher degrees of freedom are disclosed.

## Caveats

- Repository compliance is difficult without funder, journal, or institutional enforcement.
- Poorly described records can become unusable noise.
- Repositories reduce file-drawer bias only if unsuccessful studies are actually deposited.
- Sensitive data, failed pilots, and exploratory studies need governance for privacy and interpretability.
