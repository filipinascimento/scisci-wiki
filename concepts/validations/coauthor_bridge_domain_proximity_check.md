# Coauthor-bridge domain proximity check

## Summary

Indirect proximity through a disease-domain coauthor can be checked to see whether nominal outsiders actually have domain bridges.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: reviewer, coauthor tie, disease-domain publication, proposal score, or outsider subgroup.
- Typical representation: outsider subgroup comparison by domain-bridging coauthorship.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: robustness of outside-domain reviewer status.
- Empirical signature: outside-domain reviewers with and without coauthor bridges show similar or different evaluation patterns.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines reviewer-distance validation by linking it to [outside domain reviewer indicator](../measures/outside_domain_reviewer_indicator.md) and [stratified distance evaluator recruitment](../methods/stratified_distance_evaluator_recruitment.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [reviewer type interaction null](reviewer_type_interaction_null.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [reviewer proposal intellectual distance](../measures/reviewer_proposal_intellectual_distance.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Split outside-domain reviewers by prior coauthorship with disease-domain researchers.
- Estimate score differences for bridged and non-bridged outsiders.
- Use the comparison as a bridge-proximity robustness check on the outside-domain indicator.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Boudreau et al. (2016) reports that roughly half of outside-domain reviewers had a coauthor in the domain and half did not, with no evaluation differences between the subgroups.
- This supports a validation check on indirect domain proximity.

## Caveats

- A coauthor tie is a weak proxy for topical understanding.
- The subgroup check can be underpowered and domain-specific.
