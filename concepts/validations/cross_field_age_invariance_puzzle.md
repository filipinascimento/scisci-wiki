# Cross-field age invariance puzzle

## Summary

Inventor first-invention age can remain nearly constant across technological fields even when teamwork and specialization vary strongly.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: technological field, inventor, first-invention age, field depth, team size, or field-jump rate.
- Typical representation: cross-field comparison of age, depth, specialization, and team-size gradients.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: validation puzzle for knowledge-burden response margins.
- Empirical signature: mean first-invention age varies little across fields while team size and field-jump behavior vary by knowledge depth.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines knowledge-burden validation by linking it to [age at first invention](../measures/age_at_first_invention.md) and [knowledge depth team size gradient](knowledge_depth_team_size_gradient.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [knowledge depth specialization gradient](knowledge_depth_specialization_gradient.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [application area crowding arbitrage](../mechanisms/application_area_crowding_arbitrage.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Estimate field-level mean age at first patent for inventor cohorts.
- Regress first-invention age on normalized citation-tree depth and field controls.
- Compare the age gradient with team-size and field-jump gradients across the same fields.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Jones (2009) reports that most patent categories have first invention near age 29 while team size and field-jump rates differ strongly by field and knowledge depth.
- This creates a cross-sectional puzzle for how knowledge burden is absorbed.

## Caveats

- The result depends on the matched age subset and age-window restrictions.
- Cross-field averages can hide within-field career-timing heterogeneity.
