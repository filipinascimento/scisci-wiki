# Ex ante novelty-variance blind spot

## Summary

Ex ante novelty-variance blind spot is the validation problem that reviewers may discount novel proposals without perceiving the larger upside and downside variance that motivates exploratory research portfolios.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: proposal, novelty percentile, review score distribution, reviewer, funding portfolio, or ex post outcome.
- Typical representation: score variance by proposal novelty, novelty-by-score dispersion model, or comparison of ex ante review variance with ex post impact dispersion.
- Validation target: test whether reviewers recognize high-risk/high-gain potential before research outcomes are observed.
- Empirical signature: highly novel proposals receive lower mean evaluations but do not receive more dispersed ex ante evaluations.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [right-tail novelty review discount](../mechanisms/right_tail_novelty_review_discount.md) by separating mean discount from perceived variance.
- Contrasts proposal-stage review with publication-stage [novelty impact dispersion](../measures/novelty_impact_dispersion.md).
- Supports [novelty-aware review calibration](../methods/novelty_aware_review_calibration.md) and portfolio-oriented [funding incentives and exploration](../mechanisms/funding_incentives_and_exploration.md).
- Provides a concrete review-system validation for [research strategy risk-reward](../mechanisms/research_strategy_risk_reward.md).

## Operationalization

- Measure proposal novelty before review using controlled vocabulary, text, topic, or reference features.
- Estimate both mean review scores and score variance as functions of proposal novelty.
- Compare ex ante variance patterns with later outcome dispersion when funded and unfunded proposal tracking is possible.
- Separate reviewer disagreement from within-reviewer uncertainty when review forms include confidence scores.
- Test whether calibration prompts, separate upside-risk rubrics, or protected exploratory pools change perceived variance.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Boudreau et al. (2016) reports that highly novel proposals received lower evaluations.
- The paper also states that it found no evidence of greater variance in the ex ante evaluations of novel proposals.
- Boudreau et al. interpret this as raising the question of whether evaluators perceive high-variance potential before outcomes are known.
- This contrasts with portfolio arguments that innovators may trade lower expected mean performance for greater upside variance in exploratory research.

## Caveats

- Lack of ex ante score variance does not prove reviewers are wrong; novel proposals may truly have lower expected quality in a given pool.
- Review scores may compress uncertainty if rubrics ask for one scalar rather than separate feasibility, novelty, and upside scores.
- Ex post outcome tracking is needed to validate whether variance was missed or absent.
- Funded-only outcome data can understate the value of rejected high-novelty proposals.
