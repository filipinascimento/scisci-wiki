# Policy-Mention Role Reduction

## Summary

Policy-mention role reduction is the validity caveat that counting policy-document mentions reduces complex science-policy interaction roles to a simple reading-and-citing trace.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, policy actor, policy document, science-policy interaction, or policy mention.
- Typical representation: role-level information loss in quantitative policy-mention counts.
- Validation target: whether policy mentions distinguish the forms of interaction that produced policy influence.
- Empirical signature: mention counts cannot separate pure scientist, science arbiter, issue advocate, or honest-broker roles.

## Operationalization

- Code policy mentions for evidence of direct interaction, advice, advocacy, synthesis, or routine citation.
- Use surveys, interviews, or document context to infer science-policy roles.
- Report when policy counts are treated only as attention traces.
- Avoid using mention counts alone to rank broader policy influence.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Bornmann et al. (2016) reviews typologies of science-policy roles including pure scientist, science arbiter, issue advocate, and honest broker.
- The paper states that quantitative analysis of policy mentions cannot uncover these different interaction forms.
- Bornmann et al. argue that policy-document mention analysis reduces interaction to political actors reading and citing academic papers.

## Caveats

- Role coding can be subjective and may require information outside the policy document.
- Some policy influence is mediated through advisory processes without citation.
- A policy mention can still be useful evidence when the target is document attention rather than policy role.
