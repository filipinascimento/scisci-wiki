# Upward faculty placement exception audit

## Summary

Upward faculty placement exception audit studies the minority of faculty who obtain positions at institutions more prestigious than their doctoral institution, treating them as cases that may reveal compensating factors beyond pedigree.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: faculty placement, doctoral institution rank, employing institution rank, upward-placement indicator, candidate record, mentor, publication record, resources, or subfield demand.
- Typical representation: upward-placement case set, compensatory-factor model, matched comparison, qualitative case audit, or candidate-level feature table.
- Validation target: identify when doctoral prestige can be overcome and which mechanisms distinguish exceptional upward placements.
- Empirical signature: upward placements are rare but nonzero, and their predictors cannot be resolved from placement edges alone.

## Uses in Science of Science

## Operationalization

- Identify faculty placements where the employing institution ranks above the doctoral institution under a documented prestige hierarchy.
- Compare upward placements with matched lateral or downward placements from similar doctoral institutions, fields, cohorts, and demographics.
- Add candidate-level features such as publications, citations, grants, postdoctoral training, advisor status, mentoring, subfield, geography, and job-market timing.
- Test whether the same factors predict upward placement across fields or only in specific labor-market niches.
- Interpret results as mechanisms of exception, not proof that prestige constraints are absent.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Clauset, Arbesman, and Larremore (2015) reports that only 9-14% of faculty in the studied disciplines are placed at institutions more prestigious than their doctoral institution.
- The paper describes these upward placements, along with institutions that place small numbers of faculty into highly ranked institutions, as a puzzle.
- Clauset et al. state that identifying the factors that distinguish exceptional faculty and the degree to which they compensate for a low-prestige doctorate would have important implications for understanding faculty hiring mechanisms.
- They note that such a study would require detailed data on research characteristics, mentoring, institutional resources, and other factors not included in their placement-network analysis.

## Caveats

- Upward placement may reflect candidate ability, subfield demand, geography, department needs, advisor networks, or measurement error in ranks.
- Rare-event models need careful matching and uncertainty reporting.
- The existence of exceptions does not negate strong aggregate hierarchy.
