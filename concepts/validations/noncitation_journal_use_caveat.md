# Noncitation journal-use caveat

## Summary

Noncitation journal-use caveat warns that journals used for awareness, news, or review can be important and widely read while ranking low by citation frequency.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: journal, citation-frequency ranking, readership use, collection decision, or source-selection audit.
- Typical representation: citation-rank caveat paired with noncitation use evidence.
- Validation target: prevent citation frequency from being interpreted as total journal value.
- Empirical signature: journals known to be useful or widely read can rank low on times-cited lists.

## Operationalization

- Identify journals whose function is awareness, review, education, news, or professional monitoring.
- Compare citation frequency with readership, subscription, survey, or use evidence when available.
- Do not downgrade a journal solely because it is rarely cited if its primary role is noncitation use.
- Document the evaluation purpose before applying citation rankings.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Garfield (1972) states that citation frequency reflects journal value and use, but there are useful journals that are not frequently cited.
- Garfield notes that scientists may read some journals to keep up generally and rarely cite them in published work.
- The paper gives examples of popular review or news-oriented journals that rank low by times cited but should not be treated as less important or less used solely for that reason.

## Caveats

- Noncitation use is harder to measure consistently than citation frequency.
- Readership value can vary by audience, institution, and evaluation purpose.
- Citation and noncitation evidence should be combined explicitly rather than blended into an opaque score.
