# Pattern-to-policy causal gap

## Summary

Robust observational patterns do not by themselves justify policy changes without causal tests of interventions and mechanisms.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: observational pattern, policy intervention, causal mechanism, counterfactual, or evaluation outcome.
- Typical representation: descriptive-pattern to policy-intervention evidence ladder.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: causal sufficiency of evidence used for science policy design.
- Empirical signature: a descriptive regularity is cited for policy, but the mechanism and counterfactual intervention effect remain untested..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Generalizes [SciSci quasi-experimental policy evaluation](scisci_quasi_experimental_policy_evaluation.md).
- Connects prediction findings to [active science-policy experimentation](active_science_policy_experimentation.md).
- Useful for funding, hiring, and assessment reform claims.

## Operationalization

- For each proposed policy, identify the descriptive pattern, causal mechanism, intervention, and counterfactual.
- Prefer randomized, quasi-experimental, or staged rollout evidence before changing rules.
- Track intended and unintended outcomes after deployment.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Clauset et al. (2017) argues that productivity and bias patterns raise funding questions, but determining underlying causes requires further investigation and active experimentation.

## Caveats

- Some science-policy experiments have legitimacy and feasibility constraints.
- Waiting for perfect causal evidence can preserve harmful status quo policies.
