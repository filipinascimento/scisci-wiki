# Productivity-exposure peak timing

## Summary

Productivity-exposure peak timing is the validation that apparent early or mid-career peak-impact timing can be explained by when scientists produce more papers.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: career, publication sequence, highest-impact paper, productivity profile, shuffle null, or career age.
- Typical representation: peak-position distribution compared with productivity-preserving shuffled careers.
- Validation target: determine whether peak-impact timing reflects productivity exposure rather than special career-stage impact propensity.
- Empirical signature: shuffling impact values across a scientist's output sequence reproduces the observed peak-timing pattern.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [highest-impact sequence position](../measures/highest_impact_sequence_position.md) with a productivity-exposure interpretation.
- Validates the [random impact rule](../mechanisms/random_impact_rule.md) against career-age narratives.
- Relies on [within-career impact shuffle null](within_career_impact_shuffle_null.md) as the comparison design.
- Connects career-impact timing to [early-career productivity peak window](../measures/early_career_productivity_peak_window.md).

## Operationalization

- Identify each scientist's highest-impact publication and its career age or sequence position.
- Preserve each scientist's publication timing and productivity profile.
- Shuffle impact values within the career to generate the peak-timing null.
- Compare calendar-age peak distributions with sequence-normalized peak-position distributions.
- Report whether apparent early peaks disappear when productivity exposure is controlled.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sinatra et al. (2016) shows that highest-impact work often appears before 20 career years in calendar time.
- The same paper shows that productivity-preserving shuffles reproduce this pattern, while the relative sequence position of the top paper is approximately uniform.
- The motif separates a productivity-exposure explanation from a claim that individual impact potential systematically declines after early career.
- It supports the broader random-impact model by showing that more draws in a period create more opportunities for a top hit.

## Caveats

- Productivity-preserving shuffles assume impact values are exchangeable within a career conditional on the model.
- Career start and end definitions can affect apparent peak timing.
- Fields with strong age-dependent topic access or equipment access may deviate from the null.
