# Near-miss generalizability boundary

## Summary

Near-miss generalizability boundary is the scope condition that a funding-threshold result applies locally to near-threshold setbacks, not necessarily to broader or more severe failures.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: treatment contrast, funding threshold, near miss, broader failure, or external-validity claim.
- Typical representation: local-treatment-effect boundary note.
- Validation target: prevent overgeneralizing quasi-experimental near-threshold findings.
- Empirical signature: evidence is strongest for applicants close to the cutoff and weaker for distant failures.

## Operationalization

- State the running-variable band where the estimate is identified.
- Avoid generalizing to failures far from the threshold without additional evidence.
- Compare near misses to more distant applicants when available.
- Report the population and decision system to which the result applies.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Jones, and Wang (2019) explicitly frames the results around near-threshold NIH R01 applicants.
- The paper cautions against interpreting the findings as a general endorsement of roadblocks or broad failure exposure.
- This local scope condition is central to responsible use of the setback result.

## Caveats

- Local validity can still inform policy, but only for similar thresholds and applicant populations.
- Other domains may have different cumulative-advantage dynamics.
- Severe failures can have different psychological, resource, and career effects.
