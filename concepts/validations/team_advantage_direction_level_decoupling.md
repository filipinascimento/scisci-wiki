# Team-advantage direction/level decoupling

## Summary

Team advantage needs separate coding for its current level and its temporal direction, because a declining RTI can still remain above one.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, period, relative team impact, slope, endpoint level, or team/solo citation ratio.
- Typical representation: RTI level and RTI trend recorded as separate fields.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: interpretation of changing team-impact advantage.
- Empirical signature: some fields show declining RTI slopes while teams still have higher endpoint citation averages than solo work.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines team-advantage validation by linking it to [relative team impact ratio](../measures/relative_team_impact_ratio.md) and [team impact subfield prevalence](team_impact_subfield_prevalence.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [solo to team impact reversal](solo_to_team_impact_reversal.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [team growth subfield universality](team_growth_subfield_universality.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Report RTI level and RTI slope separately rather than one binary team-advantage flag.
- Flag cases where RTI declines over time but remains above one.
- Pair the distinction with field and subfield prevalence checks.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified local full text from Wuchty et al. (2007) notes minority cases such as mathematics and education where RTI declined but team citation averages remained substantially higher at the end of the period.
- The result prevents coding declining advantage as no team advantage.

## Caveats

- Slope changes can reflect field mix, citation practice, or changing solo/team denominators.
- The motif is descriptive and should not be read as a causal mechanism.
