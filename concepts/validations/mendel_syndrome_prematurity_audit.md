# Mendel-syndrome prematurity audit

## Summary

Mendel-syndrome prematurity audits test claims that a neglected paper was "ahead of its time" by combining delayed-citation evidence with historical context.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, author claim, historical case, citation trajectory, field-readiness narrative, or later uptake event.
- Typical representation: sleeping-beauty candidate list plus case notes about why early audiences did not use the work.
- Validation target: distinguish credible prematurity from retrospective rationalization or ordinary low impact.
- Empirical signature: low early uptake is followed by later use, and full-text or historical evidence explains what changed.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Turns [delayed recognition](../mechanisms/delayed_recognition.md) into an auditable claim rather than a post hoc story.
- Uses [sleeping-beauty detection](../methods/sleeping_beauty_detection.md) to surface candidates for qualitative review.
- Helps evaluation settings handle claims that work needs more time before its value is visible.
- Connects quantitative citation histories to expert comments, field readiness, and citation-context evidence.

## Operationalization

- Start from a claimed premature paper or a candidate from a sleeping-beauty screen.
- Verify low early citation attention, later awakening, and the timing of relevant field developments.
- Read the original paper, trigger citations, later reviews, and expert or historical accounts.
- Separate delayed recognition from terminological change, database coverage artifacts, and ordinary rediscovery without continuity.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from van Raan (2004) states that scientists sometimes claim papers will not be picked up because they are ahead of time.
- van Raan calls this the "Mendel syndrome" after Mendel's genetics work, which took decades to be taken up.
- The paper frames sleeping-beauty measurement as a way to answer Mendel-like claims empirically rather than treating them as anecdotes.
- Its Romans/Polchinski case adds a historical audit layer by using comments from involved scientists to interpret the awakening and by identifying a possible [affiliation-memory awakening bridge](../mechanisms/affiliation_memory_awakening_bridge.md).

## Caveats

- Prematurity is difficult to prove from citation counts alone.
- Author claims can be self-serving, and later attention can reflect fashion, reinterpretation, or review amplification.
- Strong audits require evidence about the receiving field, not only the sleeping paper.
