# Novelty-productivity-impact separation

## Summary

Novelty should be treated as a distinct outcome from productivity and citation impact when evaluating funding systems.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: publication, project, novelty score, productivity outcome, citation outcome, or funding source.
- Typical representation: construct-boundary table separating novelty, productivity, and impact outcomes.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: outcome validity in funding-model evaluation.
- Empirical signature: funding models can differ in novelty even when productivity or citation impact is not the evaluation target..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines funding-model outcome validation by linking it to [commonness based tail novelty](../measures/commonness_based_tail_novelty.md) and [citation impact indicators](../measures/citation_impact_indicators.md).
- Useful as a reusable distinction when [field normalized citation impact](../measures/field_normalized_citation_impact.md) is analyzed in science-of-science data.
- Adds cross-links to [novelty impact dispersion](../measures/novelty_impact_dispersion.md) so the motif is not read as an isolated result.

## Operationalization

- Model novelty directly, such as through journal-pair commonness, rather than using citation or publication counts as substitutes.
- Report productivity and impact as separate outcomes or explicit non-targets.
- Avoid interpreting a novelty effect as an overall creativity, impact, or efficiency effect without additional measures.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang et al. (2018) states that the study focuses on novelty rather than number of papers, citations, lab productivity, or impact.
- The motif clarifies the construct boundary of funding-model results.

## Caveats

- Related to commonness-based novelty and novelty-impact dispersion; this page is the construct-boundary motif.
- A funding design that raises novelty could still lower productivity or welfare under other objectives.
