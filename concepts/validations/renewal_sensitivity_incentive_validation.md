# Renewal-sensitivity incentive validation

## Summary

Renewal-sensitivity incentive validation tests whether a funding program's renewal decisions actually respond to the achievements that its stated incentive theory emphasizes.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: grant renewal, investigator appointment term, review cycle, funded scientist, or program cohort.
- Typical representation: renewal probability model, achievement covariates, first-versus-later term contrast, and marginal effect comparison.
- Validation target: check whether a program's real review practice matches its claimed tolerance for early failure and reward for long-run breakthrough success.
- Empirical signature: early renewal is less sensitive to short-run output, while later renewal is more responsive to high-impact achievements.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides an internal-validity check for [HHMI-NIH incentive contrast](../methods/hhmi_nih_incentive_contrast.md).
- Supports [funding autonomy and exploration](../mechanisms/funding_autonomy_exploration.md) by testing whether funding contracts truly tolerate early failure.
- Links program rhetoric to observed administrative behavior before estimating downstream scientific outcomes.

## Operationalization

- Collect appointment or grant-renewal histories.
- Define performance measures for the preceding term, including overall output and high-impact or field-transforming outputs.
- Model renewal probability as a function of preceding-term achievements and term number.
- Compare first-renewal sensitivity with later-renewal sensitivity.
- Interpret downstream program effects only after confirming that the actual review process matches the claimed incentive regime.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Azoulay et al. (2009) validates HHMI program practice by modeling first and second reappointment decisions.
- The paper reports that achievement during the preceding term increases the likelihood of second reappointment but not first reappointment.
- Azoulay et al. also report that blockbuster-paper effects are larger than total-publication effects, consistent with reviewers valuing field-transforming work over CV line counts.
- They conclude that HHMI's actual practices conform to incentive features predicted to encourage exploration.

## Caveats

- Renewal models are observational and can reflect reviewer expectations, field norms, or unobserved lab circumstances.
- A program can tolerate early failure for selected investigators while still selecting conservatively at entry.
- Renewal sensitivity can change over time as funder budgets, leadership, or evaluation criteria change.
