# Team expertise-quality nonpredictor

## Summary

Perceived replication-team expertise or quality may fail to predict replication outcomes once protocols, author review, and evidence features are considered.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: replication team, expertise rating, quality rating, protocol review, or replication outcome.
- Typical representation: moderator audit comparing team-quality indicators with replication success.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: whether failed replications are explained by poor replication-team quality.
- Empirical signature: team expertise or quality ratings show weak or null association with replication success.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects replication failure adjudication to [replication moderator battery](../methods/replication_moderator_battery.md) and [replication moderator index aggregation](../methods/replication_moderator_index_aggregation.md).

## Operationalization

- Record expertise, protocol quality, author feedback, and execution-difficulty ratings for replication teams.
- Test whether these ratings predict success after controlling for original evidence and study features.
- Use null associations to constrain post hoc claims that failures are merely low-quality replications.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Open Science Collaboration (2015) includes moderator analyses and original-author review of replication protocols.
- The reported moderator framework allows team expertise and quality to be tested as predictors rather than assumed explanations.

## Caveats

- Null findings for team-quality moderators may be limited by measurement reliability and range restriction.
- Expertise can still matter for individual cases even if it is not a broad predictor.
