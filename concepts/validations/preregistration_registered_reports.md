# Preregistration and registered reports

## Summary

Preregistration and registered reports are workflow motifs that separate prediction from postdiction by recording research questions, design choices, outcomes, and analysis plans before observing the results.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: study, analysis plan, registry entry, protocol, outcome variable, hypothesis, or peer-reviewed registered report.
- Typical representation: time-stamped protocol, pre-analysis plan, registry metadata, decision tree, or two-stage review record.
- Mechanism or measurement target: constraint before outcome observation, transparent distinction between confirmatory and exploratory analysis, and reduced selective reporting.
- Empirical signature: discoverable preregistration, declared primary outcomes, pre-specified exclusions and models, labeled deviations, and results reported regardless of outcome direction.

## Operationalization

- Inputs: registry records, protocols, manuscripts, analysis code, journal badges, registered-report review records, and deviations between plans and publications.
- Measures: preregistration presence, registry quality, specificity of analysis plan, adherence rate, outcome-switching rate, deviation transparency, and share of null or mixed results.
- Common model forms: before/after policy comparisons, registry-publication audits, reporting-bias studies, replication-outcome comparisons, and journal/funder adoption diffusion.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Nosek et al. (2018) frames preregistration as a way to distinguish predictions tested with new observations from postdictions generated after outcomes are known.
- Nosek et al. argue that preregistration preserves the interpretability of statistical inference by making the tested analysis path knowable before outcome observation, while still allowing exploratory findings to be labeled as generative.
- The same full text describes registered reports as a publication workflow in which the research question and method are reviewed before results are known, aligning gatekeeping with design quality rather than outcome appeal.
- The same full text also supports standalone motifs for [registry-publication reporting-bias audits](../methods/registry_publication_reporting_bias_audit.md), [preregistration adherence and deviation audits](preregistration_adherence_deviation_audit.md), [p-value diagnosticity under forking paths](p_value_diagnosticity_forking_paths.md), [partial-blinding and incremental preregistration](../methods/partial_blinding_incremental_preregistration.md), and [preregistration badge incentives](preregistration_badge_incentives.md).
- Additional Nosek et al. submotifs separate the specificity of the registered plan, program-level multiplicity across many preregistered attempts, holdout-based conversion from exploration to prediction, and narrative interpretation after all planned results are reported.
- A residual Nosek et al. pass separates outcome-blind amendments, longitudinal partial-blinding decay, parameterized templates, and rapid replication-as-preregistration workflows from the broader preregistration page.
- Verified full-text evidence from Simmons et al. (2011) supplies a complementary disclosure baseline for studies that are not fully preregistered.
- Verified full-text evidence from Open Science Collaboration (2015) shows preregistration in a distributed replication workflow: selected key effects, design protocols, analysis plans, and public OSF materials made each [direct replication protocol](../methods/direct_replication_protocol.md) auditable before outcome interpretation.

## Caveats

- Preregistration quality varies; vague plans do little to constrain analytic flexibility and should be evaluated through the [preregistration specificity gradient](preregistration_specificity_gradient.md).
- Deviations can be scientifically justified, but they need to be labeled and interpreted as deviations.
- Preregistration is not a substitute for statistical power, measurement quality, theory quality, or independent replication.
