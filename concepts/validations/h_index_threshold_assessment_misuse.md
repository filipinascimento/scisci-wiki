# H-index threshold assessment misuse

## Summary

H-index threshold assessment misuse occurs when hiring, promotion, or recruitment decisions use a minimum h-index as a proxy for individual research quality or promise.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: researcher, recruitment screen, promotion rule, CV, hiring committee, or h-index database.
- Typical representation: threshold rule, minimum-score filter, candidate ranking, or policy audit.
- Validation target: whether an h-index threshold is defensible for the decision unit, field, career age, and database used.
- Empirical signature: candidates are screened, ranked, or promoted by h-index cutoffs without portfolio reading and field/context adjustment.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Gives [h-index](../measures/h_index.md) a concrete responsible-metrics misuse page.
- Extends [portfolio-based researcher assessment](../methods/portfolio_based_researcher_assessment.md) by identifying a common scalar shortcut it should replace.
- Links author metrics to [data-led evaluation inversion](../mechanisms/data_led_evaluation_inversion.md), because a threshold can convert an indicator into an automatic decision rule.
- Provides a validation target for author-disambiguation, database-coverage, field-normalization, and career-age sensitivity checks.

## Operationalization

- Inspect recruitment, promotion, tenure, grant-review, and award rules for h-index cutoffs or default h-index rankings.
- Recalculate h-index across databases such as Web of Science, Scopus, Google Scholar, and local author-profile systems.
- Stratify by field, career age, publication volume, self-citation, coauthorship, and author-disambiguation uncertainty.
- Replace threshold rules with portfolio review, selected-work reading, contribution evidence, and field-aware citation context.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hicks et al. (2015) reports recruiters requesting h-index values for candidates and universities basing promotion decisions on threshold h-index values.
- The same article warns that the h-index rises with age, varies strongly by field, and depends on the database used.
- Hicks et al. conclude that reading and judging a researcher's work is more appropriate than relying on one number.
- These caveats make h-index thresholds a unit-of-analysis and context-validity failure rather than only a technical calculation issue.

## Caveats

- H-index can be useful as one descriptive input in a broader dossier.
- Threshold audits should distinguish formal rules from informal expectations embedded in letters, CVs, and committee talk.
- Replacing h-index thresholds with other single scores can reproduce the same misuse.
