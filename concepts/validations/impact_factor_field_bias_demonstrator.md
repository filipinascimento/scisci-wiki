# Impact-factor field-bias demonstrator

## Summary

Journal impact-factor contrasts across fields provide a compact demonstration of discipline-level citation-density differences.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: journal, field, impact factor, publication year, or citation-density contrast.
- Typical representation: same-year cross-field comparison of representative or top journal impact factors.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: visible diagnostic for why raw citation metrics need field normalization.
- Empirical signature: top or representative journals in some fields have impact-factor scales far above otherwise comparable journals in other fields.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines field-bias validation by linking it to [journal impact factor](../measures/journal_impact_factor.md) and [journal metric article level misuse](journal_metric_article_level_misuse.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [field normalized citation impact](../measures/field_normalized_citation_impact.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [cross field citation density sources](../mechanisms/cross_field_citation_density_sources.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Select same-year top or representative journals by field.
- Compare impact-factor scales across fields and report the ratio or gap.
- Use the contrast only to motivate article-level normalization, not as an article-quality measure.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Radicchi et al. (2008) contrasts mathematics journal impact factors with biomedical and general-science journals to illustrate large field differences.
- The example serves as an intuitive field-bias demonstrator.

## Caveats

- JIF is a journal-level proxy and should not be treated as article-level impact.
- Top-journal contrasts can exaggerate or simplify field heterogeneity.
