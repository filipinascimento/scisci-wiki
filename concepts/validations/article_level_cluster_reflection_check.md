# Article-level cluster reflection check

## Summary

Journal-level clusters should be checked against article-level citation structure to see whether source-title categories reflect document-level organization.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: journal cluster, article, citation link, paper-level structure, map category, or validation sample.
- Typical representation: journal-cluster versus paper-level citation-structure comparison.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: validation of journal-based science-map categories against article-level evidence.
- Empirical signature: updated journal categories better reflect article-level citation clusters when compared to paper-level evidence.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines science-map validation by linking it to [science map accuracy validation](science_map_accuracy_validation.md) and [paper field linkages](../representations/paper_field_linkages.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [field classifications](../measures/field_classifications.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [citation networks](../representations/citation_networks.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Compare journal-based cluster assignments against paper-level citation evidence.
- Use document-level checks to audit broad or multidisciplinary source-title labels.
- Report where no comprehensive paper-level classification exists and the validation remains partial.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Borner et al. (2012) states that the updated classification better reflected journal clusters when compared with paper-level citation data.
- The paper also notes that no comprehensive paper-level classification scheme existed at the time, making the check partial.

## Caveats

- Journal-level agreement with article-level evidence can still miss article heterogeneity inside broad journals.
- Partial paper-level evidence should not be overstated as full document-level validation.
