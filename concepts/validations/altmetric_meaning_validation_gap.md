# Altmetric meaning validation gap

## Summary

Altmetric meaning validation gap is the unresolved validation problem of what online attention indicators actually mean for research evaluation once they are shown not to be citation substitutes.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: altmetric source, paper, attention event, evaluation use case, or impact construct.
- Typical representation: construct-validity gap, mixed-methods validation agenda, source-specific interpretation table, or qualitative follow-up design.
- Validation target: determine whether altmetric traces reflect societal, cultural, policy, clinical, educational, or scholarly attention.
- Empirical signature: altmetrics show weak or moderate citation association, motivating complementarity claims but leaving their positive meaning under-specified.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [altmetric-citation association](altmetric_citation_association.md) beyond correlation into construct interpretation.
- Tests the [altmetric complementary-impact frame](../representations/altmetric_complementary_impact_frame.md) rather than assuming complementarity is already valid.

- Provides a responsible-metrics guardrail before using online attention in assessment.

## Operationalization

- Specify the construct each source is supposed to evidence, such as public discussion, expert commentary, media dissemination, policy uptake, or educational reuse.
- Pair source counts with event-context metadata, actor information, content coding, and qualitative samples.
- Validate source-specific indicators against external evidence relevant to the proposed construct.
- Report unsupported constructs as open validation gaps rather than implied impact claims.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Costas et al. (2015) states that altmetrics may complement citation analysis but that more research is needed to disentangle their meaning and value for research evaluation.
- The paper finds weak relationships between citations and altmetrics, arguing that altmetrics likely measure different types of impact.
- Costas et al. explicitly leave open the question of whether altmetrics provide evidence of other impacts not captured by citations.
- Their conclusion calls for validation of these other potential impacts, including qualitative studies.

## Caveats

- Lack of citation correlation does not automatically imply societal impact.
- Each altmetric source may require a different construct-validity argument.
- Provider coverage, platform behavior, and promotion can confound meaning.
