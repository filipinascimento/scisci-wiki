# Registered-report in-principle acceptance

## Summary

Registered-report in-principle acceptance is a two-stage publication workflow in which a study is conditionally accepted before results are known, based on the importance of the question and quality of the design.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: registered report, stage-one protocol, peer-review decision, stage-two manuscript, journal policy, or study.
- Typical representation: two-stage review record with a pre-results acceptance decision and a post-results adherence check.
- Mechanism or validation target: reduce outcome-dependent publication selection by moving gatekeeping before outcome observation.
- Empirical signature: acceptance is granted before data collection or outcome inspection, and final publication depends on following the accepted plan rather than producing positive results.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Turns [peer review and gatekeeping](../mechanisms/peer_review_gatekeeping.md) into a reproducibility intervention.
- Gives [preregistration and registered reports](preregistration_registered_reports.md) a concrete institutional form.
- Targets [selective reporting and publication bias](../mechanisms/publication_bias_selective_reporting.md) by making negative, null, or mixed results publishable when the design is sound.
- Supports policy experiments comparing journals, fields, and funders with and without registered-report tracks.

## Operationalization

- Identify registered-report policies and stage-one/stage-two decisions in journal workflows.
- Track whether in-principle acceptance occurs before outcome observation.
- Audit stage-two manuscripts for adherence to the accepted protocol, transparent deviations, and publication regardless of result direction.
- Compare result distributions, publication rates, design quality, and replication outcomes before and after adoption.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Nosek et al. (2018) describes registered reports as a publishing model in which the research question and methodology are peer reviewed before outcomes are observed.
- The same paper states that if reviewers judge the question and methodology as sufficiently strong, the paper receives in-principle acceptance.
- At second-stage review, Nosek et al. describe the evaluation target as study execution and adherence to the preregistered plan, not the perceived importance of the outcomes.
- Nosek et al. connect this workflow to reduced selective reporting and improved research designs through pre-results peer review.

## Caveats

- In-principle acceptance depends on journal compliance and clear stage-two criteria.
- The workflow can reduce result selection without guaranteeing high power, valid measurement, or broad external validity.
- Registered reports are easier for prospective studies than for opportunistic analyses of existing data.
