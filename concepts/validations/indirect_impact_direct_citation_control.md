# Indirect-impact direct-citation control

## Summary

Cited-by-big-hit outcomes should separate downstream influence from the possibility that already highly cited papers are more likely to be cited by later hits.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: focal paper, later big-hit citing paper, direct citation exposure, or indirect impact model.
- Typical representation: cited-by-big-hit model with and without direct-citation controls.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: whether novelty predicts indirect downstream impact beyond direct citation volume.
- Empirical signature: novelty effects persist after controlling for direct citations accumulated before downstream hit classification..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a control check to [cited-by big-hit indicator](../measures/cited_by_big_hit_indicator.md).
- Useful for separating direct and indirect novelty impacts.
- Connects downstream hit stimulation to hidden or indirect influence.

## Operationalization

- Model cited-by-big-hit status with and without direct-citation exposure controls.
- Report whether novelty effects persist after logged citation controls.
- Interpret adjusted and unadjusted models as different estimands: indirect-only versus total influence.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang et al. (2017) controls for direct citations in the cited-by-big-hit model, including logged nine-year citations.
- This supports a direct-citation exposure control for indirect-impact claims.

## Caveats

- Direct citations may mediate real influence, so adjustment can understate total impact.
- Big-hit labels require enough follow-up time and field normalization.
