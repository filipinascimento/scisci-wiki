# Investigation-notice timing gap

## Summary

Investigation-notice timing gap is the validation risk that a retraction notice may be issued before a misconduct investigation is complete, making the published reason provisional, incomplete, or misleading.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: retraction notice, investigation report, journal decision, institutional inquiry, or correction timeline.
- Typical representation: lag or ordering relation between notice publication and investigation conclusion.
- Validation target: whether notice text can be trusted as the final cause of retraction.
- Empirical signature: secondary sources later revise or clarify the cause listed in the original notice.

## Operationalization

- Record dates for publication, expression of concern, retraction notice, institutional investigation start, and investigation conclusion.
- Compare notice cause labels with later ORI, university, publisher, or legal records.
- Flag notices that predate final investigation findings.
- Update cause codes when secondary sources provide stronger evidence.
- Preserve original and revised classifications for auditability.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fang, Steen, and Casadevall (2012) argues that retraction notices are often issued before full investigation results are available.
- The paper reports that ORI investigations averaged more than 20 months over a 2001-2010 window, while notices may appear before those processes finish.
- This timing gap helps explain why relying only on notice text can understate misconduct.

## Caveats

- Waiting for final investigation results can delay correction of the literature.
- Some cases never receive public final investigation reports.
- A provisional notice can still be useful if it clearly labels uncertainty and links later updates.
