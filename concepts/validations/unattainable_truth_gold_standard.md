# Unattainable Truth Gold Standard

## Summary

Unattainable truth gold standard is the validation boundary that research findings are modeled against latent truth, even though complete knowledge of truth is generally unavailable.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: hypothesis, field, study, meta-analysis, or validation program.
- Typical representation: latent true-versus-null relationship table.
- Validation target: whether observed findings can be compared to a truth state that is only partially knowable.
- Empirical signature: large studies and meta-analyses improve credibility but still do not guarantee direct access to truth.

## Operationalization

- Treat truth status as latent unless supported by unusually strong convergent evidence.
- Use replications, meta-analyses, and triangulation as approximations rather than absolute labels.
- Report uncertainty about the truth standard when estimating false-positive or false-negative rates.
- Avoid interpreting nonreplication or confirmation as perfect ground truth.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ioannidis (2005) models findings against a gold standard of true relationships in a field.
- The paper later warns that knowing truth with certainty is impossible.
- Ioannidis describes large studies and confirmatory meta-analysis as better approximations that can still remain biased.

## Caveats

- Some domains have stronger gold standards than others, such as mortality outcomes or physical constants.
- Treating truth as entirely unknowable can make evaluation too pessimistic.
- The boundary should motivate stronger triangulation, not prevent validation attempts.
