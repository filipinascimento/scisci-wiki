# Subdiscipline replication stratification

## Summary

Subdiscipline replication stratification estimates replication outcomes within journals or subdisciplines so aggregate reproducibility rates do not hide field-practice differences.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: replication attempt, journal stratum, subdiscipline stratum, or design-family stratum.
- Typical representation: replication-success indicators by journal, subfield, design type, or topical area.
- Validation target: whether reproducibility estimates are stable across subdisciplinary contexts.
- Empirical signature: aggregate replication rates are accompanied by stratum-specific rates and uncertainty.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a validation layer to the [2008 psychology journal replication sampling frame](../datasets/psychology_reproducibility_sampling_frame.md).
- Makes [replication success indicator suite](../measures/replication_success_indicator_suite.md) comparable across cognitive and social-personality psychology.
- Links reproducibility evidence to [field classifications](../measures/field_classifications.md).
- Helps interpret [study power and false-positive risk](../measures/study_power_false_positive_risk.md) because design norms differ by subdiscipline.

## Operationalization

- Assign each original-replication pair to a journal, subdiscipline, or design stratum before outcome analysis.
- Compute the same replication-success indicators within each stratum.
- Compare strata using transparent denominators and uncertainty intervals.
- Treat small-stratum results as diagnostic signals rather than stable field rankings.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Open Science Collaboration (2015) reports replication counts by three journals and by cognitive versus social-personality psychology.
- OSC notes that reproducibility may vary by subdiscipline because practices such as within-subject designs can affect statistical power.
- The paper reports stratum comparisons for replication outcomes rather than relying only on the overall replication rate.

## Caveats

- Strata can be small, noisy, and sensitive to the initial sampling frame.
- Journal categories and subdiscipline labels may conflate topic, method, sample, and editorial-selection practices.
- Stratification diagnoses heterogeneity; it does not by itself identify the mechanism behind replication differences.
