# Diversity-program award-pool evaluation gap

## Summary

Diversity-program award-pool evaluation gap is the validation motif that workforce-diversity initiatives should be evaluated against the funded-grant pool, not only by documenting that programs exist.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: diversity program, grant applicant, application, review score, award outcome, or funded-grant pool.
- Typical representation: program participation, eligibility flag, award share, score distribution, or post-program funding outcome.
- Validation target: test whether interventions change downstream funding, not just training or participation.
- Empirical signature: diversity programs are present but their effect on funded-award composition is unknown or unreported.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [NIH R01 award disparities](nih_r01_award_disparities.md) from disparity measurement to intervention evaluation.
- Links [NIH training support indicators](../measures/nih_training_support_indicators.md) with [training-support disparity persistence](training_support_disparity_persistence.md).
- Supports program audits that follow applicants from training support through review and award.

## Operationalization

- Link program eligibility or participation to application, score, resubmission, and award records.
- Report funded-pool representation before and after program exposure.
- Compare program participants with matched nonparticipants or eligible nonparticipants.
- Separate pipeline growth from review-stage award conversion.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ginther et al. (2011) lists NIH diversity programs and notes that their effects on the funded NIH grant pool had not been reported.
- The paper's R01 audit therefore provides a downstream award-pool benchmark rather than a program-specific evaluation.

## Caveats

- Missing award-pool evaluation is not evidence that a program is ineffective.
- Program participation is selected and may require causal designs or careful matching.
- Long lags can separate training support from independent grant applications.
