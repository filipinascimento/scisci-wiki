# Peer-review regime-change continuity caveat

## Summary

Long review-panel windows can span procedural reforms, so pooled analyses need a continuity argument.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: review year, scoring rule, application format, page limit, review criterion, or panel process.
- Typical representation: review-regime audit documenting procedural changes and comparable core features.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: temporal comparability of peer-review scores.
- Empirical signature: pooled score-outcome estimates are more credible when core scoring and panel features remain comparable across reforms..

## Operationalization

- Document scoring-rule, page-limit, criterion, and review-process changes during the study window.
- Test or argue whether core reviewer counts, numeric scoring, and panel deliberation remain comparable.
- Add period interactions or sensitivity windows around major reforms when enough observations exist.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Li and Agha (2015) notes a 2006 NIH reform that shortened applications and changed score granularity while preserving core reviewer numbers and numeric scoring.
- The motif marks the continuity assumption behind pooling review-score eras.

## Caveats

- Unchanged formal features may hide shifts in reviewer behavior, application style, or funding competitiveness.
- Continuity claims should be refreshed when later NIH review reforms enter the sample.
