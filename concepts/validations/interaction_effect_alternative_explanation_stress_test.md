# Interaction-effect alternative-explanation stress test

## Summary

Interaction-effect alternative-explanation stress test asks whether an alternative explanation for an average result also explains the subgroup interaction pattern.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: coefficient, subgroup, interaction term, mechanism, alternative explanation, or robustness argument.
- Typical representation: average-effect explanation checked against heterogeneous effects by status, field, gender, rank, institution, or career stage.
- Validation target: avoid accepting a mechanism that fits the mean effect but fails the observed interaction structure.
- Empirical signature: plausible alternatives are downgraded when they predict similar subgroup responses but the data show patterned heterogeneity.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Strengthens [funding status-interaction test](funding_status_interaction_test.md) by making mechanism adjudication explicit.
- Supports [type-error asymmetric policy inference](type_error_asymmetric_policy_inference.md) when policy conclusions depend on which subgroup mechanism is credible.
- Applies beyond funding to [funded-only peer-review validation](funded_only_peer_review_validation.md), gender disparities, citation penalties, and metric effects.

## Operationalization

- State the alternative explanation for the average result.
- Derive its implication for the observed interaction or subgroup pattern.
- Compare that implication with estimated interaction signs, magnitudes, and robustness checks.
- Prefer mechanisms that explain both the average pattern and the heterogeneity pattern.
- Document alternatives that remain plausible because the subgroup evidence is underpowered.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Lee, and Walsh (2018) uses subgroup interactions to evaluate explanations for competitive funding and novelty.
- The paper argues that some alternative explanations for average funding differences do not account for why competitive funding is associated with lower novelty especially among low-status researchers.
- This makes the interaction pattern a mechanism stress test, not just a descriptive heterogeneity result.

## Caveats

- Interaction estimates can be noisy and sensitive to subgroup definitions.
- A rejected alternative for one subgroup pattern may remain valid in another institutional setting.
- Mechanism stress tests require transparent preregistration or disciplined reporting to avoid post hoc storytelling.
