# Accepted-sequence acceptance proxy

## Summary

Accepted publication sequences are sometimes used as a proxy for acceptance-related patterns when rejected submissions are unavailable, so the estimand should be stated explicitly.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: accepted publication sequence and unobserved submission attempt process.
- Typical representation: transition probability among accepted papers with a rejected-submission caveat.
- Validation target: boundary between accepted-paper histories and acceptance probability.
- Empirical signature: results describe observed successful publication paths, not all attempted submissions.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Reframes [accepted-publication transition denominator limit](accepted_publication_transition_denominator_limit.md) as a reusable design choice.
- Useful for venue histories, career transitions, and publication-access studies without submission data.
- Connects chaperone estimates to [peer review gatekeeping](../mechanisms/peer_review_gatekeeping.md) and denominator interpretation.

## Operationalization

- State whether the estimand is accepted-paper transition, submission acceptance, or author attempt behavior.
- Where possible, benchmark accepted-sequence estimates against journals with submission and rejection data.
- Avoid interpreting accepted sequences as direct acceptance probabilities without denominator evidence.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sekara et al. (2018) models sequences of accepted publications and explicitly notes the absence of rejected-paper data.
- This motivates treating accepted sequences as a proxy with a clear estimand boundary.

## Caveats

- This is close to the missing-denominator caveat but emphasizes the reusable proxy design.
- Self-selection into submission can move in the opposite direction from editorial acceptance.
