# Taxonomy Partition Sensitivity

## Summary

Taxonomy partition sensitivity is the validation caveat that diversity conclusions can shift with the subjective construction, partitioning, and linguistic bounds of categories before any formal diversity formula is applied.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: taxonomy, category system, field classification, portfolio option class, or reference category.
- Typical representation: category-construction sensitivity audit.
- Validation target: prevent diversity scores from appearing objective when category boundaries are arbitrary or perspective-dependent.
- Empirical signature: the same items can look more or less diverse under alternative taxonomic partitions.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Qualifies [category variety](../measures/category_variety.md), [distributional balance](../measures/distributional_balance.md), and [category disparity](../measures/category_disparity.md).

- Helps audit field, journal, topic, and technology-class diversity measures.

## Operationalization

- Record the taxonomy used to define categories.
- Test alternative partitions or levels of aggregation where possible.
- Report whether conclusions depend on broad labels, fine-grained labels, or implicit category bounds.
- Avoid interpreting variety or balance without describing how disparity shaped the category system.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Stirling (2007) argues that variety and balance cannot be characterized without first considering disparity, because disparity defines and partitions the taxonomy.
- Stirling gives examples where category choices change whether a portfolio appears diverse.
- The paper warns that variety or balance proxies can be highly sensitive to subjective taxonomy construction, arbitrary partitioning, and linguistic category bounds.

## Caveats

- Some taxonomies are more standardized than others, but standardization does not remove perspective dependence.
- Sensitivity analysis can reveal dependence without proving which taxonomy is correct.
- Very fine partitions can add noise, while coarse partitions can hide meaningful disparity.
