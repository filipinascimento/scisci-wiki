# Perceived-actual contribution boundary

## Summary

Perceived-actual contribution boundary is the guardrail that community-perceived contribution is not equivalent to the precise actual role an individual played in a paper or discovery.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author, paper, credit-share estimate, perceived contribution, actual contribution, or evaluation decision.
- Typical representation: interpretive caveat, perceived-credit label, mixed-method evaluation protocol, or sole-use warning.
- Validation target: prevent citation-inferred or community-inferred credit from being used as a complete account of actual labor.
- Empirical signature: credit-allocation methods estimate community perception and can inform but not replace coauthor evidence or qualitative evaluation.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Places a responsible-use boundary around [collective credit allocation](../measures/collective_credit_allocation.md).
- Connects [team science contribution assessment](../methods/team_science_contribution_assessment.md) with perceived recognition.

- Supports [assessment decision-responsibility retention](../methods/assessment_decision_responsibility_retention.md) in hiring, funding, and promotion.

## Operationalization

- Label citation-inferred credit as perceived or community-attributed credit.
- Avoid using perceived credit as the sole basis for individual assessment.
- Pair perceived-credit estimates with contribution statements, coauthor testimony, role descriptions, and qualitative review.
- Report known biases from reputation, seniority, and field-specific citation practices.
- Treat discrepancies between perceived and actual contribution as substantive evidence, not just model error.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Shen and Barabasi (2014) states that the method captures credit allocated based on perceived contribution rather than actual contribution.
- The authors clarify that their algorithm does not capture the precise role of an individual in a paper or discovery, only the community's perception of contribution as reflected by the body of work.
- They caution against turning the algorithm into the sole tool for credit allocation and recommend using it with other evaluation tools such as coauthor letters.

## Caveats

- Actual contribution can be inaccessible or contested.
- Community perception can be biased but still consequential for careers.
- Qualitative evidence also has bias and should be interpreted alongside transparent quantitative evidence.
