# Narrative inference after preregistration

## Summary

Narrative inference after preregistration is the validation caveat that even when all preregistered analyses are reported, the paper's interpretation can selectively emphasize a small subset of interesting results and thereby shape how the evidence is understood, cited, or reused.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: discussion section, abstract, highlighted result, evidence claim, citation context, or preregistered result family.
- Typical representation: planned-result inventory versus narrative-emphasis table, abstract-result alignment audit, interpretation-coding scheme, or citation-context follow-up.
- Validation target: whether narrative attention matches the full preregistered evidence base.
- Empirical signature: all planned tests are reported, but the manuscript foregrounds the most striking findings while downplaying null, mixed, or contradictory results.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [preregistration adherence and deviation audit](preregistration_adherence_deviation_audit.md) beyond plan execution into interpretation.
- Provides a narrative layer for [selective reporting and publication bias](../mechanisms/publication_bias_selective_reporting.md) even when tabular reporting is complete.
- Links [preregistration and registered reports](preregistration_registered_reports.md) to citation and evaluation systems, because readers often reuse the emphasized story rather than the full result matrix.
- Supports [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) by warning that citation counts can amplify selectively framed interpretations.

## Operationalization

- Compare preregistered outcomes and analyses with the abstract, title, figures, discussion, and conclusion.
- Code which results are emphasized, minimized, omitted from narrative discussion, or used as the main takeaway.
- Track whether later citing papers cite the full evidence pattern or the highlighted subset.
- Distinguish complete result reporting from balanced interpretation.
- Use registered-report review, disclosure checklists, or editorial audits to check narrative balance.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Nosek et al. (2018) notes that a study can follow a preregistered plan and still center the paper narrative on the most interesting subset of results.
- Nosek et al. argue that statistical corrections can help with quantitative multiplicity but do not fully address selective attention and interpretation.
- The same full text frames interpretation as valuable but emphasizes that transparency lets independent observers apply alternative interpretations to the full evidence base.
- This caveat explains why preregistration needs complementary reporting, review, and reading practices rather than being treated as a complete solution.

## Caveats

- Scientific writing necessarily prioritizes some findings; the problem is disproportionate or misleading emphasis, not interpretation itself.
- Narrative balance is harder to code than outcome omission or deviation.
- Some results deserve emphasis because they are theoretically central, better powered, or externally validated.
