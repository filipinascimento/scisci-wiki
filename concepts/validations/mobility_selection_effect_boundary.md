# Mobility selection-effect boundary

## Summary

Mobility selection-effect boundary is the validation requirement that studies of scientific mobility distinguish the effect of moving from the selection of already advantaged scientists into mobility.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: researcher, move event, institution, country, career trajectory, or citation outcome.
- Typical representation: selection caveat, matched-mover comparison, or pre-move performance audit.
- Validation target: prevent attributing mobile scholars' higher impact to mobility when mobility may select high-performing scientists.
- Empirical signature: mobility premiums shrink, vanish, or change interpretation after conditioning on pre-move performance and opportunity.

## Operationalization

- Measure researcher performance and opportunity before mobility.
- Compare movers with matched nonmovers or use quasi-experimental policy changes.
- Separate origin-country, destination-institution, field, and career-stage effects.
- Report whether a move itself changes impact after accounting for selection.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) notes that scientists who leave their country of origin outperform nonmovers in citation scores.
- The review warns that this may reflect a selection bias in which better scientists receive better career opportunities.
- It also reports that moving to a higher- or lower-ranked institution does not necessarily produce systematic impact changes, suggesting the individual researcher is central.

## Caveats

- Selection can operate through talent, prestige, funding, language, networks, and visa access.
- Mobility effects may differ for international, institutional, short-term, and forced moves.
- Null average effects can hide heterogeneous effects by field, destination, or career stage.
