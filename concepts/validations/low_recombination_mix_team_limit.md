# Low-Recombination Mix Team Limit

## Summary

Low-recombination mix team limit is the boundary condition that larger teams still perform poorly when papers lack both conventional grounding and tail novelty.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, team, recombination quadrant, or impact stratum.
- Typical representation: low-median-conventionality and low-tail-novelty cell in a recombination-impact surface.
- Validation target: the limits of team-size impact advantage.
- Empirical signature: large teams do not overcome a weak recombination profile when both novelty and conventional grounding are low.

## Operationalization

- Define low conventionality and low tail-novelty groups using the paper combination profile.
- Compare impact across team-size bins inside the low-low recombination region.
- Check whether the result holds across fields, years, and citation thresholds.
- Treat it as a team-advantage boundary rather than as a general claim about all teams.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Uzzi et al. (2013) reports that even teams have low impact at low levels of median conventionality and tail novelty.
- The paper's figure caption repeats the same pattern for larger teams in low-conventionality and low-novelty cells.
- This constrains interpretations of team advantage as independent of idea-combination quality.

## Caveats

- Low measured recombination can reflect coarse journal categories rather than genuinely weak ideas.
- The limit may differ in fields where impact is driven by infrastructure, data releases, or clinical utility.
- Low citation impact is not identical to low scientific value.
