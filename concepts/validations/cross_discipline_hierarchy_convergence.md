# Cross-discipline hierarchy convergence

## Summary

Cross-discipline hierarchy convergence is the validation motif that tests whether distinct fields show similar faculty prestige-hierarchy structures despite different scholarly practices.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: discipline, faculty hiring network, prestige hierarchy, placement-rank distribution, or field comparison.
- Typical representation: cross-field hierarchy table, similarity comparison, rank-change distribution panel, or replicated-network pattern.
- Validation target: determine whether faculty-market hierarchy is field-specific or a broader academic system pattern.
- Empirical signature: disparate disciplines show qualitatively and quantitatively similar steep prestige hierarchies.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Validates [institutional prestige hierarchy](../mechanisms/institutional_prestige_hierarchy.md) across fields.
- Provides a cross-field check for [faculty hiring networks](../representations/faculty_hiring_networks.md), [faculty hierarchy strength](../measures/faculty_hierarchy_strength.md), and [faculty placement rank change](../measures/faculty_placement_rank_change.md).
- Helps distinguish local disciplinary hiring customs from general academic labor-market structure.
- Motivates wider field coverage and replication before using a hiring-network result for policy.

## Operationalization

- Build comparable hiring networks for multiple disciplines with consistent inclusion rules.
- Infer prestige ranks separately by discipline.
- Compare hierarchy strength, upward-placement rates, rank-change distributions, and predictive validity.
- Check whether conclusions hold across fields with different publication, evaluation, and training norms.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Clauset et al. (2015) studies computer science, business, and history as distinct scholastic traditions.
- The paper finds common and steep hierarchical structure across these disciplines.
- In the discussion, Clauset et al. state that the disciplines exhibit qualitatively and quantitatively similar patterns despite broad differences in scholarly practices and evaluation standards.
- They also cite similar single-discipline results in several other fields, supporting cross-discipline convergence as a replication target.

## Caveats

- Three fields are informative but not sufficient for all of academia.
- Similar hierarchy shape does not mean identical mechanisms across disciplines.
- Differences in field size, institution definitions, and data completeness can create apparent convergence or divergence.
