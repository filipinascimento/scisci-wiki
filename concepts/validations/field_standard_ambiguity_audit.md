# Field-standard ambiguity audit

## Summary

Field-standard ambiguity audit is a validation check that asks whether analytic norms are too heterogeneous for one choice to be clearly right and another clearly wrong.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: analytic decision, exclusion threshold, transformation, covariate, field norm, or robustness claim.
- Typical representation: norm inventory, robustness grid, reviewer survey, methods comparison, or decision-tree audit.
- Validation target: identify choices whose apparent arbitrariness creates researcher-degrees-of-freedom risk.
- Empirical signature: multiple defensible field-standard choices produce materially different results.

## Operationalization

- Enumerate common field-standard options for the analytic choice under review.
- Ask whether each option would be accepted by knowledgeable reviewers before seeing results.
- Re-estimate results across the accepted option set.
- Report which conclusions depend on one defensible but non-unique standard.
- Use preregistration or explicit decision rules when ambiguity is likely.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Simmons et al. (2011) uses reaction-time outlier thresholds to show that field norms can permit several defensible choices.
- The paper argues that flexibility is dangerous partly because researchers can justify choices after seeing results.
- This motif treats norm ambiguity as a validation target rather than assuming a single correct analytic path.
- It supports reporting requirements that expose arbitrary choices and robustness alternatives.

## Caveats

- Some fields legitimately allow context-dependent analytic decisions.
- Norm inventories can lag behind methodological improvements.
- Robustness across many choices does not remove other sources of bias such as selective study reporting.
