# Self-Citation Opportunity-Set Confound

## Summary

Self-citation opportunity-set confound is the risk that group differences in self-citation rates arise from unequal prior-paper stocks, productivity, or career persistence rather than different self-citation behavior at equal output.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author, authorship, prior-paper set, career stage, gender group, or self-citation event.
- Typical representation: prior-output control, same-paper-count comparison, opportunity-set caveat, or productivity-adjusted self-citation check.
- Validation target: separate behavioral self-promotion differences from mechanical differences in what a scholar could self-cite.
- Empirical signature: an aggregate self-citation gap shrinks, disappears, or changes interpretation after conditioning on prior publication opportunities.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Qualifies [gendered self-citation gap](../mechanisms/gendered_self_citation_gap.md) and [authorship-normalized self-citation rate](../measures/authorship_normalized_self_citation_rate.md).
- Connects self-citation analysis to [gendered seniority-pipeline confound](gendered_seniority_pipeline_confound.md) and broader career-persistence differences.
- Provides a construct-validity layer for [SSRN self-citation replication check](ssrn_self_citation_replication_check.md).
- Helps avoid interpreting citation-count differences as pure preference or behavior when opportunity sets differ.

## Operationalization

- Define the set of prior papers available to each author or authorship before the citing paper.
- Compare self-citation rates within strata of prior paper count, career age, publication history, or field.
- Where person-level disambiguation is unavailable, state that opportunity-set control is incomplete.
- Use external replication data with author histories when available, but document selection into that data source.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from King et al. (2017) states that without tracking individual authors over time, the JSTOR analysis cannot control for career stage or productivity.
- The paper notes that men may have more prior papers available to self-cite, which could generate an aggregate gap even if men and women with the same output self-cite identically.
- King et al.'s SSRN appendix reports a similar aggregate gap, but notes that within equal paper-count strata men do not appear to self-cite appreciably more than women.
- The same discussion uses a simplified example to show how unequal paper-count distributions can produce self-citation gaps without behavioral differences.

## Caveats

- Conditioning on prior output can itself control away structural inequality in publication opportunity.
- Author histories require disambiguation, which can introduce homonym, synonym, and name-change errors.
- Equal paper counts do not guarantee equal field, career-stage, topic, or collaboration opportunity sets.
