# Digital Library Interface Blindspot

## Summary

Digital library interface blindspot is the caveat that expanding electronic access does not determine discovery outcomes because browsing, search, ranking, and linking interfaces mediate what users find and cite.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: digital library, search interface, archive, online journal platform, or discovery system.
- Typical representation: access-optimism caveat paired with interface-behavior mechanisms.
- Validation target: prevent online availability from being equated with broad knowledge exposure.
- Empirical signature: users with more access can still converge on narrower or more recent material if the interface favors search and links.

## Operationalization

- Distinguish access availability from discovery behavior.
- Record interface features such as search prominence, browsing paths, ranking rules, and citation links.
- Compare access-expansion effects under different interface designs.
- Avoid claiming exposure diversity from coverage alone.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Evans (2008) opens by contrasting optimistic digital-library claims with the overlooked role of the user-information interface.
- Evans argues that browsing and searching technologies had received little discussion despite their potential effect on science and scholarship.
- The paper then links interface behavior to narrower and more recent citation patterns.

## Caveats

- Some interfaces can broaden discovery when they make older, peripheral, or diverse material visible.
- The blindspot is not a claim against digital access; it is a warning about treating access as sufficient.
- Interface effects should be separated from content coverage, field composition, and publisher policies.
