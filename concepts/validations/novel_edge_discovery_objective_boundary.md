# Novel-edge discovery objective boundary

## Summary

Novel-edge discovery objective boundary is the validation warning that search-efficiency models optimized for discovering new relationships do not necessarily optimize robustness, error reduction, translation, replication, or individual career goals.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: objective function, search simulation, candidate experiment, knowledge graph, field policy, or evaluation metric.
- Typical representation: experiments per novel edge, relative loss, alternative objective set, robustness-weighted search, or repetition value.
- Validation target: whether a model's definition of efficient discovery matches the scientific or policy question being asked.
- Empirical signature: a strategy that is efficient for novel-edge discovery may be inefficient for validation, clinical development, safety, or publication incentives.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Bounds interpretation of [knowledge-network search efficiency](../measures/knowledge_network_search_efficiency.md).
- Connects [collective discovery incentive misalignment](../mechanisms/collective_discovery_incentive_misalignment.md) to explicit objective-function design.
- Provides a responsible-metrics guardrail for using discovery simulations in policy.
- Links exploratory search to [replication and reproducibility](replication_reproducibility.md), because repetition can be valuable even when it does not add a new graph edge.

## Operationalization

- State the objective function being optimized and what it excludes.
- Compare novel-edge discovery against alternative objectives such as minimizing false positives, increasing robustness, validating prior edges, clinical usefulness, safety, or social value.
- Add diminishing returns for repeated tests when repetition is scientifically useful.
- Report whether individual scientist objectives, such as publications and citations, diverge from the collective objective.
- Treat objective choice as part of model specification, not an afterthought.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Rzhetsky, Foster, Foster, and Evans (2015) defines search efficiency as experiments performed relative to novel edges discovered.
- The authors explicitly state that this definition prizes discovery of novel relationships above all else.
- In the discussion, they note alternative objective functions including minimizing error, increasing robustness of discovered knowledge, and valuing productive repetition for medical and industrial development.
- They also distinguish the global objective of science from local individual objectives such as maximizing publications or future citations.

## Caveats

- Novel-edge discovery is a useful baseline objective for exploration, but it is not a universal welfare function.
- Alternative objective functions may require data that are harder to observe than edge discovery.
- Adding many objectives can obscure tradeoffs unless the weighting scheme is explicit.
- Repetition can be valuable for validation but wasteful when it only duplicates already reliable knowledge.
