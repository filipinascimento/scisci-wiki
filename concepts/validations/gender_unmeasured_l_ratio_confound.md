# Gender-unmeasured L-ratio confound

## Summary

Flat-team analyses without gender controls retain a plausible unmeasured confound because access to lead and support roles can be gendered.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: team, author gender, inferred demographic label, L-ratio, productivity outcome, or role assignment.
- Typical representation: residual demographic-confounding audit for L-ratio outcome models.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: gender-related robustness gap in team-flatness inference.
- Empirical signature: L-ratio estimates may shift when gender composition or gender-by-role terms are included.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines flat-team confounding validation by linking it to [gendered contribution archetype gap](../mechanisms/gendered_contribution_archetype_gap.md) and [gendered author position gap](../measures/gendered_author_position_gap.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [gendered seniority pipeline confound](gendered_seniority_pipeline_confound.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [gender race stratification](../mechanisms/gender_race_stratification.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Add inferred or self-reported gender composition and gender-by-role interaction terms where ethically appropriate.
- Stratify L-ratio effects by career stage and field to test role-access differences.
- Report coverage, uncertainty, and ethical limits of any demographic inference.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified local full text from Xu et al. (2022) reports career-age and team-size controls but no gender variable or gender-stratified robustness check.
- The motif records an absence/caveat rather than a reported Xu finding.

## Caveats

- Name-based gender inference can be ethically and technically fragile.
- A missing gender control does not prove bias; it identifies a residual robustness need.
