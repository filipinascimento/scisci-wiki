# Assessment report policy boundary

## Summary

The assessment report policy boundary distinguishes policy-facing evidence syntheses from government policy documents so that policy-document mentions are not automatically interpreted as direct political impact.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: policy-related document, assessment report, government document, evidence synthesis, source organization, policy mention, or document type.
- Typical representation: document-type label, source-type typology, government/non-government split, or sensitivity analysis by policy-document class.
- Validation target: separate preliminary knowledge-transfer documents from documents that more directly express policy uptake or governmental use.
- Empirical signature: tracked policy sources include documents written by researchers for policy audiences as well as documents from government bodies.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Qualifies [policy document mentions](../measures/policy_document_mentions.md) by asking what kind of policy-related document contains the reference.
- Complements [policy mention context audit](policy_mention_context_audit.md) with a source-type boundary check.
- Helps interpret [policy-source corpus construction](../methods/policy_source_corpus_construction.md) and [policy source list transparency](policy_source_list_transparency.md).
- Links to [societal impact case studies](../methods/societal_impact_case_studies.md), because direct impact claims require more than a reference in a preparatory assessment report.

## Operationalization

- Classify tracked documents by source type: government policy, intergovernmental report, assessment report, NGO report, think-tank report, guideline, or other policy-related source.
- Record whether the document is produced by policymakers, researchers, boundary organizations, or advocacy organizations.
- Analyze policy mentions separately for government documents and assessment reports when the claim concerns political impact.
- Pair document-type classification with source-list transparency and context audits.
- Report sensitivity of impact conclusions to excluding assessment reports or other preliminary evidence-synthesis documents.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Bornmann, Haunschild, and Marx (2016) notes that Altmetric analyzes documents from governments as well as documents written by researchers who summarize research for politicians.
- Bornmann et al. describe assessment reports as narrative reviews or meta-analyses that synthesize research for potential political readers.
- The paper argues that assessment reports are preliminary stages of impact on politics; direct political impact requires analyzing documents from governments.
- This distinction supports treating policy-document mentions as policy-related attention unless document type and context justify a stronger policy-impact claim.

## Caveats

- Assessment reports can still be important pathways into policy and should not be discarded automatically.
- Government documents can cite research superficially, indirectly, or defensively, so document type alone is not semantic validation.
- Source classifications may be ambiguous for intergovernmental agencies, boundary organizations, and commissioned expert reports.
