# Registered-report second-stage adherence review

## Summary

Registered-report second-stage adherence review evaluates whether a completed registered report followed the accepted protocol and executed the study well, rather than judging the importance of the observed outcomes.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: registered report, stage-two manuscript, accepted protocol, deviation, execution-quality check, or reviewer decision.
- Typical representation: post-results review checklist tied to the stage-one in-principle acceptance.
- Validation target: keep publication gatekeeping aligned with design quality and plan adherence after results are known.
- Empirical signature: reviewers assess execution and deviations while not re-ranking the paper by outcome novelty or positivity.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Complements [registered-report in-principle acceptance](registered_report_in_principle_acceptance.md), which occurs before results are known.
- Adds an enforcement layer to [preregistration and registered reports](preregistration_registered_reports.md).
- Overlaps with [preregistration adherence and deviation audit](preregistration_adherence_deviation_audit.md), but is embedded in journal peer review.
- Connects open-science workflow design to [peer review and gatekeeping](../mechanisms/peer_review_gatekeeping.md).

## Operationalization

- Compare the stage-two manuscript with the accepted protocol, planned sample, outcomes, exclusions, and analysis plan.
- Identify deviations and classify whether they are justified, transparent, and outcome-independent.
- Review whether the study was executed with adequate quality and whether all planned analyses are reported.
- Exclude outcome importance, statistical significance, or narrative excitement from the second-stage acceptance decision.
- Archive stage-one and stage-two review records when journal policy allows.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Nosek et al. (2018) describes registered reports as a two-stage review process.
- The paper states that after the study is conducted, second-stage reviewers evaluate study execution and adherence to the preregistered plan rather than the perceived importance of outcomes.
- This motif separates post-results adherence review from the earlier in-principle acceptance decision.

## Caveats

- Second-stage review depends on clear stage-one protocols; vague plans weaken adherence checks.
- Journals need policies for justified deviations, failed implementation, and unexpected measurement problems.
- Reviewer behavior can still drift toward outcome-based judgment unless criteria are explicit.
