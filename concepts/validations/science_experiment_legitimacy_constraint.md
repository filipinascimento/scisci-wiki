# Science-experiment legitimacy constraint

## Summary

Science-experiment legitimacy constraint is the validation and governance problem that randomized experiments on scientific careers, institutions, or funding systems can alter outcomes in tax-supported systems and therefore face ethical and political pushback.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: policy experiment, research institution, funding program, scientist, or evaluation intervention.
- Typical representation: experiment legitimacy caveat, intervention governance constraint, or quasi-experiment preference.
- Validation target: identify when randomized trials in science policy are feasible, legitimate, and ethically defensible.
- Empirical signature: causal inference relies more heavily on quasi-experimental designs because direct randomization would affect real careers or institutions.

## Operationalization

- Identify who or what would be randomized and what outcomes could be altered.
- Assess consent, fairness, public funding obligations, and institutional legitimacy.
- Prefer natural experiments or transparent pilot designs when direct randomization is contested.
- Document governance safeguards and affected-party risks before interpreting experimental results.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) says experimenting on science may be one of SciSci's largest challenges.
- The review notes that randomized controlled trials altering outcomes for individuals or institutions supported by tax dollars are likely to elicit criticism and pushback.
- It expects quasi-experimental approaches to prevail in the near future.

## Caveats

- Legitimacy constraints do not eliminate the need for causal evidence.
- Some low-risk interventions, lotteries, or opt-in pilots may be ethically feasible.
- Quasi-experiments can reduce legitimacy concerns but introduce their own identification assumptions.
