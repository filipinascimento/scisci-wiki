# Assessment reform-alignment precondition

## Summary

Assessment reform-alignment precondition is the requirement that local committees, institutional leadership, policies, and implementation guidance align before researcher-assessment reforms can replace entrenched proxy-based practice.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: department, committee, institution, promotion policy, implementation plan, or reform package.
- Typical representation: readiness or alignment condition attached to a research-assessment intervention.
- Validation target: whether a reform can be implemented rather than only endorsed.
- Empirical signature: committees need shared rules, leadership support, and explicit guidance to change hiring or promotion criteria.

## Operationalization

- Check whether institutional policy, departmental criteria, committee training, dossier templates, and candidate guidance all reflect the same reform.
- Identify whether committees have authority to de-emphasize legacy indicators.
- Record implementation milestones, responsible offices, and monitoring plans.
- Audit whether new criteria are applied consistently across hiring, promotion, tenure, and annual review.
- Treat principle statements without aligned implementation as incomplete reform.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Moher et al. (2018) argues that hiring, promotion, and tenure committees need actionable steps and institutional support to modify assessment practice.
- The paper notes that changing assessment requires stakeholders to align criteria, implementation strategies, and monitoring.
- This motif distinguishes reform adoption from reform implementation.

## Caveats

- Alignment can become bureaucratic compliance if local values and evidence are not revisited.
- Top-down policy can fail if committees lack resources or if criteria are too vague.
- Strong local alignment can still reproduce local bias unless criteria are transparent and audited.
