# Predictive-evaluation false-negative harm taxonomy

## Summary

Automated impact prediction can harm science when false negatives suppress future discoveries, novel ideas, underrepresented groups, interdisciplinary work, or new fields.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: prediction system, false negative, proposal, paper, researcher, field, or harm category.
- Typical representation: harm taxonomy for false-negative predictive evaluation errors.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: suppression risk created by rejecting or defunding work predicted to underperform.
- Empirical signature: false-negative rates are elevated for novelty, interdisciplinarity, demographic groups, or emerging fields..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds harm categories to [automated predictive-evaluation safeguards](automated_predictive_evaluation_safeguards.md).
- Links [prediction feedback loops](prediction_feedback_loops.md) to selection effects.
- Supports novelty-sensitive and plural evaluation portfolios.

## Operationalization

- Track false-negative rates by novelty, interdisciplinarity, demographic group, and emerging-field status.
- Require harm audits before using forecasts for rejection, defunding, or hiring filters.
- Report suppressed-discovery scenarios in model documentation.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Clauset et al. (2017) warns that prediction tools can inhibit discovery, marginalize underrepresented groups, exclude novel ideas, and discourage interdisciplinary work and field formation.

## Caveats

- Harm categories are both normative and empirical.
- A low overall error rate can still hide concentrated harms.
