# Career-survivorship exit-censoring boundary

## Summary

Flags that observed career-impact cohorts can contain persistent mid- and late-career scientists while excluding early exits from academia.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author career, publication record, exit event, or career-impact cohort.
- Typical representation: selection and censoring audit for persistent publishing careers.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: survivorship and exit censoring in career-impact models.
- Empirical signature: long-career estimates exclude short or discontinued publication trajectories..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a selection boundary to [APS-WoS career impact panel](../datasets/aps_wos_career_impact_panel.md).
- Useful for interpreting Q estimates and random-impact rules in persistent-career samples.
- Connects publication-conditioned data to career exit mechanisms.

## Operationalization

- Report excluded short careers, model continuation separately, and compare long-career estimates against broader author panels.
- Distinguish publication inactivity from verified career exit.
- Run sensitivity checks including shorter careers where possible.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sinatra et al. (2016) notes that the main observed cohort contains persistent mid- and late-career scientists and excludes early exits.
- This supports a survivorship boundary for career-impact findings.

## Caveats

- Disappearing from publication records is not always equivalent to leaving science.
- Including short careers can introduce noisy author disambiguation and field-entry issues.
