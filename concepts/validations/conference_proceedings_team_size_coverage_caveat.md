# Conference-proceedings team-size coverage caveat

## Summary

Journal-only databases can distort team-size/disruption inference in fields where conferences are primary research outputs.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, document type, and indexed output universe.
- Typical representation: journal-only versus proceedings-inclusive team-size/disruption comparison.
- Validation target: document-type coverage bias in team-size disruption studies.
- Empirical signature: fields with conference-centered output show weaker or different journal-only patterns.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [conference proceedings coverage gap](conference_proceedings_coverage_gap.md) into team-size/disruption inference.
- Useful for computer science and engineering fields where proceedings carry major research contributions.
- Connects [Web of Science](../datasets/web_of_science.md) coverage to document-type filtering.

## Operationalization

- Replicate team-size/disruption analysis in proceedings-inclusive sources.
- Compare journal-only versus all-output slopes and audit proceedings/journal duplicates.
- Report document-type coverage and citation-window differences by field.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wu et al. (2019) identifies engineering and computer science as consistent exceptions and attributes this partly to conference-proceedings norms versus Web of Science journal indexing.
- This supports a coverage caveat specific to team-size inference.

## Caveats

- Proceedings inclusion changes citation windows, duplicate risk, and venue norms, not just coverage completeness.
- Some fields have mixed conference/journal publication cultures.
