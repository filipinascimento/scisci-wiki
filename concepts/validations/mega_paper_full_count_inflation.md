# Mega-paper full-count inflation

## Summary

Mega-paper full-count inflation is the validation risk that very large collaborations make author-level citation concentration look sharper when every coauthor receives full credit for the same highly cited paper.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author, field, mega-paper, collaboration regime, citation percentile, or counting-rule comparison.
- Typical representation: full-count versus fractional-count citation shares, with and without mega-paper fields.
- Validation target: determine whether rising elite citation shares reflect individual impact or large-collaboration credit multiplication.
- Empirical signature: full-count citation concentration rises sharply in fields with many-author mega-papers, while fractional counts or field exclusions dampen the trend.

## Operationalization

- Identify fields or subfields with many-author mega-papers.
- Compute full-count and fractional-count citation concentration separately.
- Re-estimate concentration after excluding mega-paper-heavy fields.
- Compare the timing of concentration jumps with the launch of large-scale collaborative experiments.
- Report whether the substantive conclusion survives fractional counting and field exclusion.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Nielsen and Andersen (2021) shows a sharp post-2011 rise in full-count citation concentration in physics and astronomy.
- The paper attributes this rise to large-scale collaborative experiments, including the Large Hadron Collider, producing highly cited mega-papers with unusually many coauthors.
- Nielsen and Andersen therefore exclude physics and astronomy from the main detailed trend analysis and show that citation concentration still rises incrementally in the remaining fields.
- Their full-count/fractional-count comparison makes mega-paper sensitivity a necessary validation for author-level citation inequality studies.

## Caveats

- Excluding mega-paper fields can understate genuine concentration in disciplines where large collaborations are the normal production mode.
- Fractional counting can understate legitimate joint contribution if all coauthors make essential contributions.
- The validation does not identify whether mega-paper credit is fair; it only tests sensitivity of concentration estimates.
