# Multiple-discovery status-visibility test

## Summary

The multiple-discovery status-visibility test evaluates whether, among near-simultaneous independent discoveries, the version reported by higher-status scientists receives faster or broader attention than comparable versions reported by less-known scientists.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: multiple discovery, parallel paper set, scientist, discovery version, citation trajectory, or readership event.
- Typical representation: matched set of independent discovery reports with author status and post-publication visibility outcomes.
- Validation target: whether status affects communication visibility net of discovery content and timing.
- Empirical signature: within the same multiple-discovery event, higher-status discoverers receive earlier citations, more readership, or broader diffusion.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Tests the communication side of the [Matthew effect](../mechanisms/matthew_effect.md) without comparing unrelated papers.
- Provides a design for separating recognition-driven visibility from intrinsic topic importance.
- Operationalizes [multiple-discovery redundancy function](../mechanisms/multiple_discovery_redundancy_function.md) and [eminent-scientist focalization](../mechanisms/eminent_scientist_focalization.md) in matched historical discovery sets.
- Links historical qualitative evidence from the [Nobel laureate interview corpus](../datasets/nobel_laureate_interview_corpus.md) to citation-index and readership data.
- Helps validate [recognition-visibility coupling](../mechanisms/recognition_visibility_coupling.md) and [senior coauthor credit shadow](../mechanisms/senior_coauthor_credit_shadow.md).

## Operationalization

- Build a corpus of independent multiple discoveries using historical records, priority disputes, review articles, prize narratives, or expert-curated discovery sets.
- Identify discovery reports, publication dates, author status before the discovery, institution, venue, and field.
- Measure visibility with early citations, citation speed, readership, mentions, review uptake, textbook uptake, or citation-context centrality.
- Compare higher- and lower-status reports within each discovery event, controlling for timing, venue, language, accessibility, and field.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Merton (1968) proposes examining citation indexes to test whether, in multiple discoveries by scientists of markedly unequal rank, work by higher-rank scientists is cited more promptly and widely.
- Merton also proposes interviews with working scientists about reading practices as an additional data source for the hypothesis.
- The same paper argues that visibility depends not only on how many times a discovery is independently made but also on the standing of the scientists who made it.
- This page is therefore the validation counterpart to the mechanism pages for redundant discovery diffusion and eminent-scientist focalization.

## Caveats

- Independent discovery sets are hard to define because discovery claims, timing, and conceptual equivalence are historically contested.
- Higher-status scientists may publish in higher-visibility venues or have better communication resources, requiring careful controls.
- Citation and readership outcomes can reflect later prize narratives or retrospective consolidation, not only immediate visibility.
