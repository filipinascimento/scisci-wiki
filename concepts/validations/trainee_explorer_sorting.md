# Trainee explorer sorting

## Summary

Trainee explorer sorting is the validation problem that exploratory students and postdocs may sort into protected, high-autonomy labs, making program effects partly a lab-composition effect.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: trainee, mentor, lab, funding program, early-career prize, publication, or career trajectory.
- Typical representation: trainee placement counts, future-prize outcomes, trainee publication records, mentor-lab funding status, or student/postdoc inflow.
- Validation target: distinguish a funding program's incentive effect on the principal investigator from selection of unusually exploratory trainees into treated labs.
- Empirical signature: treated labs train more high-potential or exploration-oriented trainees than controls, and those trainees contribute to observed output differences.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [funding-program alternative-mechanism audit](funding_program_alternative_mechanism_audit.md) with a specific remaining selection channel.
- Connects elite-program effects to [mentorship and chaperone effects](../mechanisms/mentorship_chaperone_effects.md) and [early-career sponsorship](../mechanisms/early_career_sponsorship.md).
- Helps interpret [funding autonomy and exploration](../mechanisms/funding_autonomy_exploration.md) when the treatment bundle changes the lab's attractiveness to junior scientists.
- Provides a trainee-composition caveat for [HHMI-NIH incentive contrast](../methods/hhmi_nih_incentive_contrast.md).

## Operationalization

- Compare student and postdoc inflows before and after funding-program appointment.
- Track trainee outcomes such as early-career prizes, independent positions, topic novelty, and publication/citation trajectories.
- Separate principal-investigator behavior from trainee-authored or trainee-driven outputs where authorship data permit.
- Test whether treated-control output differences shrink after accounting for trainee composition and trainee prior promise.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Azoulay, Graff Zivin, and Manso (2009) treats trainee sorting as a plausible alternative interpretation of HHMI effects.
- The paper reports that HHMI investigators train substantially more future early-career prize winners than controls in their sample.
- Azoulay et al. argue that principal investigators are unlikely to self-apply into HHMI because appointment depends on limited institutional nominations, but talented graduate students and postdocs may sort into HHMI-funded labs.
- The authors present this as a remaining channel consistent with observed differences, while still emphasizing the incentive features of the program.

## Caveats

- Future prizes are high-threshold outcomes and may miss broad trainee quality.
- Trainee sorting may be part of the real treatment bundle if program design intentionally creates better training environments.
- Distinguishing trainee-driven outputs from mentor-driven lab outputs is difficult when author contribution data are unavailable.
