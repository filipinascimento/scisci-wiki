# Static Science-Map Temporal Limit

## Summary

Static science-map temporal limit is the caveat that a single structural map can support navigation and comparison but cannot show field birth, evolution, decline, frontier activity, or merging and splitting without dynamic layers.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: static map, map snapshot, field, cluster, research front, or temporal overlay.
- Typical representation: snapshot boundary statement, dynamic-map desideratum, temporal overlay requirement, or map-update caveat.
- Validation target: prevent a cross-sectional science map from being interpreted as a model of scientific change.
- Empirical signature: the map describes broad structure at one time point but cannot identify dynamics without repeated snapshots or temporal indicators.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Qualifies [science maps](../representations/science_maps.md), [science map overlays](../representations/science_map_overlays.md), and decision-support map uses.
- Motivates [science map update workflows](../methods/science_map_update_workflows.md) and temporal overlays such as [topic prevalence dynamics](../methods/topic_prevalence_dynamics.md).
- Links structural maps to [research fronts](../mechanisms/research_fronts.md), which require movement and emergence evidence.
- Helps separate map-based field navigation from claims about field evolution.

## Operationalization

- State the map's source year or snapshot period.
- Avoid drawing claims about birth, death, acceleration, merging, or frontier status from a single static map.
- Use repeated maps, temporal clustering, citation trajectories, average publication year, or activity overlays when dynamics are the target.
- Preserve map-version metadata so changes can be attributed to science dynamics rather than classification or layout updates.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Boyack, Klavans, and Borner (2005) describes their whole-science map as a static map that supports decision-making and teaching of the general structure of science.
- The paper explicitly notes that the static map does not reveal how disciplines are born, evolve, or die.
- Boyack et al. point toward dynamic maps that show activity, scientific frontiers, and merging or splitting scientific areas as the needed extension.

## Caveats

- Static maps can still be useful baselines for navigation, portfolio overlays, and local neighborhood inspection.
- Differences between two static maps can reflect layout, source coverage, or classification changes as well as scientific dynamics.
- Dynamic maps require stronger versioning and comparability controls than one-time maps.
