# Competitive contributorship dispute risk

## Summary

Using self-reported contribution statements in evaluation may create incentives for disputes, strategic role claims, or contribution misbehavior.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: evaluation setting, contribution statement, role claim, dispute, correction, hiring metric, or promotion metric.
- Typical representation: risk audit for evaluative use of self-reported contribution claims.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: incentive effects of contribution metrics in competitive evaluation.
- Empirical signature: role claims become more disputed or inflated when they are tied to evaluation stakes.

## Operationalization

- Flag settings where contribution statements feed hiring, promotion, funding, or metric dashboards.
- Audit corrections, disputes, implausible role inflation, and inconsistencies across statements.
- Separate descriptive contribution analysis from evaluative scoring whenever possible.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified local full text from Robinson-Garcia et al. (2020) warns that scrutinizing self-reported contribution statements in competitive evaluation could produce disputes or misbehavior.
- The motif extends responsible-metrics concerns to contributorship data.

## Caveats

- The paper frames this as a proposed risk, not a directly measured outcome.
- Dispute data may be private or institutionally sensitive.
