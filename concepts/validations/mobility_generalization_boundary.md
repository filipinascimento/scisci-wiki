# Mobility generalization boundary

## Summary

Mobility generalization boundary is the validation caveat that findings from one discipline, publication corpus, or career-longevity cohort may not generalize to all scientific mobility.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: corpus, field, cohort, career-length threshold, mobility event, or external dataset.
- Typical representation: scope boundary attached to mobility estimates.
- Validation target: prevent overgeneralizing mobility patterns estimated from a restricted field or long-lived author sample.
- Empirical signature: authors explicitly compare the study corpus with broader datasets or identify where replication is needed.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Qualifies findings from the [Physical Review mobility panel](../datasets/physical_review_mobility_panel.md).
- Connects mobility estimates to broader sources such as [Web of Science](../datasets/web_of_science.md) and [Google Scholar](../datasets/google_scholar.md).
- Provides a boundary for [scientific mobility](../mechanisms/scientific_mobility.md) and [career mobility distance decay](../measures/career_mobility_distance_decay.md).
- Encourages replication across disciplines, countries, periods, and shorter-career samples.

## Operationalization

- State the field, publication venue, years, career-length filters, and affiliation-coverage requirements.
- Compare sample composition with broader bibliographic or profile datasets.
- Reestimate mobility metrics under relaxed filters when possible.
- Treat external generalization as an empirical question, not an assumption.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Deville et al. (2014) is based on scientists observed in Physical Review records with sufficiently long publication careers.
- The paper notes that broader datasets such as Web of Science and Google Scholar could extend the analysis beyond physics and beyond the focal cohort.
- This scope statement marks a generalization boundary for claims about scientific mobility patterns.

## Caveats

- Longer observed careers may select for more productive, more stable, or more mobile scientists.
- Physics mobility norms may differ from biomedical, social-science, engineering, or humanities careers.
- External datasets bring their own affiliation disambiguation and coverage errors.
