# Self-Citation Equity-Intervention Backlash Risk

## Summary

Self-citation equity-intervention backlash risk is the warning that encouraging under-self-citing groups to self-cite more can trigger social penalties or irrelevant citation inflation, so metric correction may be preferable.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: evaluation policy, citation practice intervention, self-promotion behavior, or self-citation-adjusted metric.
- Typical representation: intervention caveat, backlash-risk statement, metric-exclusion recommendation, or responsible-metrics design.
- Validation target: avoid equity interventions that reproduce the behavior being critiqued or penalize the intended beneficiaries.
- Empirical signature: the paper recommends awareness or self-citation-excluding metrics instead of simple exhortations to self-cite more.

## Operationalization

- Identify whether an intervention changes behavior, changes metrics, or changes interpretation.
- Check whether the behavior being encouraged can create backlash, gaming, or irrelevant citations.
- Prefer evaluation designs that report or exclude self-citations when self-citation behavior is the source of bias.
- Pair metric design with qualitative and field-specific assessment rather than self-promotion mandates.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from King et al. (2017) discusses social sanctions and backlash against women who self-promote.
- The paper explicitly warns that simply encouraging women to cite their own work more is not a simple solution because it may cause backlash and may increase irrelevant self-citations.
- King et al. suggest that hiring and tenure committees should be aware of gendered self-citation differences and consider impact measures that exclude self-citations and cumulative advantages from them.

## Caveats

- Excluding self-citations can remove legitimate intellectual continuity and field-building signals.
- Backlash risk is socially contextual and may vary by field, career stage, institution, and evaluation setting.
- Metric correction alone does not address underlying status, productivity, or opportunity inequalities.
