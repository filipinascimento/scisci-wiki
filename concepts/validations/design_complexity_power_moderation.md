# Design complexity-power moderation

## Summary

The relationship between design complexity and replication success should be interpreted together with statistical power and subdisciplinary context.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: study design, complexity rating, replication power, subdiscipline, or replication outcome.
- Typical representation: moderator model including design complexity, expected power, and subdiscipline strata.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: interaction between implementation difficulty and evidential strength.
- Empirical signature: complex designs show different replication rates depending on whether replication power and subdiscipline are controlled.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects replication moderator validation to [subdiscipline replication stratification](subdiscipline_replication_stratification.md) and [replication power expectation gap](replication_power_expectation_gap.md).

## Operationalization

- Code design complexity or implementation challenge for each replication target.
- Model replication success with complexity, achieved power, and subdiscipline indicators.
- Avoid treating complexity as a failure explanation without power and context controls.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Open Science Collaboration (2015) reports moderator analyses, expected power, and subdiscipline stratification.
- Those analyses motivate complexity-power moderation rather than one-dimensional difficulty explanations.

## Caveats

- Complexity ratings may be subjective and partly confounded with research area.
- High power does not remove all implementation or construct-validity differences.
