# Disciplinary Discovery-Modality Heterogeneity

## Summary

Disciplinary discovery-modality heterogeneity is the validation caveat that search, browse, print, and online discovery behaviors vary by field, so interface effects should be interpreted as field-conditioned.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, journal, subfield, researcher group, or discovery modality.
- Typical representation: behavioral heterogeneity caveat or fixed-effect motivation.
- Validation target: avoid treating a single discovery-channel effect as universal across fields.
- Empirical signature: fields differ in preferences for online browsing, print browsing, search, purchasing, or citation-link following.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Qualifies [online availability panel identification](online_availability_panel_identification.md).
- Provides a behavioral moderator for [online search-browse substitution](../mechanisms/online_search_browse_substitution.md).
- Helps interpret [commercial-free access channel contrast](../methods/commercial_free_access_channel_contrast.md).
- Supports field-stratified tests of [electronic access citation narrowing](../mechanisms/electronic_access_citation_narrowing.md).

## Operationalization

- Stratify discovery-behavior evidence by field or subfield.
- Include field, journal, or subfield fixed effects when estimating online-availability effects.
- Test whether the effect of access route differs by field-specific reading practice.
- Avoid pooling fields without reporting heterogeneity or fixed-effect assumptions.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Evans (2008) notes disciplinary differences in reading and discovery behavior, including online browsing preferences in biology and print-oriented behavior among medical professionals.
- Evans later uses fixed-effect specifications to compare journals and subfields only to themselves over time because reading and research patterns vary substantially by area.
- The caveat explains why the study design emphasizes within-field or within-journal shifts.

## Caveats

- Field fixed effects can control stable differences but not all changing field-specific interface adoption.
- Behavioral heterogeneity can also occur within fields by institution, career stage, and access regime.
- The caveat should not be used to dismiss broad patterns without testing moderators.
