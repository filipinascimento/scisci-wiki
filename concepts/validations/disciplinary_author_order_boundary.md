# Disciplinary author-order boundary

## Summary

Disciplinary author-order boundary states that author-order priors are useful only where a field has stable authorship conventions; alphabetic and cross-disciplinary systems break simple order-based credit.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, discipline, author list, authorship convention, prior, or credit estimate.
- Typical representation: guardrail for when first-author, last-author, or ordered-author priors can be used.
- Validation target: prevent author-order information from being applied outside the disciplinary contexts where it has meaning.
- Empirical signature: credit estimates treat order as a prior only in fields where order convention is interpretable.

## Operationalization

- Identify the discipline or field of the focal paper.
- Document whether author order encodes first-author, last-author, descending-contribution, alphabetical, or consortium conventions.
- Use author-order priors only when the convention is stable and relevant.
- Avoid simple order-based credit in alphabetic, hyperauthored, or cross-disciplinary collaborations unless external contribution evidence exists.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Shen and Barabasi (2014) contrasts biology, physical sciences, and particle physics author-order conventions.
- The paper notes that alphabetic author lists make contribution difficult to infer without exogenous information.
- Shen and Barabasi evaluate author-order priors by discipline and emphasize that credit allocation should not rely mechanically on order across all fields.

## Caveats

- Field conventions change over time and can differ by journal, subfield, or collaboration type.
- Author-order priors can reinforce local hierarchy and exclude invisible labor.
- Contribution statements may help but are not always comparable or complete.
