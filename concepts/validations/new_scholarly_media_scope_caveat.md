# New scholarly-media scope caveat

## Summary

New scholarly-media scope caveat is the warning that conventional journal citation panels may miss blogs, wikis, preprints, online peer-review venues, and other emerging media that route scholarly attention differently.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scholarly communication channel, citation panel, blog post, wiki page, preprint, online review venue, web link, or conventional journal article.
- Typical representation: scope note, excluded-media audit, journal-only denominator, web-link coverage check, or alternative-media sensitivity analysis.
- Validation target: avoid generalizing journal-index findings to all online scholarly communication when newer media are outside the dataset.
- Empirical signature: excluded channels have different age, diversity, link persistence, or attention-routing patterns from indexed journal articles.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Qualifies [electronic access citation narrowing](../mechanisms/electronic_access_citation_narrowing.md) and [CI-FSO online availability panel](../datasets/ci_fso_online_availability_panel.md).

- Encourages modern replications to add preprint servers, platform logs, blogs, wikis, recommendation systems, and open-review venues.
- Helps distinguish online journal access from broader online scholarly communication.

## Operationalization

- State whether the corpus includes only conventional journals or also preprints, blogs, wikis, online review venues, data repositories, and social platforms.
- Compare citation and link age, source breadth, and link persistence across included and excluded media where possible.
- Track web-link rot and ephemeral URLs separately from formal references.
- Treat conclusions about "online science" as journal-specific unless nonjournal channels are observed.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Evans (2008) states that the study focuses on conventional journals and therefore does not capture newer scientific media such as science blogs, wikis, and online outlets experimenting with alternative peer-review models.
- Evans notes that these media likely link to very recent scientific developments, often through ephemeral web links.
- The paper also allows that these newer channels may point to more diverse materials, making them a scope limitation for journal-only citation evidence.
- This caveat separates the observed narrowing of conventional journal references from broader claims about every online scholarly medium.

## Caveats

- New media can both broaden and narrow attention depending on platform design, audience, and recommendation systems.
- Many web links are ephemeral and difficult to recover historically.
- Coverage has changed substantially since the Evans 1998-2005 availability window, so current studies need fresh data sources.
