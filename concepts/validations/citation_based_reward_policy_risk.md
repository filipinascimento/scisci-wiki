# Citation-based reward policy risk

## Summary

Citation-based reward policy risk is the evaluation risk created when grants, awards, salaries, or institutional rewards rely on citation indicators whose predictability limits and uncertainty are not respected.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: reward system, grant decision, salary decision, award procedure, or citation indicator.
- Typical representation: policy-risk warning, metric-use caveat, or citation-based incentive audit.
- Validation target: whether citation indicators are accurate enough for high-stakes allocation.
- Empirical signature: metrics used for rewards lack demonstrated predictive power or construct validity for the decision.

## Operationalization

- Identify citation indicators used in reward decisions.
- Document the decision stakes and unit of analysis.
- Test whether the indicator predicts the intended long-run target.
- Add uncertainty, qualitative review, or non-citation evidence before allocation.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang et al. (2013) states that impact factor, Hirsch index, and short-term citation measures enter reward procedures and grants despite limited predictive power.
- The paper contrasts these practices with a journal-independent long-term-impact estimate.
- It closes by arguing that mechanistic understanding of community response is needed before using citation measures in consequential settings.

## Caveats

- Some decisions may legitimately use citation evidence as one input.
- Citation risk varies with decision stakes, time horizon, and field.
- Better prediction can still create incentive distortions if rewards are attached mechanically.
