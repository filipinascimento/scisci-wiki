# Altmetric-citation maturation asymmetry

## Summary

Altmetric-citation maturation asymmetry is the caveat that online attention often arrives quickly while citations accumulate slowly, so early altmetric traces cannot be treated as mature citation substitutes.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, altmetric event, citation, publication month, or citation window.
- Typical representation: source-specific online attention timing compared with citation accumulation windows.
- Validation target: whether fast altmetric signals can replace, forecast, or complement slower citation indicators.
- Empirical signature: online events are observed soon after publication, while citation counts remain immature for years.

## Operationalization

- Record publication date, online attention dates, and citation census dates.
- Compare altmetric counts measured close to publication with citations after multiple windows.
- Report whether the analysis is testing early detection, replacement, prediction, or complementary attention.
- Avoid treating low correlations as failure if the constructs mature on different schedules.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Costas et al. (2015) stresses that altmetric activity is earlier and more immediate than citation accumulation.
- The paper warns that altmetrics should not be treated as simple replacements for citation impact, partly because citations require longer maturation.
- Their weak positive correlations between altmetric sources and citations are interpreted through this timing and construct-asymmetry frame.

## Caveats

- Some altmetric sources also have delayed or backfilled events.
- Older papers can receive renewed online attention, so fast timing is not universal.
- Citation maturation differs by field, document type, and database coverage.
