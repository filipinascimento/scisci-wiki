# Descriptive-association causal-force gap

## Summary

Descriptive-association causal-force gap is the validation gap between large-scale associations found in science-of-science data and evidence that a specific structure or intervention actually causes an outcome.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: association, scientific structure, outcome, policy intervention, or causal claim.
- Typical representation: causal-boundary warning, counterfactual gap, or descriptive-to-policy validation step.
- Validation target: prevent descriptive regularities from being translated into policy as if they were causal.
- Empirical signature: strong observed associations remain policy-ambiguous without experiments, quasi-experiments, matching, or other causal designs.

## Operationalization

- Label descriptive, predictive, and causal claims separately.
- Ask what counterfactual would be required to turn an association into a causal claim.
- Use matching, natural experiments, randomized trials, or instrumental variation where feasible.
- Report policy recommendations as provisional when causal force is not established.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) says many descriptive SciSci studies reveal strong associations between structure and outcomes.
- The review states that whether a specific structure causes an outcome often remains unexplored.
- It calls causal estimation a needed future development and points to econometric matching and counterfactual scenarios.

## Caveats

- Some policy decisions must proceed with imperfect causal evidence.
- Causal designs can improve identification while narrowing scope or external validity.
- Predictive accuracy does not by itself establish causal force.
