# Preprint citation-exposure metric bias

## Summary

Preprint citation-exposure metric bias is the caveat that fields with strong preprint circulation can be misrepresented by journal-citation indicators that count formal journal citations within fixed windows.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: journal, field, preprint culture, citation window, or journal metric.
- Typical representation: metric bias caused by citations accruing before or outside formal journal publication.
- Validation target: identify when journal-citation metrics miss the real exposure path for articles.
- Empirical signature: fields with heavy preprint use show metric behavior not fully explained by journal citation windows.

## Operationalization

- Identify fields or journals where preprints circulate before formal publication.
- Compare preprint citations, journal citations, and metric windows.
- Check whether journal indicators undercount or mis-time early attention.
- Report preprint exposure as a source of field-specific metric bias.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from West et al. (2010) discusses field differences affecting journal metrics, including physics preprint practices.
- The paper notes that citation timing and formal journal citation windows can affect Article Influence and related indicators.
- This motivates preprint-aware interpretation of journal citation metrics.

## Caveats

- Preprint citation behavior varies by field and time period.
- Some metrics may capture later formal citations even if early exposure occurs through preprints.
- Preprint bias interacts with citation-window length, indexing coverage, and publication delay.
