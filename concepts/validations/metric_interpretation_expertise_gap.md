# Metric-interpretation expertise gap

## Summary

Evaluation systems become risky when organizations use metric platforms without bibliometric expertise, interpretation guidance, or methodological advice.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: evaluation report, dashboard, evaluator, administrator, or metric platform.
- Typical representation: expertise-availability audit for metric interpretation.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: organizational capacity to interpret and qualify quantitative indicators.
- Empirical signature: metric outputs are reported to administrators without expert review or interpretive caveats..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds capacity checks to [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md).
- Explains how [metric platform proliferation](../mechanisms/metric_platform_proliferation.md) can outpace expertise.
- Supports contestability through [evaluation-contestation access gap](evaluation_contestation_access_gap.md).

## Operationalization

- Audit evaluator training and method signoff.
- Require indicator interpretation notes in metric reports.
- Track cases where dashboard outputs are used without expert review.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hicks et al. (2015) describes metric-reliant evaluation spreading into organizations that lack good-practice knowledge and notes that experts are often absent when evaluations reach administrators.

## Caveats

- Expertise reduces misuse risk but does not make a weak indicator valid.
- Expert authority can also become a gatekeeping mechanism if not transparent.
