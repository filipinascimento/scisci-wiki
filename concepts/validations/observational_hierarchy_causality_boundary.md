# Observational hierarchy causality boundary

## Summary

Observational hierarchy causality boundary is the caveat that associations between team hierarchy and scientific outcomes remain noncausal without exogenous team-structure variation.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: team, paper, author, L-ratio, outcome model, or fixed-effect comparison.
- Typical representation: causal caveat attached to observational role-structure estimates.
- Validation target: distinguish robust association from causal effect of hierarchy.
- Empirical signature: results persist under controls and fixed effects but still lack randomized or quasi-experimental identification.

## Operationalization

- State whether hierarchy measures are observational.
- Compare same-size teams and within-author changes to reduce confounding.
- Identify remaining threats such as project selection, field norms, seniority, and unobserved team quality.
- Propose experimental or quasi-experimental designs when making policy claims.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Xu, Wu, and Evans (2022) uses controls and fixed-effect models to compare outcomes across teams with different L-ratio values.
- The paper explicitly notes that causality remains unclear even though same-researcher comparisons are suggestive.
- This caveat is important because team flatness could reflect the kind of work being attempted rather than causing novelty directly.

## Caveats

- Overstating causal language can turn a useful descriptive motif into a misleading management prescription.
- Fixed effects do not remove time-varying project selection.
- Direct intervention evidence would be needed to estimate the causal effect of changing team flatness.
