# Field Gender-Composition Self-Citation Threshold Check

## Summary

Field gender-composition self-citation threshold check tests whether a field's gender composition or token-status threshold explains self-citation behavior before attributing gaps to field mix.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, subfield, authorship gender composition, self-citation rate, or threshold level.
- Typical representation: proportion-women field ordering, linear composition test, threshold test, or token-status caveat.
- Validation target: determine whether self-citation gaps are stronger in male-dominated fields or after crossing representation thresholds.
- Empirical signature: self-citation rates or ratios are modeled against field gender composition and candidate representation thresholds.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Qualifies [gendered self-citation gap](../mechanisms/gendered_self_citation_gap.md) and [subfield self-citation ratio heterogeneity](../measures/subfield_self_citation_ratio_heterogeneity.md).
- Connects self-citation behavior to [gendered discipline output sorting](../measures/gendered_discipline_output_sorting.md) and [local gender-disparity micro-mechanisms](../mechanisms/local_gender_disparity_micro_mechanisms.md).
- Uses field assignments from [hierarchical map-equation field classification](../methods/hierarchical_map_equation_field_classification.md).
- Helps separate field-composition explanations from generalized status or self-promotion mechanisms.

## Operationalization

- Estimate the proportion of women authorships or authors in each field or subfield.
- Compute men and women self-citation rates within the same field partitions.
- Test linear association between field gender composition and gender-specific self-citation rates.
- Test threshold candidates, such as token-status cutoffs, while documenting whether the composition measure is authorships or disambiguated people.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from King et al. (2017) asks whether field gender composition correlates with self-citation rates.
- The paper reports no significant linear relationship between women's or men's self-citation rates and the proportion of women authorships in a field.
- King et al. also test threshold-style interpretations and report little evidence of a cutoff at which self-citation behavior changes.
- The appendix emphasizes that their available composition measure is authorships, not the proportion of people in the field, because the authors are not fully disambiguated.

## Caveats

- Authorship composition can differ from workforce composition because productivity and authorship norms vary by field.
- Threshold effects may vary by institution, subfield, seniority, or local culture rather than broad field.
- Sparse fields can yield unstable self-citation ratios and confidence intervals.
