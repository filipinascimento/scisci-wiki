# Self-Report Network Mapping Limits

## Summary

Self-report network mapping limits are the scale and subjectivity constraints that arise when social networks are reconstructed from surveys or interviews.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: respondent, reported tie, social network, community, or survey instrument.
- Typical representation: elicited friendship or acquaintance graph with known response burden.
- Validation target: whether self-reported ties can support large, comparable network measurements.
- Empirical signature: small samples, high labor cost, and inconsistent meanings of acquaintance or friendship.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Motivates publication-trace methods such as [coauthorship networks](../representations/coauthorship_networks.md).
- Clarifies why [publication-record network scale advantage](../methods/publication_record_network_scale_advantage.md) matters.
- Adds a measurement caveat to [coauthorship affiliation-network validity](coauthorship_affiliation_network_validity.md).

## Operationalization

- Document the tie question and respondent burden.
- Compare sample size and tie-definition consistency with bibliometric or administrative traces.
- Report likely recall and interpretation biases.
- Use self-reports when the target is subjective relationship meaning, not only large-scale topology.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Newman (2001) states that survey and interview network studies are labor intensive and usually limited to tens or hundreds of people.
- The same passage notes sensitivity to subjective bias because respondents differ in what they consider an acquaintance.
- Newman uses these limits to motivate precise, large-scale coauthorship-network reconstruction from publication records.

## Caveats

- Self-report data can capture informal collaboration and relationship strength better than byline traces.
- Survey limits depend on design, sampling frame, and respondent incentives.
- Bibliometric substitutes introduce their own validity boundaries.
