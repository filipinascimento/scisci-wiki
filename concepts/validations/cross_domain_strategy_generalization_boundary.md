# Cross-domain strategy generalization boundary

## Summary

Cross-domain strategy generalization boundary is the validation caution that career-strategy regularities found across science, art, and film may still differ in strength, measurement, and mechanism by domain.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: creative domain, career-history dataset, representation method, effect size, style/topic unit, or domain-specific mechanism.
- Typical representation: cross-domain comparison table, domain-specific robustness check, method-difference caveat, or generalization boundary.
- Validation target: distinguish broad regularity from identical mechanism across domains.
- Empirical signature: the same qualitative pattern appears in multiple domains, but effect sizes, representation spaces, career structures, and collaboration meanings differ.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Qualifies [cross-domain creative career histories](../datasets/cross_domain_creative_career_histories.md) and [career work embedding trajectories](../methods/career_work_embedding_trajectories.md).

- Helps interpret why [hot-streak team-size phase shift](hot_streak_team_size_phase_shift.md) is science-specific even when exploration/exploitation patterns are cross-domain.
- Supports cautious transfer of creative-career findings into science policy, art markets, or film-industry decisions.

## Operationalization

- Report domain-specific datasets, output definitions, impact measures, and representation methods.
- Compare qualitative direction and quantitative effect size separately.
- Run within-domain robustness checks before claiming cross-domain universality.
- Avoid applying domain-specific mechanisms such as scientific team structure to domains where the entity has no equivalent.
- Identify whether differences arise from data, methods, institutions, or actual career dynamics.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Liu et al. (2021) finds exploration-before and exploitation-during hot-streak patterns across artists, film directors, and scientists.
- The discussion notes that there may be important domain-specific differences in the role of exploration, exploitation, and success.
- The paper reports preliminary evidence that exploration and exploitation levels in science appear stronger than in art or film directing.
- Liu et al. also note that the number of styles or topics varies substantially across domains and that differences may come from data and methods as well as real domain differences.

## Caveats

- Cross-domain consistency can mask different causal mechanisms.
- Domain-specific impact measures, such as citations, auction prices, and ratings, are not directly interchangeable.
- Representation methods differ: images, plots/casts, and co-citing networks encode different aspects of creative work.
- Policy transfer should be treated as a hypothesis requiring domain-specific validation.
