# Funding-program alternative-mechanism audit

## Summary

Funding-program alternative-mechanism audit tests whether an observed program effect reflects incentive design rather than resources, prestige, peer effects, trainee sorting, or latent selection.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: funding program, funded scientist, comparison scientist, lab, trainee, collaboration tie, paper, or grant application.
- Typical representation: mechanism checklist, falsification outcome, mediation audit, spillover check, or alternative-explanation discussion.
- Validation target: separate the effect of a funding contract from correlated status, resource, selection, and network mechanisms.
- Empirical signature: the main treatment interpretation remains plausible after alternative channels are checked or bounded.

## Operationalization

- Identify plausible alternative mechanisms before interpreting a treatment estimate.
- Test resource effects with funding levels, output per dollar, or application behavior.
- Separate reduced conventional funding into application substitution, conditional review scores, and award outcomes.
- Test status or ascription effects with pre-treatment work, citation-rate shifts, or reputation controls.
- Test peer and collaboration spillovers by identifying treated-control coauthors.
- Test trainee sorting by tracking whether high-potential students and postdocs disproportionately enter treated labs.
- State which alternatives are ruled out, dampen the estimate, remain unresolved, or imply a different interpretation.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Azoulay et al. (2009) explicitly audits alternative explanations for HHMI effects after estimating treatment effects.
- The paper discusses whether results are driven by resources rather than productivity, HHMI status and ascription, peer effects from treated-control collaboration, explorer-type selection, and trainee sorting.
- Azoulay et al. argue that pre-appointment citation-rate shifts are too small to explain the results as pure status effects and that treated-control collaboration would likely dampen rather than inflate the estimated HHMI effect.
- They treat trainee sorting into HHMI labs as a plausible remaining channel, while still arguing that incentive features are the likely driver of observed differences.
- The status and trainee channels are now stored as separate reusable validation motifs for other elite-program and prize-assignment studies.
- The same full text uses application behavior, priority scores, per-dollar publication output, and scalability caveats as checks on simple incentive-effect interpretations.

## Caveats

- Alternative-mechanism audits are usually partial and cannot prove a single mechanism.
- Some alternatives, such as prestige and resources, may be part of the program's real treatment bundle.
- A remaining unresolved mechanism should be represented as uncertainty, not hidden in the causal claim.
