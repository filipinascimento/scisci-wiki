# Disparity-abundance viability boundary

## Summary

A category that is highly disparate may still be too rare or nonviable to support the intended diversity interpretation, so disparity must be read with abundance and balance.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: category, abundance share, disparity value, viability threshold, or portfolio option.
- Typical representation: joint check of disparity, abundance, and balance before interpreting portfolio diversity.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: boundary between distant options and viable diversity contributions.
- Empirical signature: a distant category contributes little to realized diversity if its abundance is negligible or below the analysis threshold.

## Operationalization

- Inspect category shares for highly disparate categories before interpreting diversity gains.
- Set and disclose minimum abundance or viability thresholds when needed.
- Report whether diversity changes come from meaningful option support or tiny distant tails.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Stirling (2007) argues that variety, balance, and disparity are individually insufficient and must be considered jointly.
- This motivates checking that disparity contributions are supported by enough abundance to be analytically meaningful.

## Caveats

- Viability thresholds can suppress emerging categories if set too high.
- Tiny categories may be strategically important even when they contribute little to scalar diversity.
