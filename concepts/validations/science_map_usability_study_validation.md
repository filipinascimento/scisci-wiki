# Science-map usability study validation

## Summary

Science-map usability study validation checks whether different user groups can read, interpret, and use a science map for real navigation, overlay, and comparison tasks.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: map user, task, interaction, overlay, interpretation, or usability-study finding.
- Typical representation: formal or informal usability study across novice and expert users.
- Validation target: ensure that a science map is not only structurally accurate but usable for intended audiences.
- Empirical signature: users can perform tasks such as zooming, filtering, reading overlays, and comparing expertise profiles without systematic misunderstanding.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a human-validation layer to [science-map desiderata checklist](science_map_desiderata_checklist.md).
- Complements [science map accuracy validation](science_map_accuracy_validation.md), which focuses more on structural quality.
- Applies directly to [science map overlays](../representations/science_map_overlays.md) and interactive map deployments.

## Operationalization

- Define representative map-reading and overlay tasks.
- Recruit users from relevant groups, such as students, researchers, funders, and administrators.
- Observe errors, task completion, interpretation differences, and feature use.
- Revise map design, legends, tooltips, and aggregation levels based on the findings.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Borner et al. (2012) lists interactivity, zoom, filter, details-on-demand, legends, and tooltips as desirable map features.
- The discussion states that current work aims to study UCSD map usability through formal and informal studies in VIVO and Sci2.
- The paper emphasizes that novice and expert users may read and interpret the map differently.

## Caveats

- Usability findings can depend on the interface, not only the underlying science map.
- A map can be usable for one task and misleading for another.
- User studies need repeated updates when the map, audience, or deployment environment changes.
