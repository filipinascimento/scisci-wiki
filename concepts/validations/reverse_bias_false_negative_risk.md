# Reverse bias false-negative risk

## Summary

Reverse bias false-negative risk is the validation concern that true relationships can be hidden or annulled by measurement error, inefficient data use, analytic choices, or interests that suppress significant findings.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: study, true relationship, measurement process, analysis plan, reporting process, or field.
- Typical representation: false-negative bias mechanism, missed-detection audit, suppressed-finding risk, or reverse-bias sensitivity analysis.
- Validation target: avoid treating all bias as producing false positives; some bias reduces or hides true effects.
- Empirical signature: credible evidence suggests a true effect exists but study design, measurement, analysis, or reporting makes it disappear.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Complements [research bias parameter](../measures/research_bias_parameter.md), which usually emphasizes distortion toward positive findings.
- Links [study power and false-positive risk](../measures/study_power_false_positive_risk.md) to measurement quality and reporting incentives.
- Helps interpret null results in [negative findings evidence value](../mechanisms/negative_findings_evidence_value.md).
- Provides a caveat for fields where interests may bury inconvenient findings or where noisy measurement masks real relationships.

## Operationalization

- Audit measurement error, outcome reliability, missing data, and analysis inefficiency.
- Check whether prespecified primary outcomes or known high-quality measures show different patterns from flexible secondary analyses.
- Look for unpublished, buried, or selectively de-emphasized significant findings.
- Use sensitivity analyses to estimate how much measurement error or reporting suppression would be needed to hide a plausible effect.
- Distinguish reverse bias from ordinary type-II error due to low power.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ioannidis (2005) explicitly notes that true findings can occasionally be annulled by reverse bias.
- Ioannidis lists large measurement errors, inefficient data use, failure to notice significant relationships, and conflicts of interest that bury significant findings as examples.
- The paper states that reverse bias can be modeled analogously to ordinary bias, but suggests it is probably less common across diverse biomedical fields.
- Ioannidis distinguishes reverse bias from chance variability that misses a true relationship.

## Caveats

- Reverse bias is difficult to quantify without access to protocols, raw data, unpublished analyses, or adjudicated true effects.
- The possibility of reverse bias should not be used to rescue weak claims without independent evidence.
- A field can suffer from both false-positive bias and false-negative reverse bias at the same time.
