# First-author credential null boundary

## Summary

First-author credential null boundary is the validation motif that authorship-position signals can differ in grant-review relevance, so first-author output should not be assumed to carry the same signal as last-author or PI-like output.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: applicant, publication, author position, bibliometric credential, or grant-review outcome.
- Typical representation: first-author count, last-author share, PI-role proxy, biomedical author order, or null credential effect.
- Validation target: test authorship-position indicators separately rather than collapsing them into one productivity signal.
- Empirical signature: one authorship-position measure predicts grant success while another has no significant association.

## Operationalization

- Estimate first-author, last-author, corresponding-author, and total-publication indicators separately.
- Stratify by field and career stage.
- Test whether authorship-position indicators add explanatory power beyond citation and productivity measures.
- Avoid exporting biomedical author-order interpretations to fields with alphabetical or equal-contribution norms.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ginther et al. (2011) reports that the share of last-authored papers predicts R01 award success, while first-authored paper count does not have a significant effect in the full model.
- The contrast shows that not all byline credentials are interchangeable in grant-review contexts.

## Caveats

- Null findings can reflect measurement noise or field heterogeneity.
- Authorship position is an imperfect proxy for contribution.
- Grant-review criteria may change over time and by study section.
