# Parametric Basis Substitution Boundary

## Summary

Parametric basis substitution boundary is the validation rule that domain-specific parametric diversity models are preferable when well grounded, while non-parametric heuristics are needed when no uniquely plausible structure exists.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: diversity index, portfolio appraisal, category system, or disparity model.
- Typical representation: method-selection boundary between parametric and non-parametric diversity measures.
- Validation target: avoid replacing grounded domain structure with generic indices, or imposing parametric assumptions where they are unsupported.
- Empirical signature: strong theoretical or empirical structure justifies parameters in some domains, but other domains lack a robust basis.

## Operationalization

- Identify whether the application has an empirically grounded parametric model of diversity.
- If yes, justify any non-parametric substitution.
- If no, state why a general heuristic or non-parametric index is being used.
- Report assumptions about category structure, abundance distribution, covariance, or distance geometry.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Stirling (2007) reviews domains where diversity can be tied to specific parameters, such as covariance in finance or branching structures in phylogenetics.
- Stirling notes that even mature fields often substitute non-parametric measures and that many fields lack a uniquely plausible parametric basis.
- The paper also warns that parameterization assumptions can be heroic when categorization and variance are complex.

## Caveats

- Non-parametric does not mean assumption-free.
- Parametric models can be useful even if imperfect, especially when assumptions are testable.
- The boundary should be revisited as better domain structure or data become available.
