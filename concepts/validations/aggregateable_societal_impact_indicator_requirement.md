# Aggregateable societal-impact indicator requirement

## Summary

For institutional assessment, a societal-impact indicator must support standardization, aggregation, continuous tracking, and entity comparison.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: societal-impact indicator, institution, field, time period, or assessment entity.
- Typical representation: maturity checklist for denominator coverage, normalization, aggregation, and comparability.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: whether an indicator can support institutional-level aggregation.
- Empirical signature: candidate indicators differ in their ability to be standardized, tracked, normalized, and compared across units..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds an aggregation requirement to [target-oriented impact metrics](../measures/target_oriented_impact_metrics.md).
- Useful for deciding whether sparse policy mentions can support institutional assessment.
- Connects indicator maturity to coverage thresholds and time-area normalization.

## Operationalization

- Evaluate candidate indicators for denominator coverage, field/time normalization, aggregation stability, and entity-level comparability.
- Report maturity gaps before institutional ranking or benchmarking.
- Separate aggregateability from substantive validity.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Bornmann et al. (2016) notes that quantitative indicators are attractive because they can be standardized and aggregated for continuous university-level comparison.
- The discussion motivates aggregateability as a requirement for some assessment contexts.

## Caveats

- Aggregateability is not validity; source and context audits remain necessary.
- Strong aggregation can erase local mission and qualitative context.
