# Collaboration-spillover null bias

## Summary

Treatment-control collaboration can contaminate comparisons and bias estimated funding-program effects toward zero.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: treated scientist, control scientist, coauthorship tie, peer effect, outcome spillover, or treatment contrast.
- Typical representation: spillover audit over treatment-control coauthorship networks.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: interference and contamination in matched career comparisons.
- Empirical signature: control outcomes may improve when controls collaborate with treated scientists, attenuating treatment estimates..

## Operationalization

- Count coauthorship ties between treated and control scientists before and after treatment.
- Assess whether collaborations plausibly transmit resources, ideas, or prestige from treated to controls.
- Run sensitivity checks excluding close collaborators or modeling network interference.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Azoulay et al. (2009) reports many coauthorship ties between HHMI investigators and controls and argues that such peer effects would improve controls and reduce estimated HHMI effects.
- The motif treats treatment-control collaboration as a possible null bias.

## Caveats

- The argument assumes spillovers are positive and mainly flow from treated scientists to controls.
- Collaboration can also reflect preexisting similarity or reciprocal benefits.
