# Authorship-control sequence amplification

## Summary

Authorship-control sequence amplification is the validation motif that explore-then-exploit effects can be compared before and after collaboration and authorship controls to test whether the sequence signal is robust or mediated.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist career, entropy sequence, authorship pattern, team size, collaboration control, or effect-size estimate.
- Typical representation: uncontrolled sequence effect, controlled sequence effect, team-size adjustment, authorship adjustment, or amplification after controls.
- Validation target: determine whether collaboration structure explains, masks, or amplifies exploration-exploitation associations.
- Empirical signature: the sequence effect remains or grows after controlling for authorship and collaboration variables.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [exploration-exploitation effect-size modesty](exploration_exploitation_effect_size_modesty.md).
- Connects sequence analysis to [hot-streak team-size phase shift](hot_streak_team_size_phase_shift.md).
- Helps separate intellectual search dynamics from team-composition dynamics.

## Operationalization

- Estimate explore-then-exploit associations with and without team-size, author-position, and collaboration controls.
- Report whether controls attenuate, eliminate, or amplify the sequence coefficient.
- Treat collaboration variables as possible confounders, mediators, or mechanisms.
- Run sensitivity checks for single-author, first-author, and corresponding-author subsets where possible.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Liu et al. (2021) states that the exploration-to-exploitation effect appears to magnify after accounting for authorship and collaboration in scientific careers.
- The paper also reports team-size changes around hot-streak onset, making collaboration controls central to interpretation.
- This motif isolates the validation logic around controls rather than the team-size finding itself.

## Caveats

- Authorship and collaboration may be mediators rather than confounders.
- Overcontrol can remove part of the mechanism being studied.
- Supplementary control results require careful replication across fields and authorship conventions.
