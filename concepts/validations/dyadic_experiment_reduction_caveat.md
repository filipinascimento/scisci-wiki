# Dyadic experiment-reduction caveat

## Summary

Dyadic experiment-reduction caveat notes that multi-entity scientific investigations are often reduced to pairwise candidate relationships, making them tractable while omitting higher-order experiment structure.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: experiment, entity pair, candidate edge, multi-entity study, chemical relationship, or knowledge graph.
- Typical representation: dyadic edge approximation, pairwise candidate set, higher-order omission caveat, or model-scope note.
- Validation target: clarify when an edge-search model simplifies real experiments into pairwise relationships.
- Empirical signature: scientists may study several entities together, but the model treats discovery as selecting and testing entity pairs.

## Operationalization

- Define the modeled discovery unit as a pairwise edge.
- State which higher-order combinations, conditions, dosages, methods, or contexts are omitted.
- Test whether conclusions persist under hypergraph, multiplex, or multi-entity formulations when data permit.
- Avoid interpreting dyadic edge discovery as a complete representation of experimental design.
- Record the simplification as part of the model boundary.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Rzhetsky et al. (2015) states that scientists select a pair of entities from all possible pairs and test that pair for an empirical relationship in the model.
- The paper notes that scientists often study several entities in combination, which complicates modeling.
- The authors therefore approximate the discovery process with dyadic strategies.

## Caveats

- Pairwise edges can hide interaction effects and context-dependent outcomes.
- Higher-order experiments may be more expensive, riskier, and more informative than dyadic tests.
- Dyadic reductions can still be useful when pairwise traces dominate the available literature data.
