# H-index extreme-tail field gap

## Summary

Cross-field h-index differences can be larger in elite tails than at average levels, so field adjustment should examine distributional tails, not only central tendencies.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field-level author h-index distribution.
- Typical representation: median, high-percentile, and maximum h-index comparisons by field.
- Validation target: field differences in attainable h at elite ranks.
- Empirical signature: between-field gaps widen at the 90th, 99th, or recognition-cohort tail.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines the [h-index field-scale caveat](h_index_field_scale_caveat.md) for prize, academy, and elite-career comparisons.
- Connects author metrics to [citation elite concentration](../measures/citation_elite_concentration.md) and large-team citation environments.
- Useful when calibrating field-normalized h-index benchmarks for senior scholars.

## Operationalization

- Build author h-index distributions by field, cohort, and database source.
- Compare median, upper percentiles, maximum values, and recognition-cohort tails rather than a single field multiplier.
- Test whether field-normalized thresholds preserve comparable tail selectivity.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hirsch (2005) contrasts high-end h-index values in life sciences and physics and notes that the elite-tail gap can exceed the average gap.
- The observation motivates distribution-aware field normalization.

## Caveats

- Extreme tails are sensitive to database coverage, author disambiguation, team size, and living/deceased cohort composition.
- Tail field gaps should not be interpreted as field quality differences.
