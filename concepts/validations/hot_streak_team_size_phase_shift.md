# Hot-streak team-size phase shift

## Summary

Hot-streak team-size phase shift is the pattern where scientists publish with smaller teams before a hot streak and larger teams during the hot streak.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist career, paper, author team, hot-streak-aligned window, team-size distribution, or randomized-career baseline.
- Typical representation: onset-aligned team-size trajectory and observed-over-randomized team-size ratios.
- Validation target: test whether exploration and exploitation around hot streaks are accompanied by collaboration-structure changes.
- Empirical signature: team size drops below expectation before onset and rises above expectation during the hot streak.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Links [exploration-exploitation career shifts](../mechanisms/exploration_exploitation_career_shifts.md) to [team size disruption](../mechanisms/team_size_disruption.md).
- Adds collaboration structure to [scientific career impact dynamics](../mechanisms/scientific_career_impact.md) rather than treating hot streaks as purely individual.
- Provides a career-event version of small-team exploration and large-team development motifs.
- Helps generate hypotheses about how collaboration changes mediate movement from exploration to exploitation.
- Splits collaborator renewal into [fresh collaborator hot-streak onset](../mechanisms/fresh_collaborator_hot_streak_onset.md).

## Operationalization

- Identify hot-streak onset and duration for scientists.
- Track team size for papers before and during the hot streak.
- Compare observed team-size trajectories with randomized onset baselines.
- Compute distribution ratios of real team sizes to randomized-career team sizes.
- Test robustness by field, self-citation adjustment, publication year, research field, and career-stage controls.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Liu et al. (2021) analyzes collaboration patterns around hot-streak onset for scientific careers only.
- The paper reports that scientists are more likely to explore with small teams before a hot streak and exploit with large teams after the hot streak begins.
- Liu et al. measure both average team-size dynamics around onset and a team-size distribution normalized by randomized careers.
- The paper also reports an increase in new collaborators at hot-streak onset and notes robustness across disciplines, self-citation adjustment, and regression controls.

## Caveats

- The team-size result is observational and may be mediated by topic, funding, institution, or career-stage changes.
- Team roles and collaboration meanings vary across domains, so the paper restricts this analysis to science.
- Larger teams during exploitation may reflect scaling, lab growth, or opportunity rather than a deliberate strategy.
- Team-size and collaborator changes remain subject to [hot-streak observational causal boundary](hot_streak_observational_causal_boundary.md).
