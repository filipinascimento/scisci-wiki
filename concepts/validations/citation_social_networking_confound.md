# Citation social-networking confound

## Summary

Citation social-networking confound is the risk that citation impact reflects social familiarity, collaboration ties, or geographic proximity as well as intellectual merit.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: citation edge, collaboration edge, city pair, author pair, paper, or field.
- Typical validation: compare citation flows with collaboration or social-contact networks.
- Validation target: interpretation of citations as merit or knowledge-use evidence.
- Empirical signature: citation probabilities are higher among socially or geographically connected actors.

## Operationalization

- Measure citation flows and collaboration flows over the same units.
- Test whether citation probability changes with prior collaboration, distance, or shared location.
- Interpret residual citation effects after accounting for social exposure.
- Report when citations may reflect visibility or familiarity rather than only merit.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Pan, Kaski, and Fortunato (2012) discusses how social networking can influence citation patterns.
- The paper studies both citation and collaboration networks between cities, making it possible to compare intellectual and social flows.
- It cautions that citations can be affected by geography and social interaction.

## Caveats

- Social exposure can be a legitimate route of knowledge diffusion, not only a bias.
- Collaboration and citation data are both incomplete traces of social interaction.
- Confounding strength can vary by field, distance, and career stage.
