# Statistics source-field delayed-recognition audit

## Summary

Statistics appears as an undernoticed source field for major sleeping beauties and method diffusion.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: high-B paper, journal field, statistical method, citing field, method diffusion path, or top sleeping-beauty list.
- Typical representation: field-origin audit of high-B papers with special attention to statistics.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: source-field visibility in delayed-recognition cases.
- Empirical signature: top WoS sleeping beauties include statistics papers that awakened through cross-field method use..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines field-origin validation by linking it to [statistical method delayed recognition](../mechanisms/statistical_method_delayed_recognition.md) and [sleeping beauty field origin profile](../measures/sleeping_beauty_field_origin_profile.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [cross disciplinary awakening](../mechanisms/cross_disciplinary_awakening.md) is interpreted from citation histories.
- Adds cross-links to [social science sleeping beauty presence audit](social_science_sleeping_beauty_presence_audit.md) so delayed-recognition and ranking motifs stay connected.

## Operationalization

- Rank high-B papers by field and flag source disciplines newly visible relative to prior sleeping-beauty literature.
- Inspect method diffusion across citing fields for statistics-origin cases.
- Compare journal-field labels with citation-context or application-field labels.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ke et al. (2015) notes two statistics papers among top WoS sleeping beauties, including Pearson's 1901 paper and Wilson's 1927 interval paper.
- The motif identifies statistical methods as a source of delayed recognition beyond physics-centered examples.

## Caveats

- Journal field assignment is coarse.
- Statistics papers often awaken through cross-field method adoption, so citing-field evidence is needed.
