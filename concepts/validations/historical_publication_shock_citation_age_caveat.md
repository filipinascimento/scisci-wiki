# Historical publication-shock citation-age caveat

## Summary

Historical publication-shock citation-age caveat warns that war, crisis, database disruption, or other publication-volume shocks can distort citation-age curves and inferred literature-growth rates.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: cited publication year, citation-age curve, field, historical period, database snapshot, or growth-index estimate.
- Typical representation: dips or troughs in cited-year distributions relative to a fitted exponential growth baseline.
- Validation target: avoid interpreting production shocks as ordinary citation aging, obsolescence, or research-front immediacy.
- Empirical signature: anomalous troughs appear at historically disrupted publication years and recover symmetrically or according to known production changes.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Qualifies [research-front citation share](../measures/research_front_citation_share.md) and [citation multiplicity-age gradient](../measures/citation_multiplicity_age_gradient.md).
- Adds historical-period controls to [citation aging and obsolescence](../mechanisms/citation_aging_obsolescence.md) analyses.
- Protects [growth-normalized citation-age baseline](../methods/growth_normalized_citation_age_baseline.md) and [citation immediacy effect curve](../measures/citation_immediacy_effect_curve.md) estimates from treating production shocks as normal age effects.
- Connects citation-age methods to [citation data census dates](../methods/citation_data_census_dates.md) and database coverage audits.
- Helps modern studies handle pandemic, war, database, or policy shocks in publication and citation denominators.

## Operationalization

- Plot cited-year or citation-age distributions against a [growth-normalized citation-age baseline](../methods/growth_normalized_citation_age_baseline.md).
- Mark known publication shocks, database expansions, source coverage changes, and field disruptions.
- Exclude, model separately, or sensitivity-test affected years when estimating growth and immediacy curves.
- Compare citation dips with independent publication-count or workforce indicators.
- Report whether the shock affects cited-paper supply, citing-paper demand, or both.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Price (1965) uses cited-year distributions from the 1961 Science Citation Index and observes dips associated with World War I and World War II.
- Price interprets the troughs as declines in paper production, not as normal citation aging.
- The paper notes that production dropped to roughly half of expected levels around the wartime troughs and then recovered.
- Price cautions against using affected intervals for normal growth-index comparisons.
- The same citation-age curve is otherwise used to separate long-run literature growth from the immediacy effect.

## Caveats

- Historical shocks can overlap with source-coverage changes, language shifts, and changes in reference practices.
- Not every anomaly in a citation-age curve has an obvious external event explanation.
- Removing shock years can bias estimates if the shock itself is substantively part of the research question.
