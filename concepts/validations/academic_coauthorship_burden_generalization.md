# Academic coauthorship burden generalization

## Summary

Rising coauthorship outside patenting provides external evidence that knowledge burden pushes innovation toward teams.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: publication, author team, field, cohort, doctorate duration, or coauthorship trend.
- Typical representation: cross-domain validation using academic authors-per-paper trends.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: generalizability of team response to knowledge burden beyond patents.
- Empirical signature: authors per paper and team-authored shares rise in academic publication data as knowledge-burden theory predicts.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines knowledge-burden validation by linking it to [team authorship share](../measures/team_authorship_share.md) and [mean team size growth](../measures/mean_team_size_growth.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [team impact advantage](../mechanisms/team_impact_advantage.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [doctoral coauthorship burden triangulation](doctoral_coauthorship_burden_triangulation.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Measure authors per paper and team-authored share by field and cohort.
- Compare coauthorship growth with doctoral-duration and first-invention-age trends.
- Use academic publication data as external validation for patent-based teamwork patterns.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Jones (2009) states that teamwork trends generalize beyond patents and cites broad increases in journal coauthorship across science, engineering, and social science.
- This separates the coauthorship validation from doctorate-duration evidence.

## Caveats

- The motif is close to doctoral-coauthorship burden triangulation but focuses on the publication-team validation channel.
- Coauthorship can grow for many reasons besides knowledge burden.
