# Expert-Curated Historiograph Coding Precondition

## Summary

Expert-curated historiograph coding precondition is the requirement that historical citation-network nodes, subject codes, and edge decisions be curated by domain expertise before structural interpretation.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: historiograph node, milestone event, subject category, citation edge, or historical citation corpus.
- Typical representation: expert-coding prerequisite for citation-network construction.
- Validation target: ensure that structural results rest on defensible historical and topical coding.
- Empirical signature: structural conclusions depend on curated event selection, subject categories, and direct or indirect citation coding.

## Operationalization

- Document how milestone events or papers were selected.
- Record who performed subject coding and what expertise justified the classifications.
- Distinguish direct citations from less direct citation chains if the analysis depends on edge type.
- Treat downstream path or component analysis as conditional on this construction layer.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hummon and Doreian (1989) describes a DNA historiograph built from a carefully identified set of research productions grouped into milestone events.
- The paper reports subject coding into broad areas including nucleic acid chemistry, protein chemistry, genetics, and microbiology.
- Hummon and Doreian rely on Garfield et al.'s postgraduate-level domain expertise and describe the earlier citation sifting as a prerequisite for their structural analysis.

## Caveats

- Expert curation can improve validity while introducing judgment and reproducibility concerns.
- Historical accounts can omit relevant side lines or later-recognized contributions.
- The precondition is strongest for small, historical event networks and may need adaptation for large automated corpora.
