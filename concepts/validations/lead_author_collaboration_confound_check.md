# Lead-author collaboration-confound check

## Summary

Lead-author collaboration-confound check tests whether a career-treatment effect is actually explained by changes in team position or collaborator status.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, focal scientist, author position, collaborator status, or treatment group.
- Typical representation: restricted lead-author analysis plus controls for collaborator quality or status.
- Validation target: distinguish individual career response from team-status confounding.
- Empirical signature: the focal effect persists among lead-author papers or after collaborator-status controls.

## Uses in Science of Science

## Operationalization

- Identify focal-author position or contribution role where available.
- Restrict outcomes to lead-author papers or include lead-author indicators.
- Add collaborator impact, prestige, or team-size controls.
- Compare estimates with and without collaboration restrictions.
- Treat persistent effects as less likely to be pure collaborator-status artifacts.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Jones, and Wang (2019) checks whether advantageous collaborations explain the near-miss performance gap.
- The paper restricts analyses using lead-author publication information and collaborator status.
- Similar conclusions under these restrictions reduce concern that the result is only a team-status effect.

## Caveats

- Author position does not perfectly measure contribution in all fields.
- Collaborator status is itself a possible response to a setback.
- Team composition can mediate rather than confound the career effect.
