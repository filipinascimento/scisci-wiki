# Low-hanging-fruit synchrony test

## Summary

Low-hanging-fruit synchrony test uses similar decline patterns across many fields as evidence against explanations based only on field-specific depletion of easy discoveries.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, year, disruption trend, or explanatory mechanism.
- Typical representation: cross-field comparison of disruption declines.
- Validation target: whether low-hanging-fruit explanations predict more field heterogeneity than observed.
- Empirical signature: many fields show synchronous decline despite having different discovery histories and opportunity structures.

## Operationalization

- Estimate disruption trends by field, technology class, or research area.
- Compare the timing and slope of declines across areas.
- Ask whether a candidate explanation predicts synchronized or field-specific decline.
- Use fixed-effect or decomposition models to separate field, author, and year contributions.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Park, Leahey, and Funk (2023) notes that similar disruption declines across fields are hard to reconcile with low-hanging-fruit accounts.
- The paper argues that fields would be unlikely to exhaust easy opportunities at the same rate or time.
- Additional decomposition analyses point away from simple field-specific explanations.

## Caveats

- Synchrony does not rule out opportunity depletion if the depletion driver is systemic.
- Field categories are coarse and can hide subfield variation.
- The test is an explanation screen, not a complete causal identification strategy.
