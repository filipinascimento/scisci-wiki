# Interdisciplinarity citation-window reversal

## Summary

Interdisciplinarity citation-window reversal is the validation pattern where some interdisciplinarity dimensions have negative short-term citation associations but positive long-term citation associations.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, interdisciplinarity component, citation window, or evaluation indicator.
- Typical representation: side-by-side short-window and long-window citation models.
- Validation target: whether early citation windows misclassify slow-diffusing interdisciplinary work.
- Empirical signature: variety or disparity is penalized under short windows but rewarded under longer windows.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Stress-tests [citation window selection](../methods/citation_window_selection.md) for interdisciplinary research.
- Provides empirical support for [interdisciplinary citation delay](../mechanisms/interdisciplinary_citation_delay.md).
- Extends the logic of [novelty short-window undercount](novelty_short_window_undercount.md) to field-spanning knowledge combinations.
- Helps evaluation systems decide when long-window or trajectory measures should supplement early citation counts.

## Operationalization

- Compute interdisciplinarity dimensions such as [category variety](../measures/category_variety.md), [distributional balance](../measures/distributional_balance.md), and [category disparity](../measures/category_disparity.md).
- Estimate models for short-term and long-term citation counts using a common paper cohort and comparable controls.
- Test whether coefficient signs or effect interpretations change across citation windows.
- Pair the window comparison with a direct [citation speed-delay measure](../measures/citation_speed_delay_measure.md) when annual citation histories are available.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Thijs, and Glanzel (2015) estimates 3-year and 13-year citation models for 2001 Web of Science articles.
- The paper finds that variety and disparity have negative effects on short-term citations while they have positive associations with long-term citations.
- Wang et al. report that the positive long-run effects of variety and disparity take time to show and that short windows would systematically underestimate papers high on these dimensions.
- Their direct citation-delay regressions support the same interpretation: higher variety and disparity are associated with slower citation accumulation.

## Caveats

- A window reversal is an association unless the research design isolates causal diffusion mechanisms.
- The pattern can depend on field taxonomy, journal controls, and the exact short and long windows.
- Reversal for one interdisciplinarity dimension should not be generalized to all forms of interdisciplinary work.
