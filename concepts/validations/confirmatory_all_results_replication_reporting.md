# Confirmatory all-results replication reporting

## Summary

Confirmatory all-results replication reporting is a bias-reduction rule in which preregistered replication tests report every result regardless of outcome.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: replication project, pre-analysis plan, confirmatory test, or completed replication report.
- Typical representation: complete results table, preregistration-to-report crosswalk, and archived data/materials.
- Validation target: reduction of publication, selection, and reporting bias in replication estimates.
- Empirical signature: all planned replication outcomes enter the aggregate evidence base, including null and weak effects.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Operationalizes [preregistration and registered reports](preregistration_registered_reports.md) in replication consortia.
- Provides a direct shield against [selective reporting and publication bias](../mechanisms/publication_bias_selective_reporting.md).
- Strengthens [direct replication protocol](../methods/direct_replication_protocol.md) because interpretable replication estimates require complete outcome reporting.
- Clarifies [replication effect-size shrinkage](../measures/replication_effect_size_shrinkage.md) by reducing post-outcome filtering.

## Operationalization

- Register the replication analysis before outcome observation.
- Track every confirmatory test from the pre-analysis plan to the final report.
- Report all replication p values, effect estimates, and confidence intervals in the public project dataset.
- Distinguish confirmatory tests from exploratory follow-up analyses.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Open Science Collaboration (2015) states that there was no publication bias in the replication studies because all results were reported.
- OSC also states that selection and reporting biases were avoided because all replication tests were confirmatory tests based on pre-analysis plans.
- The paper argues that this maximizes interpretability of replication p values and effect estimates.

## Caveats

- Complete reporting does not remove original-study publication bias.
- Deviations from registered plans still need transparent annotation.
- All-results reporting improves estimate interpretability but does not guarantee identical samples, settings, or procedures.
