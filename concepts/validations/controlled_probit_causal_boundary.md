# Controlled-probit causal boundary

## Summary

Controlled-probit causal boundary is the validation motif that nested adjusted probit models support descriptive disparity decomposition but do not by themselves establish causal attribution.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: adjusted regression, marginal effect, funding disparity, covariate block, or causal claim.
- Typical representation: probit marginal effect, nested model, covariate adjustment, descriptive association, or causal boundary statement.
- Validation target: keep adjusted funding gaps separate from causal explanations.
- Empirical signature: a study reports adjusted associations and explicitly avoids interpreting them as causal mechanisms.

## Operationalization

- Label marginal effects from adjusted probit or logit models as associations.
- Separate covariate attenuation from mechanism proof.
- Pair adjusted models with audit, quasi-experimental, policy-change, or randomized-information designs for causal questions.
- Report which covariates may be post-treatment or mediating variables.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ginther et al. (2011) reports marginal effects from nested probit models and frames estimates as correlations.
- The paper uses the models to characterize persistent adjusted award disparities, not to prove a single causal source.

## Caveats

- Descriptive adjusted gaps remain important evidence for policy attention.
- Causal interpretation may be possible with additional design assumptions.
- Overcontrol can remove pathways that are themselves part of inequity.
