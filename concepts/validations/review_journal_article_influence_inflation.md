# Review-journal Article Influence inflation

## Summary

Review-journal Article Influence inflation is the tendency for review journals to score unusually high on per-article journal metrics because review articles attract many citations.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: journal, article type, review journal, nonreview journal, citation metric, or ranking list.
- Typical representation: review-journal rank flags, separate review versus nonreview comparisons, or article-type sensitivity analysis.
- Validation target: whether high per-article influence reflects review-article citation practices rather than comparable original-research influence.
- Empirical signature: annual review and review-series journals cluster near the top of per-article influence rankings.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a document-type caveat to [Article Influence Score](../measures/article_influence_score.md) and [journal impact factor](../measures/journal_impact_factor.md).
- Helps interpret [Article Influence versus impact-factor divergence](article_influence_impact_factor_divergence.md) when review journals shift rankings.
- Links journal metrics to [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) by discouraging comparisons across unlike journal functions.
- Complements [citation window selection](../methods/citation_window_selection.md) and field checks because review articles can have distinct citation timing.

## Operationalization

- Flag review journals or review-heavy journals in the ranking set.
- Compare review journals with other review journals and nonreview journals with other nonreview journals.
- Report whether the metric counts all article types together or separates reviews, original research, and other documents.
- Inspect top-rank lists for review-series concentration.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from West et al. (2010) states that review journals score higher on Article Influence because individual review articles receive many citations.
- The paper recommends comparing nonreview journals with nonreview journals and review journals with review journals for applications where review status matters.
- West et al.'s top Article Influence table includes multiple annual review and nature review journals, illustrating the pattern.
- This caveat applies even though Article Influence uses recursive journal prestige rather than only raw average citation counts.

## Caveats

- Review journals can be genuinely valuable and should not be penalized automatically.
- Review status is not binary; some journals publish mixed article types.
- Article-type effects can vary by field and citation window.
- The validation is about comparison context, not about removing review journals from every analysis.
