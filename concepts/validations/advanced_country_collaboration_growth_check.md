# Advanced-Country Collaboration Growth Check

## Summary

Advanced-country collaboration growth check tests whether international collaboration growth also occurs among advanced countries, which limits explanations based only on peripheral capacity building.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: country group, collaboration growth trend, international coauthorship network, or explanation test.
- Typical representation: falsification or stress test for capacity-only explanations.
- Validation target: distinguish self-organized collaboration growth from explanations based only on developing-country entry.
- Empirical signature: advanced countries also increase international collaboration, including outside a single regional policy program.

## Operationalization

- Stratify collaboration growth by advanced, peripheral, and developing country groups.
- Check whether advanced countries also increase cross-border coauthorship.
- Compare regional policy areas with non-member advanced countries.
- Treat capacity diffusion as incomplete if advanced-country growth remains large.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wagner and Leydesdorff (2005) states that developing-country capacity growth helps countries join the international network.
- The paper then asks why advanced-country scientists are also increasingly willing to collaborate across borders, including the United States and Switzerland in the core.
- Wagner and Leydesdorff summarize that peripheral capacity needs cannot solely explain international collaboration because advanced countries also increase participation.

## Caveats

- Advanced-country growth does not rule out capacity, policy, or geography mechanisms; it shows they are insufficient alone.
- Country-group definitions should be explicit.
- Field composition and database coverage can affect apparent growth rates.
