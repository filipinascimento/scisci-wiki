# Communication-technology initiation caveat

## Summary

Communication-technology initiation caveat is the validation warning that email, the Internet, and other communication tools may facilitate collaboration after contact but do not necessarily initiate scientific collaboration.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: collaboration claim, communication technology, field, coauthorship trend, or collaboration initiation event.
- Typical representation: facilitator-versus-driver distinction, initiation-channel audit, or communication-technology explanation check.
- Validation target: prevent communication infrastructure from being treated as a sufficient cause of collaboration growth.
- Empirical signature: collaborations often begin through face-to-face meetings, conferences, field sites, facilities, or existing networks, while digital tools support later exchange.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds an initiation-specific caveat to [communication-technology jump test](communication_technology_jump_test.md).
- Refines the [international collaboration explanation matrix](../methods/international_collaboration_explanation_matrix.md), where communication technology appears as a candidate explanation for collaboration growth.
- Helps separate collaboration maintenance from partner discovery in [international collaboration networks](../representations/international_collaboration_networks.md).
- Applies to claims that remote communication alone explains cross-border collaboration growth.

## Operationalization

- Distinguish first-contact channel, collaboration-maintenance channel, data-sharing channel, and publication-production channel.
- Use surveys, interviews, acknowledgments, conference records, facility logs, and collaboration histories when available.
- Test whether collaboration growth accelerates around technology adoption through [communication-technology jump test](communication_technology_jump_test.md).
- Treat communication technology as an enabling condition unless timing and initiation evidence support a stronger causal claim.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wagner and Leydesdorff (2005) reviews the argument that the Internet caused growth in international collaboration.
- The paper concludes that Internet and information technology are enabling factors, but cites evidence that nearly all collaborations begin with face-to-face meetings.
- Wagner and Leydesdorff state that after collaboration is underway, researchers use the Internet to exchange data and text.
- Their conclusion is that information and communications technology should be considered a facilitating agent, not a driver for the initiation of geographically dispersed collaboration.

## Caveats

- Digital platforms can initiate collaboration in some newer fields, especially after broad adoption of preprints, code platforms, virtual conferences, and open data repositories.
- The caveat should be revisited with post-2020 collaboration data and field-specific evidence.
- Face-to-face initiation does not imply that digital communication is unimportant for collaboration scale, speed, or inclusiveness.
