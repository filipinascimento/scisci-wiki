# Success-screened effort trace

## Summary

Published chemical relationships are an informative but filtered trace of underlying research effort because failed and unconventional experiments are underobserved.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: publication, experiment, chemical relationship, failed test, publication selection, or observed choice.
- Typical representation: selection model where publication-derived edges reveal only success-screened research choices.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: visibility boundary for inferring experiment-choice strategies from publications.
- Empirical signature: published edges track research effort only after filtering by experimental success and publication selection..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines experiment-choice validation by linking it to [unpublished failure censoring](unpublished_failure_censoring.md) and [publication bias selective reporting](../mechanisms/publication_bias_selective_reporting.md).
- Useful as a reusable distinction when [accepted publication transition denominator limit](accepted_publication_transition_denominator_limit.md) is analyzed in science-of-science data.
- Adds cross-links to [scientific problem choice](../mechanisms/scientific_problem_choice.md) so the motif is not read as an isolated result.

## Operationalization

- Treat publication-derived edges as observed choices after screening by experimental success and publication selection.
- Compare inferred strategies with registries, protocols, laboratory records, or failure reports when available.
- Test whether failure rates or publication selection change over time and distort inferred conservatism.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Rzhetsky et al. (2015) notes that the empirical network underrepresents risky unsuccessful choices and relies on the assumption that published choices track effort reasonably well.
- The motif makes publication traces a selected effort proxy rather than a full experiment log.

## Caveats

- If failure rates or publication selectivity change sharply, inferred strategy shifts may partly reflect visibility changes.
- Published choices can overrepresent successful local search and underrepresent abandoned distant search.
