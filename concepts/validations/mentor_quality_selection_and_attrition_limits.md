# Mentor-quality selection and attrition limits

## Summary

Mentor-quality selection and attrition limits are causal caveats for mentorship or chaperone studies where strong proteges may select into strong mentors and many unsuccessful or exiting scientists are missing from publication-only records.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: protege, mentor, author-venue transition, publication history, career exit, or observational mentorship study.
- Typical representation: selection-by-mentor quality caveat plus missing-exit-state caveat.
- Validation target: avoid interpreting mentor or venue-experience associations as clean causal mentorship effects.
- Empirical signature: a study observes successful accepted-publication paths but cannot recover unobserved protege quality, mentor selection, or scientists who leave before later publications.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [accepted-publication transition denominator limit](accepted_publication_transition_denominator_limit.md) from missing submissions to missing people and selection processes.
- Applies to [mentorship and chaperone effects](../mechanisms/mentorship_chaperone_effects.md) and [venue chaperone effect](../mechanisms/venue_chaperone_effect.md).
- Links chaperone designs to [top scientist selection confound](top_scientist_selection_confound.md) and [unpublished-failure censoring](unpublished_failure_censoring.md).
- Motivates matched designs, exit-state modeling, and data that include rejected submissions or non-continuing scientists.

## Operationalization

- State whether mentor-protege matching, submission attempts, and career exits are observed.
- Distinguish author-transition associations from causal claims about mentoring quality.
- Include controls or matching variables for prior productivity, early impact, institution, field, and senior collaborator status when possible.
- Treat missing later publications as potential attrition rather than only absence of success.
- Propose follow-up designs using advisor assignment, lab shocks, submission systems, surveys, or linked employment data.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sekara et al. (2018) states that their venue-experience design averages over authors with very different success levels.
- The authors note that good proteges tend to find good mentors and that many young authors leave science altogether.
- Sekara et al. therefore stress that their results are not designed to answer deeper mentor-protege questions.
- The paper also states that the available data cannot pinpoint which facet of experience matters most or what share of a senior author's apprentices are successfully chaperoned.

## Caveats

- Selection and attrition caveats do not erase the observed venue-history pattern.
- Some omitted variables are part of the mechanism rather than nuisances, so causal diagrams should be explicit.
- Publication-only data are especially weak for people who attempt but fail to enter a venue or leave science before later publication.
