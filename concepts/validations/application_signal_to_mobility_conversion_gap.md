# Application signal to mobility conversion gap

## Summary

Application increases after policy shocks are weak leading indicators unless linked to realized enrollment, affiliation, output, and retention.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: application funnel, scholar pipeline, or mobility-policy response.
- Typical representation: conversion rates from application to admission, enrollment, affiliation, publication, and retention.
- Validation target: whether application signals translate into realized scientific mobility and capacity.
- Empirical signature: application spikes do not necessarily become sustained talent inflows or output gains.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a funnel validation to [central-country mobility displacement](../mechanisms/central_country_mobility_displacement.md).
- Useful for studying destination countries that receive increased applications after restrictions elsewhere.
- Connects mobility policy to [mobility restriction scientific capacity contingency](../mechanisms/mobility_restriction_scientific_capacity_contingency.md).

## Operationalization

- Track applications, offers, admissions, enrollment, affiliation records, publications, citations, and retention for exposed cohorts.
- Compare application shocks with capacity constraints in universities, labs, visas, and funding programs.
- Separate temporary interest from realized movement and long-run integration.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sugimoto et al. (2017) notes increases in applications to Canada after US political change while emphasizing that capacity determines whether talent is actually cultivated.
- This motivates treating applications as an early signal that requires conversion validation.

## Caveats

- Application changes can be symbolic, duplicated, or short-lived.
- Admissions data may be protected and hard to link to later publication identities.
