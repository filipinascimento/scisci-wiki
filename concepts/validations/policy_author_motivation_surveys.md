# Policy-author motivation surveys

## Summary

Policy-author motivation surveys ask the creators of policy-related documents why specific scholarly works were mentioned, cited, omitted, or used, providing direct validation for policy-attention indicators.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: policy author, policy document, mentioned paper, unmentioned source paper, source organization, policy field, or document section.
- Typical representation: survey instrument, interview protocol, motivation code, use-context label, or triangulated citation-use audit.
- Validation target: determine whether observed [policy document mentions](../measures/policy_document_mentions.md) reflect knowledge use, background citation, organizational routine, advocacy, discoverability, or other mechanisms.
- Empirical signature: policy authors report heterogeneous reasons for mention and non-mention that raw counts cannot distinguish.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Turns [policy mention context audit](policy_mention_context_audit.md) into an author-side validation design.
- Tests whether [public and policy attention](../mechanisms/public_policy_attention.md) indicators capture substantive research use or source-list visibility.
- Helps interpret [policy mention enrichment profiles](../methods/policy_mention_enrichment_profiles.md), especially when reviews, older papers, or certain fields are overrepresented.
- Provides a suggested experiment for [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) before policy mentions are used in evaluation.

## Operationalization

- Sample policy documents and mentioned scholarly works across sources, fields, document types, and mention counts.
- Contact document authors, analysts, or source organizations with structured questions about why the paper was cited, how it was found, and whether unmentioned work was used.
- Code responses into motivation categories such as evidence synthesis, background framing, legal or guideline requirement, author familiarity, citation copying, advocacy, or incidental bibliography.
- Compare author-reported use with document-section context, reference-list position, source organization, and paper attributes.
- Include non-mentioned but relevant papers to distinguish absence of a mention from absence of use.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Bornmann, Haunschild, and Marx (2016) explicitly proposes surveys of policy-document authors as a future way to understand the motivations behind policy mentions.
- Bornmann et al. argue that low policy-mention counts raise unresolved questions about whether mentions reflect paper relevance, researcher-policy interaction, ongoing relationships, or efforts by policy organizations to include research.
- The same paper cautions that non-mentioned papers may still be relevant and simply unknown to policy makers, making author-side evidence important for interpreting zeros.
- Verified full-text evidence from Haunschild and Bornmann (2017) recommends against using policy-related mentions for impact measurement until source coverage improves, reinforcing the need for validation studies before evaluation use.

## Caveats

- Survey response bias may be high, especially for older documents or large organizations.
- Authors may not remember citation choices or may report institutional rationales rather than individual behavior.
- Survey evidence validates sampled contexts; it does not remove coverage bias or document deduplication problems.
