# Hot-streak metric orthogonality

## Summary

Hot-streak metric orthogonality is the validation finding that hot-streak count captures a career dimension not reducible to ordinary metrics such as average impact, productivity, career length, or elevated-state magnitude.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: career, hot-streak count, impact metric, productivity metric, career duration, or fitted elevated-performance value.
- Typical representation: one-streak versus multi-streak comparison across ordinary career metrics.
- Validation target: test whether hot-streak structure is merely a proxy for being more productive, longer-lived, or higher-impact overall.
- Empirical signature: careers with one hot streak and careers with multiple hot streaks show no detectable differences in standard performance or career-length distributions.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Qualifies [single-hot-streak uniqueness](single_hot_streak_uniqueness.md) by showing that ordinary career metrics do not explain who receives one versus multiple fitted intervals.
- Strengthens [hot streaks](../mechanisms/hot_streaks.md) as a temporal structure distinct from standard productivity and impact measures.
- Links to [hot-streak productivity invariance](hot_streak_productivity_invariance.md), which asks whether output rises during the interval.
- Provides a responsible-metrics caveat for evaluation systems that treat mean impact, productivity, or career length as sufficient career summaries.

## Operationalization

- Group careers by fitted hot-streak count, commonly one versus more than one.
- Compare average impact, number of works, career length, and elevated-state parameter distributions across groups.
- Repeat comparisons by domain and career inclusion threshold.
- Treat non-differences as evidence that hot-streak timing/count is not captured by ordinary summary metrics.
- Report model-selection assumptions because hot-streak count is fitted rather than directly observed.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Liu et al. (2018) compares individuals with one hot streak and individuals with more than one hot streak across artists, directors, and scientists.
- Extended Data Fig. 10 reports distributions of average impact, number of works, career length, and elevated hot-streak performance for the groups.
- The paper states that there is no detectable difference in typical performance metrics, including impact, productivity, and career length.
- Liu et al. interpret this as evidence that hot streaks capture an orthogonal dimension to current metrics characterizing individual careers.

## Caveats

- Orthogonality is shown for the metrics tested, not for every possible career feature.
- No detectable difference can reflect limited power or measurement noise in some strata.
- Hot-streak count is model-dependent, so metric comparisons should be repeated when model specification changes.
