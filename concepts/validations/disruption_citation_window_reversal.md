# Disruption citation-window reversal

## Summary

Disruption can appear negatively related to impact in short windows but positively related in long windows, making evaluation horizon a sign-changing design choice.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: disruption score, citation impact, and citation window.
- Typical representation: disruption-impact association by 5-, 10-, 20-, and 30-year windows.
- Validation target: horizon sensitivity and possible sign reversal in disruption evaluation.
- Empirical signature: the same disruption measure changes association sign as citation windows lengthen.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [citation window selection](../methods/citation_window_selection.md) into disruption validation.
- Useful for evaluating short-window penalties against disruptive science.
- Connects team-size disruption to [novelty short-window undercount](novelty_short_window_undercount.md).

## Operationalization

- Recompute disruption-citation associations across multiple citation windows.
- Report sign changes by field, cohort, team size, and data source.
- Use older cohorts for long-window validation and note database coverage drift.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wu et al. (2019) reports that disruption-impact correlation is negative within ten years and positive over thirty years in both a 1970 cohort and the full Web of Science set.
- This makes citation horizon a sign-changing design choice.

## Caveats

- Long-window validation requires older cohorts and may not transfer to recent fields.
- Older database coverage can differ from current indexing.
