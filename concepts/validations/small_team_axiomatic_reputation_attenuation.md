# Small-team axiomatic reputation attenuation

## Summary

Small-team axiomatic reputation attenuation is the validation motif that reputation effects may be weaker in fields where small teams and axiomatic contributions make paper quality easier to attribute and evaluate directly.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, paper, author team, reputation-effect estimate, or contribution type.
- Typical representation: small team size, proof-based field, axiomatic discovery, weak reputation coefficient, or author-credit clarity.
- Validation target: test whether reputation effects vary with field organization and contribution evaluability.
- Empirical signature: reputation-premium estimates are lower in fields with smaller teams and more direct proof-based evaluation.

## Operationalization

- Estimate reputation-effect parameters by field or contribution type.
- Interact author reputation with team size, author-order norms, and theory/proof labels.
- Compare fields after normalizing citation density and publication rates.
- Check whether direct content evaluability reduces reliance on author-status signals.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Petersen et al. (2014) reports less prominent reputation effects in mathematics and suggests small team sizes and axiomatic discovery as possible reasons.
- The result positions field organization as a moderator of reputation effects.
- This motif is a validation caveat for applying one reputation model uniformly across science.

## Caveats

- Mathematics differs from biomedical science in citation density, database coverage, collaboration norms, and publication pace.
- Small teams can still generate strong status signals.
- Axiomatic contribution labels require careful operationalization.
