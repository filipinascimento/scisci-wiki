# Authorship-threshold drift caveat

## Summary

Authorship-threshold drift caveat is the validation motif that rising author counts can reflect changing norms for who receives byline credit, not only real growth in collaborative labor.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: byline, authorship norm, team-size trend, contribution threshold, or field period.
- Typical representation: coauthor credit threshold, contribution-statement evidence, acknowledgment shift, or author-count inflation caveat.
- Validation target: prevent byline-size trends from being interpreted mechanically as collaboration-size trends.
- Empirical signature: author-count growth may coincide with changing credit rules, contribution norms, or acknowledgment practices.

## Operationalization

- Pair author-count trends with journal authorship policies, acknowledgments, and contribution statements.
- Compare fields with stable versus changing authorship conventions.
- Track nonauthor contributor mentions where available.
- Treat byline size as an imperfect proxy for actual labor and coordination.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Milojevic (2014) explicitly leaves open whether core-team growth reflects real collaboration growth or changing thresholds for who is credited as an author.
- The paper flags this as an implication caveat beyond its team-size distribution model.

## Caveats

- Authorship-threshold drift is hard to measure retrospectively.
- Real collaboration growth and credit-threshold changes can occur simultaneously.
- Contribution statements are unavailable for many historical periods.
