# Nonretraction integrity warning states

## Summary

Nonretraction integrity warning states are correction-system labels such as expressions of concern, serious reservations, partial retractions, and suspected-but-unretracted misconduct cases.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: article, warning notice, expression of concern, partial retraction, editorial note, investigation record, or unretracted suspected case.
- Typical representation: multi-state correction label rather than a binary retracted/not-retracted flag.
- Validation target: avoid undercounting integrity problems that have not yet produced a formal full retraction.
- Empirical signature: public warnings or documented suspicions remain attached to articles that are still indexed as unretracted.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [unretracted misconduct dark figure](unretracted_misconduct_dark_figure.md) with observable intermediate warning states.
- Qualifies [time-to-retraction](../measures/time_to_retraction.md), because warning states can appear before or instead of formal retraction.
- Adds a state-space layer to [retraction notice transparency](retraction_notice_transparency.md) and [retraction cause taxonomies](../methods/retraction_cause_taxonomies.md).
- Supports [continued access to retracted articles](../methods/continued_access_retracted_articles.md) by requiring article-status metadata beyond a single retraction flag.

## Operationalization

- Define correction states: no concern, correction, expression of concern, serious reservation, partial retraction, full retraction, investigation finding, and unresolved suspected misconduct.
- Preserve state dates and transitions rather than overwriting earlier states.
- Link warning states to notices, institutional findings, PubMed/Crossref updates, and watchdog records.
- Analyze warning-to-retraction transitions and warning-only persistence.
- Keep warning labels separate from proven misconduct labels unless an investigation or notice supports the cause.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fang, Steen, and Casadevall (2012) states that not all articles suspected of fraud have been retracted.
- The paper gives examples in which journals expressed serious reservations or documented concerns, yet the original articles remained unretracted.
- Fang et al. also describe suspected fabricated work remaining in the literature, supporting a correction-state spectrum broader than full retraction.
- This validates multi-state integrity coding rather than a binary retraction indicator.

## Caveats

- Warning states can be vague, legally constrained, or later resolved without retraction.
- Treating warnings as misconduct can create false positives; the state label and evidence basis must be preserved.
- Coverage of expressions of concern and other warnings varies across databases and publishers.
