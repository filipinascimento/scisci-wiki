# Open data and materials insufficiency

## Summary

Open data and materials insufficiency is the validation warning that posting raw data and materials is valuable but may not expose undisclosed analytic flexibility quickly enough to evaluate a claim.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: article, raw dataset, materials archive, reviewer, reader, analytic decision, or redacted study element.
- Typical representation: open-data caveat, reanalysis burden, disclosure complement, or missing-analysis-path audit.
- Validation target: determine whether transparency infrastructure actually reveals the flexibility behind a reported result.
- Empirical signature: data are available, but readers must reconstruct analyses or cannot see redacted conditions, variables, exclusions, or pilot-study decisions.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Complements [open science interventions](open_science_interventions.md) by distinguishing data availability from analytic-decision disclosure.
- Adds a boundary condition to [researcher-degrees disclosure checklist](../methods/researcher_degrees_disclosure_checklist.md).
- Supports [replication analysis reproduction audit](../methods/replication_analysis_reproduction_audit.md) while noting that reanalysis is not a substitute for transparent reporting.
- Helps interpret [analytic flexibility and false-positive risk](analytic_flexibility_false_positives.md) in studies that post data but not full analytic histories.

## Operationalization

- Check whether all variables, conditions, exclusions, covariates, and stopping rules are described in the manuscript or supplement.
- Compare posted data and materials against the reported study design.
- Determine whether a reader can identify key degrees of freedom without rerunning many analyses.
- Require disclosure of analytic alternatives that materially affect the claim.
- Treat open data as necessary but not sufficient for evaluating hidden flexibility.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Simmons et al. (2011) supports journals requiring authors to make materials and data public.
- The paper nevertheless argues that posting materials and data alone is unlikely to solve the researcher-degrees-of-freedom problem.
- Simmons et al. state that readers should not need to download data and rerun analyses to discover that a result depends on a specific covariate.
- They also warn that if a journal allows redacting a condition from the article, it may also allow redaction from raw data or materials, undermining transparency.

## Caveats

- Open data remain crucial for error detection, reuse, and independent reanalysis.
- Some privacy, consent, or proprietary constraints can limit raw-data sharing.
- The insufficiency claim targets undisclosed flexibility, not all benefits of data sharing.
- Strong repositories with analysis scripts, codebooks, and preregistered plans can reduce this limitation.
