# Established-Classic Confirmation Stress Test

## Summary

Established-classic confirmation stress test is a retrospective validation strategy that subjects widely accepted findings to large, minimally biased studies to test whether canonical claims survive.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: established finding, classic claim, field consensus, replication program, or large study.
- Typical representation: high-prior accepted claim tested under stronger design conditions.
- Validation target: whether field classics remain true under large-scale, low-bias confirmation.
- Empirical signature: some canonical claims fail when retested despite their prior acceptance.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a retrospective target for [large-scale concept testing](../methods/large_scale_concept_testing.md).
- Connects [replication and reproducibility](replication_reproducibility.md) to high-prior claims.
- Uses [negative findings evidence value](../mechanisms/negative_findings_evidence_value.md) when classics fail.
- Complements [single-team significance overemphasis](single_team_significance_overemphasis.md).

## Operationalization

- Identify established claims with high field acceptance and feasible ethical retesting.
- Design large studies with minimized bias and transparent protocols.
- Compare confirmation rates with the claim's prior acceptance and citation status.
- Track whether failures update textbooks, guidelines, or field narratives.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ioannidis (2005) argues that some high prior odds can be ascertained.
- The paper recommends large, minimally biased studies for relatively established findings when ethically acceptable.
- Ioannidis explicitly expects several established classics to fail such tests.

## Caveats

- Classic findings may be context-dependent rather than false.
- Ethical or logistical constraints can prevent retesting.
- Retesting canonical claims can be shaped by prestige, reputational stakes, and publication bias.
