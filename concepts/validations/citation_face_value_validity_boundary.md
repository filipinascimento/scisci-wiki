# Citation Face-Value Validity Boundary

## Summary

Citation face-value validity boundary is the caveat that citation ties can be used as explicit knowledge links only after acknowledging self-citation, negative citation, window dressing, and motivated flattery.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: citation edge, historiograph link, citation network, or citation-based metric.
- Typical representation: construct-validity caveat attached to citation-link interpretation.
- Validation target: prevent citation edges from being treated as unqualified intellectual endorsement or content transfer.
- Empirical signature: citation networks remain useful, but individual ties may mix substantive, strategic, critical, and social motives.

## Operationalization

- State what a citation edge is assumed to mean in the analysis.
- Audit self-citation, negative-citation, ceremonial-citation, and strategic-citation channels where possible.
- Use content reading, citation-context classification, or sensitivity checks when the interpretation depends on edge meaning.
- Treat aggregate citation structures as more robust than any single tie unless tie-level validation is available.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hummon and Doreian (1989) starts from Garfield's claim that citations are explicit content linkages and then lists problems with taking citations at face value.
- Their caveats include self-citation, negative citation, window dressing, and politically motivated flattery.
- The same passage keeps citation analysis useful by noting that citation rates can still correlate with peer judgments across many disciplines.

## Caveats

- This boundary does not invalidate citation-network analysis; it specifies the assumptions under which citation edges can be interpreted.
- The importance of each citation motive varies by field, document type, and evaluation use.
- Edge-level correction can be costly and may not be feasible for large historical networks.
