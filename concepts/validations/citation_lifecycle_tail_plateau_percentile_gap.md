# Citation life-cycle tail-plateau percentile gap

## Summary

Mid-high cited papers show faster early decay and lower long-run plateaus than top-decile papers.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: citation percentile stratum, normalized trajectory, early drop, plateau level, or peak-year cohort.
- Typical representation: citation-stratum comparison of post-peak life-cycle tails.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: heterogeneity in citation decay by impact stratum.
- Empirical signature: the 11-30 percent citation stratum has a sharper early peak and lower plateau than the top decile..

## Operationalization

- Compare peak-normalized trajectories for top-10 percent papers against the 11-30 percent citation window.
- Measure early drop size and final plateau level by stratum.
- Use the comparison to distinguish elite longevity from general high-citation behavior.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Parolo et al. (2015) states in Appendix B that 11-30 percent papers have a more concentrated early peak, more rapid decay, and a lower final plateau than top-decile papers.
- The motif records citation-stratum heterogeneity in attention decay.

## Caveats

- The appendix is a robustness view and may not support fine-grained inference outside selected percentile bands.
- Percentile strata can mix fields with different publication and citation practices.
