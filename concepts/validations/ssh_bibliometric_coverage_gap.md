# SSH bibliometric coverage gap

## Summary

The SSH bibliometric coverage gap is the validation problem that social sciences and humanities outputs are underrepresented or distorted in citation databases because of books, local languages, regional journals, and different publication rhythms.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: SSH field, language group, book, regional journal, national database, citation source, or evaluation portfolio.
- Typical representation: source-coverage comparison, language/source audit, book-inclusion check, or field-specific missing-output estimate.
- Validation target: test whether citation indicators capture the outputs that matter for social sciences and humanities.
- Empirical signature: Web of Science and Scopus coverage underrepresents SSH, arts and humanities, non-English journals, regional journals, and books relative to field-relevant output lists.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Specializes [citation database coverage sensitivity](citation_database_coverage_sensitivity.md) for field and language coverage.

## Operationalization

- Compare database-indexed SSH output against national, institutional, regional, or field-specific source lists.
- Stratify coverage by language, document type, source type, field, and publication channel.
- Report whether books, edited volumes, local-language journals, and regional journals are included.
- Use field-specific indicator suites when one global citation database cannot represent the field's communication system.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Waltman (2016) summarizes SSH differences: stronger national and regional orientation, more books, slower theoretical development, less collaboration, and more communication to non-scholarly publics.
- Waltman reports that WoS coverage of SSH is limited because many national and regional SSH journals are not indexed and books historically were not included.
- The review notes evidence of a 20 to 25 percent overrepresentation of English-language SSH journals in WoS.
- Waltman also reports country and regional audits showing that local SSH databases can include substantial additional output beyond Web of Science.

## Caveats

- SSH coverage gaps are field-, country-, language-, and period-specific.
- Adding local or book sources can improve recall while reducing comparability if citation practices differ strongly.
- Coverage validation should distinguish missing scholarly outputs from outputs intentionally outside the evaluation scope.
