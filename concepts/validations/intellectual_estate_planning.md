# Intellectual estate planning

## Summary

Intellectual estate planning is the validation caveat that an anticipated scientific exit can let a star designate successors or transfer projects, making some mortality shocks partly a planned succession event.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: star scientist, collaborator group, subfield, death event, succession plan, or anticipated-exit subsample.
- Typical representation: sudden versus anticipated death split, collaborator succession note, pre-death health or planning flag, or robustness table.
- Validation target: distinguish exogenous field-leadership removal from planned handoff to incumbent collaborators.
- Empirical signature: anticipated-death cases could show stronger collaborator continuity or weaker outsider entry than sudden-death cases if succession planning matters.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a succession caveat to [superstar death event study](../methods/superstar_death_event_study.md).
- Complements [mortality-event pretrend validation](mortality_event_pretrend_validation.md) by asking whether anticipation changes post-event field organization.
- Helps interpret [collaborator/non-collaborator field flow](../measures/collaborator_noncollaborator_field_flow.md) when collaborator output changes after the death.
- Connects field-leadership turnover to [circle of academic life](../mechanisms/circle_of_academic_life.md) and career-stage replacement mechanisms.

## Operationalization

- Classify deaths as sudden, anticipated, or ambiguous using obituaries, medical histories, retirement timing, or other documented signals.
- Compare collaborator and non-collaborator entry patterns by anticipation status.
- Test whether collaborator continuity, funding continuity, or reference-to-star rates differ in anticipated deaths.
- Treat planned handoff as an identification caveat rather than automatically excluding anticipated cases.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Azoulay, Fons-Rosen, and Graff Zivin (2019) explicitly separates sudden from anticipated star deaths.
- The paper notes that anticipated illness could allow a star to arrange an intellectual succession by collaborators before death.
- Its appendix reports similar non-collaborator entry patterns for sudden and anticipated deaths, weakening but not eliminating the succession concern.
- This makes intellectual estate planning a reusable caveat for mortality-event designs in science-of-science.

## Caveats

- Anticipation status can be mismeasured because health information and succession plans are not uniformly observable.
- A similar treatment effect in sudden and anticipated groups does not rule out all succession behavior.
- Succession planning can preserve valuable knowledge even if it complicates causal interpretation.
- The concept should not be used to infer intentional gatekeeping without direct evidence.
