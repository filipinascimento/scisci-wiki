# Single-team significance overemphasis

## Summary

Single-team significance overemphasis is the validation problem that one team's statistically significant result receives too much attention when many teams are testing the same or similar questions.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: significant finding, research team, claim family, field, or repeated-testing set.
- Typical representation: isolated-positive warning, totality-of-evidence requirement, competing-team count, or first-positive publication audit.
- Validation target: prevent field credibility from being inferred from one significant result without the denominator of tested teams and hypotheses.
- Empirical signature: a field contains many teams or datasets, but attention concentrates on a single positive claim.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [field testing multiplicity](../mechanisms/field_testing_multiplicity.md) into a reporting and interpretation caveat.
- Links [hot-field Proteus phenomenon](../mechanisms/hot_field_proteus_phenomenon.md) to the overinterpretation of first or isolated positives.
- Supports [negative findings evidence value](../mechanisms/negative_findings_evidence_value.md) and evidence synthesis that includes all teams.
- Provides a practical warning for media, journals, funders, and reviewers evaluating isolated significant results.

## Operationalization

- Identify how many teams, datasets, analyses, or related hypotheses have addressed the same claim family.
- Compare the highlighted significant result with the full set of available positive, null, and negative evidence.
- Audit whether earlier or concurrent negative results were unpublished, ignored, or framed as less newsworthy.
- Treat first positives in hot fields as provisional until independent evidence accumulates.
- Use registries, preprints, meta-analyses, and data-sharing networks to recover the denominator of testing.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ioannidis (2005) argues that many research questions are addressed by multiple independent teams.
- The paper warns that it is misleading to emphasize the statistically significant finding of any single team because what matters is the totality of evidence.
- Ioannidis models repeated independent testing and shows that PPV declines as more teams test the same question and attention focuses on at least one positive result.
- The same section connects this dynamic to hot fields where teams prioritize their most impressive positive results and negative results become visible mainly as refutations of prominent positives.

## Caveats

- A single-team finding can be important when it is strongly designed, high-prior, or independently corroborated by other evidence.
- The relevant testing denominator can be hard to observe in unregistered fields.
- Totality-of-evidence interpretation must still account for study quality and dependence among teams.
