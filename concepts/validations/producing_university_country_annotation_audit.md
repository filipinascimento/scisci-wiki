# Producing-university country annotation audit

## Summary

Makes doctoral-country coding an auditable metadata step because origin composition and attrition claims depend on it.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: producing university, country code, and faculty doctorate record.
- Typical representation: double-coded country annotation with agreement and reconciliation provenance.
- Validation target: reliability of doctoral-country metadata.
- Empirical signature: country codes are reviewed and agreement rates are reported before mobility or attrition claims.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds metadata validation to [faculty doctoral-origin composition](../measures/faculty_doctoral_origin_composition.md).
- Useful when studying [international-doctorate faculty attrition](../mechanisms/international_doctorate_attrition.md).
- Connects faculty origin labels to broader [scientific mobility](../mechanisms/scientific_mobility.md) caveats.

## Operationalization

- Double-code institution countries, reconcile conflicts, rerun researcher audit, and store provenance.
- Report inter-annotator agreement and unresolved cases.
- Distinguish university country from citizenship, birthplace, training trajectory, and national origin.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wapman et al. (2022) reports that producing-university countries were hand determined after Mechanical Turk annotations with high agreement and researcher review.
- The process supports country coding as a validation step rather than a trivial lookup.

## Caveats

- University country is not citizenship, birthplace, or national origin.
- Historical border changes and institution mergers can complicate annotation.
