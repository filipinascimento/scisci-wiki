# Funding-model cost-benefit boundary

## Summary

Novelty evidence alone cannot decide which funding model is better without broader cost-benefit assessment.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: funding model, novelty outcome, productivity, review cost, administrative burden, equity outcome, or policy objective.
- Typical representation: evaluation-boundary ledger over benefits, costs, and distributional outcomes.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: policy validity of funding-model comparisons.
- Empirical signature: a narrow novelty result does not settle the overall superiority of competitive or block funding..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines funding-policy validation by linking it to [proposal burden research time crowding](../mechanisms/proposal_burden_research_time_crowding.md) and [type error asymmetric policy inference](type_error_asymmetric_policy_inference.md).
- Useful as a reusable distinction when [new public management funding shift](../mechanisms/new_public_management_funding_shift.md) is analyzed in science-of-science data.
- Adds cross-links to [funding allocation design alternatives](../methods/funding_allocation_design_alternatives.md) so the motif is not read as an isolated result.

## Operationalization

- Evaluate funding designs across novelty, productivity, impact, equity, proposal burden, review time, and administrative cost.
- Separate narrow construct results from broader funding-policy recommendations.
- Report which objectives are excluded from the available evidence.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang et al. (2018) says the study answers a narrow novelty question and does not settle overall funding-model superiority.
- The motif adds a broad evaluation boundary to novelty-focused funding evidence.

## Caveats

- Overlaps with proposal-burden motifs but extends the boundary to full funding-model evaluation.
- Cost-benefit assessment requires value judgments about novelty, productivity, equity, and administrative legitimacy.
