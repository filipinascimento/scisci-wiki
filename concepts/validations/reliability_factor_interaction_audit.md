# Reliability-Factor Interaction Audit

## Summary

Reliability-factor interaction audit checks whether factors that affect research credibility interact or counteract each other before interpreting any one reliability factor in isolation.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, design setting, evidence domain, or credibility model.
- Typical representation: interaction audit among power, bias, prior odds, field heat, and study scale.
- Validation target: whether simple PPV-factor interpretations are misleading because factors covary.
- Empirical signature: a field's credibility profile changes when correlations among effect size, study size, interests, standards, and multiplicity are modeled jointly.

## Operationalization

- Model credibility factors jointly rather than one at a time.
- Test whether fields with low expected effects compensate with larger studies or stronger standards.
- Estimate whether hot fields have both higher prejudice and stronger study designs.
- Report countervailing patterns before deriving policy prescriptions.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ioannidis (2005) warns that credibility factors influence each other.
- The paper notes that fields with small perceived effects may perform larger studies, while prejudice in hot fields can reduce credibility.
- Ioannidis also notes that strong interests or hot fields may sometimes promote larger studies and better standards.

## Caveats

- Interaction audits require more data than simple PPV scenarios.
- Some interactions are unobserved or field-specific.
- Joint models can hide mechanism details if they collapse all factors into a single credibility score.
