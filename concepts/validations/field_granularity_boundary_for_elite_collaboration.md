# Field-Granularity Boundary For Elite Collaboration

## Summary

Field-granularity boundary for elite collaboration is the validation limit created when elite-coauthor analyses operate at broad discipline levels instead of subfields or topics.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: discipline, subfield, topic, author, top-coauthor tie, or citation field.
- Typical representation: caveat or sensitivity check around field granularity.
- Validation target: whether top-coauthor exposure and later impact are measured at the right intellectual scale.
- Empirical signature: cross-discipline top-coauthor cases or within-discipline topic differences may be missed.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a boundary to [four-discipline long-lived career panel](../datasets/four_discipline_long_lived_career_panel.md).
- Connects career analyses to [domain-specific SciSci studies](../methods/domain_specific_scisci_studies.md).
- Motivates subfield tests using [discipline-subdiscipline map hierarchy](../representations/discipline_subdiscipline_map_hierarchy.md) or topic models.

## Operationalization

- Identify whether top status is defined by broad discipline, subfield, or topic.
- Test whether top-coauthor effects hold under finer field assignments.
- Track cross-discipline coauthorship that broad panels may miss.
- Use automated topic identification or publication-level field classification where available.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Li et al. (2019) states that the data do not identify junior researchers coauthoring with top scientists from different disciplines.
- The paper also notes that analyses are performed at aggregate discipline level rather than individual fields of research.
- It proposes automated topic identification from full text as a way to investigate same-subfield relationships more deeply.

## Caveats

- Finer granularity can improve construct validity but reduce sample size and increase classification noise.
- Broad disciplines may still be appropriate for stable top-cited status definitions.
- Topic models and field classifiers introduce their own validation problems.
