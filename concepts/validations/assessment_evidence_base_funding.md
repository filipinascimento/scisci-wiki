# Assessment evidence-base funding

## Summary

Assessment evidence-base funding allocates explicit resources to study whether researcher-assessment criteria, indicators, dashboards, and policies actually improve science.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: funder program, meta-research grant, assessment reform, institution, department, promotion policy, or indicator.
- Typical representation: funding call, research-on-research portfolio, cluster-randomized trial, stepped-wedge rollout, interrupted time series, audit-and-feedback study, or evaluation grant.
- Validation target: determine which assessment practices are accurate, feasible, fair, behavior-changing, and resistant to gaming.
- Empirical signature: assessment reforms are paired with funded evaluation designs and publicly available evidence on effects and unintended consequences.

## Operationalization

- Reserve funding for meta-research on assessment criteria, indicator validity, implementation burden, and behavior change.
- Require assessment reforms to include evaluation designs, preferably experimental or quasi-experimental when feasible.
- Track outcomes such as reporting completeness, openness, reproducibility, social uptake, equity, workload, gaming, and scientific quality.
- Publish criteria, data, code, and implementation reports so other institutions can reuse the evidence.
- Continue funding repeated evaluations because indicator effects can change after adoption and optimization.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Moher et al. (2018) identifies funding research that provides an evidence base for assessing science and faculty as the fifth assessment principle.
- Table 2 calls for experimental paradigms to evaluate assessment initiatives and asks whether a new intervention has its intended effect.
- Moher et al. give a cluster-randomized trial example in which universities would be allocated to a data-sharing policy or standard practice and compared on later outcomes.
- The implementation section recommends documenting criteria changes and evaluating them through experimental approaches, stepped-wedge designs, interrupted time series, and audit and feedback.
- The paper notes that some funders had already started research-on-research funding streams, while the evidence base remained uneven across assessment topics.

## Caveats

- Evaluation funding may be vulnerable to political pressure when results threaten existing assessment practices.
- Some outcomes, such as creativity, societal benefit, or reproducibility, require long observation windows.
- Randomized designs can be difficult when policies affect careers.
- New validated indicators can later become invalid if they become optimization targets.
