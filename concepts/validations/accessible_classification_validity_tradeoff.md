# Accessible classification validity tradeoff

## Summary

Accessible classification validity tradeoff is the caveat that widely available field classifications can be pragmatically useful while only partially matching empirically derived citation or topical clusters.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field classification, journal subject category, citation cluster, science map, or interdisciplinarity metric.
- Typical representation: usability-versus-validity tradeoff for a classification scheme.
- Validation target: prevent accessible taxonomies from being treated as ground truth.
- Empirical signature: researchers use available classifications while acknowledging mismatch with citation-derived structure.

- Qualifies [field classifications](../measures/field_classifications.md).

## Operationalization

- State why the accessible classification is used.
- Compare it with citation-based or expert-curated alternatives when feasible.
- Report known mismatches, broad categories, and ambiguous assignments.
- Treat conclusions as conditional on the classification scheme.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Porter and Rafols (2009) discusses the use of Web of Science subject categories despite known limitations.
- The paper uses accessible categories to support reproducible interdisciplinarity measurement and mapping.
- It also treats category validity as imperfect, requiring triangulation and caveats.

## Caveats

- Accessibility can bias method choice toward convenient but noisy classifications.
- Some fields are poorly represented by journal-level categories.
- A classification can be useful for trends while unsuitable for fine-grained causal claims.
