# Implicit-parameterized nonparametric index

## Summary

A nominally nonparametric diversity index can still contain implicit parameters through property weights, category definitions, exponents, and disparity coding choices.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: diversity index, exponent, weight, category system, disparity matrix, or parameter setting.
- Typical representation: parameter audit for diversity formulas presented as general or nonparametric.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: hidden assumptions in diversity measurement.
- Empirical signature: diversity rankings change when implicit weights, exponents, or category definitions are varied.

## Operationalization

- List formula choices, exponents, category partitions, balance functions, and disparity weights.
- Run sensitivity analyses over plausible implicit parameters.
- Report when a supposedly general index embeds a particular normative or geometric preference.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Stirling (2007) develops a general non-parametric heuristic while emphasizing sensitivity to perspectives, category definitions, and property weights.
- The paper therefore motivates auditing implicit parameterization even in general diversity formulas.

## Caveats

- Parameter sensitivity is not a defect by itself; it clarifies what the measure values.
- The relevant parameter range depends on the appraisal context.
