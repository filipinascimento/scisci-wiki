# Journal coverage budget constraint

## Summary

Journal coverage budget constraint is the source-selection caveat that journal inclusion reflects finite indexing budgets, continuity costs, reader recommendations, and opportunity costs, not only citation criteria.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: journal, indexing service, source list, annual coverage decision, or selection policy.
- Typical representation: source-selection criteria plus budget, continuity, and omission tradeoff notes.
- Validation target: prevent database coverage from being treated as purely objective citation-based inclusion.
- Empirical signature: otherwise eligible journals can be delayed, omitted, or removed because coverage resources are finite.

## Operationalization

- Document citation, editorial, reader-recommendation, and budget criteria used for source inclusion.
- Track deferred, omitted, added, and removed journals with reasons.
- Record continuity constraints that make dropping a journal difficult.
- Treat database source coverage as a designed and resource-constrained panel.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Garfield (1972) states that objective criteria alone do not solve journal coverage decisions.
- Garfield describes reader recommendations for journals that perform poorly in citation studies and warns against ignoring such recommendations solely on citation data.
- The paper also describes annual budgeting, finite additions to ISI services, continuity expectations, and the opportunity cost of retaining weaker journals while omitting stronger ones.

## Caveats

- Budget constraints do not excuse opaque or biased source-selection decisions.
- Reader recommendations can reflect professional self-interest or national pride as well as genuine coverage gaps.
- Historical ISI constraints may not map directly onto modern digital indexing costs, but the governance logic remains relevant.
