# Impact-horizon alignment grid

## Summary

Prediction targets should be aligned with short-, medium-, and long-run impact horizons so early indicators are not overextended to delayed discoveries.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: forecast horizon, citation window, impact model, paper, field, or delayed-recognition case.
- Typical representation: forecast horizon and impact-window alignment matrix.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: misalignment between early indicators and delayed impact outcomes.
- Empirical signature: model validity differs across short citation windows, citation peaks, common-knowledge timing, and delayed recognition cases..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects [citation trajectory models](../methods/citation_trajectory_models.md) to responsible forecast windows.
- Adds a validation grid for [long-term citation prediction](../methods/long_term_citation_prediction.md).
- Useful when sleeping beauties or delayed recognition are plausible.

## Operationalization

- Specify forecast horizon, evaluation window, and delayed-recognition sensitivity for every impact model.
- Stress-test forecasts against citation peaks, common-knowledge timing, and dormant-then-awakening papers.
- Report horizon-specific calibration rather than one accuracy score.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Clauset et al. (2017) discusses models that predict citation peaks and time to common knowledge, then contrasts them with delayed-recognition cases that may awaken only after science advances.

## Caveats

- Citation horizons capture only one form of impact.
- Fields with slow recognition may remain undercounted even with longer windows.
