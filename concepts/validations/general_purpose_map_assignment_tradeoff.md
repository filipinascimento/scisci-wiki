# General-purpose map assignment tradeoff

## Summary

General-purpose map assignment tradeoff is the tension between task-specific source assignments, equal-sized classes, and a broadly usable science map that remains locally and globally accurate.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: source title, subdiscipline assignment, map use case, class balance, or map accuracy criterion.
- Typical representation: validation caveat explaining why one assignment scheme may not optimize every downstream task.
- Validation target: keep a general science map useful across many overlays without overfitting to one task.
- Empirical signature: the map preserves general structure even though some use cases might prefer different journal assignments.

## Operationalization

- Identify major intended use cases for the map.
- Evaluate whether source-title assignments work for overlays at individual, institutional, national, and field levels.
- Compare class balance with local and global structural accuracy.
- Document where a general-purpose assignment may be suboptimal for specialized tasks.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Borner et al. (2012) states that different map uses may benefit from different journal-to-subdiscipline assignments.
- The paper argues that the main purpose of a science map and classification system is generality and utility across applications.
- It also notes the conflict between equal-sized subdisciplines and locally/globally accurate science-map structure.

## Caveats

- A general-purpose map can obscure local domain distinctions.
- Equal class sizes are not always desirable or accurate.
- Specialized applications may need task-specific reassignment or paper-level classifications.
