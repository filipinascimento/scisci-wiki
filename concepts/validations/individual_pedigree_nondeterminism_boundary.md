# Individual pedigree nondeterminism boundary

## Summary

Prevents system-level prestige gradients from being used as deterministic claims about individual faculty careers.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: system-level placement pattern versus individual career claim.
- Typical representation: hierarchy results paired with individual residual variance and usage caveats.
- Validation target: boundary between aggregate prestige structure and individual prediction.
- Empirical signature: strong system gradients coexist with many individual exceptions and unexplained trajectories.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a usage guardrail to [faculty placement predictive validity](faculty_placement_predictive_validity.md).
- Useful when faculty prestige maps are interpreted for candidate-level assessment.
- Connects structural hierarchy to [placement residual contingency portfolio](placement_residual_contingency_portfolio.md).

## Operationalization

- Report individual-level variance and residual space alongside hierarchy results.
- Route candidate-level claims to richer residual or counterfactual analyses rather than deterministic pedigree interpretation.
- Flag prose that turns aggregate gradients into individual destiny claims.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wapman et al. (2022) states that the results are system-wide rather than individual-level causal predictions and emphasizes that pedigree is not destiny.
- This supports a stronger usage boundary for hierarchy maps.

## Caveats

- The boundary does not deny structural inequality; it prevents overclaiming about individual cases.
- Individual-level prediction may still be possible with additional covariates and uncertainty.
