# Racial-reckoning policy-intervention test

## Summary

Racial-reckoning policy-intervention test evaluates whether interventions after major equity shocks changed scientific workforce, topic, or impact outcomes.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: policy intervention, institution, field, author group, topic, or time window.
- Typical representation: pre/post, difference-in-differences, or event-study evaluation around equity interventions.
- Validation target: whether calls for transparency and accountability produced measurable change.
- Empirical signature: post-intervention shifts in representation, topic access, funding, or citation outcomes exceed plausible baseline trends.

## Operationalization

- Identify concrete interventions after events such as Black Lives Matter, COVID-19 inequity attention, or institutional accountability reforms.
- Define treated and comparison institutions, fields, or programs.
- Track representation, topic portfolios, funding, retention, and impact outcomes.
- Use event-study or difference-in-differences designs where feasible.
- Report null or mixed effects, not only positive reform stories.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Kozlowski et al. (2022) calls for testing whether heightened attention to racial inequity and transparency has changed scientific outcomes.
- The paper links such tests to policy interventions aimed at the scientific workforce and knowledge base.
- This motif makes intervention evaluation an explicit next step after descriptive inequality measurement.

## Caveats

- Equity interventions often occur simultaneously, complicating attribution.
- Effects may appear slowly through training, hiring, and retention pipelines.
- Public statements without implementation should not be coded as treatments.
