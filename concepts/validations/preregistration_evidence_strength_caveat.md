# Preregistration evidence-strength caveat

## Summary

The preregistration evidence-strength caveat is the boundary condition that preregistration has strong theoretical and correlational support, but its causal effect on reproducibility still needs direct experimental evidence.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: preregistered study, non-preregistered study, reproducibility outcome, transparency intervention, or evaluation design.
- Typical representation: evidence-strength label attached to claims about preregistration benefits.
- Validation target: distinguish plausible mechanism, correlational evidence, and causal proof for open-science reforms.
- Empirical signature: authors claim preregistration should improve inference while acknowledging that the reproducibility effect is not fully experimentally established.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Qualifies broad claims about [preregistration and registered reports](preregistration_registered_reports.md).
- Connects preregistration reform to [replication and reproducibility](replication_reproducibility.md) without treating the policy as already proven in every setting.
- Encourages evaluation through [registry-publication reporting-bias audits](../methods/registry_publication_reporting_bias_audit.md), [preregistration adherence and deviation audits](preregistration_adherence_deviation_audit.md), and replication outcomes.
- Gives [open science interventions](open_science_interventions.md) an evidence hierarchy for policy adoption.

## Operationalization

- Label evidence for preregistration as theoretical, correlational, quasi-experimental, experimental, or implementation evidence.
- Separate effects on reporting bias, interpretability, statistical diagnosticity, replication success, and publication outcomes.
- Track whether the preregistration was specific, discoverable, followed, and audited.
- Compare outcomes before and after registry, badge, journal, or funder policies when possible.
- Treat field-general claims as provisional until tested across research designs and incentive environments.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Nosek et al. (2018) argues that preregistration is theoretically strong because it separates prediction from postdiction before outcomes are known.
- The same paper states that the case is empirically supported by correlational evidence but does not yet rest on sufficient experimental evidence establishing superiority for reproducibility.
- This caveat keeps preregistration in the wiki as a reform with a clear mechanism and a still-active evaluation agenda.

## Caveats

- This caveat should not be read as evidence against preregistration; it is a claim about the strength and type of evidence.
- Effects may differ by field, preregistration quality, study design, and enforcement.
- Reproducibility is only one outcome; preregistration can also improve interpretability, detectability of deviations, and reporting transparency.
