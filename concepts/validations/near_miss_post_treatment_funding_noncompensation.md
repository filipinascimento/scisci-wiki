# Near-miss post-treatment funding noncompensation

## Summary

Near-miss post-treatment funding noncompensation tests whether near-miss applicants outperform later because they receive replacement funding elsewhere, rather than because the setback changes selection or behavior.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: applicant, grant, funding agency, acknowledgment, post-treatment window, or funding trajectory.
- Typical representation: NIH, NSF, non-NIH/non-NSF, and acknowledgment-based funding comparisons after treatment.
- Validation target: rule out compensatory funding as the explanation for near-miss performance advantage.
- Empirical signature: near-miss applicants do not receive more post-treatment support from alternative funders than narrow-win applicants.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Strengthens [early-career setback and resilience](../mechanisms/early_career_setback_resilience.md) and [survivor-conditioned setback advantage](survivor_conditioned_setback_advantage.md).
- Adds a funding-dynamics channel to [funding threshold quasi-experiments](../methods/funding_threshold_quasi_experiments.md).
- Uses Dimensions-like grant traces and funding acknowledgments as external checks on NIH-only funding histories.

## Operationalization

- Track direct focal-agency funding after the near-threshold decision.
- Add other major funders, such as NSF, and broader international grant databases where possible.
- Audit publication acknowledgments for additional funding support.
- Compare post-treatment funding between near misses and narrow wins over fixed windows.
- Interpret performance differences cautiously if near misses obtain compensatory support elsewhere.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Jones, and Wang (2019) shows that narrow wins receive substantially more NIH funding immediately after treatment.
- The paper checks NSF funding and broader funding support from agencies beyond NIH and NSF using Dimensions grant histories.
- Wang et al. also manually check acknowledgments and report that near misses did not acquire more alternative funding than narrow wins in the early post-treatment period.

## Caveats

- Grant databases and acknowledgments can miss institutional, philanthropic, informal, or international support.
- Noncompensation in funding dollars does not rule out compensation through mentoring, collaboration, or protected time.
- Funding-source comparisons need harmonized windows and inflation adjustments when dollar amounts are used.
