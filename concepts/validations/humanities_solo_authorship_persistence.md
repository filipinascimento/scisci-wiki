# Humanities Solo-Authorship Persistence

## Summary

Humanities solo-authorship persistence is the caveat that positive teamwork trends can coexist with an overwhelmingly solo-authored output level in arts and humanities fields.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: humanities field, subfield-year cell, solo-authored output share, or team-authorship trend.
- Typical representation: team-authorship level and slope reported together, solo-majority flag, or broad-domain contrast.
- Validation target: prevent a positive teamwork trend from being interpreted as team dominance where solo authorship remains the modal practice.
- Empirical signature: team-authorship share rises over time but remains a minority of outputs in arts and humanities.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Qualifies [team-authorship share](../measures/team_authorship_share.md) and [team-growth subfield universality](team_growth_subfield_universality.md) by separating slope from level.
- Links team-production measures to [SSH bibliometric coverage gap](ssh_bibliometric_coverage_gap.md) and [authorship-output indicator boundary](authorship_output_indicator_boundary.md).
- Helps compare team science across domains without importing science-and-engineering collaboration norms into humanities evaluation.
- Provides a field-specific check for [team-growth driver portfolio](../mechanisms/team_growth_driver_portfolio.md).

## Operationalization

- Report both the change in team-authorship share and the absolute solo/team levels for each broad domain.
- Stratify arts and humanities separately from science, engineering, social sciences, and patents.
- Avoid labeling a domain as team-dominated when the majority of outputs remain solo-authored.
- Audit whether books, monographs, and other humanities outputs outside the citation database would change the inferred solo/team balance.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wuchty, Jones, and Uzzi (2007) finds a positive trend toward teams in arts and humanities, but states that solo authors still produce almost 90 percent of papers in that branch.
- The same paper reports that only 85.2 percent of arts and humanities subfields show increased teamwork, lower than science and engineering, social sciences, or patents.
- Wuchty et al. note that humanities team-growth rates are lower than other domains, even while the general shift toward teamwork is visible.

## Caveats

- Web of Science coverage of arts and humanities omits many books, local-language outputs, and non-journal genres.
- Solo authorship is not equivalent to solitary work; informal collaboration, archives, editors, assistants, and peer networks can remain invisible.
- The caveat is about levels and interpretation, not a denial that teamwork can grow in humanities fields.
