# Assessment audit-feedback loops

## Summary

Assessment audit-feedback loops monitor whether assessment reforms are actually adopted, report the results back to decision makers, and use the feedback to revise criteria or implementation.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: institution, department, funder, promotion committee, grant-review panel, assessment policy, or responsible-indicator dashboard.
- Typical representation: audit report, feedback memo, adherence dashboard, independent monitoring file, before-after criteria inventory, or implementation scorecard.
- Validation target: whether assessment reform changes committee behavior rather than remaining a declaration.
- Empirical signature: committees or funders document policy adherence, proxy de-emphasis, responsible-indicator use, and unintended effects over time.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Gives [research assessment implementation experiments](research_assessment_implementation_experiments.md) a practical monitoring instrument.
- Tests whether [transparent assessment-criteria publication](../methods/transparent_assessment_criteria_publication.md) is followed in actual decisions.
- Detects symbolic adoption of [responsible researcher assessment principles](../methods/responsible_researcher_assessment_principles.md).
- Provides an early warning system for [indicator systemic effects](../mechanisms/indicator_systemic_effects.md), gaming, and workload redistribution.
- Links assessment reform to broader [open science interventions](open_science_interventions.md) and [registry-publication reporting-bias audit](../methods/registry_publication_reporting_bias_audit.md) workflows.

## Operationalization

- Define the assessment policy being audited: JIF de-emphasis, portfolio review, open-research rewards, complete-reporting rewards, RIAS dashboards, or revised CV fields.
- Collect evidence from committee instructions, dossiers, minutes where appropriate, criteria checklists, decision letters, and post-decision surveys.
- Compare observed practice with the stated criteria and with the pre-reform baseline.
- Feed results back to committees, institutional leaders, funders, and affected researchers.
- Repeat the audit across cycles to identify drift, symbolic compliance, gaming, and field or career-stage inequities.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Moher et al. (2018) argues that DORA-like commitments need monitoring because declarations alone do not specify whether committees adhere to or implement them.
- Table 2 proposes that funders or academic institutions could pay for independent audit and feedback, with results made openly available.
- The same table describes audit and feedback as a way to monitor whether promotion-and-tenure committees actually de-emphasize weak indicators such as journal impact factor.
- Moher et al. also suggest audit and feedback as a way to improve uptake of complete and transparent reporting initiatives.
- The paper places audit-feedback inside an evaluation agenda, including experimental and quasi-experimental designs where feasible.

## Caveats

- Audits can become performative if they check paperwork rather than committee behavior or outcomes.
- Confidential personnel decisions limit what can be made open.
- Feedback may create pressure for easy-to-score compliance metrics unless qualitative evidence is preserved.
- Repeated audits can add administrative burden and may shift work to junior faculty unless resourced explicitly.
