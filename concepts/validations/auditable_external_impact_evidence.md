# Auditable External-Impact Evidence

## Summary

Auditable external-impact evidence is the validation standard that societal impact should be demonstrated through recorded influence on non-academic actors rather than assumed from academic visibility.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, researcher, institution, policy document, case study, or external actor.
- Typical representation: evidence trace linking research to non-academic use.
- Validation target: whether a claimed societal impact has auditable external evidence.
- Empirical signature: references, citations, discussion, adoption, or outcome changes can be traced to identifiable non-academic organizations or actors.

## Operationalization

- Define the target actor or organization outside academia.
- Record the evidence trace: reference, citation, discussion, guideline use, clinical change, or economic outcome.
- Separate demonstrated external impact from plausible or desired impact.
- Audit whether the evidence shows use, influence, or merely awareness.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Bornmann et al. (2016) defines societal impact as auditable or recorded influence on non-academic organizations or actors.
- The paper states that societal impacts should be demonstrated rather than assumed.
- It lists references, citations, and discussion of research as possible forms of external-impact evidence.

## Caveats

- Auditable traces can miss tacit, indirect, or confidential forms of influence.
- Evidence of mention is not always evidence of uptake, change, or benefit.
- External impact can be positive, negative, or ambiguous.
