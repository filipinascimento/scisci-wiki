# Narrative-CV self-presentation bias

## Summary

Narrative-CV self-presentation bias is the risk that narrative portfolios reward storytelling, self-marketing, or institutionally coached framing rather than the underlying quality and contribution of a scientist's work.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: narrative CV, portfolio dossier, researcher statement, hiring file, promotion case, committee, or assessment rubric.
- Typical representation: narrative portfolio audit, reviewer calibration exercise, dossier-quality comparison, writing-support sensitivity check, or self-presentation bias assessment.
- Validation target: whether narrative assessment adds context without advantaging the best narrators or most resourced candidates.
- Empirical signature: evaluation scores vary with narrative coherence, polish, institutional support, or self-promotional framing after holding research contributions constant.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Qualifies [portfolio-based researcher assessment](../methods/portfolio_based_researcher_assessment.md) and [metrics support qualitative assessment](../methods/metrics_support_qualitative_assessment.md).
- Identifies a failure mode in reforms that replace simple metrics with narrative dossiers but do not calibrate reviewers.
- Complements [transparent assessment-criteria publication](../methods/transparent_assessment_criteria_publication.md), because criteria can limit how much narrative style affects decisions.
- Links assessment reform to equity concerns in [assessment audit-feedback loops](assessment_audit_feedback_loops.md).
- Provides a validation target for narrative CV experiments and hiring/promotion rubric studies.

## Operationalization

- Compare narrative-dossier ratings with structured evidence on outputs, roles, openness, mentoring, service, and field context.
- Blind or standardize narrative sections where possible, or use structured prompts rather than unconstrained essays.
- Provide reviewers with rubrics that separate contribution evidence from writing quality.
- Audit whether narrative advantages differ by language background, gender, race, institution, career stage, or access to mentoring and administrative support.
- Test whether adding narratives improves decision quality beyond portfolios and contextualized metrics.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Moher et al. (2018) reviews the ACUMEN performance-portfolio proposal, which includes narrative, academic age, expertise, outputs, and influence.
- Moher et al. report a concern that narrative portfolios can pressure researchers to shape achievements into compelling stories.
- The paper warns that self-marketing can become more pervasive and interfere with research quality.
- It also notes that valuable researchers may lack a coherent story or strong narrative ability.
- This makes narrative CVs a reform mechanism that needs validation rather than a neutral replacement for bibliometric assessment.

## Caveats

- Narrative evidence is not inherently bad; it can reveal context, role, and contribution that metrics miss.
- Structured narratives can still privilege access to writing support and institutional coaching.
- Removing narratives entirely may make committees fall back on hidden prestige cues.
- Validation should compare narrative CVs with other dossier formats, not with a metric-only straw baseline.
