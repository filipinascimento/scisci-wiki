# New-renewal application signal split

## Summary

New and competing-renewal applications provide a signal-strength split for testing whether peer-review scores predict outcomes when prior project evidence differs.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: R01 application, new grant, competing renewal, priority score, citation outcome, or grant history.
- Typical representation: subgroup validation by application renewal status.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: review-score predictive validity under different prior-evidence conditions.
- Empirical signature: score-outcome associations persist for new applications even though renewals have more prior project evidence..

## Operationalization

- Estimate score-outcome relationships separately for new R01s and competing-renewal R01s.
- Use renewal status as a proxy for the amount of prior project-specific evidence available to reviewers.
- Compare whether score gradients strengthen when prior output signals are richer.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Li and Agha (2015) reports stronger prediction for renewals but a substantial score-citation relationship even for new applications.
- The split supports peer-review predictive validity beyond continuation-project information.

## Caveats

- Renewal status also reflects incumbent advantage, continuation value, and accumulated laboratory infrastructure.
- New applications can still carry applicant prestige and prior publication signals.
