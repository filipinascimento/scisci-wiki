# Artist-era hot-streak invariance

## Summary

Artist-era hot-streak invariance is the validation motif that hot-streak signatures in art careers should persist across historical era splits rather than being artifacts of one market or archive period.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: artist career, career-start cohort, hit-work sequence, shuffled career, or era-stratified robustness test.
- Typical representation: pre-1850 artist cohort, 1850-1900 cohort, hit-distance enrichment, streak-length distribution, or era-invariance check.
- Validation target: test whether hot-streak patterns survive historical changes in market records and archival coverage.
- Empirical signature: hit clustering and streak-length excess remain visible after splitting artist careers by start era.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds archival-era robustness to [hot streaks](../mechanisms/hot_streaks.md).
- Validates [top-hit distance enrichment curve](../measures/top_hit_distance_enrichment_curve.md) and [longest high-impact streak excess](longest_high_impact_streak_excess.md) in historical art data.
- Supports cross-domain interpretation of [cross-domain creative career histories](../datasets/cross_domain_creative_career_histories.md).

## Operationalization

- Stratify creative careers by start-year or market-era bins.
- Recompute hit-distance enrichment and streak-length distributions against shuffled-career nulls.
- Compare the magnitude and uncertainty of hot-streak signatures by era.
- Report archive and market-coverage differences across era bins.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Liu et al. (2018) reports extended-data checks that repeat core hot-streak tests for artists beginning in different historical periods.
- The era splits show that the main hit-clustering patterns are not confined to one artist vintage cohort.
- This motif isolates historical robustness from the broader cross-domain hot-streak finding.

## Caveats

- The validation is art-specific and does not remove auction survival or price-record biases.
- Era bins can still hide changes in geography, genre, and market institutions.
- Similar vintage checks may be needed for film and scientific careers with different data sources.
