# Authorship-output indicator boundary

## Summary

Authorship-output indicator boundary is the validation concern that indexed journal authorship is only one trace of scientific activity and can miss books, conference proceedings, data, software, invisible labor, and uncredited contributions.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, authorship, publication database, document type, research output, or contribution channel.
- Typical representation: document-type boundary statement, excluded-output audit, authorship-norm caveat, or contribution-trace sensitivity check.
- Validation target: preventing publication authorship counts from being interpreted as total research activity or total contribution.
- Empirical signature: conclusions are explicitly bounded to indexed articles and reviews, not all scientific work.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a construct-validity boundary for [gendered fractional authorship ratio](../measures/gendered_fractional_authorship_ratio.md) and [WoS global gender bibliometric panel](../datasets/wos_global_gender_bibliometric_panel.md).
- Connects gender-disparity analysis to broader [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) cautions about narrow output indicators.
- Links authorship counts to richer contribution traces such as [author contribution statements](../datasets/author_contribution_statements.md) and [contribution role vectors](../representations/contribution_role_vectors.md).
- Applies beyond gender studies whenever publication counts stand in for scholarly activity.

## Operationalization

- State the indexed source and document types included in the output measure.
- List excluded scholarly outputs such as books, conference proceedings, datasets, code, infrastructure, peer review, mentoring, and uncredited technical work.
- Document field differences in authorship attribution, alphabetical order, consortium authorship, and contribution criteria.
- Where possible, triangulate article authorship with contribution statements, acknowledgements, software repositories, datasets, patents, grants, or books.
- Avoid person-level productivity claims when the unit is authorship records rather than disambiguated individuals.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Lariviere et al. (2013) states that authorship of papers is only one of many indicators of research activity.
- The paper notes that the analysis includes journal articles, but not books, conference proceedings, database construction, or code.
- Lariviere et al. also warn that some women may contribute to research activities without appearing as authors.
- The supplement bounds the dataset to Web of Science articles and review articles and excludes editorials, letters, and book reviews.
- The supplement further notes that WoS coverage is especially limited for social sciences and humanities output outside journal articles and English-language articles.

## Caveats

- Broadening output coverage can introduce new source and attribution biases.
- Authorship-output boundaries vary by field, country, language, and publication culture.
- Not every excluded activity has a standardized, ethical, or comparable data trace.
- A boundary statement does not fix measurement bias; it only makes interpretation more defensible.
