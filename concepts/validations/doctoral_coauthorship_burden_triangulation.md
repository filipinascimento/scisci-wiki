# Doctoral-Coauthorship Burden Triangulation

## Summary

Doctoral-coauthorship burden triangulation validates the burden-of-knowledge mechanism by linking patent evidence to doctoral-duration, postdoctoral, Nobel-age, and academic coauthorship trends.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: inventor cohort, doctoral cohort, field, coauthorship corpus, or knowledge-burden mechanism.
- Typical representation: multi-evidence triangulation across training length and teamwork indicators.
- Validation target: whether rising knowledge burden appears beyond a single patent dataset.
- Empirical signature: longer training and more teamwork rise alongside specialization and knowledge-depth measures.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Strengthens [burden of knowledge](../mechanisms/burden_of_knowledge.md) with non-patent evidence.
- Connects [human-capital transfer bottleneck](../mechanisms/human_capital_transfer_bottleneck.md) to observed training-duration trends.
- Links [age at first invention](../measures/age_at_first_invention.md) with [mean team-size growth](../measures/mean_team_size_growth.md).
- Bridges patent evidence to [coauthorship networks](../representations/coauthorship_networks.md).

## Operationalization

- Assemble independent indicators of training duration, doctoral age, postdoctoral frequency, and teamwork.
- Compare temporal trends with patent-based first-invention age and team-size trends.
- Check whether trends are broad across fields rather than confined to a single technology class.
- Use triangulation as support for a shared mechanism, not as proof that all indicators have one cause.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Jones (2009) notes rising doctoral age across major fields and longer doctoral programs.
- The paper cites rising postdoctoral frequency in life sciences and increasing age at doctorate among Nobel Prize winners.
- Jones also connects the mechanism to broad increases in academic coauthorship across science, engineering, and social sciences.

## Caveats

- Doctoral duration and coauthorship can rise for institutional reasons unrelated to knowledge burden.
- Nobel and postdoctoral evidence covers selective populations.
- Triangulation supports mechanism plausibility but does not identify a single causal pathway.
