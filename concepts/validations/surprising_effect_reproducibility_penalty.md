# Surprising-effect reproducibility penalty

## Summary

Effects judged more surprising or less expected may have lower reproducibility because low prior plausibility increases false-positive or context-dependence risk.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: effect, prior plausibility judgment, surprise rating, replication outcome, or moderator score.
- Typical representation: moderator variable linking surprise or prior expectation to replication success.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: prior-plausibility contribution to reproducibility gradients.
- Empirical signature: replication success declines for effects rated as more surprising or less initially plausible.

## Operationalization

- Collect prior-plausibility or surprise ratings before observing replication results where possible.
- Model replication outcome as a function of surprise alongside original p-value, effect size, power, and implementation difficulty.
- Report whether surprise remains predictive after aggregating moderator batteries.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Open Science Collaboration (2015) tests moderators of reproducibility including features related to original evidence and effect characteristics.
- The study motivates surprise or expectation as part of a reproducibility-penalty battery.

## Caveats

- Surprise ratings are subjective and can be field-culture dependent.
- A surprising effect can be true; the motif is a risk signal, not a rejection rule.
