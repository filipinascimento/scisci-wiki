# Science-map mental-model fit

## Summary

Science-map mental-model fit is the validation requirement that a science map's layout, labels, and clusters match how intended users understand the domain.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: map cluster, label, discipline region, user interpretation, or map-reading task.
- Typical representation: cognitive or interpretive fit between map structure and user expectations.
- Validation target: make abstract semantic maps learnable and recognizable.
- Empirical signature: users can identify major domain regions and interpret overlays after learning the map.

## Operationalization

- Evaluate whether cluster labels and positions correspond to recognizable domain structure.
- Test whether users can locate fields, interpret neighboring regions, and understand overlay patterns.
- Compare map learning demands across novice and expert users.
- Revise labels, colors, legends, or aggregation levels where interpretation fails.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Borner et al. (2012) argues that users must learn science maps much like they learn geographic maps.
- The paper states that clusters should have meaningful labels and match the viewer's mental model of the domain.
- It describes the UCSD map as a two-level map with labeled disciplines and subdisciplines supporting overlay exploration.

## Caveats

- Mental-model fit can vary by discipline, role, and cultural background.
- A familiar map can still be structurally inaccurate.
- Improving recognizability can conflict with algorithmic layout fidelity.
