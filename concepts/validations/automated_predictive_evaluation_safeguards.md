# Automated predictive-evaluation safeguards

## Summary

Automated predictive-evaluation safeguards are validation and governance requirements for systems that forecast the future impact of proposals, manuscripts, scholars, teams, or institutions.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: prediction model, evaluation workflow, proposal, manuscript, scholar, institution, subgroup, or portfolio.
- Typical representation: audit checklist covering accuracy, calibration, fairness, accountability, transparency, novelty effects, and deployment consequences.
- Validation target: ensure predictive tools do not narrow discovery, magnify inequality, or substitute crude correlations for expert judgment.
- Empirical signature: model deployment is evaluated by portfolio outcomes and subgroup effects, not only retrospective prediction accuracy.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [prediction feedback loops](prediction_feedback_loops.md) into concrete safeguards for automated evaluation systems.
- Connects [scientific discovery prediction](../methods/scientific_discovery_prediction.md) with [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md), [peer review and gatekeeping](../mechanisms/peer_review_gatekeeping.md), and [SciSci quasi-experimental policy evaluation](scisci_quasi_experimental_policy_evaluation.md).
- Applies to proposal triage, manuscript triage, hiring, promotion, prizes, strategic funding, and institutional ranking.
- Requires special audits for novelty, interdisciplinarity, underrepresented groups, and early-career researchers.
- Also applies to [mind-machine science partnerships](../methods/mind_machine_science_partnerships.md) when AI systems move from search support into evaluation or resource allocation.

## Operationalization

- Validate model accuracy and calibration by field, cohort, institution type, career stage, and demographic subgroup where ethically and legally appropriate.
- Compare deployed outcomes with randomized, phased, or quasi-experimental evaluation designs.
- Audit whether recommendations concentrate resources, penalize novelty, discourage interdisciplinarity, or reduce field diversity.
- Include explicit out-of-distribution audits for novel discoveries and design links to [feedback-resistant success measures](../methods/feedback_resistant_success_measures.md).
- Document features, training data, uncertainty, limitations, decision authority, appeal routes, and human oversight.
- Monitor post-deployment adaptation, gaming, and feedback loops.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Clauset, Larremore, and Sinatra (2017) warns that funders, publishers, and universities may build automated systems to evaluate the future impact of proposals, manuscripts, or young scholars from bibliographic databases.
- The paper argues that such systems could discourage innovation and exacerbate existing inequalities by learning trivial correlations with crude indicators of past success.
- Clauset et al. state that prediction tools should not inhibit future discovery, marginalize underrepresented groups, exclude novel ideas, discourage interdisciplinary work, or suppress new fields.
- The paper's warning that data-mining models learn from past science is split out as [novel-discovery out-of-distribution limit](novel_discovery_out_of_distribution_limit.md).
- The same text invokes fairness, accountability, and transparency in machine learning as principles that should guide predictive tools for science evaluation.
- This warning is now split into mechanism pages for [predictable-discovery selection pressure](../mechanisms/predictable_discovery_selection_pressure.md), [risk-averse scientist selection](../mechanisms/risk_averse_scientist_selection.md), and [scientific ecosystem diversity](../mechanisms/scientific_ecosystem_diversity.md).

## Caveats

- A technically accurate model can still fail as policy if it changes incentives or allocates opportunity unfairly.
- Transparency can conflict with privacy, proprietary data, and gaming risks.
- Safeguards require institutional authority and ongoing monitoring, not only one-time model validation.
