# Citation elite age-cohort confound

## Summary

Citation elite age-cohort confound is the risk that cumulative citation-based elite status partly reflects researcher age or cohort exposure rather than only current influence or quality.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author, cohort, career age, citation elite, field, or observation window.
- Typical representation: elite membership by career age, age-stratified citation concentration, or cohort-normalized elite shares.
- Validation target: test whether citation-elite membership is robust to career-age and exposure-time differences.
- Empirical signature: cumulative citation totals are strongly correlated with age, motivating within-cohort or career-stage comparisons.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Qualifies [citation elite concentration](../measures/citation_elite_concentration.md) and [author citation Gini](../measures/author_citation_gini.md).

- Provides a guardrail for author-level evaluation systems that use cumulative citation totals.
- Complements [h-index seniority benchmarks](../measures/h_index_seniority_benchmarks.md) and [career-long and single-year author impact](../measures/career_long_single_year_author_impact.md).

## Operationalization

- Define career age from first publication, first indexed output, degree year, or another documented marker.
- Recompute elite membership and inequality statistics within age cohorts.
- Compare cumulative citation measures with recent-window or single-year author impact measures.
- Report whether older cohorts dominate the elite tail and whether trends persist within comparable cohorts.
- Separate age effects from field, institution, collaboration, and disambiguation effects.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Nielsen and Andersen (2021) states that per-author cumulative citation rates make citation-elite membership strongly correlated with age.
- The paper identifies age-cohort analysis as a future research direction for understanding citation concentration.
- Nielsen and Andersen also note that authorship-position changes among elite scientists should be studied in fields where first and last authorship are important.
- This makes age and career-stage stratification a required validation before interpreting citation elite status as contemporaneous status or merit.

## Caveats

- Career age is hard to define consistently across countries, fields, and interrupted careers.
- Within-cohort analysis can reduce sample size and make field-specific elite tails unstable.
- Age adjustment may remove meaningful cumulative-career influence if the target construct is lifetime recognition.
