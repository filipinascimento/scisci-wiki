# Communication-technology jump test

## Summary

Communication-technology jump test asks whether a collaboration trend has a discrete acceleration around a communication-technology adoption period, rather than only a long-running secular increase.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: year, field-year cell, collaboration share, or intervention-adjacent period.
- Typical representation: year-on-year changes, event-year discontinuity estimate, pretrend and posttrend comparison, or domain-specific jump table.
- Validation target: whether reduced communication costs plausibly explain a sudden collaboration increase.
- Empirical signature: an event-period increase exceeds the typical annual change and is followed by a changed level or slope.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Sharpens claims about the "death of distance" by testing timing, not just geographic correlation.
- Complements [geographic reach-frequency decoupling](../mechanisms/geographic_reach_frequency_decoupling.md), because frequency can rise without much increase in average tie distance.
- Provides a robustness frame for [between-school collaboration share](../measures/between_school_collaboration_share.md) time series.
- Helps separate long-running organizational changes from communication infrastructure shocks.
- Complements [communication-technology initiation caveat](communication_technology_initiation_caveat.md), which separates collaboration initiation from collaboration maintenance.

## Operationalization

- Choose an event window tied to a plausible technology or infrastructure transition.
- Estimate annual changes in collaboration share before, during, and after the event window.
- Compare the event-year change with typical year-on-year changes and domain-specific variation.
- Interpret a jump cautiously when pretrends are strong or when the post-event slope returns to the earlier trajectory.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Jones et al. (2008) checks whether 1998 shows an unusual increase in U.S. science and engineering between-school collaboration.
- The paper reports a 3.4 percentage point jump in science and engineering in 1998, compared with a typical annual increase of about 0.8 percentage points, and gives a significance level below 0.0001.
- The same evidence reports that the growth rate after the jump continues at about the pre-1998 rate and that social sciences show little unusual acceleration.
- Jones et al. therefore treat recent communication technologies as an incomplete explanation for multi-university collaboration growth, because much of the growth predates the tested jump.

## Caveats

- A single event year may not capture gradual adoption, database coverage changes, policy shifts, or grant-program changes.
- Significance of a level jump does not establish that communication technology caused the jump.
- Collaboration data can be affected by address-recording changes around the same period.
- Different fields may adopt communication technologies on different timelines.
- Communication tools can support maintenance and data exchange even when they do not initiate collaboration.
