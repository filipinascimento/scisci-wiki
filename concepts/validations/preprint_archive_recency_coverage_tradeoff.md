# Preprint-archive recency-coverage tradeoff

## Summary

Preprint-archive recency-coverage tradeoff is the validation caveat that preprint archives can provide timely collaboration traces while remaining less complete than professionally curated bibliographic databases.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: bibliographic source, preprint archive, collaboration network, field panel, or time window.
- Typical representation: coverage-versus-timeliness caveat, source comparison, or database provenance note.
- Validation target: avoid treating fast preprint traces as complete records of field collaboration.
- Empirical signature: a preprint archive is more current for some communities but misses papers or authors covered by curated sources.

## Operationalization

- Record whether a collaboration network is built from preprints, journals, curated indexes, or mixed sources.
- Compare coverage, update frequency, and source-maintenance rules across data sources.
- Report fields or subfields for which preprint posting is common enough to support inference.
- Avoid interpreting absent preprint ties as absent collaboration without source-coverage checks.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Newman (2004) describes the Physics E-print Archive as a timely source of publication records.
- The same section says preprint archive coverage is less complete than professionally maintained sources such as Medline.
- Newman also notes that the studied databases are more complete than a single journal while still not claiming to document every paper.

## Caveats

- Preprint adoption differs strongly by field and period.
- Recency can improve timing analyses while worsening representativeness.
- Cross-source author disambiguation can create additional error.
