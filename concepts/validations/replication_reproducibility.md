# Replication and reproducibility

## Summary

Replication and reproducibility motifs focus on whether scientific claims, analyses, and findings can be independently repeated, reproduced, or robustly validated.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: claim, experiment, dataset, analysis pipeline, paper, field, or replication attempt.
- Typical representation: replication outcome table, effect-size comparison, code/data audit, or evidence graph.
- Mechanism or measurement target: reliability of findings and research practices.
- Empirical signature: replication rates, effect-size shrinkage, reproducibility failures, and robustness to analytic choices.

## Operationalization

- Inputs: original studies, replication studies, data/code availability, protocols, effect sizes, p-values, preregistration, citations.
- Measures: replication success, reproducibility status, effect-size ratio, p-curve, bias indicators, code/data availability.
- Common model forms: meta-analysis, reproducibility audit, registered replication, multiverse analysis.

## Evidence and Validations

- Ioannidis provides a foundational argument about false findings; large collaborations provide empirical replication estimates.
- Verified full-text evidence from Ioannidis (2005) frames false-positive risk as a function of study power, bias, prior probabilities, flexibility, and selective reporting.
- The same verified full text splits replication risk into lower-level components: [pre-study odds](../measures/pre_study_odds.md), [study power and false-positive risk](../measures/study_power_false_positive_risk.md), [research bias parameter](../measures/research_bias_parameter.md), [field testing multiplicity](../mechanisms/field_testing_multiplicity.md), [hot-field Proteus phenomenon](../mechanisms/hot_field_proteus_phenomenon.md), and [null-field bias estimation](null_field_bias_estimation.md).
- Verified full-text evidence from Simmons et al. (2011) operationalizes one mechanism behind replication failures: undisclosed flexibility in collection, exclusion, analysis, and reporting decisions can raise the actual false-positive rate far above the nominal threshold.
- Verified full-text evidence from Nosek et al. (2018) turns that mechanism into a workflow distinction between prediction and postdiction, making preregistration a validation practice rather than only a transparency norm.
- Verified full-text evidence from Fang et al. (2012) adds a post-publication correction lens: formal retractions are visible correction events, but they are delayed, detection-dependent, and often incompletely explained.
- Verified full-text evidence from Open Science Collaboration (2015) makes the motif empirical at scale: 100 direct replications were run from a defined psychology sampling frame, with multiple non-equivalent success criteria and public replication materials.
- The same full text separates the protocol layer [direct replication protocol](../methods/direct_replication_protocol.md), the denominator layer [2008 psychology journal replication sampling frame](../datasets/psychology_reproducibility_sampling_frame.md), the measurement layer [replication success indicator suite](../measures/replication_success_indicator_suite.md), and the outcome pattern [replication effect-size shrinkage](../measures/replication_effect_size_shrinkage.md).
- The OSC evidence now also separates focal-effect selection, feasibility attrition, effect harmonization, subjective replication judgment, meta-analytic recombination, and independent analysis reproduction as distinct reproducibility design components.
- The narrower mechanism page [selective reporting and publication bias](../mechanisms/publication_bias_selective_reporting.md) covers the visibility-selection layer, while [open science interventions](open_science_interventions.md) covers reform portfolios that try to reduce it.
- The page should be used as a validation motif for proposed science-of-science indicators: high citation impact, novelty, or attention does not by itself establish robust empirical truth.

## Caveats

- Replication definitions vary by field.
- A failed replication can reflect theory scope, implementation differences, measurement changes, or original false positives.
- Retraction, reproducibility failure, and preregistration status measure different parts of the validity pipeline and should not be collapsed into one score.
