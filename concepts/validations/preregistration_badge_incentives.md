# Preregistration badge incentives

## Summary

Preregistration badge incentives are journal, funder, or platform signals that make preregistered studies visible and rewardable in the publication record.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: badge, article, journal policy, funder incentive, challenge award, registry record, or adoption cohort.
- Typical representation: badge indicator, incentive-adoption timeline, before/after policy comparison, or article-level compliance audit.
- Validation target: whether low-cost incentives shift researcher behavior toward preregistration and transparent reporting.
- Empirical signature: higher preregistration uptake, visible article labels, registry links in papers, or increased compliance after badge adoption.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Splits the incentive layer out of [open science interventions](open_science_interventions.md).
- Provides an intervention design for increasing use of the [preregistration registry ecosystem](../datasets/preregistration_registry_ecosystem.md).
- Complements mandates such as clinical-trial registration and standards such as TOP Guidelines.
- Gives [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) a cautionary example: badges should be interpreted as signals to audit, not simple quality scores.

## Operationalization

- Identify journals, funders, or platforms that introduced preregistration badges or awards.
- Link articles to badge status, registry records, and publication dates.
- Compare preregistration uptake before and after adoption, or against non-adopting controls.
- Audit badge quality by checking whether registry links exist, plans are public, and reports adhere to registered plans.
- Separate symbolic badge adoption from substantive preregistration quality.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Nosek et al. (2018) describes journals adopting preregistration badges that visibly credit authors on published articles.
- Nosek et al. compare these incentives to open-data badges, which were associated with a large increase in data sharing in an initial test.
- The same full text describes the Preregistration Challenge as offering monetary awards for publishing preregistered studies.
- Nosek et al. frame badges, challenges, journal policies, and registered reports as complementary incentive changes that align career interests with preregistration.

## Caveats

- Badges can become symbolic if registry records are vague, private, or not checked.
- Incentives can increase uptake without guaranteeing adherence or analysis quality.
- Badge effects should be evaluated with controls for field, journal, article type, and policy timing.
- Visible labels can create reputation signals that need quality audits.
