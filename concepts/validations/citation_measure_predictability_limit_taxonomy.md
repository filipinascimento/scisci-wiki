# Citation-measure predictability-limit taxonomy

## Summary

Citation-measure predictability-limit taxonomy separates the main reasons common citation indicators fail as long-run predictors: journal proxy error, age exposure, early-recognition blind spots, and cross-field citation-practice incommensurability.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: citation indicator, paper, journal proxy, field comparison, or evaluation decision.
- Typical representation: limitation taxonomy, threat model, or validation checklist.
- Validation target: whether short-window and venue-based citation indicators can predict long-term paper impact.
- Empirical signature: different indicators fail for different reasons, so one correction does not solve all prediction limits.

## Operationalization

- Identify the citation measure being used and the forecast target.
- Classify threats from venue proxying, exposure time, delayed recognition, and field practice differences.
- Test each threat separately when possible.
- Avoid using a single citation correction as a universal long-term impact predictor.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Song, and Barabasi (2013) frames impact factor and short-term citations as lacking predictive power for long-term impact.
- The introduction distinguishes journal impact factor limitations, raw citation age bias, delayed recognition for paradigm-changing discoveries, and incompatible field and journal traditions.
- The paper's policy discussion returns to these limitations when discussing reward procedures and grant decisions.

## Caveats

- The taxonomy diagnoses predictability limits, not all possible metric harms.
- Some limitations interact, such as field tempo and early-citation windows.
- Later citation models may reduce but not eliminate these threats.
