# Single replication truth-status boundary

## Summary

Single replication truth-status boundary is the validation principle that one replication can update confidence but cannot definitively establish that an effect is true or false.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: original finding, replication finding, direct replication, or cumulative evidence set.
- Typical representation: boundary statement, confidence update, uncertainty-reduction path, and planned follow-up evidence.
- Validation target: prevent binary truth claims from a single replication result.
- Empirical signature: replication reports distinguish reliability evidence from definitive theoretical confirmation or falsification.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a narrow caveat to [replication and reproducibility](replication_reproducibility.md).
- Frames [replication meta-analytic recombination](../methods/replication_meta_analytic_recombination.md) as cumulative evidence rather than final proof.
- Connects [innovation-verification resource balance](../mechanisms/innovation_verification_resource_balance.md) to uncertainty reduction.
- Reinforces [scientometric measure construct validation](scientometric_measure_construct_validation.md) when replication outcomes are converted into indicators.

## Operationalization

- Report whether the replication increases, decreases, or leaves unchanged confidence in the original result.
- Avoid labeling a single success as proof that a theory is correct.
- Avoid labeling a single failure as proof that the original effect was false positive.
- Use multiple replications, moderator tests, meta-analysis, and theoretical triangulation for stronger status claims.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Open Science Collaboration (2015) states that successful direct replication primarily supports reliability, not theoretical understanding.
- OSC warns that replication failure can reflect design, sample, setting, or procedure differences rather than a false original finding.
- The paper concludes that the project established zero effects as definitively true and zero effects as definitively false, describing science as cumulative uncertainty reduction.

## Caveats

- This boundary should not be used to dismiss serious replication failures.
- Some safety-critical or high-cost settings may require stronger action after one failure than ordinary epistemic language suggests.
- The boundary is about truth status, not about whether a replication changes practical decision weights.
