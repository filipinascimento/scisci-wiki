# Exploratory-confirmatory disclosure boundary

## Summary

The exploratory-confirmatory disclosure boundary is a validation motif that asks whether analyses generated after inspecting data are clearly labeled as exploratory, separately confirmed, or incorrectly presented as prospective tests.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: analysis, hypothesis, result claim, manuscript section, preregistration deviation, or replication follow-up.
- Typical representation: exploratory/confirmatory label, deviation table, analysis provenance log, preregistration comparison, or replication status.
- Validation target: preserve inferential meaning by distinguishing discovery-oriented search from prospective evidence testing.
- Empirical signature: a claim is supported by the same data that generated it, but the manuscript does or does not disclose that status.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines the broader [prediction-postdiction boundary](prediction_postdiction_boundary.md) into a manuscript-audit and reviewer-enforcement target.
- Explains why [analytic flexibility and false-positive risk](analytic_flexibility_false_positives.md) is most damaging when exploratory paths are described as confirmatory evidence.
- Gives [preregistration and registered reports](preregistration_registered_reports.md) a post hoc complement: deviations and discoveries can remain useful when their evidential status is explicit.
- Connects [HARKing and hindsight bias](../mechanisms/harking_hindsight_bias.md) to practical disclosure, replication, and review decisions.

## Operationalization

- Compare manuscript claims with preregistrations, protocols, timestamps, code commits, lab records, or author disclosures.
- Label each major claim as prospectively specified, exploratory, transparent deviation, or independently replicated.
- Audit whether exploratory findings are followed by direct confirmatory tests or [exact-replication flexibility adjudication](../methods/exact_replication_flexibility_adjudication.md).
- Track whether journals and reviewers request clear labels for post hoc analyses, robustness searches, and theory revisions.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Simmons et al. (2011) argues that exploratory research is appropriate when it is reported as exploratory or confirmed through exact replication.
- The same paper shows why undisclosed flexibility is dangerous: common analytic decisions can make an exploratory search appear to be a planned confirmatory test.
- Verified full-text evidence from Nosek et al. (2018) generalizes the distinction by separating prediction from postdiction and by treating preregistration as a way to preserve that distinction.
- Together, these papers make the boundary a validation problem rather than a rule against exploration.

## Caveats

- Exploratory work can be scientifically valuable and should not be downgraded solely because it is post hoc.
- A confirmatory label is only useful if the analysis plan was specific enough to constrain decisions.
- Existing or archival data can support confirmatory tests only when the relevant outcome information was not available during specification.
- Boundary audits can be incomplete when preregistrations, code, or reviewer histories are unavailable.
