# Peer-review cost-accounting omission

## Summary

Reviewer labor, applicant effort, and administrative overhead are a missing cost side of peer-review value-added estimates.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: reviewer hour, applicant hour, panel meeting, administrative cost, grant application, or opportunity cost.
- Typical representation: cost-accounting ledger paired with peer-review predictive-validity estimates.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: welfare interpretation of peer-review value after accounting for review costs.
- Empirical signature: peer review can have predictive value while still imposing large labor and opportunity costs..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines peer-review welfare validation by linking it to [proposal burden research time crowding](../mechanisms/proposal_burden_research_time_crowding.md) and [peer review gatekeeping](../mechanisms/peer_review_gatekeeping.md).
- Useful as a reusable distinction when [grant peer review value added](grant_peer_review_value_added.md) is analyzed in science-of-science data.
- Adds cross-links to [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) so the motif is not read as an isolated result.

## Operationalization

- Pair score predictive validity with estimates of reviewer time, applicant time, panel administration, resubmission effort, and opportunity costs.
- Report benefit-cost measures separately from predictive-validity coefficients.
- Compare review-system designs under both selection quality and review burden.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Li and Agha (2015) explicitly states that the analysis does not account for peer-review costs, especially reviewer time investment.
- This marks a cost boundary on the welfare interpretation of review-score value.

## Caveats

- Cost accounting can change policy conclusions even when score predictive validity is positive.
- Applicant and reviewer costs are hard to observe consistently across programs.
