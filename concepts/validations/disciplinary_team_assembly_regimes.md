# Disciplinary Team-Assembly Regimes

## Summary

Disciplinary team-assembly regimes are cross-field patterns in team-formation parameters that reveal when fields follow a common collaboration regime or depart from it.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: discipline, journal set, creative domain, or collaboration network.
- Typical representation: field-level comparison of `p`, `q`, repeat-link fraction, and giant-component size.
- Validation target: whether a team-assembly model generalizes across domains.
- Empirical signature: most fields cluster near similar parameter regimes, while outlier fields show distinct repeat-collaboration or component structure.

## Operationalization

- Estimate newcomer share, repeat-collaboration probability, repeat-link fraction, and giant-component size for each field.
- Compare fields against the same team-size-controlled assembly model.
- Identify fields near the tipping line and fields with unusually dense repeat collaboration.
- Relate regime position to journal impact, task structure, infrastructure needs, or historical field organization.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Guimera et al. (2005) reports field-level `p`, `q`, repeat-link fraction, and largest-component size for Broadway musicals and four scientific fields.
- The paper finds four of five creative networks close to the tipping line, with astronomy as an exception marked by higher repeated incumbent collaboration.
- Guimera et al. suggest that four fields may share a similar optimal parameter set while astronomy lacks the same performance correlations.

## Caveats

- Regime estimates depend on journal-set boundaries and historical coverage.
- Similar parameter values do not prove identical social mechanisms.
- Outlier regimes may reflect instrumentation, infrastructure, large-team norms, or data construction.
