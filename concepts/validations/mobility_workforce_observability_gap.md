# Mobility workforce observability gap

## Summary

National workforce surveys can describe the size and composition of a scientific workforce while missing movement, destinations, networks, and impact effects.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientific workforce dataset, survey, or administrative record.
- Typical representation: coverage audit for affiliation histories, mobility events, collaboration ties, and impact outcomes.
- Validation target: whether workforce data can observe dynamic mobility and its consequences.
- Empirical signature: static workforce counts exist while longitudinal movement and network fields are absent.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects [scientific mobility](../mechanisms/scientific_mobility.md) to data-readiness audits.
- Useful before using national workforce snapshots to study brain circulation or mobility policy.
- Links survey-style human-capital measures to [affiliation trajectory reconstruction](../methods/affiliation_trajectory_reconstruction.md).

## Operationalization

- Audit whether datasets include longitudinal affiliation, origin, destination, collaboration network, output, and citation fields.
- Record which mobility mechanisms can be tested and which remain unobservable.
- Pair static workforce data with publication-affiliation histories when movement is central.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sugimoto et al. (2017) frames the problem by noting that less is known about how often researchers move, where they go, what networks they form, and how mobility affects impact.
- This supports a validation motif around observability rather than a single empirical result.

## Caveats

- The motif is a data-design gap, not a causal test.
- Publication affiliations can miss nonpublishing labor, teaching, industry work, and temporary visits.
