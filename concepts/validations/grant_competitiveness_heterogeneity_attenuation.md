# Grant competitiveness heterogeneity attenuation

## Summary

Grant competitiveness heterogeneity attenuation treats unobserved selectivity differences inside a broad competitive-grant category as measurement error that can dampen estimated funding-model effects.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: grant program, project, funding category, selectivity level, coefficient, or interaction estimate.
- Typical representation: within-category heterogeneity caveat, program-selectivity sensitivity, or attenuation-bias argument.
- Validation target: assess whether a coarse competitive-funding indicator hides variation in competition intensity.
- Empirical signature: broader funding categories produce conservative or blurred estimates relative to finer program-level selectivity measures.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Qualifies [competitive-funding share threshold](../measures/competitive_funding_share_threshold.md) and [fractional project-funding attribution](../measures/fractional_project_funding_attribution.md).
- Pairs with [funding definition sensitivity](funding_definition_sensitivity.md) when broad funding categories are recoded.
- Helps interpret [funding status-interaction tests](funding_status_interaction_test.md) when low- and high-status researchers face different grant markets inside the same category.

## Operationalization

- Record the finest available funder, program, award mechanism, success rate, and review route for each project.
- Compare results using broad competitive-versus-block indicators against finer selectivity bins.
- Interpret null or small coefficients cautiously when the observed category pools highly selective awards with routine or entitlement-like awards.
- Test whether status interactions strengthen when competition intensity is measured more precisely.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Lee, and Walsh (2018) notes that their competitive-grant measure can include grants with different levels of competitiveness.
- The paper treats this unobserved heterogeneity as a limitation that would likely attenuate differences between competitive and block funding.
- This caveat supports recording selectivity and program structure in future funding-model studies rather than treating all competitive grants as equivalent.

## Caveats

- Heterogeneity does not always imply attenuation; misclassification can change signs if funding subtypes have different mechanisms.
- Program-level success rates may be unavailable, time-varying, or strategically defined.
- Finer categories can reduce power and raise multiple-comparison problems.
