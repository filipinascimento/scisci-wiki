# Replication same-population CI assumption

## Summary

Confidence-interval coverage comparisons assume that original and replication studies estimate effects from the same underlying population and construct.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: original effect, replication effect, confidence interval, sampled population, construct, or protocol deviation.
- Typical representation: coverage-validation assumption linking original and replication estimands.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: interpretability of original confidence-interval coverage by replication effects.
- Empirical signature: replication effects fall outside original confidence intervals, but the inference depends on whether the same estimand was targeted.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects replication interval validation to [replication ci coverage shortfall](replication_ci_coverage_shortfall.md) and [replication effect harmonization](../methods/replication_effect_harmonization.md).

## Operationalization

- Document population, setting, measurement, and protocol differences between original and replication studies.
- Use original confidence-interval coverage only when the same-estimand assumption is plausible.
- Pair coverage checks with fidelity review and effect harmonization notes.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Open Science Collaboration (2015) compares replication effects with original confidence intervals.
- The authors explicitly note assumptions and interpretation limits when treating original and replication estimates as sampling the same effect.

## Caveats

- A coverage failure can reflect true heterogeneity rather than original false positivity.
- The same-population assumption is often strongest for tightly standardized lab effects and weaker for context-sensitive effects.
