# Active-PI definition robustness

## Summary

Active-PI definition robustness checks whether career-setback results depend on defining active scientists through future grant-system activity versus broader publication activity.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: principal investigator, grant-system activity, publication activity, attrition indicator, or survivor sample.
- Typical representation: active-PI definition compared with alternative active-scientist definitions.
- Validation target: ensure attrition and survivor-conditioned effects are not artifacts of one activity definition.
- Empirical signature: main setback patterns persist when activity is defined through grants, publications, or combined traces.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Validates [early-career setback attrition](../mechanisms/early_career_setback_attrition.md).
- Supports [survivor-conditioned setback advantage](survivor_conditioned_setback_advantage.md) and [setback-effect robustness grid](setback_effect_robustness_grid.md).
- Provides a general outcome-definition audit for career studies using administrative funding systems.

## Operationalization

- Define the primary active status, such as remaining in the NIH system through later applications or awards.
- Define alternative active statuses, such as publishing after treatment or appearing in external scholarly databases.
- Recompute attrition and survivor-conditioned outcomes under each definition.
- Report whether the substantive conclusion changes when activity is observed outside the focal administrative system.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Jones, and Wang (2019) defines active PIs using future NIH-system activity and measures permanent disappearance from that system.
- The paper reports that near misses have substantially higher attrition from the NIH system.
- Wang et al. also examine alternative active-scientist definitions, including publishing scientists, as part of the robustness checks.

## Caveats

- Administrative-system exit is not the same as leaving science.
- Publication activity can continue through collaborations, non-PI roles, or non-NIH funding.
- Activity definitions can change subgroup composition and should be reported with denominators.
