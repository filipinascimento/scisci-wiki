# Workforce-output parity divergence

## Summary

Workforce-output parity divergence is the validation warning that demographic parity in the scientific workforce can differ sharply from parity in indexed publication output.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: country, field, workforce survey, publication corpus, gendered authorship aggregate, or demographic group.
- Typical representation: workforce share versus indexed authorship share, headcount-output comparison, or indicator-boundary note.
- Validation target: prevent publication authorship ratios from being interpreted as direct workforce composition.
- Empirical signature: a country can report workforce gender parity while its indexed publication output remains far from parity.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Sharpens the boundary around [gendered fractional authorship ratio](../measures/gendered_fractional_authorship_ratio.md).
- Extends [authorship-output indicator boundary](authorship_output_indicator_boundary.md) to demographic parity claims.
- Helps [gender and race stratification](../mechanisms/gender_race_stratification.md) analyses avoid mixing labor-force, authorship, and productivity denominators.
- Provides a guardrail for [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) in equity dashboards.

## Operationalization

- State whether the denominator is people, authorships, papers, full-time-equivalent researchers, faculty, applicants, or another unit.
- Compare publication-output indicators with workforce surveys only when coverage, country, field, and year boundaries are explicit.
- Report excluded research outputs such as books, software, data, conference papers, local-language journals, and nonpublication labor.
- Treat divergence between workforce and output measures as a research question rather than a measurement error by default.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Lariviere et al. (2013) distinguishes their Web of Science authorship-output indicator from a complete measure of research activity.
- They report that fewer than six percent of countries reach parity in their indexed authorship-output measure, while a UNESCO workforce statistic indicates a larger share of countries with equal numbers of men and women scientists.
- The paper's discussion explicitly warns that authorship is only one indicator and omits books, conference papers, data, code, and other scientific activity.
- This divergence motivates separate treatment of workforce composition, authorship output, author position, collaboration, and citation impact.

## Caveats

- Workforce data and bibliometric data often use different field boundaries, country rules, and time windows.
- Publication-output gaps can reflect seniority, role allocation, resource access, collaboration access, source coverage, or database selection.
- Workforce parity does not imply parity in funding, prestige, leadership, citations, or career security.
- Publication-output parity does not imply workforce parity if output is concentrated among a small subset of people.
