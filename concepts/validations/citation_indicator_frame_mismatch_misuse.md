# Citation-indicator frame mismatch misuse

## Summary

Captures misuse from comparing calculations with different sources, windows, self-citation rules, field frames, or metric definitions as if they were commensurable.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: metric comparison, evaluation dossier, or author ranking.
- Typical representation: frame metadata checklist for source, date, window, self-citation policy, field taxonomy, and career scope.
- Validation target: comparability of citation-indicator calculations.
- Empirical signature: rank or score differences arise from frame mismatch rather than author performance.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [citation impact indicators](../measures/citation_impact_indicators.md) with a misuse-specific frame audit.
- Useful before comparing CV metrics, database exports, or annual author-table releases.
- Connects author assessment to [citation window selection](../methods/citation_window_selection.md) and census-date provenance.

## Operationalization

- Require source, census date, citation window, self-citation policy, field taxonomy, author role, document types, and career window before comparison.
- Block or flag comparisons where frames do not align.
- Where frames differ intentionally, interpret differences as sensitivity analyses rather than performance gaps.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ioannidis et al. (2019) flags unstandardized calculations, different frames, and failure to account for field as assessment misuse.
- The standardized database is proposed as a way to reduce this frame mismatch.

## Caveats

- Aligning frames reduces one misuse mode but does not prove the metric measures quality.
- Some legitimate questions require different frames, but the difference must be explicit.
