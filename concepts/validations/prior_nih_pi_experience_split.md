# Prior-NIH PI experience split

## Summary

Applicants without prior NIH principal-investigator funding provide a subgroup where grant-system reputation signals should be weaker.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: principal investigator, prior NIH award, R01 application, priority score, citation outcome, or publication history.
- Typical representation: subgroup validation by prior NIH PI funding status.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: peer-review predictive validity among less grant-established applicants.
- Empirical signature: review scores predict outcomes similarly for applicants with and without prior NIH grants..

## Operationalization

- Stratify applications by whether the PI previously held NIH funding as a primary recipient.
- Compare score gradients after controlling for publication and institutional prestige.
- Use the subgroup to test whether grant-system reputation is driving the score-outcome relationship.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Li and Agha (2015) reports similar score-citation associations for applicants with and without prior NIH grants.
- The subgroup weakens a pure prior-grant-reputation interpretation of review-score value.

## Caveats

- No-prior-NIH applicants can still have publication prestige, institutional support, and informal reputation.
- The split is a validation check, not a randomized removal of reputation signals.
