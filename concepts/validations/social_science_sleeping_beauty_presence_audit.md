# Social-science sleeping-beauty presence audit

## Summary

Claims that sleeping beauties are absent from social science should be audited against broader multidisciplinary data, field coverage, and case inventories.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field-level delayed-recognition prevalence and case list.
- Typical representation: high-B prevalence by field plus manually inspected social-science cases.
- Validation target: whether apparent field absence is substantive or caused by corpus and threshold choices.
- Empirical signature: broader datasets reveal social-science high-B cases missed by narrower screens.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Pairs [sleeping-beauty field-origin profile](../measures/sleeping_beauty_field_origin_profile.md) with corpus-sensitivity checks.
- Useful when comparing delayed recognition across natural sciences, biomedical fields, and social sciences.
- Connects case inventories to [field classification sensitivity](field_classification_sensitivity.md).

## Operationalization

- Compute high-B prevalence across field groupings and inspect whether social-science cases survive alternative thresholds.
- Compare datasets with different social-science coverage, including books or non-journal outputs where available.
- Audit whether previous absence claims came from field classification, threshold definition, or data-source coverage.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ke et al. (2015) reports social-science examples among high-B cases and discusses Zachary's karate-club work as a delayed-recognition case.
- The finding challenges claims based on narrower datasets or threshold rules that sleeping beauties do not occur in social science.

## Caveats

- Social-science citation data can underrepresent books, non-English work, and slower citation channels.
- Field labels can split interdisciplinary social-science methods across multiple categories.
