# Locally relevant research metrics

## Summary

Locally relevant research metrics protect work whose value depends on regional, national, language-specific, policy, clinical, cultural, or community context.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, book, language corpus, regional journal, policy contribution, local problem domain, institution, or evaluation unit.
- Typical representation: local-output coverage audit, language/source inclusion check, regional relevance category, or non-English reference set.
- Validation target: test whether evaluation data and indicators recognize locally important research.
- Empirical signature: rankings or scores change when non-English, regional, book, policy, or locally focused outputs are included.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [citation database coverage sensitivity](citation_database_coverage_sensitivity.md) from technical coverage to evaluation fairness.
- Gives [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) a concrete audit for language and regional bias.
- Links [Web of Science](../datasets/web_of_science.md), [OpenAlex](../datasets/openalex.md), and [Dimensions](../datasets/dimensions.md) coverage choices to local research missions.
- Helps evaluate whether incentives suppress context-specific work in favor of globally visible journals.

## Operationalization

- Identify research outputs relevant to local, national, regional, linguistic, policy, clinical, or community needs.
- Compare whether those outputs are captured in the databases used for evaluation.
- Audit whether journal-impact, citation, and ranking indicators penalize non-English or locally engaged outputs.
- Use local-language, book, policy, clinical, or regional source layers when the evaluation mission requires them.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hicks et al. (2015) argues that excellence in locally relevant research should be protected.
- Hicks et al. state that English-language publication and Web-of-Science-indexed impact factors can suppress social sciences, humanities, and regionally engaged work.
- The article gives examples where local labor law, elderly family health care, immigrant employment, and region-specific epidemiology can be undervalued by high-impact English-language journal incentives.
- The evidence motivates a coverage validation: test whether the selected databases and indicators can identify locally relevant excellence before using them for evaluation.
- Verified full-text evidence from Waltman (2016) adds the database-coverage mechanism: SSH outputs are often local, regional, book-based, and non-English, making them vulnerable to undercoverage in standard citation databases.

## Caveats

- Local relevance should be assessed with domain expertise, not assumed from language or venue alone.
- Local-output indicators can become parochial or low-quality if they are not validated.
- Coverage audits should distinguish missing data from legitimately out-of-scope outputs.
