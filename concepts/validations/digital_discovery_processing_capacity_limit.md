# Digital discovery processing-capacity limit

## Summary

The digital discovery processing-capacity limit is the caveat that search engines and online access improve discovery, but bounded human attention still limits how much expanding literature can be processed and cited.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, reader, field, search system, or publication cohort.
- Typical representation: publication growth and discovery technology considered alongside fixed or slowly growing human processing capacity.
- Validation target: whether digital access is sufficient to counteract attention decay from literature growth.
- Empirical signature: improved search coexists with shorter attention windows and faster turnover in cited papers.

## Operationalization

- Compare publication growth, citation half-life, and reference-list growth over time.
- Record major changes in digital discovery, indexing, online access, and search infrastructure.
- Test whether attention windows still shrink after accounting for improved discovery.
- Treat reader time and cognitive processing as constrained resources rather than assuming search fully solves overload.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Parolo et al. (2015) discusses the tension between improved digital access and the accelerating growth of scientific output.
- The paper argues that even with better search tools, bounded human processing can maintain or intensify competition for citation attention.
- This supports interpreting publication growth as an attention constraint rather than only a discoverability problem.

## Caveats

- Digital discovery can affect fields differently.
- Citation decay may reflect changing norms, reference-list length, and topic turnover in addition to processing limits.
- The concept is a boundary condition, not a direct measure of individual reading capacity.
