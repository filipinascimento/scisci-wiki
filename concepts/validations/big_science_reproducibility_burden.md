# Big-Science Reproducibility Burden

## Summary

Big-science reproducibility burden is the validation boundary that independent replication can become practically or economically infeasible when frontier science requires enormous shared infrastructure.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: large instrument, mega-collaboration, experiment, field, or replication attempt.
- Typical representation: replication feasibility constraint attached to infrastructure scale.
- Validation target: whether reproducibility expectations are realistic under big-science cost and coordination regimes.
- Empirical signature: frontier instruments require such large resources that duplicating the initial effort is prohibitive.

## Operationalization

- Identify experiments whose claims depend on large specialized instruments or global collaborations.
- Estimate the cost, personnel, time, and institutional coordination required for independent duplication.
- Distinguish analytic reproducibility, internal cross-checks, and full independent replication.
- Compare validation standards across small-lab, team-science, and big-science settings.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) states that frontier instruments have become too large and precise for most individual investigators or institutions to afford.
- The review cites the Large Hadron Collider as requiring more than 10,000 scientists and engineers from more than 100 countries.
- Fortunato et al. argue that the burden of reproducibility may require duplicating initial efforts that are not practically or economically feasible.

## Caveats

- Big science can still support reproducibility through independent analyses, internal detector redundancy, open data, or cross-experiment comparison.
- Costly replication is not the same as impossible replication.
- The motif should not excuse weak documentation or inaccessible data when those are feasible to improve.
