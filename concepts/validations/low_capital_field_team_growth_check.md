# Low-capital field team-growth check

## Summary

Low-capital field team-growth check tests whether rising teamwork also appears in fields where laboratory scale, expensive equipment, or capital-intensive infrastructure are less plausible explanations.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, subfield, broad domain, year, authorship group, or technology category.
- Typical representation: team-authorship-share and mean-team-size trends stratified by capital intensity or laboratory dependence.
- Validation target: evaluate whether the rise of teamwork can be reduced to big-science scale and capital intensity.
- Empirical signature: teamwork grows substantially in low-capital or less laboratory-dependent fields as well as in capital-intensive fields.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides an explanation check for [mean team-size growth](../measures/mean_team_size_growth.md) and [team-authorship share](../measures/team_authorship_share.md).
- Complements [team-size field-growth control](team_size_field_growth_control.md) by testing a driver other than field population size.
- Helps interpret [team-growth subfield universality](team_growth_subfield_universality.md) when the growth appears outside large laboratory sciences.
- Connects [team impact advantage](../mechanisms/team_impact_advantage.md) to broader mechanisms such as [burden of knowledge](../mechanisms/burden_of_knowledge.md), specialization, and reduced communication costs.

## Operationalization

- Classify fields by laboratory dependence, capital intensity, equipment scale, or other project-resource proxies.
- Track [team-authorship share](../measures/team_authorship_share.md) and [mean team-size growth](../measures/mean_team_size_growth.md) within each field or subfield over time.
- Compare slopes and first-to-last-window changes across high-capital and low-capital fields.
- Pair the contrast with field-size controls, authorship-norm checks, and citation-impact comparisons.
- Re-estimate the check in current Dimensions or OpenAlex panels before treating historical results as current.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wuchty, Jones, and Uzzi (2007) states that teamwork trends are equally strong in the social sciences, where big-science scale, complexity, and costs are much less significant than in laboratory sciences.
- The same paper reports that social-science team-authored papers rose from 17.5% in 1955 to 51.5% in 2000.
- Wuchty et al. highlight mathematics as a low-capital hard-science example: team-authored work rose from 19% to 57%, and mean team size rose from 1.22 to 1.84.
- In their discussion, Wuchty et al. argue that capital intensity may explain some laboratory sciences but is unlikely to explain similar team-size growth in mathematics, economics, and sociology.

## Caveats

- Low-capital fields still require time, data, software, fieldwork, or institutional resources; the contrast does not prove that capital intensity is irrelevant.
- Field labels are coarse proxies for project-level resource requirements.
- Authorship norms and publication coverage differ across social sciences, mathematics, humanities, patents, and laboratory sciences.
- The check should be paired with direct resource measures when available.
