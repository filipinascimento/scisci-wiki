# Q-analysis content-core validation

## Summary

Q-analysis content-core validation checks whether the substantive content core of a research specialty aligns with the citation-network main path.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: event-by-content matrix, subject-area code, content complex, main-path node set, or specialty network.
- Typical representation: Q-analysis equivalence classes, content-core graph, or comparison between content complexes and main-path membership.
- Validation target: whether a structural citation backbone runs through the specialty's substantive content core.
- Empirical signature: main-path nodes share the field's dominant content category or pass through the Q-analysis core.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds content validation to [main path analysis](../methods/main_path_analysis.md), which otherwise operates on citation structure.
- Helps distinguish structurally central but substantively peripheral paths from paths through the field's core problem area.
- Provides a bridge between citation-network methods and coded historical or topical content.
- Supports mixed-method science maps when expert-coded content is available.

## Operationalization

- Code nodes in a citation historiograph by research-area or content attributes.
- Build a node-by-content incidence matrix.
- Perform Q-analysis or an equivalent content-complex analysis.
- Identify the dominant content core and compare it with the main path.
- Report mismatches as potential signs of missing edges, side branches, or multiple specialty cores.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hummon and Doreian (1989) codes DNA milestone events by nucleic acid chemistry, protein chemistry, genetics, and microbiology.
- Their modified Q-analysis identifies nucleic chemistry as the core of the DNA content complex.
- The authors report that all main-path events contain nucleic chemistry as all or part of their content, providing corroborative evidence for the main path.

## Caveats

- Content codes can be coarse, historically contested, or dependent on expert judgment.
- A dominant content core may obscure interdisciplinary side paths that are important for later developments.
- Q-analysis validation is strongest for small curated networks; large corpora may require scalable approximations.
