# Preregistration adherence and deviation audit

## Summary

Preregistration adherence and deviation audit is the validation practice of checking whether a published study followed its registered plan and transparently labeled justified departures from that plan.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: preregistered plan, protocol item, deviation, manuscript result, outcome variable, analysis model, or study report.
- Typical representation: adherence checklist, deviation table, plan-versus-execution matrix, or stage-two review record.
- Validation target: whether preregistration actually preserved the inferential boundary it was meant to create.
- Empirical signature: deviations are disclosed, timed, justified, and interpreted separately from planned tests.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a quality-control layer for [preregistration and registered reports](preregistration_registered_reports.md).
- Separates symbolic adoption from substantive compliance in [open science interventions](open_science_interventions.md).
- Supports [registry-publication reporting-bias audits](../methods/registry_publication_reporting_bias_audit.md).
- Gives [registered-report in-principle acceptance](registered_report_in_principle_acceptance.md) a second-stage adherence check.
- Complements the [preregistration specificity gradient](preregistration_specificity_gradient.md), because adherence to a vague plan does not provide the same inferential constraint as adherence to a specific plan.
- Leaves a further interpretive layer for [narrative inference after preregistration](narrative_inference_after_preregistration.md).

## Operationalization

- List all preregistered hypotheses, outcomes, exclusions, transformations, covariates, and analysis models.
- Compare each item with the executed study and published report.
- Classify departures by timing: before outcome observation, after outcome observation, or unclear.
- Code whether each deviation is disclosed and justified.
- Interpret post-outcome deviations as informative but less confirmatory unless independently retested.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Nosek et al. (2018) states that the benefits of preregistration are lost if researchers do not follow their preregistrations.
- The same full text explains that deviations from plans are common and do not necessarily rule out testing predictions if they are documented before outcomes are observed.
- Nosek et al. argue that even post-outcome deviations are more interpretable when transparently reported, because observers can assess the deviation and rationale.
- Their registered-report description makes adherence to the preregistered plan a central second-stage review criterion.
- Nosek et al. also caution that complete adherence is not the end of validation: selective narrative emphasis can still shape interpretation after planned analyses are reported.

## Caveats

- Deviations can be scientifically necessary when data collection fails, assumptions are violated, or measurement problems emerge.
- Adherence audits should not punish transparent correction of invalid plans.
- A perfectly adhered-to but vague preregistration may still provide little inferential protection.
- The timing of deviations is often difficult to verify without logs or versioned records.
