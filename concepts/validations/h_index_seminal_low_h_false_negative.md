# H-index seminal low-h false negative

## Summary

A low h-index can miss scientists with a small number of exceptional contributions, making h-index stronger evidence for sustained output than for absence of major impact.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author citation portfolio or evaluation decision.
- Typical representation: h-index alongside top-paper citations, top-percentile shares, and citation concentration.
- Validation target: false negatives caused by a few high-impact papers below a broad-output threshold.
- Empirical signature: low or moderate h but extreme top-publication impact or high concentration in one or a few papers.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Turns [h-index tail insensitivity](h_index_tail_insensitivity.md) into an evaluation-specific false-negative audit.
- Supports responsible use of h-index in [portfolio-based researcher assessment](../methods/portfolio_based_researcher_assessment.md).
- Useful for comparing inventors, theorists, method developers, or small-lab researchers whose output is sparse but influential.

## Operationalization

- For each author, report h-index together with maximum citations, top-1/top-3 citation share, top-percentile publication counts, and citation Gini or concentration measures.
- Flag cases where top-paper impact exceeds a field-year percentile while h-index remains below a decision threshold.
- Audit whether committee decisions or models would change when seminal-paper indicators are included.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hirsch (2005) discusses the limitation that a few seminal papers with extraordinary citation counts can be muted by h-index.
- This supports treating low h as nondispositive evidence in author assessment.

## Caveats

- Single-paper outliers can reflect large teams, methods, data resources, or citation fashions rather than individual contribution.
- This audit complements rather than replaces contribution and authorship-role evidence.
