# Unretracted misconduct dark figure

## Summary

Unretracted misconduct dark figure is the validation caveat that formal retraction data capture only detected, investigated, and corrected misconduct, leaving some suspected or documented fraudulent work in the active literature.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: publication, suspected-misconduct case, investigation record, expression of concern, unretracted article, or retraction corpus.
- Typical representation: dark-figure caveat, unretracted suspected-fraud list, expression-of-concern follow-up audit, or retraction-underestimate statement.
- Validation target: prevent retraction counts from being interpreted as the prevalence of misconduct or fraud.
- Empirical signature: external evidence raises serious concerns about publications that remain unretracted.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Qualifies [misconduct retraction share](../measures/misconduct_retraction_share.md), [fraud retraction-rate trend](../measures/fraud_retraction_rate_trend.md), and [retractions and scientific self-correction](retractions_self_correction.md).

- Supports audits that follow expressions of concern, institutional reports, journalist investigations, and watchdog records through to formal correction status.

## Operationalization

- Track retracted articles separately from expressions of concern, institutional misconduct findings, unresolved investigations, and public allegations.
- For suspected cases, record source type, evidence strength, investigation status, and whether a publisher issued a correction, expression of concern, or retraction.
- When reporting retraction rates, state explicitly that they are lower bounds on detected misconduct and not direct prevalence estimates.
- Run sensitivity analyses that add plausible unretracted cases only as scenario bounds, not as confirmed retractions.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fang, Steen, and Casadevall (2012) states that not all articles suspected of fraud have been retracted.
- The paper gives examples where serious reservations or evidence of fabrication existed while some original articles remained in the literature.
- Fang et al. conclude that the number of articles retracted because of fraud underestimates the actual number of fraudulent articles in the literature.
- This dark-figure problem persists even after [retraction source reconciliation](../methods/retraction_source_reconciliation.md), because reconciliation improves cause coding among known retractions but does not discover every unretracted case.

## Caveats

- Suspected misconduct is not the same as proven misconduct; scenario bounds should preserve evidence categories.
- Some unretracted articles may be partly reliable, disputed, or awaiting investigation.
- Public evidence can be uneven across countries, institutions, journals, and fields.
