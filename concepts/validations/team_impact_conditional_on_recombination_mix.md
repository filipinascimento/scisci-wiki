# Team Impact Conditional on Recombination Mix

## Summary

Team impact conditional on recombination mix is the validation that larger teams retain an impact advantage even after papers are compared within similar novelty-conventionality profiles.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, team-size bin, field, year, or recombination profile.
- Typical representation: regression or stratified comparison within bins of median conventionality and tail novelty.
- Validation target: whether team impact advantage is only compositional selection into favorable recombination profiles.
- Empirical signature: larger teams have higher impact across most novelty-conventionality combinations.

## Operationalization

- Bin papers by [median conventionality](../measures/median_conventionality.md) and tail-novelty status.
- Estimate impact models separately by team size or include team-size interactions.
- Add field fixed effects, year controls, and citation-window checks.
- Interpret residual team effects separately from teams' tendency to produce favorable recombination mixes.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Uzzi et al. (2013) bins papers into 11 median-conventionality categories and runs separate regressions by category and team size with field fixed effects.
- The paper reports that larger teams show higher impact at virtually all mixes of tail novelty and median conventionality.
- This supports a residual team advantage beyond teams' greater tendency to incorporate high-impact recombination mixes.

## Caveats

- Conditional comparisons do not fully eliminate selection into teams, venues, topics, or resources.
- Citation impact may reward visibility or field scale rather than contribution quality.
- The result depends on journal-pair recombination measures and their granularity.
