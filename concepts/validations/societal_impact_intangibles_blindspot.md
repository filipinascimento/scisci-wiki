# Societal-impact intangibles blindspot

## Summary

Quantitative societal-impact metrics can miss intangible outputs such as generated knowledge, training, capacity, practice change, and institutional learning.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: impact indicator, assessment case, intangible output, or evaluation framework.
- Typical representation: indicator coverage checklist for tangible and intangible impact dimensions.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: blind spots of quantitative societal-impact indicators.
- Empirical signature: important impact dimensions are absent from policy mentions, patent citations, or other count indicators..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a validation guardrail to [societal impact case studies](../methods/societal_impact_case_studies.md).
- Supports [metrics-support qualitative assessment](../methods/metrics_support_qualitative_assessment.md).
- Useful when policy mentions are treated as comprehensive societal-impact evidence.

## Operationalization

- Audit each indicator against dimensions including knowledge, training, capacity, practices, and institutional learning.
- Record which dimensions require qualitative evidence or case narratives.
- Report missing dimensions before aggregating impact indicators.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Bornmann et al. (2016) notes that available metrics reveal only part of the larger picture and struggle with intangible impacts.
- This motivates an explicit blindspot audit.

## Caveats

- Intangibles are hard to compare and may need qualitative evidence.
- Making intangibles measurable can distort what institutions value.
