# Personal-best timing/magnitude decoupling

## Summary

The timing of a scientist's highest-impact paper and the magnitude of its impact can have different predictability profiles.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: career, paper sequence position, personal-best paper, citation magnitude, or prediction target.
- Typical representation: two-target career peak model separating timing and magnitude.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: career peak predictability decomposition.
- Empirical signature: models predict the magnitude of a career-best paper better than its sequence position, or vice versa..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects [highest impact sequence position](../measures/highest_impact_sequence_position.md) to [Q-model career prediction](../methods/q_model_career_prediction.md).
- Extends [random impact rule](../mechanisms/random_impact_rule.md) with a target-split validation.
- Useful for career evaluation and forecasting caveats.

## Operationalization

- Evaluate highest-impact sequence position and eventual citation magnitude as separate targets.
- Report whether predictors improve timing, magnitude, or only productivity exposure baselines.
- Check robustness to citation window and authorship credit choices.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Clauset et al. (2017) says personal-best timing is uncorrelated with career order, while the citation level of the personal best is partly predictable from persistent author-level visibility patterns.

## Caveats

- The motif relies on citation-defined impact and may not transfer to noncitation achievements.
- Career selection and survivorship can affect personal-best distributions.
