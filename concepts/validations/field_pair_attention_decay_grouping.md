# Field-pair attention-decay grouping

## Summary

Medicine/Biology and Chemistry/Physics form paired regimes in decay-rate and half-life trends.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, broad discipline, decay-rate slope, half-life trend, publication growth rate, or citation percentile.
- Typical representation: field-pair comparison over decay rates and half-life slopes.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: disciplinary grouping in attention-decay dynamics.
- Empirical signature: Physics and Chemistry generally decay faster than Biology and Medicine in the studied field grouping..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines field-level citation-aging validation by linking it to [field specific attention decay gradient](field_specific_attention_decay_gradient.md) and [citation decay rate alpha](../measures/citation_decay_rate_alpha.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [publication volume rescaled half life](../measures/publication_volume_rescaled_half_life.md) is interpreted from citation histories.
- Adds cross-links to [field publication growth rate delta](../measures/field_publication_growth_rate_delta.md) so delayed-recognition and ranking motifs stay connected.

## Operationalization

- Fit field-specific linear trends for alpha or half-life.
- Compare slope clusters across the four broad fields.
- Report whether field-pair patterns survive publication-volume rescaling.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Parolo et al. (2015) reports paired behavior in decay-rate slopes and half-life trends, with Physics/Chemistry generally decaying faster than Biology/Medicine.
- The motif records an empirical grouping rather than only a monotone field gradient.

## Caveats

- Only four broad, high-volume fields are tested.
- Topic-level groupings could differ from broad-field pairs.
