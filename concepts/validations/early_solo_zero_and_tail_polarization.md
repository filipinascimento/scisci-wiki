# Early solo zero-and-tail polarization

## Summary

Early solo-authored work can be overrepresented both among uncited outputs and among the highest citation bins, so mean impact alone can hide polarization.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: solo paper, team paper, citation bin, period, domain, or zero-citation flag.
- Typical representation: joint zero-citation and upper-tail comparison by team status.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: distributional validation of solo/team impact patterns.
- Empirical signature: early solo papers show higher zero-citation probability and higher extreme-tail probability in some domains.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines team-impact distribution validation by linking it to [team solo citation frequency ratio](../measures/team_solo_citation_frequency_ratio.md) and [team high citation tail dominance](../mechanisms/team_high_citation_tail_dominance.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [solo to team impact reversal](solo_to_team_impact_reversal.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [citation distribution scaling](../measures/citation_distribution_scaling.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Compare zero-citation rates and upper-tail citation probabilities by solo/team status.
- Estimate the pattern separately by domain and early/late period.
- Report uncertainty for sparse upper-tail bins and avoid mean-only interpretation.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified local full text from Wuchty et al. (2007) reports that early solo papers in science and engineering and social sciences were more likely than team papers to receive no citations and also more likely to reach the highest citation counts.
- The motif separates distributional polarization from average team advantage.

## Caveats

- Zero citations reflect database coverage and field citation culture as well as influence.
- Extreme-tail bins are sparse and sensitive to pooling choices.
