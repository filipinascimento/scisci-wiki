# Low-track-record applicant subgroup validation

## Summary

Low-track-record applicant subgroup validation tests peer-review predictive validity among applicants whose observable reputation is weaker or less established.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: applicant subgroup, grant, prior funding history, career stage, review score, or future output.
- Typical representation: score-outcome model estimated for new grants, applicants without prior NIH funding, junior investigators, or low-prior-output investigators.
- Validation target: determine whether review scores predict outcomes when applicant reputation is less informative.
- Empirical signature: review scores retain predictive power in low-track-record subgroups.

## Operationalization

- Define low-track-record groups using prior grants, prior publications, career age, first-time award status, or junior-investigator flags.
- Estimate the focal review-score or funding model separately within these groups.
- Compare with established-investigator estimates to assess whether predictive validity depends on accumulated reputation.
- Report uncertainty because low-track-record groups can have fewer observations and more heterogeneous trajectories.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Li and Agha (2015) tests NIH peer-review score prediction among new grants, investigators without prior NIH funding, and junior researchers.
- The paper uses these subgroups to evaluate whether the score-outcome relationship is merely a reflection of established reputation.
- Li and Agha report that review scores continue to predict future outputs in these lower-track-record groups.

## Caveats

- Junior or first-time applicant definitions vary across agencies and eras.
- Lower prior output can mix early-career promise, field norms, career interruptions, and institutional resources.
- Strong subgroup score prediction does not guarantee fairness or equal access to funding.
