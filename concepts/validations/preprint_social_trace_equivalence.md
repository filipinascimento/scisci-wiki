# Preprint Social-Trace Equivalence

## Summary

Preprint social-trace equivalence is the validation claim that unrefereed preprints can be as informative as peer-reviewed papers for identifying coauthorship ties.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, preprint, publication record, coauthorship edge, source database, or field panel.
- Typical representation: inclusion rule that treats preprint coauthorship as a social tie.
- Validation target: whether peer-review status matters for using a record as a collaboration trace.
- Empirical signature: a preprint may vary in quality but still documents a real coauthorship event.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Supports [coauthorship source-domain panels](../datasets/coauthorship_source_domain_panels.md) that include preprint archives.
- Refines [collaboration source coverage bias](collaboration_source_coverage_bias.md).
- Connects [new scholarly media scope caveat](new_scholarly_media_scope_caveat.md) to coauthorship-network construction.

## Operationalization

- Separate the record's quality-evaluation status from its byline evidence.
- Include preprints when the target is coauthorship or acquaintance, with provenance labels.
- Compare results with and without preprint sources if publication culture differs by field.
- Avoid using preprint inclusion as evidence of equivalent scientific quality.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Newman (2001) notes that some source databases contain unrefereed preprints.
- The paper argues that even if unrefereed preprints are lower average quality, they are every bit as good as refereed papers as indicators of social connection.
- This supports using the Los Alamos e-Print Archive and similar sources for collaboration-network edges.

## Caveats

- Preprint adoption differs across fields and periods.
- Some preprints may never become formal papers, and duplicate preprint-paper records need reconciliation.
- The equivalence applies to social-trace validity, not impact, quality, or peer-review outcomes.
