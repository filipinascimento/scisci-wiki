# Public-agency accountability constraint

## Summary

Public funders may favor project-based support because it is more defensible under political, geographic, and distributional accountability constraints.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: funding agency, governance type, congressional oversight, institution spread, project award, or investigator award.
- Typical representation: policy-design constraint table over accountability and defensibility requirements.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: external-validity boundary for translating private-foundation funding designs.
- Empirical signature: public agencies face constraints that make impersonal project funding easier to justify than elite person-level funding..

## Operationalization

- Code funder governance type, public accountability, distributional obligations, and project-versus-person award logic.
- Compare whether high-touch person-level designs survive scale, transparency, and geographic equity requirements.
- Treat political defensibility as a design constraint rather than as an inefficiency alone.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Azoulay et al. (2009) cautions that NIH faces political constraints and must spread support across institutions, making impersonal project funding easier to defend.
- The motif marks a policy external-validity boundary for HHMI-style designs.

## Caveats

- This is a policy-design caveat rather than a direct test of public-agency alternatives.
- Accountability constraints can protect fairness and legitimacy while also limiting risk-taking.
