# Outgoing Self-Citation Metric Boundary

## Summary

Outgoing self-citation metric boundary distinguishes self-citations made by an author from self-origin citations received by an author, noting that the incoming version requires stronger author disambiguation.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author, authorship, outgoing reference, incoming citation, or self-citation edge.
- Typical representation: outgoing self-citation rate, incoming self-origin share, metric boundary statement, or disambiguation requirement.
- Validation target: prevent self-citation findings based on outgoing references from being interpreted as received-impact decomposition.
- Empirical signature: a study measures the fraction of an author's outgoing references that cite their own work, but cannot measure what fraction of received citations are self-originated.

## Operationalization

- State whether the metric counts self-citations in outgoing references or incoming citations.
- For outgoing metrics, define the denominator as references, author-to-author citation pairs, authorships, or papers.
- For incoming metrics, require author identity resolution across citing and cited records.
- Avoid translating outgoing self-citation behavior into received-impact shares without separate incoming-citation evidence.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from King et al. (2017) defines their self-citation rate as the fraction of outgoing citations an author makes to their own other papers.
- The paper notes that it would be interesting to measure the fraction of incoming citations an author receives from their own papers.
- King et al. state that they cannot consider the incoming metric without author disambiguation.

## Caveats

- Outgoing self-citation rates describe citing behavior, not the composition of a scholar's received impact.
- Incoming self-origin shares require person-level identities and are sensitive to disambiguation quality.
- Team-authored papers complicate both outgoing and incoming denominators because many author pairs can be generated by one paper citation.
