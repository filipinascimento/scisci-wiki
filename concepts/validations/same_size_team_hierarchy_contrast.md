# Same-size team hierarchy contrast

## Summary

Same-size team hierarchy contrast compares teams with the same author count but different L-ratios to separate internal hierarchy from raw team size.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, team-size bin, field-year cohort, L-ratio percentile, or matched team set.
- Typical representation: within-team-size regression, stratified L-ratio curves, matched team comparison, or size-controlled outcome model.
- Validation target: show that team structure matters beyond team size.
- Empirical signature: at similar team sizes, flatter teams differ in novelty, development, citation timing, and role-specific productivity.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Tests a key assumption behind [team hierarchy and flatness](../mechanisms/team_hierarchy_flatness.md).
- Complements [team size and disruption](../mechanisms/team_size_disruption.md) by showing that large versus small is not the only relevant team-science axis.
- Supports outcome-specific measures such as [developmental index](../measures/developmental_index.md), [citation-window impact split](../measures/citation_window_impact_split.md), and [lead-role ratio](../measures/lead_role_ratio.md).

## Operationalization

- Group or control papers by team size.
- Compare L-ratio variation within each team-size stratum.
- Estimate outcome gradients for novelty, developmental index, lead/support productivity, short-term citations, and long-term citations.
- Add field, year, author, and career-age controls where data permit.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Xu, Wu, and Evans (2022) states that smaller teams tend to have higher L-ratio, but there is substantial L-ratio variance among teams of the same size.
- The paper uses this variance to disentangle the relative effects of team hierarchy from team size.
- Xu et al. report that higher L-ratio is associated with higher top-novelty probability, lower developmental-index percentile, less short-term citation impact, more long-term impact, and different productivity gradients for lead versus support authors.

## Caveats

- Same-size comparisons still may differ in field, institution, funding, career stage, and project type.
- The contrast depends on accurate role prediction for papers without contribution statements.
- Team size itself can interact with L-ratio, so stratified results should not be overgeneralized across all team sizes.
