# Proposal-submission observability gap

## Summary

Proposal-submission observability gap is the inability to distinguish applicant self-screening from reviewer selection when the full pool of submitted and unsubmitted proposals is not observed.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: latent idea, submitted proposal, reviewed proposal, funded project, or applicant status group.
- Typical representation: missing-data caveat for funding-selection studies.
- Validation target: whether observed funded outputs can identify where novelty loss enters the funding pipeline.
- Empirical signature: studies observe funded projects or publications but not the full proposal pool needed to separate mechanisms.

## Operationalization

- Map which funding-pipeline stages are observed: ideas, pre-proposals, submissions, reviews, awards, outputs.
- State whether rejected proposals or unsubmitted ideas are missing.
- Use pipeline-complete data when identifying selection mechanisms.
- Treat funded-output-only analyses as unable to locate the exact stage of novelty loss.
- Propose pre-registration, intent-to-submit surveys, or administrative proposal data as remedies.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Lee, and Walsh (2018) notes that their data do not observe the full proposal pool.
- The paper therefore cannot fully distinguish whether low-status researchers submit safer proposals or reviewers select safer proposals from their submissions.
- This limitation matters because both mechanisms imply different policy interventions.
- The motif is a general design warning for funding-policy studies using only funded outputs.

## Caveats

- Observing submitted proposals still does not reveal unsubmitted ideas.
- Proposal records may be confidential or inaccessible.
- Output novelty can differ from proposal novelty because projects change after funding.
