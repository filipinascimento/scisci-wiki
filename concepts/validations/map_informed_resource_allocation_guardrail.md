# Map-informed resource allocation guardrail

## Summary

Map-informed resource allocation guardrail is the requirement that science maps used in funding or portfolio decisions be validated and paired with deliberative review rather than treated as stand-alone allocation engines.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: science map, funding decision, portfolio review, laboratory unit, discipline, or review process.
- Typical representation: validation checklist, map-quality score, decision protocol, or human-review requirement.
- Validation target: prevent map geometry and clusters from being mistaken for objective policy evidence.
- Empirical signature: map-based recommendations are evaluated against accuracy, peer review, and decision accountability before changing resources.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a policy-facing guardrail to [science map accuracy validation](science_map_accuracy_validation.md).
- Connects map building to [peer review gatekeeping](../mechanisms/peer_review_gatekeeping.md) and [assessment decision-responsibility retention](../methods/assessment_decision_responsibility_retention.md).
- Helps distinguish exploratory portfolio maps from operational resource-allocation tools.
- Supports responsible use of [science maps](../representations/science_maps.md) in laboratories, universities, funders, and science-policy agencies.

## Operationalization

- State whether a map is being used for navigation, communication, monitoring, or direct allocation.
- Require evidence on local accuracy, structural accuracy, coverage, and usability before policy deployment.
- Pair map outputs with peer review, expert interpretation, and documented decision authority.
- Record how the map changed or did not change the allocation recommendation.
- Treat validation failures as grounds for nonuse or limited exploratory use.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Boyack, Klavans, and Borner (2005) warns that inferior science maps can misallocate funding when used in S&T decision contexts.
- The same source explicitly recommends using science maps in concert with established processes such as peer review rather than as the sole basis for funding decisions.
- Their disciplinary map is framed as decision support for resource allocation among disciplines, which makes validation a governance requirement rather than only a technical nicety.

## Caveats

- A validated map can still encode source-coverage, field-size, and layout biases.
- The guardrail reduces decision risk but does not remove politics or value choices from allocation.
- Peer review can introduce its own conservatism and status biases, so the combination should be audited.
