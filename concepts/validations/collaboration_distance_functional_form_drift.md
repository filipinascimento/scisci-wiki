# Collaboration distance functional-form drift

## Summary

Collaboration distance functional-form drift is the validation motif that the mathematical form of distance decay in collaboration can vary across periods, corpora, and communication regimes.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: collaboration edge, distance bin, field-period corpus, decay function, or model comparison.
- Typical representation: power-law decay, exponential decay, lognormal fit, cutoff model, or temporal form shift.
- Validation target: avoid assuming one universal distance-decay function for collaboration networks.
- Empirical signature: one corpus supports a power-law distance curve while another earlier or narrower corpus supports an exponential curve.

## Operationalization

- Fit power-law, exponential, lognormal, spline, and cutoff models to the same distance-flow data.
- Compare forms across time windows, fields, and database coverage.
- Report goodness-of-fit, residuals, and tail behavior.
- Test whether communication technology or field composition explains functional-form changes.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Pan et al. (2012) reports a power-law collaboration distance curve and contrasts it with earlier exponential findings.
- The paper suggests that differences may relate to corpus scale or changes in communication technology.

## Caveats

- Functional-form differences can result from binning, truncation, geocoding, or self-link handling.
- A good statistical fit does not identify the underlying mechanism.
- Distance decay can vary by collaboration type.
