# Universal citation-distribution mechanism gap

## Summary

Universal citation-distribution mechanism gap is the caveat that empirical collapse of field-normalized citation distributions does not by itself explain the generative mechanisms producing that collapse.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: citation distribution, field-normalized citation score, generative model, paper fitness, or cumulative-advantage process.
- Typical representation: empirical scaling collapse paired with an unresolved mechanism layer.
- Validation target: distinguish descriptive universality from causal explanation.
- Empirical signature: distributions align after normalization, while the paper notes that the mechanism behind the form remains open.

## Operationalization

- Demonstrate citation-distribution collapse across fields or years.
- Specify which generative mechanisms could produce the observed form.
- Test whether candidate mechanisms reproduce both the collapsed curve and deviations from it.
- Treat empirical universality as a phenomenon requiring further explanation.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Radicchi et al. (2008) reports a universal-looking normalized citation distribution.
- The authors state that the mechanism behind this regularity remains to be explained.
- This separates the evidence for normalization from the broader theory of why citation distributions have that form.

## Caveats

- Later generative models may close part of the mechanism gap for specific corpora.
- Empirical collapse can be sensitive to field boundaries, uncited papers, and document filtering.
- Mechanism gaps do not invalidate normalization, but they limit causal interpretation.
