# Publication-conditioned SciSci blind spot

## Summary

Publication-conditioned SciSci blind spot is the validity problem that many science-of-science datasets observe only projects, ideas, and strategies successful enough to become publications, omitting failed, abandoned, rejected, or unpublished attempts.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: attempted project, idea, hypothesis, experiment, proposal, submission, preprint, publication, or failed result.
- Typical representation: publication-conditioned corpus caveat, missing-attempt denominator, failure registry, proposal-publication linkage, or hidden pipeline note.
- Validation target: distinguish properties of published science from properties of scientific work as attempted.
- Empirical signature: conclusions about risk, novelty, strategy, reproducibility, or discovery change when failed or unpublished attempts are observed.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Generalizes [unpublished-failure censoring](unpublished_failure_censoring.md) from project-strategy studies to the broader science-of-science framework.
- Connects [publication bias and selective reporting](../mechanisms/publication_bias_selective_reporting.md), the file drawer problem, and [failed-experiment publication value](../mechanisms/failed_experiment_publication_value.md).
- Provides a data-design target for registries, proposal corpora, preprints, lab notebooks, failed-experiment repositories, and funding-application linkages.
- Helps interpret models of novelty, risk, productivity, and discovery that use publications as the primary record of science.
- Covers venue-transition variants such as [accepted-publication transition denominator limit](accepted_publication_transition_denominator_limit.md), where accepted publication histories omit submissions and rejected manuscripts.
- Includes trend-specific assumptions such as [published-effort stationarity assumption](published_effort_stationarity_assumption.md) when publication-only corpora are used to infer changing scientific choices.

## Operationalization

- State whether the dataset captures attempts, proposals, submissions, protocols, preprints, registered reports, failed experiments, or only published papers.
- For publication-only corpora, label findings as conditional on publication and avoid estimating attempt-level success rates without assumptions.
- Where possible, link publications to grant applications, trial registrations, preregistrations, preprints, rejected manuscripts, or failed-experiment records.
- Report plausible missing-attempt mechanisms: negative results, nonpublishable failures, resource attrition, desk rejection, non-submission, and selective reporting.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) states that most science-of-science research focuses on publications as primary data sources.
- The review warns that this means insights are limited to ideas successful enough to merit publication.
- Fortunato et al. argue that most scientific attempts fail and that knowing when, why, and how ideas fail is essential for understanding and improving science.
- The same passage connects this blind spot to the reproducibility crisis, file drawer problem, and the broader pipeline of creative activity.

## Caveats

- Failure data can be sensitive, incomplete, and difficult to compare across fields.
- Not every failed attempt is scientifically informative; some fail for logistical, ethical, or resource reasons.
- Publication-conditioned studies remain useful when their conditioning is explicit and the target question is published literature.
