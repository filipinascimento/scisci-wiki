# Diversity-as-option rhetoric risk

## Summary

Diversity-as-option rhetoric risk is the validation warning that diversity is a property of a whole system, so policy claims can be misleading when they label a favored individual option as "diverse."

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: policy claim, portfolio option, technology pathway, research program, funding proposal, or evaluation narrative.
- Typical representation: rhetoric audit, system-property check, option-versus-portfolio distinction, or diversity-claim validation.
- Validation target: determine whether a diversity claim describes the system mix or merely rebrands a specific option.
- Empirical signature: a proposal invokes diversity benefits while concentrating resources in one option or failing to define the portfolio baseline.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Protects [diversity measures](../measures/diversity_measures.md) from being used as a slogan detached from portfolio structure.
- Extends [diversity proxy-substitution risk](diversity_proxy_substitution_risk.md) from metric design to policy rhetoric and evaluation narratives.
- Qualifies [diversity-premium trade-off](../mechanisms/diversity_premium_tradeoff.md) because the premium applies to a system mix, not automatically to each option.
- Supports [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) by requiring claims about diversity to specify the denominator, category system, and counterfactual portfolio.

## Operationalization

- Identify the portfolio or system over which diversity is claimed.
- Separate option-level attributes from system-level distributional properties.
- Check whether the favored option increases variety, balance, disparity, or a justified composite relative to the current portfolio.
- Ask whether the diversity claim names a mechanism such as resilience, learning, innovation, or pluralism.
- Treat unsupported diversity language as advocacy rather than evidence.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Stirling (2007) states that appeals to diversity can be little more than rhetoric.
- The paper emphasizes that diversity is an irreducible property of a system rather than a quality of an individual element or option.
- Stirling warns that diversity is often invoked in policy debates as if it were a property of a particular option that may actually be favored for other reasons.
- The full text therefore motivates a validation check that distinguishes system diversity from option branding.

## Caveats

- A single option can increase system diversity when it adds a genuinely new and underrepresented category.
- Rhetorical misuse does not imply that the policy goal is wrong; it means the diversity mechanism has not been demonstrated.
- Some governance goals involve representation or equity beyond formal portfolio diversity and should be measured directly.
