# Career-prediction nonstationarity caution

## Summary

Career-prediction nonstationarity caution is the validation motif that models of scientific career growth should be stress-tested for nonlinear, time-varying social mechanisms before evaluative use.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: career, prediction model, cohort, time split, career stage, or evaluation decision.
- Typical representation: nonstationary career growth, nonlinear dynamics, cohort drift, stage-specific error, or predictive uncertainty.
- Validation target: prevent career-prediction models from being used as stable evaluation tools without temporal robustness checks.
- Empirical signature: model parameters or error profiles change across cohorts, fields, stages, or policy environments.

## Uses in Science of Science

## Operationalization

- Validate models on future cohorts rather than only random holdouts.
- Report calibration and error by career stage, field, seniority, and cohort.
- Include uncertainty intervals and sensitivity to changing citation supply.
- Separate descriptive career dynamics from prescriptive evaluation tools.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Petersen et al. (2014) warns that social complexity, nonlinearities, and nonstationarities can lead to misuse of career metrics.
- The paper's reputation model is descriptive but explicitly cautions against simplistic prediction and evaluation.
- This motif captures that warning as a reusable validation requirement.

## Caveats

- Petersen et al. (2014) states the caution but does not provide a full prediction-audit protocol.
- Some short-horizon predictions may be stable for narrow tasks.
- Nonstationarity can come from measurement changes as well as social mechanisms.
