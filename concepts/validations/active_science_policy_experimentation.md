# Active science-policy experimentation

## Summary

Active science-policy experimentation tests science policies through deliberate randomized, phased, or quasi-experimental interventions rather than relying only on observational bibliographic patterns.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: policy intervention, funder, journal, institution, field, scientist, proposal, or evaluation workflow.
- Typical representation: randomized trial, A/B test, phased rollout, natural experiment, regression discontinuity, or matched policy comparison.
- Mechanism or measurement target: whether changing the scientific environment causes changes in discovery, equity, novelty, reliability, or portfolio diversity.
- Empirical signature: policy-exposed units are compared to credible counterfactuals with pre-specified outcomes and unintended-consequence audits.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides an experimental complement to [SciSci quasi-experimental policy evaluation](scisci_quasi_experimental_policy_evaluation.md).
- Tests whether [predictable-discovery selection pressure](../mechanisms/predictable_discovery_selection_pressure.md), [prediction feedback loops](prediction_feedback_loops.md), or indicator systemic effects occur after policy changes.
- Supports funder, publisher, and university experiments on grant review, peer review, recommender systems, metrics, open science, team assembly, and portfolio design.
- Helps distinguish descriptive predictability from actionable policy knowledge.
- Provides the intervention-testing layer for [funding-allocation design alternatives](../methods/funding_allocation_design_alternatives.md).

## Operationalization

- Define the policy lever, randomization or rollout unit, comparison group, outcomes, and guardrail metrics before implementation.
- Include outcomes for productivity, novelty, disruption, replication, equity, field diversity, career retention, and delayed effects.
- Monitor adaptation, gaming, and spillovers because scientists respond strategically to incentives.
- Pair experiments with qualitative feedback and data-governance review when the intervention affects careers or funding.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Clauset, Larremore, and Sinatra (2017) argues that science itself can be probed using the scientific method.
- The essay states that ecological research relies on observational study plus active experimentation, while most science-of-science work remains observational.
- Clauset et al. call for care, boldness, and bravery from funding agencies, publishers, and administrators that define the adaptive landscape.
- This provides a validation motif for moving from observed patterns and predictions to tested science-policy interventions.

## Caveats

- Experiments affecting funding, hiring, publication, or career outcomes raise ethical and political constraints.
- Local policy experiments may not generalize across countries, fields, or institution types.
- Short experiments can miss delayed recognition, long-run career effects, and ecosystem adaptation.
