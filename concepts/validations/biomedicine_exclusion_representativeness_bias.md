# Biomedicine exclusion representativeness bias

## Summary

Biomedicine exclusion representativeness bias is the risk that collaboration studies omitting biomedicine underrepresent science because biomedical research is a large share of civilian scientific output.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: collaboration dataset, field panel, source bibliography, or science-wide inference.
- Typical validation: compare included and excluded field domains before generalizing collaboration patterns.
- Validation target: field representativeness of collaboration-network samples.
- Empirical signature: conclusions from physics, mathematics, or narrower sources may not transfer to biomedicine-heavy science as a whole.

## Operationalization

- Report which major fields are included and excluded.
- Compare field sizes and collaboration norms before making science-wide claims.
- Treat biomedical omission as a source-scope threat when the target population is all science.
- Add a biomedical source or state the narrower inference domain.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Newman (2004) states that excluding biomedicine would omit an important part of civilian science.
- The paper includes MEDLINE specifically to broaden collaboration-network evidence beyond earlier non-biomedical panels.
- This motivates treating field exclusion as a representativeness issue, not only a data convenience.

## Caveats

- Biomedicine is not automatically representative of all science either.
- Field inclusion can improve representativeness while introducing source-specific coverage differences.
- The appropriate field scope depends on the research question.
