# Group-status measure generalizability boundary

## Summary

Status effects measured through group membership need validation against individual eminence, performance, and reputation measures.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: researcher, rank, gender, core-university indicator, individual performance, reputation, or status proxy.
- Typical representation: external-validity audit for group-level status proxies.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: generalizability of status-contingent funding effects.
- Empirical signature: group categories can reveal status patterns while leaving individual-level status mechanisms undermeasured..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines status-measure validation by linking it to [respondent author status proxy](../methods/respondent_author_status_proxy.md) and [former imperial university core status proxy](../measures/former_imperial_university_core_status_proxy.md).

## Operationalization

- Document whether status is measured by rank, gender, institution, individual performance, citation standing, or reputation.
- Replicate status interactions with individual status and past-performance measures when possible.
- Separate group-identity status from individual eminence and resource access.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang et al. (2018) notes that status is measured by group categories and calls for future work with individual high- and low-status measures.
- The motif marks an external-validity boundary for status-contingent results.

## Caveats

- Group proxies can mix status, field composition, career stage, and institutional resources.
- Individual status measures may introduce their own citation and recognition biases.
