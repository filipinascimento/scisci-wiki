# Evaluated-party data verification

## Summary

Evaluated-party data verification lets the people or organizations being assessed inspect the records, matches, outputs, and calculations used in their evaluation.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: researcher, institution, paper list, publication match, author disambiguation result, citation count, or evaluation record.
- Typical representation: self-verification workflow, audit portal, correction window, third-party data audit, or contested-record log.
- Validation target: detect data errors before indicators affect decisions.
- Empirical signature: evaluated units can review and correct publication lists, author matches, affiliation links, citation records, and indicator inputs.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects [author name disambiguation](../methods/author_name_disambiguation.md), [paper-author-affiliation linkages](../representations/paper_author_affiliation_linkages.md), and [citation database coverage sensitivity](citation_database_coverage_sensitivity.md) to evaluation governance.
- Gives [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) a concrete procedural safeguard.
- Helps distinguish measurement error from substantive performance differences.
- Supports institutional research information systems and third-party audits.
- Complements [evaluation-contestation access gap](evaluation_contestation_access_gap.md), where the problem is access to methodological evidence as well as access to raw records.

## Operationalization

- Provide evaluated researchers or institutions with the publication set and calculated indicators before final decisions.
- Log corrections to missing outputs, duplicate records, wrong-author matches, affiliation errors, document types, and source coverage.
- Budget time and money for data-quality checks rather than treating them as optional cleanup.
- Keep correction provenance so downstream analyses can separate original provider data from verified evaluation data.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hicks et al. (2015) says those evaluated should be allowed to verify data and analysis.
- Hicks et al. argue that researchers included in bibliometric studies should be able to check whether their outputs have been correctly identified.
- The article recommends self-verification or third-party audit and says universities can implement these checks in research information systems.
- Hicks et al. explicitly note that accurate high-quality data take time and money to collate and process.

## Caveats

- Self-verification can introduce strategic corrections unless audit rules are clear.
- Verification access may be unequal if some researchers have more administrative support.
- Record correction is not enough when the chosen indicator is invalid for the evaluation question.
