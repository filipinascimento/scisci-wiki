# Societal-Impact Comprehensiveness-Feasibility Tradeoff

## Summary

Societal-impact comprehensiveness-feasibility tradeoff is the validation tension between measuring the many dimensions of real-world impact and keeping indicators feasible, comparable, and auditable.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: societal-impact model, indicator set, evaluation system, case study, or institution.
- Typical representation: tradeoff between multidimensional completeness and operational simplicity.
- Validation target: whether an impact measure is both meaningful enough and practical enough for assessment.
- Empirical signature: comprehensive models become infeasible, while simple counts miss time lags, attribution, transferability, and negative effects.

## Operationalization

- List which impact dimensions an indicator captures and which it omits.
- Assess feasibility in data collection, comparability, cost, and auditability.
- Explicitly document time-lag, attribution, transferability, and positive/negative effect limitations.
- Pair quantitative traces with qualitative evidence when necessary.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Bornmann et al. (2016) states that research impacts are complex, nonlinear, and unpredictable.
- The paper warns that measurement often counts what is easy rather than what matters.
- Bornmann et al. note that multidimensional models may be comprehensive but lack a workable balance between comprehensiveness and feasibility.

## Caveats

- Feasible indicators can become harmful if they dominate evaluation.
- Comprehensive case studies can become non-comparable or selectively narrated.
- The right tradeoff depends on target actor, decision context, and evidence burden.
