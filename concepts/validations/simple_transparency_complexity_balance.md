# Simple-transparency complexity balance

## Summary

Responsible indicators should be simple enough to inspect but not so simplistic that they distort the research process they represent.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: indicator, formula, scorecard, ranking, evaluation report, or decision rule.
- Typical representation: auditability-versus-fidelity tradeoff for indicator construction.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: balance between transparent simplicity and construct-faithful complexity.
- Empirical signature: a simple indicator is reproducible but loses important dimensions, or a complex indicator is faithful but hard to audit..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [transparent metric construction](../methods/transparent_metric_construction.md).
- Provides a counterweight to [indicator false precision](indicator_false_precision.md).
- Useful when choosing field-specific indicator suites.

## Operationalization

- Test whether users can reproduce the indicator.
- Document simplifications and lost constructs.
- Run sensitivity checks showing whether simpler versions distort rankings or decisions.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hicks et al. (2015) praises simplicity for transparency but warns that simplistic indicators can distort the record and should remain faithful to research complexity.

## Caveats

- The right complexity level depends on decision stakes, field heterogeneity, and data quality.
- Complexity can obscure normative choices if documentation is weak.
