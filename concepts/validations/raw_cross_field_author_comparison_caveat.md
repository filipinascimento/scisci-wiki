# Raw cross-field author comparison caveat

## Summary

Raw cross-field author comparison caveat warns that author citation metrics should not be compared directly across distant fields because citation densities, coverage, and evaluation frames differ.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author metric, field, subfield, citation density, evaluation case, or ranking table.
- Typical representation: warning label, field-relative percentile requirement, or cross-field comparison exclusion.
- Validation target: prevent author-level raw citations, h-index values, or composite scores from being interpreted as universal quality scores.
- Empirical signature: evaluation workflows compare authors within field/subfield reference sets and report field-density context.

## Operationalization

- Identify the fields and subfields represented by each author profile.
- Compare raw citation indicators only within documented reference sets or translate them into field-relative percentiles.
- Report database, citation-window, self-citation, and classification caveats before making comparisons.
- Avoid using one global author rank for hiring, promotion, tenure, prizes, or funding decisions.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ioannidis et al. (2019) says comparisons between scientific fields with different citation densities are tenuous.
- The paper provides field and subfield percentiles so any scientist with at least five Scopus papers can be ranked against standards in their own field or subfield.
- Ioannidis et al. state that citation metrics should be used in a systematic, context-specific, and field-adjusted way and explicitly discourage raw comparisons across very different fields.

## Caveats

- Field-relative ranks can still hide sub-subfield differences and interdisciplinary work.
- Field labels inherit errors from the classification and source database.
- This caveat addresses comparability, not whether citation metrics measure quality or societal value.
