# Placement network manipulation resistance

## Summary

Placement network manipulation resistance is the argument that decentralized, public, and costly hiring outcomes are harder to game than self-reported or arbitrarily weighted ranking inputs.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: ranking input, faculty placement, department, institution, or field.
- Typical representation: comparison between placement-derived hierarchy and conventional input or reputation ranking systems.
- Validation target: robustness of placement-derived prestige signals to deliberate manipulation.
- Empirical signature: rank information is inferred from many independent hiring decisions rather than a small set of directly optimized reporting fields.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a metric-governance caveat for [faculty prestige-rank inference](../methods/faculty_prestige_rank_inference.md).
- Links faculty placement metrics to [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) and ranking-system misuse.
- Complements [faculty placement predictive validity](faculty_placement_predictive_validity.md) by asking whether the input signal is gameable.
- Helps distinguish observed career-flow evidence from institutional self-presentation.

## Operationalization

- Identify whether a ranking input is self-reported, survey-based, resource-based, citation-based, or inferred from public outcomes.
- Assess whether institutions can directly optimize the input without improving the construct of interest.
- For faculty hiring, compare costly placement outcomes with conventional rankings based on selectivity, resources, reputation, or publication measures.
- Track whether public awareness of the metric changes hiring, reporting, or program-building behavior over time.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Clauset et al. (2015) contrasts faculty-placement rankings with ranking systems based on reputational surveys, wealth, selectivity, and other institutional inputs.
- The paper argues that faculty placement data are public, consequential, and decentralized, making them less directly manipulable than many ranking ingredients.
- The validation is conceptual rather than complete: hiring markets can still be influenced by strategic behavior and status feedback.

## Caveats

- Manipulation resistance is not manipulation immunity.
- If placement rankings become high-stakes, departments may alter hiring, placement support, or reporting behavior.
- Outcome-based rankings can still encode historical inequality, discrimination, and cumulative advantage.
