# Principal-investigator stage parity expectation

## Summary

Principal-investigator stage parity expectation is the hypothesis motif that once scientists reach independent PI eligibility, earlier pipeline attrition does not justify expecting lower award rates among comparable applicants.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: principal investigator, applicant, faculty-stage cohort, award probability, or pipeline-stage comparison.
- Typical representation: PI-stage conditioning, faculty parity benchmark, survivor-stage comparison, or independent-applicant parity expectation.
- Hypothesis target: separate earlier representation attrition from review-stage award disparities.
- Empirical signature: groups with comparable faculty-stage persistence still show different award probabilities at the PI application stage.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [gender and race stratification](../mechanisms/gender_race_stratification.md) into grant-review stage expectations.
- Connects [workforce output parity divergence](../validations/workforce_output_parity_divergence.md) to [NIH R01 award disparities](../validations/nih_r01_award_disparities.md).
- Supports designs that condition on applicant eligibility rather than broad population denominators alone.

## Operationalization

- Define the PI-eligible or independent-investigator population.
- Compare award probabilities conditional on application and career stage.
- Use workforce or tenure parity data as context, not as a substitute for award models.
- Report earlier pipeline attrition separately from review-stage conversion.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ginther et al. (2011) notes parity in tenure among Black and white faculty at doctoral and Research I institutions, then contrasts this with persistent R01 award gaps.
- The paper uses this contrast to argue that application-stage disparities require review-stage and career-resource explanations.

## Caveats

- Conditioning on PI stage can hide earlier structural attrition.
- Faculty parity evidence may not cover all fields, institutions, or appointment types.
- Applicant quality and resources still require careful measurement.
