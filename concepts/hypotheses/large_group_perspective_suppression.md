# Large-group perspective suppression

## Summary

Larger groups may suppress divergent perspectives through fewer generated ideas, weaker recall, rejection of outside views, and viewpoint neutralization.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: team cognition, collaboration process, or paper team.
- Typical representation: team size linked to idea diversity, text diversity, reference diversity, or external-field uptake.
- Hypothesized mechanism: large-group process reduces divergent perspectives and favors development of established lines.
- Empirical signature: team-size disruption gradients are mediated by reduced cognitive or textual diversity.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a cognitive channel to [team-size breadth-depth search tradeoff](../mechanisms/team_size_breadth_depth_search_tradeoff.md).
- Connects disruption to [team hierarchy flatness](../mechanisms/team_hierarchy_flatness.md) and coordination limits.
- Useful for designing surveys or lab studies that observe team process directly.

## Operationalization

- Link team size to text diversity, reference diversity, contribution-role spread, meeting/survey traces, or external-field uptake.
- Test mediation between team size and disruption while controlling field, year, and citation opportunity.
- Compare bibliometric proxies with direct team-process evidence when available.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wu et al. (2019) summarizes prior group research on fewer ideas, less learned-information recall, outside-perspective rejection, and viewpoint neutralization in larger groups.
- Wu et al. use that literature as a possible explanation for large teams developing existing directions.

## Caveats

- The paper imports this evidence from prior group studies and does not observe internal cognition.
- Large groups can also deliberately structure subteams to preserve viewpoint diversity.
