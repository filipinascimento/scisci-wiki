# Hook-tail two-mode transfer

## Summary

Hook-tail two-mode transfer is the hypothesis motif that skewed bibliometric distributions may require separate mechanisms for the body and tail, analogous to the hook-plus-tail structure in team-size distributions.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: bibliometric distribution, distribution body, distribution tail, mixture model, or generative mechanism.
- Typical representation: hook-plus-tail, two-mode mixture, body-tail mechanism split, or one-law rejection.
- Hypothesis target: test whether distributions such as team size or citations arise from multiple generative processes.
- Empirical signature: a single global law fits poorly because the low-value body and high-value tail have different mechanisms.

## Operationalization

- Fit body-tail mixture models to skewed bibliometric distributions.
- Compare single-law fits with two-process models using held-out likelihood and diagnostics.
- Interpret body and tail components as separate candidate mechanisms.
- Test whether component boundaries are stable across fields and periods.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Milojevic (2014) compares the two-mode team-size structure with citation distributions that may reflect direct and indirect citation modes.
- The comparison is presented as an analogy and future direction rather than a validated result for citations.

## Caveats

- Mixture components can be statistical conveniences without distinct mechanisms.
- Component boundaries may be sensitive to binning and sample size.
- Transfer from team size to citations requires separate validation.
