# Team-scale risk-tolerance asymmetry

## Summary

Team scale may change project risk tolerance: small teams have more upside and less institutional downside, while large teams favor safer opportunities with visible audiences.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: team, project, field-year, or funding context.
- Typical representation: team size by novelty, disruption, citation variance, and failure proxy.
- Hypothesized mechanism: small teams can pursue untested high-upside work while large teams avoid visible failure.
- Empirical signature: small teams show higher disruption, delayed upside, and larger low-impact tails.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a behavioral mechanism to [team size and disruption](../mechanisms/team_size_disruption.md).
- Connects team scale to [research strategy risk reward](../mechanisms/research_strategy_risk_reward.md) and exploration incentives.
- Useful for policy discussions about maintaining a portfolio of team sizes.

## Operationalization

- Compare team size against novelty/disruption, citation variance, low-citation tails, prize enrichment, and available failure traces by field/year.
- Separate published outcomes from available traces of unpublished or abandoned projects.
- Test whether funding and institutional stakes moderate the team-size gradient.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wu et al. (2019) contrasts large-team sure bets with small teams pursuing untested high-growth and high-failure opportunities.
- The paper frames this as a plausible explanation for the observed team-size disruption pattern.

## Caveats

- The risk-tolerance mechanism is not directly observed in Wu et al.'s data.
- Unpublished failures remain mostly invisible in bibliometric sources.
