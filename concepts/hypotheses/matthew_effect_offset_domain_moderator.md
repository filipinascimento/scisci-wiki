# Matthew-effect offset domain moderator

## Summary

Matthew-effect offset domain moderator is the hypothesis that failure advantages are more visible in domains where success-driven cumulative advantage is weaker.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientific domain, career system, setback, cumulative-advantage strength, or funding regime.
- Typical representation: domain-level moderator hypothesis.
- Hypothesis target: when setback resilience can overcome the Matthew effect.
- Empirical signature: near-miss benefits are larger where early success yields weaker resource, recognition, or attention compounding.

## Operationalization

- Estimate cumulative-advantage strength across domains or funding systems.
- Compare setback effects where early success creates different levels of future advantage.
- Test interactions between setback status and domain-level success compounding.
- Avoid assuming one domain's setback response transfers universally.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Jones, and Wang (2019) discusses how Matthew-effect dynamics may shape whether failure advantages appear.
- The paper suggests broader tests across settings where cumulative advantage from success may differ.
- This remains a moderator hypothesis rather than a direct cross-domain estimate in the paper.

## Caveats

- Measuring domain-level Matthew effects is nontrivial.
- Domains differ in labor markets, funding dependence, publication cultures, and team structures.
- Failure advantages may be masked by attrition even where survivor impact rises.
