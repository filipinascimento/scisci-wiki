# Unobservable effort-grit residual

## Summary

Unobservable effort-grit residual is the unresolved mechanism class that attributes post-setback improvement to difficult-to-measure changes in effort, signaling, grit, or perseverance.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, setback, latent trait, effort response, signal, or residual mechanism.
- Typical representation: unobserved-channel caveat in a mechanism battery.
- Hypothesis target: whether psychological or behavioral responses explain performance gains after failure.
- Empirical signature: observable mechanisms fail to account for the effect, leaving effort or perseverance channels plausible.

## Operationalization

- Exhaust observable channels such as collaboration, topic shifts, funding substitution, and institutional moves.
- Collect surveys, application histories, effort proxies, or lab activity traces where possible.
- Test whether self-reported or behavioral effort changes predict post-setback outcomes.
- Preserve residual uncertainty when these data are unavailable.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Jones, and Wang (2019) discusses effort, signaling, grit, and perseverance as possible unobservable channels.
- The paper's observable mechanism checks do not fully explain the post-setback performance gap.
- This residual class names the remaining mechanism space without claiming it is directly proven.

## Caveats

- Effort and grit are vulnerable to post-hoc storytelling.
- Latent-trait explanations can obscure institutional constraints.
- Direct measurement may require sensitive survey or behavioral data.
