# Nonresident researcher exodus risk

## Summary

Political exits and mobility restrictions may increase attrition risk for nonresident researchers at affected institutions.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: nonresident researcher, institution, country, or policy exposure.
- Typical representation: pre/post retention, affiliation exits, or migration hazards for exposed groups.
- Hypothesized mechanism: uncertainty about rights, funding access, and career prospects pushes mobile researchers away.
- Empirical signature: exposed nonresident scholars exit affected institutions at higher rates than comparable resident or unexposed scholars.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects [scientific mobility](../mechanisms/scientific_mobility.md) to policy-induced attrition risks.
- Useful for studying Brexit-like exits, visa restrictions, or funding-program exclusions.
- Pairs naturally with [regional mobility net balance](../measures/regional_mobility_net_balance.md) and capacity-contingency analyses.

## Operationalization

- Identify nonresident or foreign-origin researchers in affected institutions before the policy event.
- Estimate retention, affiliation exits, destination shifts, and output changes against resident or unexposed comparison groups.
- Model policy announcement and implementation windows separately.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sugimoto et al. (2017) describes British institutions facing a potential exodus of nonresident EU researchers after the UK decision to leave the European Union.
- The article frames this as a risk, so the motif belongs as a hypothesis unless later outcome data are linked.

## Caveats

- Prospective exodus language is not an observed outcome in the paper.
- Researchers can change affiliation records, funding ties, or citizenship status without leaving a country.
