# Narrow-win idea-commitment test

## Summary

Narrow-win idea-commitment test is the hypothesis that funded narrow winners may remain committed to initially proposed ideas, limiting later exploration.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: funded applicant, proposal idea, post-award paper, research topic, or career window.
- Typical representation: hypothesis comparing early and later post-award output topics.
- Hypothesis target: whether initial funding locks winners into proposal trajectories.
- Empirical signature: funded winners show less topic change or exploration after the award than comparable near misses.

## Operationalization

- Compare proposal text or funded aims with post-award publications.
- Track topic shifts for narrow winners and near misses.
- Separate early post-award work from later work.
- Test whether commitment predicts lower hit-paper probability or lower novelty.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Jones, and Wang (2019) raises the possibility that funded winners stay committed to initially proposed ideas.
- The paper discusses early and later post-award outputs as a way to probe this mechanism.
- It treats the mechanism as suggestive rather than resolved.

## Caveats

- Proposal texts and project aims may not be available for all grants.
- Commitment can be productive rather than harmful.
- Near misses may also continue the same ideas without the grant.
