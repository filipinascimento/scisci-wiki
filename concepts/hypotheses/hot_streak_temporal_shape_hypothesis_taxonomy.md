# Hot-streak temporal-shape hypothesis taxonomy

## Summary

Hot-streak temporal-shape hypothesis taxonomy is the hypothesis motif that alternative performance-shape functions encode competing explanations for how high-impact career episodes arise.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: career sequence, performance state, temporal shape, hot-streak model, or mechanism class.
- Typical representation: trapezoid, right-skewed burst, left-skewed burst, inverted-U, tent function, or fixed-duration opportunity.
- Hypothesis target: translate verbal mechanisms such as breakthrough, repeated collaboration, gradual peak, and dissipating advantage into testable temporal shapes.
- Empirical signature: a candidate shape fits the sequence of high-impact works better than shuffled or constant-performance alternatives.

## Operationalization

- Define candidate temporal functions before model comparison.
- Fit each shape to career-level impact sequences using common likelihood or information criteria.
- Compare shapes against randomized career order and constant-performance nulls.
- Link each shape to a substantive mechanism hypothesis.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Liu et al. (2018) describes alternative right, isosceles, and left trapezoids, inverted-U, and tent-like functions as candidate hot-streak origins.
- The paper ultimately finds that simpler hot-streak performance-state models account for observed temporal regularities better than those alternatives.
- This motif preserves the taxonomy as a reusable hypothesis set.

## Caveats

- Liu et al. (2018) rejects these alternatives as sufficient in its setting.
- Shape fit does not identify causal origins without external mechanism measures.
- Multiple mechanisms can produce similar temporal patterns.
