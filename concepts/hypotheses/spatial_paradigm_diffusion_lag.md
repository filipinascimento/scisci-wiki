# Spatial paradigm diffusion lag

## Summary

Spatial paradigm diffusion lag is the hypothesis motif that geocoded citation flows can measure how quickly results, ideas, or paradigms are recognized across geographic space.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: focal discovery, citing city, citing country, first-citation lag, cumulative citation lag, or paradigm diffusion path.
- Typical representation: first-citation distance lag, spatial recognition delay, city-country citation wave, or geographic diffusion curve.
- Hypothesis target: use spatial citation networks to study idea diffusion timing.
- Empirical signature: geographically distant or weakly connected places cite a focal discovery later than nearby or strongly connected places.

## Operationalization

- Select focal discoveries or paradigmatic papers.
- Geocode citing papers and compute first-citation or cumulative-citation lags by city/country distance.
- Control for field, language, collaboration ties, and city research output.
- Compare diffusion lags across disciplines and historical periods.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Pan et al. (2012) frames citation networks as a way to study how quickly scientific results and paradigms are recognized across geography.
- The paper proposes this affordance but does not estimate spatial diffusion lags directly.

## Caveats

- Citation lag can reflect database coverage, publication delays, or field norms rather than recognition speed.
- Paradigm boundaries are difficult to define.
- Collaboration ties can confound distance and awareness.
