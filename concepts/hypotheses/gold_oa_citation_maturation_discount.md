# Gold OA citation maturation discount

## Summary

Gold OA's lower observed normalized citation impact may reflect newer, smaller, regional, multilingual, or still-maturing journal ecosystems rather than OA status itself.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: Gold OA journal, article, field, language, country, or index-entry cohort.
- Typical representation: decomposition of Gold OA citation outcomes by journal ecosystem maturity.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: possible maturation mechanism behind lower Gold OA citation outcomes.
- Empirical signature: Gold OA citation gaps shrink or change after accounting for journal age, language, region, and indexing history..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a maturation hypothesis to [open access citation advantage](../mechanisms/open_access_citation_advantage.md).
- Useful for avoiding simplistic claims that Gold OA itself depresses citation impact.
- Connects OA outcomes to coverage constraints in regional, multilingual, and newer journals.

## Operationalization

- Decompose Gold OA citation outcomes by journal age, index-entry year, language, country, mega-journal status, and field.
- Compare Gold OA journals with matched non-Gold journals of similar age and field.
- Report whether prestige accumulation and database coverage mediate observed differences.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Piwowar et al. (2018) explains Gold's lower citation impact through newer or smaller OA journals, historically underindexed regions, non-English venues, prestige accumulation, and mega-journal growth.
- The paper frames this as a plausible explanation rather than a causal test.

## Caveats

- The hypothesis requires independent validation and cannot be inferred from route labels alone.
- Journal maturity can be confounded with field, author selection, and publisher strategy.
