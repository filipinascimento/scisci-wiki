# Grant-horizon hot-streak alignment

## Summary

Grant-horizon hot-streak alignment is the hypothesis motif that funding periods may support or misalign with the time scale of explore-then-exploit career episodes.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: grant, funded career window, entropy phase, hot-streak duration, or funding policy.
- Typical representation: grant duration, exploration phase, exploitation phase, hot-streak onset, or funding-horizon match.
- Hypothesis target: test whether funding designs can support the temporal structure of hot streaks.
- Empirical signature: grants that span exploration-to-exploitation transitions align with higher odds of hot-streak onset or sustained high-impact output.

## Operationalization

- Link grant start and end dates to publication sequences and entropy phases.
- Test whether grant windows precede, overlap, or follow exploration-to-exploitation transitions.
- Compare grant durations with estimated hot-streak durations by field and career stage.
- Evaluate funding policies that explicitly reserve time for exploration before deliverables.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Liu et al. (2021) notes funding implications because hot streaks and grants often occupy comparable multi-year time scales.
- The paper does not link grant records to careers, so the motif is best treated as a hypothesis for future study.
- This page preserves the funding-design implication as a testable motif.

## Caveats

- Grant receipt is endogenous to reputation, topic, institution, and prior productivity.
- Grant duration similarity alone does not show causal support for hot streaks.
- Unfunded resources and institutional support may also shape career phase transitions.
