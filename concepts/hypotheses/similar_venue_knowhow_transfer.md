# Similar-venue know-how transfer

## Summary

Experience in one venue may transfer to analogous journals with similar audiences, formats, and editorial expectations, not only to the exact same journal.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author, venue family, journal similarity group, or career trajectory.
- Typical representation: prior experience in related venues as exposure for later target-venue senior authorship.
- Hypothesized mechanism: tacit publication know-how generalizes across venue families.
- Empirical signature: prior experience in similar venues predicts later success beyond same-journal history.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [tacit venue submission knowledge transfer](../mechanisms/tacit_venue_submission_knowledge_transfer.md) from exact journals to venue families.
- Useful for science-map or journal-similarity approaches to publishing experience.
- Connects chaperoning to [external journal-list field-panel construction](../methods/external_journal_list_field_panel_construction.md).

## Operationalization

- Define journal similarity by field, audience, prestige tier, editorial format, citation profile, or submission requirements.
- Model related-venue junior experience as an exposure for later senior authorship in target venues.
- Compare exact-journal, same-family, and unrelated-venue experience in the same model.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sekara et al. (2018) emphasizes tacit publication-process knowledge around venue expectations.
- The main empirical measure is same-journal history, so cross-venue transfer remains a follow-up hypothesis.

## Caveats

- Venue similarity definitions can be circular if they use the same authorship or citation outcomes being explained.
- Same-field prestige and institutional selection may mimic transferable know-how.
