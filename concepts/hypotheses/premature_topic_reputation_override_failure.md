# Premature-topic reputation override failure

## Summary

Established author reputation may not overcome field unreadiness when a topic is premature.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, author reputation, topic readiness, early citation failure, awakening, or high-B case.
- Typical representation: hypothesis linking high-status authorship to persistent early attention failure.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: limits of reputation effects under premature topic conditions.
- Empirical signature: even prominent authors can produce high-B papers when the field is not ready to use the contribution..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines prematurity mechanism hypothesis by linking it to [premature topic attention failure](../mechanisms/premature_topic_attention_failure.md) and [reputation effects](../mechanisms/reputation_effects.md).

## Operationalization

- Identify high-B papers by already prominent authors.
- Compare early attention failure and later uptake against author-reputation controls.
- Test whether field-readiness measures moderate any reputation protection effect.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ke et al. (2015) infers from APS topic groupings that premature topics may fail to attract attention even when introduced by authors with strong reputations.
- The paper cites the EPR paper as corroborating evidence for reputation-resistant prematurity.

## Caveats

- The claim is qualitative in the main text and needs external reputation measurement.
- A famous author can still shape later rediscovery, even if reputation does not prevent sleep.
