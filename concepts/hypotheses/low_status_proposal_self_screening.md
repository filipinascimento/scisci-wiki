# Low-status proposal self-screening

## Summary

Low-status proposal self-screening is the hypothesis that vulnerable applicants submit more conventional proposals because they anticipate that reviewers will reject risky or novel ideas.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: applicant, proposal idea, submitted proposal, unsubmitted idea, funding program, or status group.
- Typical representation: pre-submission selection mechanism or missing-proposal-pool hypothesis.
- Hypothesis target: conformity that occurs before peer review rather than only during review.
- Empirical signature: low-status applicants' submitted proposals are less novel than their latent idea pool, especially in competitive funding systems.

## Operationalization

- Compare submitted proposals with pre-proposal idea records, registrations, or survey-reported unsubmitted ideas.
- Test whether low-status applicants report avoiding risky proposals more often.
- Examine whether programs with lower entry cost or novelty-protective review reduce the status gap.
- Separate applicant self-screening from reviewer rejection using data on the full proposal pipeline.
- Pair proposal data with eventual output novelty where possible.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Lee, and Walsh (2018) discusses the possibility that low-status researchers choose safer projects in anticipation of competitive review.
- The paper notes that without observing submitted and unsubmitted proposals, the study cannot fully separate self-screening from reviewer selection.
- This makes low-status proposal self-screening a hypothesis generated by the funding-status pattern, not a directly proven mechanism in the paper.
- The hypothesis is useful because it identifies a missing data layer for future funding-policy experiments.

## Caveats

- Self-screening is difficult to observe because unsubmitted ideas leave few records.
- Survey reports of avoided proposals may suffer recall or justification bias.
- Reviewer selection and applicant self-screening can reinforce each other.
