# Hot-topic post-setback shift

## Summary

Hot-topic post-setback shift is the hypothesis that scientists who persist after a setback may redirect toward hotter research areas.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, post-setback paper, topic, hot-topic indicator, or career window.
- Typical representation: topic-change mechanism hypothesis with hot-topic share as evidence.
- Hypothesis target: whether topic redirection partly explains post-setback performance gains.
- Empirical signature: near misses increase participation in research fronts after setback.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects [research fronts](../mechanisms/research_fronts.md) to career-shock response.
- Uses biomedical topic signals such as [proposal MeSH novelty percentile](../measures/proposal_mesh_novelty_percentile.md) and [MeSH keyword trajectory shift](../measures/mesh_keyword_trajectory_shift.md).
- Adds a candidate channel to [observable setback-mechanism battery](../validations/observable_setback_mechanism_battery.md).

## Operationalization

- Define hot topics using citation growth, publication growth, MeSH growth, or topic trend slopes.
- Track topic portfolios before and after the setback.
- Compare near misses with narrow wins and clear winners.
- Test whether hot-topic shifts mediate impact outcomes.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Jones, and Wang (2019) explores whether near-miss scientists publish in hotter topic areas after treatment.
- The paper finds only suggestive tendency and reports that this channel does not explain the full performance gap.
- The motif is therefore a hypothesis and partial-mechanism probe, not a settled mechanism.

## Caveats

- Hot topics can be measured many ways.
- Moving into hot areas can be cause, consequence, or correlate of collaboration changes.
- Topic shifts may be constrained by existing expertise and funding.
