# Creative labor-force novelty waste

## Summary

Creative labor-force novelty waste is the consequence that science systems lose potential breakthroughs when junior, female, or peripheral-institution researchers are discouraged from pursuing novel work.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: researcher group, funding system, novelty output, underused talent pool, or policy regime.
- Typical representation: consequence motif linking inequitable funding incentives to lost exploration capacity.
- Consequence target: long-run scientific progress foregone because creative capacity is constrained among lower-status researchers.
- Empirical signature: groups with less status produce less novelty under competitive funding even though they may pursue novel work under more secure support.

## Operationalization

- Estimate novelty-output gaps by career stage, gender, institution status, or other status measures.
- Test whether secure or flexible funding narrows those gaps.
- Translate subgroup novelty losses into system-level counts, rates, or counterfactual output.
- Distinguish productivity loss from novelty loss.
- Connect observed patterns to funding-allocation reforms or protected exploration programs.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Lee, and Walsh (2018) concludes that competitive funding may discourage novel research by junior, female, and peripheral-university researchers.
- The paper warns that such discouragement wastes creative capacity and can harm long-term scientific progress.
- The consequence follows from their status-contingent funding-novelty findings rather than a separate macro-level experiment.
- It makes equity in funding design a system-performance issue, not only a fairness issue.

## Caveats

- The paper's empirical setting is Japan and may not transfer unchanged to all funding systems.
- Novelty measures based on journal combinations capture one dimension of creativity.
- Estimating wasted novelty requires counterfactual assumptions about what constrained researchers would otherwise produce.
