# Novelty-underinvestment selection pathway

## Summary

Review discounts for novel proposals can become systematic underinvestment in exploratory science.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: proposal, novelty score, review score, funding rank, exploratory project, or portfolio share.
- Typical representation: selection pathway from novelty penalty to funded-portfolio novelty shortfall.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: downstream consequence of novelty bias in peer review.
- Empirical signature: the funded portfolio contains fewer high-novelty proposals under observed rankings than under novelty-adjusted rankings.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines consequences of peer-review bias by linking it to [right tail novelty review discount](../mechanisms/right_tail_novelty_review_discount.md) and [novelty penalty](../mechanisms/novelty_penalty.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [funding incentives and exploration](../mechanisms/funding_incentives_and_exploration.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [novelty aware review calibration](../methods/novelty_aware_review_calibration.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Estimate the novelty penalty in proposal scores.
- Simulate funding under observed rankings and novelty-adjusted rankings.
- Track novelty shares among submitted, funded, and later successful projects.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Boudreau et al. (2016) argues that discounting novel proposals relative to true quality implies underinvestment and that conventional aggregation or blinding cannot solve the problem.
- The discussion also notes that novelty penalties may discourage experimentation.

## Caveats

- The pathway depends on unobserved counterfactual true quality and outcomes of unfunded proposals.
- A novelty correction can overfund infeasible proposals if feasibility is not measured separately.
