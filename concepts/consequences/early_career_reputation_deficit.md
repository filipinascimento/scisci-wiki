# Early-career reputation deficit

## Summary

Early-career reputation deficit is the consequence motif that junior scientists may receive less early attention because they lack the author-reputation premium that helps papers cross visibility thresholds.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: junior author, early-career paper, citation rate, reputation level, or career transition.
- Typical representation: low initial reputation, delayed attention, citation crossover barrier, or junior visibility deficit.
- Consequence target: show how reputation-dependent attention can disadvantage early-career scientists.
- Empirical signature: matched junior-authored papers receive lower early citation rates than papers by high-reputation authors, especially below citation crossover thresholds.

## Operationalization

- Match papers by field, year, venue, topic, team size, and early quality proxies.
- Compare early citation rates by author reputation and career age.
- Estimate whether junior papers take longer to reach citation crossover thresholds.
- Test whether mentorship, venue, or coauthor reputation offsets the deficit.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Petersen et al. (2014) argues that young scientists lacking reputation can be negatively affected by social stratification in attention allocation.
- The paper's reputation model implies that author reputation matters most for papers below a citation crossover threshold.
- This motif captures the career consequence of a citation-rate reputation premium.

## Caveats

- Junior authors differ in resources, mentorship, topic choice, and venue access.
- Senior coauthors can either offset or obscure junior reputation deficits.
- Early citations are not the only form of recognition or career opportunity.
