# Scholar stranding and work interruption

## Summary

Mobility restrictions can interrupt scientific work by stranding scholars abroad, blocking visits, or severing international engagement.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scholar, visit, collaboration, affiliation episode, or policy exposure.
- Typical representation: interruption events, gaps, missed travel, delayed output, and collaboration pauses.
- Consequence: mobility policy disrupts ongoing research activity rather than only future flows.
- Empirical signature: exposed scholars show travel disruptions, affiliation gaps, collaboration breaks, or publication delays.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a downstream consequence to [mobility policy shock evaluation](../validations/mobility_policy_shock_evaluation.md).
- Useful for studying short-run disruption from bans, visa denials, or border closures.
- Connects mobility restrictions to [mobility-collaboration complementarity](../mechanisms/mobility_collaboration_complementarity.md).

## Operationalization

- Measure visa or travel disruptions, interrupted visits, missed conferences, affiliation gaps, collaboration pauses, and publication delays.
- Compare exposed scholars with matched unexposed scholars in the same field, career stage, and origin-destination routes.
- Separate immediate work interruption from long-run migration or retention outcomes.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sugimoto et al. (2017) notes that mobility orders stranded scholars abroad and prevented international scientific work.
- The language motivates a consequence motif around work interruption, not only migration counts.

## Caveats

- Administrative disruption data are hard to observe and may be confounded with broader geopolitical shocks.
- Publication delays can arise from field conditions, funding, or coauthor disruptions unrelated to mobility policy.
