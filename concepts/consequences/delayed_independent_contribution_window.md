# Delayed independent contribution window

## Summary

Rising frontier-entry age compresses the career interval available for independent inventive contribution.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: inventor, career cohort, first invention age, doctoral age, or active innovation years.
- Typical representation: life-cycle window from completed training or first invention to end of active inventive career.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: career-time consequence of knowledge-burden-driven training delays.
- Empirical signature: later first invention or longer training leaves fewer remaining years for independent inventive output.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines knowledge-burden consequences by linking it to [age at first invention](../measures/age_at_first_invention.md) and [human capital transfer bottleneck](../mechanisms/human_capital_transfer_bottleneck.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [doctorate duration frontier entry signal](../measures/doctorate_duration_frontier_entry_signal.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [knowledge burden scale effects constraint](../mechanisms/knowledge_burden_scale_effects_constraint.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Measure age at first patent or first independent output.
- Compare it with age at highest degree or doctorate completion.
- Estimate active innovation years remaining after first observed invention across cohorts.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Jones (2009) frames first invention as the boundary between pre-innovation training and active innovation.
- Jones argues that longer educational duration reduces the portion of life left for innovation.

## Caveats

- This is the downstream life-cycle consequence, not the raw age-at-first-invention measure.
- Patent-based timing can miss unpublished or nonpatented creative contributions.
