# Small-team population shrinkage

## Summary

The long-run decline in small-team prevalence reduces the population base from which disruptive and delayed-recognition work disproportionately emerges.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field-year small-team share and disruption capacity.
- Typical representation: share of papers or patents below a team-size threshold over time.
- Consequence: shrinking small-team population may lower aggregate production of disruptive work.
- Empirical signature: small-team share declines while small teams retain higher disruption rates.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Turns [team size and disruption](../mechanisms/team_size_disruption.md) into a system-level capacity concern.
- Connects team-size distribution trends to [small-team success ripple effect](../mechanisms/small_team_success_ripple_effect.md).
- Useful for funding-policy discussions about preserving small-team ecology.

## Operationalization

- Track small-team share by field/year and output type.
- Multiply by team-size-specific disruption rates to estimate field-level disruptive-capacity change.
- Test alternative small-team thresholds and field-specific authorship norms.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wu et al. (2019) reports large declines in the share of small teams across Web of Science articles and patents over the observation periods.
- The decline changes the base rate of teams most associated with disruption.

## Caveats

- Author-count thresholds are field-specific, and small teams are not automatically disruptive.
- Large teams can contain small subteams whose contribution is hidden by byline size.
