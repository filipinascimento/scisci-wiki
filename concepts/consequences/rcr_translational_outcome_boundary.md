# RCR translational-outcome boundary

## Summary

RCR measures scholarly citation influence and should be separated from clinical, human-health, engineering, software, patent, and economic outcomes.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: article, citation audience, applied output, translational trace, patent, clinical outcome, or portfolio goal.
- Typical representation: audience-target boundary separating scholarly influence from downstream use.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: scope of citation metrics in translational evaluation.
- Empirical signature: RCR is informative for scholarly audience influence but insufficient when the evaluation target is applied uptake or social benefit.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines responsible use of article metrics by linking it to [citation influence quality boundary](../validations/citation_influence_quality_boundary.md) and [funding portfolio outcome suite](../methods/funding_portfolio_outcome_suite.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [target oriented impact metrics](../measures/target_oriented_impact_metrics.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [science technology lag](../measures/science_technology_lag.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- State whether the target audience is publishing scholars, clinicians, engineers, policymakers, or other applied users.
- Add downstream-output traces such as patents, software, clinical guidelines, trials, or health outcomes when translation is the target.
- Report RCR as one scholarly-influence input inside a broader outcome suite.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hutchins et al. (2016) states that RCR is not designed for long-term impact or narrow applied audiences and that health, software, patent, clinical, and economic outcomes need additional metrics.
- This creates a clear boundary between citation influence and translational impact.

## Caveats

- The boundary is close to broader citation-quality caveats but focuses specifically on applied audience mismatch.
- Low RCR does not imply low clinical or technological value.
