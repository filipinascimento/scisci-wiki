# Science-of-team-science process scope

## Summary

Science-of-team-science process scope represents team science as a broader construct than coauthorship counts, including organization, communication, coordination, conduct, and policy relevance.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: team, project, communication process, coordination structure, byline, or team-science policy.
- Typical representation: team process layer, coauthorship proxy, communication evidence, task allocation, or governance structure.
- Representation target: distinguish byline-size studies from richer process-level studies of team science.
- Empirical signature: a study operationalizes teams with publications but frames team science as a wider set of organizational processes.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects [team assembly mechanisms](../mechanisms/team_assembly_mechanisms.md) to process-level team-science research.
- Links [team science contribution assessment](../methods/team_science_contribution_assessment.md), [coauthorship networks](coauthorship_networks.md), and [science as a multiscale network](science_as_multiscale_network.md).
- Helps readers interpret byline-based team models as one layer of the team-science construct.

## Operationalization

- Label whether a study observes bylines, contribution statements, communication, project governance, or task allocation.
- Separate team-size distribution results from claims about team process quality.
- Use process evidence when evaluating policy interventions in team science.
- Link coauthorship data with surveys, contribution statements, or project management traces where available.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Milojevic (2014) introduces science of team science with organizational, communicative, and policy motivations even though the empirical model uses article teams.
- This creates a useful scope distinction between team-size modeling and process-level team science.

## Caveats

- The Milojevic model itself does not measure communication or coordination directly.
- Process-level data are often hard to collect at scale.
- Coauthorship remains a useful but partial representation.
