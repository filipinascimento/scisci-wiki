# Topic-clustered sleeping-beauty histories

## Summary

High-beauty papers can cluster into coarse topical groups with similar citation trajectories, suggesting topic-level sleep.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: high-B paper, topic, co-citation cluster, keyword set, citation history, or trajectory group.
- Typical representation: topic-labeled groups of high-B citation histories.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: topic-level structure in delayed-recognition trajectories.
- Empirical signature: top sleeping beauties share coarse topics and similar delayed citation shapes..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines delayed-recognition representation by linking it to [premature topic attention failure](../mechanisms/premature_topic_attention_failure.md) and [awakening cocitation keyword diagnosis](../methods/awakening_cocitation_keyword_diagnosis.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [beauty coefficient](../measures/beauty_coefficient.md) is interpreted from citation histories.
- Adds cross-links to [ranked sleeping beauty case inventory](ranked_sleeping_beauty_case_inventory.md) so delayed-recognition and ranking motifs stay connected.

## Operationalization

- Rank high-B papers and assign topics using co-citation, keywords, manual labels, or field tags.
- Compare citation-trajectory similarity within and across topic labels.
- Use topic clusters to distinguish isolated delayed papers from delayed research themes.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ke et al. (2015) notes that top APS sleeping beauties naturally group into a small number of coarse topics with remarkably similar citation histories.
- The motif represents delayed recognition as a property of topics as well as individual papers.

## Caveats

- Main-text topic labels are high level and can be subjective without a released clustering protocol.
- Similar histories do not prove a shared mechanism.
