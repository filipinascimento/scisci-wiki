# Consensus-aligned circular map ordering

## Summary

Consensus-aligned circular map ordering arranges science-map disciplines and subdisciplines around a circle using an external consensus map and local factor-analysis ordering.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: discipline region, subdiscipline cluster, journal cluster, consensus-map order, or circular coordinate.
- Typical representation: one-dimensional circular science map aligned to a consensus ordering of major domains.
- Representation target: reduce map complexity while preserving the major structural sequence of science.
- Empirical signature: disciplines follow a circular order consistent with consensus maps, while subdisciplines are ordered by local similarity structure.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [Riemannian science-map projections](riemannian_science_map_projections.md) from geometry to ordering logic.
- Supports readable [science map overlays](science_map_overlays.md).
- Connects [journal-cluster science backbones](journal_cluster_science_backbones.md) to a simplified circular display.

## Operationalization

- Start from a higher-dimensional or two-dimensional science map.
- Align major disciplines to an external consensus map order.
- Order subdisciplines within each discipline using local factor loadings or comparable similarity structure.
- Use the circular display for compact overlays, competency maps, or communication.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Borner et al. (2012) describes a one-dimensional circular UCSD map that preserves the key structure of the two-dimensional map.
- The paper aligns the 13 disciplines with a consensus map derived from 20 global maps of science.
- It orders subdisciplines and journals within regions using factor analysis.

## Caveats

- Circular ordering simplifies structure and can hide cross-cutting links.
- Consensus maps may preserve older classification assumptions.
- Factor-analysis ordering choices should be documented because nearby positions guide interpretation.
