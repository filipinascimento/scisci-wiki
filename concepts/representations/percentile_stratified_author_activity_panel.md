# Percentile-stratified author activity panel

## Summary

Percentile-stratified author activity panel is a representation that compares author activity, citation, and collaboration trajectories across citation-percentile strata.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author, percentile stratum, year, publication count, citation rate, collaborator count, or coauthor team size.
- Typical representation: aligned multi-panel figure with percentile groups on shared time or career axes.
- Representation target: show which activity channels differ between citation elites and lower-percentile authors.
- Empirical signature: output, citation, coauthorship, and collaborator trends are plotted for the same percentile-defined author groups.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Makes [citation elite productivity divergence](../mechanisms/citation_elite_productivity_divergence.md) visible as a multi-channel pattern.
- Links [full versus fractional citation counting](../measures/full_fractional_citation_counting.md) to collaboration structure.
- Complements [publication rate normalized rank](../measures/publication_rate_normalized_rank.md) and [collaborator count distribution](../measures/collaborator_count_distribution.md).
- Gives [citation elite concentration](../measures/citation_elite_concentration.md) a diagnostic representation beyond a single inequality curve.

## Operationalization

- Rank authors into citation percentile groups using the study's citation-counting and normalization rules.
- For each group, calculate yearly or career-age trends in output, citations, coauthors per paper, and unique collaborators.
- Plot all channels with consistent group labels and denominators.
- Distinguish full-count and fractional-count outcomes where collaboration expansion can change interpretation.
- Report uncertainty or sample sizes for sparse percentile groups.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Nielsen and Andersen (2021) compares citation percentiles through aligned panels of publication output, citation rates, coauthorship, and collaborator trends.
- Their activity-panel view shows that highly cited authors differ not only by citation totals but also by output volume and collaboration breadth.
- The motif captures a reusable representation for inequality decomposition.
- It is most useful when paired with a denominator audit and a clear author-population rule.

## Caveats

- Percentile groups can mix cohort, field, and country effects unless the ranking procedure controls them.
- Activity panels are descriptive; causal claims about why elites publish or collaborate more need additional designs.
- Full-count collaboration panels can exaggerate the apparent output advantage of large teams.
