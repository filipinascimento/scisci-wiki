# Collaboration organizing-driver quadrants

## Summary

Collaboration organizing-driver quadrants represent international collaboration regimes by crossing top-down versus bottom-up organization with centralized versus distributed research location.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, subfield, research program, collaboration portfolio, or policy intervention.
- Typical representation: four-quadrant typology with megascience, coordinated, resource-dependent, and participatory collaboration regimes.
- Mechanism or measurement target: distinguish institutional and intellectual drivers that shape international collaboration.
- Empirical signature: different subfields occupy different quadrants, but network self-organization can appear across quadrants.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Gives a field-level representation for [subfield international coauthorship networks](subfield_international_coauthorship_networks.md).
- Helps compare policy-built collaboration with self-organized partner choice in [international collaboration networks](international_collaboration_networks.md).
- Provides a design frame for testing whether [international collaboration preferential attachment](../mechanisms/international_collaboration_preferential_attachment.md) dominates field-specific organizational constraints.

## Operationalization

- Define a collaboration corpus by field, subfield, journal set, topic model, or research program.
- Classify the research location as centralized or distributed.
- Classify the organizing force as top-down or bottom-up.
- Assign cases to megascience, coordinated, resource-dependent, or participatory quadrants.
- Compare degree distributions, clustering, country cores, and field outcomes across quadrants.
- Treat top-down quadrant placement as a policy-structure hypothesis that still needs [constructed collaboration sustainability limit](../validations/constructed_collaboration_sustainability_limit.md) checks.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wagner and Leydesdorff (2005) uses a schematic with organized versus spontaneous collaboration on one axis and distributed versus centralized research on the other.
- They identify megascience, coordinated, resource-dependent, and participatory quadrants and choose six subfield cases to span these drivers.
- The same paper concludes that, although these drivers continue to operate, preferential attachment across the examined fields became more influential for international collaboration structure during the 1990s.
- The policy implication is that megascience and coordinated programs can create links, but durability depends on whether constructed collaboration is supported by local capability and reward-compatible scientific work.

## Caveats

- The quadrants are heuristic rather than mutually exclusive field types.
- A top-down program can still contain self-organizing teams, and bottom-up collaboration can respond to funding incentives.
- Classifying a field once can hide within-field variation across instruments, data sources, institutions, and countries.
