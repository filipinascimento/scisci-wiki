# Knowledge-Circle Expertise Arcs

## Summary

Knowledge-circle expertise arcs represent application areas as circular knowledge spaces where innovators cover only arcs of foundational knowledge, making breadth and implementation coverage explicit.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: innovator, application area, knowledge type, or team.
- Typical representation: circular frontier with individual expertise arcs and depth of knowledge.
- Representation target: how individual specialization and team coverage relate to a complete application-area knowledge space.
- Empirical signature: deeper knowledge increases education cost for a given breadth, while narrower arcs require team assembly for implementation.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a representation for [burden of knowledge](../mechanisms/burden_of_knowledge.md).
- Connects [minimum-expertise team assembly](../mechanisms/minimum_expertise_team_assembly.md) to a formal coverage problem.
- Helps explain [education-specialization substitution](../mechanisms/education_specialization_substitution.md).
- Relates application-area choice to [application-area crowding arbitrage](../mechanisms/application_area_crowding_arbitrage.md).

## Operationalization

- Represent each application area as a knowledge space with breadth and depth dimensions.
- Assign individuals a location and expertise interval within the space.
- Treat implementation as requiring coverage of the whole application-area circle.
- Use the representation to reason about specialization, team size, and education cost.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Jones (2009) formally arranges foundational knowledge types in each application area around a circle.
- Jones defines an innovator's choice as an application area, a point on the circle, and a breadth interval.
- The model makes education cost a function of chosen breadth multiplied by prevailing depth of knowledge.

## Caveats

- The circle is a stylized representation, not a literal map of knowledge.
- It assumes one-dimensional circular coverage where real knowledge spaces are multidimensional and uneven.
- Empirical use requires strong assumptions about what counts as foundational knowledge coverage.
