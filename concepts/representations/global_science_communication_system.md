# Global science communication system

## Summary

International collaboration can be represented as a global communication system layered above national, regional, and local science systems.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: country, field, coauthorship tie, communication layer, national system, or global network.
- Typical representation: multilevel coauthorship communication system linking global and local layers.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: supranational representation of scientific communication through collaboration traces.
- Empirical signature: country collaboration links form a global layer that feeds back into national and regional science systems.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines international-collaboration representation by linking it to [international collaboration networks](international_collaboration_networks.md) and [local global knowledge bridge](../mechanisms/local_global_knowledge_bridge.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [country collaboration core](../measures/country_collaboration_core.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [collaboration regime policy leverage](../methods/collaboration_regime_policy_leverage.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Model country and subfield coauthorship as a global communication layer.
- Compare feedback from global links into national, regional, and local systems.
- Track global-to-local knowledge-flow bridges while treating coauthorship as a trace rather than communication itself.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified local full text from Wagner and Leydesdorff (2005) frames international collaboration as a communication system and describes feedback into national, regional, and local systems.
- The motif provides the representational frame for international collaboration networks.

## Caveats

- Coauthorship is only a trace of communication, not the full communication process.
- Country-level aggregation can hide institutional and individual mechanisms.
