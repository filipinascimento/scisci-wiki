# Knowledge-frontier envelope

## Summary

Knowledge-frontier envelope represents the shared perceived boundary between what a scientific community currently knows and what remains open for investigation.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, proposal, evaluator, concept space, research front, knowledge stock, or scientific community.
- Typical representation: frontier boundary, open-knowledge stock, known-versus-unknown demarcation, topic frontier, or proposal position relative to prior work.
- Representation target: make the frontier concept explicit before operationalizing it with controlled vocabularies, embeddings, citations, or science maps.
- Empirical signature: proposals and discoveries can be described as looking beyond, crossing, or extending the perceived boundary of established knowledge.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides the conceptual layer behind [MeSH knowledge-frontier space](mesh_knowledge_frontier_space.md) and related operational frontier maps.
- Links science maps, [research fronts](../mechanisms/research_fronts.md), novelty measures, and peer review to a common representation of known and unknown territory.
- Helps separate incremental within-paradigm advance from work that asks evaluators to look beyond their own expertise.
- Connects proposal evaluation to [expected-unexpected discovery spectrum](expected_unexpected_discovery_spectrum.md) and [novelty and conventionality](../mechanisms/novelty_conventionality.md).

## Operationalization

- Define the community, corpus, time cutoff, and representation used to mark current knowledge.
- Represent known territory with controlled vocabulary terms, term pairs, citations, embeddings, topic models, ontologies, or graph neighborhoods.
- Locate focal proposals, papers, patents, or discoveries relative to that prior territory.
- Distinguish frontier extension from evaluator-proposal distance, because a proposal can be novel relative to the field and near or far from a given evaluator.
- Audit whether the frontier is treated as universally shared or community-specific.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Boudreau, Guinan, Lakhani, and Riedl (2016) describes publication, conferences, training, and related diffusion channels as creating a common stock of open knowledge.
- The same full text defines a commonly perceived knowledge frontier or envelope that demarcates what is currently known from what remains to be investigated.
- Boudreau et al. argue that new research creates intellectual distance because evaluators must look beyond the existing knowledge frontier, and that more novel departures move farther from prevailing paradigms.
- Their operational study uses PubMed and MeSH to instantiate this frontier in biomedical proposal review.

## Caveats

- The frontier is perceived and community-dependent; what is unknown to one field may be routine in another.
- Operational frontier maps inherit database coverage, vocabulary, and indexing limits.
- A frontier boundary is not a quality ranking: crossing it can signal breakthrough potential, infeasibility, poor framing, or measurement noise.
- Historical reconstructions can make frontier positions look clearer than they were at decision time.
