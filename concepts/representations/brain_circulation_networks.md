# Brain circulation networks

## Summary

Brain circulation networks represent international researcher mobility as flows and affiliation chains linking countries, rather than one-way brain drain or brain gain.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: country, region, researcher, affiliation chain, origin-destination flow, or mobility path.
- Typical representation: weighted directed country-flow network, circulation matrix, country bridge map, or mobility-path network.
- Mechanism or measurement target: how mobile scholars connect national systems, move knowledge, and create international research capacity.
- Empirical signature: central nodes, bridge countries, return paths, and repeated affiliation chains reveal circulation beyond migration counts.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a network representation for [scientific mobility](../mechanisms/scientific_mobility.md) and [international mobility typologies](../measures/international_mobility_typologies.md).
- Builds from tabular [origin-destination mobility matrices](origin_destination_mobility_matrix.md) when the evidence is a survey or country-pair flow table.
- Complements [international collaboration networks](international_collaboration_networks.md) by tracking people and affiliation chains rather than coauthored papers alone.
- Supplies the structure for [country mobility role typology](../measures/country_mobility_role_typology.md) and [mobility policy shock evaluation](../validations/mobility_policy_shock_evaluation.md).
- Connects mobility to [science as a multiscale network](science_as_multiscale_network.md), where scientists, institutions, countries, and papers are coupled layers.
- Inherits national-aggregation limits captured by [within-country mobility omission](../validations/within_country_mobility_omission.md).

## Operationalization

- Reconstruct author-level country affiliation sequences from publications or workforce records.
- Convert sequences into weighted edges among countries or regions, preserving direction, timing, return moves, and multi-affiliation ties.
- Measure centrality, bridge roles, net gain/loss, route concentration, and disruption sensitivity under simulated or observed policy restrictions.
- Add route-level impact and circulation detail through [route-specific high-impact mobility corridors](../measures/route_specific_high_impact_mobility_corridors.md), [student-mediated return circulation](../mechanisms/student_mediated_return_circulation.md), and [dynamic human-capital exchange indicators](../methods/dynamic_human_capital_exchange_indicators.md).
- Compare mobility-flow networks with coauthorship networks and citation-impact outcomes.
- Preserve traveler subtypes such as [non-directional traveler affiliation pattern](../measures/non_directional_traveler_affiliation_pattern.md) when affiliation chains cannot be reduced to a single directed move.
- Track [scientific destination-hub dominance](../measures/scientific_destination_hub_dominance.md) before treating one central destination as a resilient circulation system.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sugimoto et al. (2017) argues that brain circulation is a better description than simple brain drain or gain because many scientists do not cut ties with their country of origin.
- The paper reports that mobile scientists often build chains of affiliations linking nations together, and many return to their home country.
- The same evidence motivates corridor-specific impact measures and return-circulation mechanisms because flows differ by route and are often mediated by student and early-career pathways.
- Sugimoto et al. use circulation networks to map researcher flows and identify the United States, United Kingdom, France, Canada, and Germany as prominent nodes in the global scientific network.
- The paper also frames the United Kingdom as a bridge for European scientists to other parts of the world, showing that country roles can matter even when a country is not the largest migration destination.

## Caveats

- Circulation networks depend on affiliation disambiguation, country assignment, and publication coverage.
- Edges can mix relocation, dual affiliation, temporary visits, remote affiliation, and collaboration-mediated address changes.
- Network centrality does not prove causal contribution to impact or knowledge transfer.
- Country-flow networks can miss domestic moves among institutions, cities, or regions.
