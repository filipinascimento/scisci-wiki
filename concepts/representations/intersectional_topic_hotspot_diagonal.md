# Intersectional topic-hotspot diagonal

## Summary

Intersectional topic-hotspot diagonal is a race-gender topic-map reading strategy that highlights topics simultaneously feminized and racialized.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: topic, race-gender group, topic-map quadrant, or overrepresentation ratio.
- Typical representation: diagonal region in a topic portfolio plot.
- Representation target: identify topic areas where multiple demographic dimensions jointly concentrate.
- Empirical signature: topics lie in a high-representation quadrant for both gender and racialized group participation.

## Operationalization

- Plot topics by gender share and race-group share.
- Highlight the quadrant where both shares are high.
- Label topics along the diagonal that combine feminization and racialization.
- Inspect whether those topics differ in citation density or prestige.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Kozlowski et al. (2022) interprets topics along a quadrant diagonal that are both highly feminized and highly represented by racialized groups.
- Examples include topics linked to Black women and Latinx women authorship patterns.
- The representation helps expose intersectional topic clustering that single-axis plots would miss.

## Caveats

- Quadrant thresholds are visual and should be documented.
- Sparse groups can make hotspot labels unstable.
- Topic labels require manual inspection and may simplify complex topic contents.
