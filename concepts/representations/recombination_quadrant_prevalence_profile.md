# Recombination quadrant prevalence profile

## Summary

Recombination quadrant prevalence profile represents how a publication population is distributed across high and low conventionality and high and low tail novelty categories.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, novelty-conventionality quadrant, publication cohort, field, or hit-rate cell.
- Typical representation: four-cell quadrant table with population shares and outcome rates.
- Representation target: show both how common a recombination strategy is and how it relates to impact.
- Empirical signature: the highest hit-rate quadrant can be a minority strategy rather than the dominant population mode.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds composition information to the [novelty-conventionality quadrant typology](../measures/novelty_conventionality_quadrant_typology.md).
- Pairs [hit-paper probability](../measures/hit_paper_probability.md) with the share of papers in each recombination cell.
- Helps distinguish high-impact strategy prevalence from high-impact strategy payoff.
- Supports comparisons across fields, decades, teams, and data sources.

## Operationalization

- Classify papers into high or low median conventionality and high or low tail novelty.
- Count the share of the publication cohort in each of the four cells.
- Estimate hit-paper probability or another outcome rate within each cell.
- Display prevalence and outcome rates together so rare high-payoff cells are visible.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Uzzi et al. (2013) presents hit probabilities conditional on high or low median conventionality and high or low tail novelty.
- The paper reports that papers combining high median conventionality and high tail novelty have a hit rate nearly double the background rate.
- The same figure reports the percentage of WoS papers in each quadrant, giving prevalence values alongside hit probabilities.
- Uzzi et al. report similar findings across decades, alternate hit thresholds, and field controls.

## Caveats

- High and low thresholds should be defined before comparing prevalence across cohorts.
- A quadrant cell can have high payoff but low prevalence, which affects population-level contribution.
- Quadrant profiles simplify continuous recombination measures and can hide nonlinear effects.
