# Discipline-specific OA route ecology

## Summary

Fields differ not only in OA prevalence but in their dominant route ecology, such as repository-heavy physics and mathematics versus Gold or Hybrid-heavy biomedical fields.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, OA route, article cohort, repository infrastructure, or journal set.
- Typical representation: field-by-route matrix with route shares and dominant infrastructure annotations.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: disciplinary route ecology of open access.
- Empirical signature: fields with similar OA prevalence rely on different route mixes and infrastructures..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds route ecology to [open access route typology](../datasets/open_access_route_typology.md).
- Useful for comparing repository-dependent and publisher-dependent OA systems.
- Connects OA measurement to [field classifications](../measures/field_classifications.md) and Web of Science discipline panels.

## Operationalization

- Build field-by-route matrices with field taxonomy, route shares, and dominant infrastructure annotations.
- Separate multidisciplinary journal handling from field-specific journal handling.
- Track route ecology over publication years to distinguish growth from field composition.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Piwowar et al. (2018) reports field differences in OA prevalence and route mix, including arXiv-driven Green OA in physics and mathematics.
- The study also reports low OA in fields such as chemistry and engineering.

## Caveats

- Journal-level field assignment and multidisciplinary journals can distort route profiles.
- Field-specific repositories can make Green OA appear differently across sources.
