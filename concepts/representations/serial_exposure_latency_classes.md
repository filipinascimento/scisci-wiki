# Serial exposure latency classes

## Summary

Serial exposure latency classes represent field adoption as multiple successive exposed states when becoming an active contributor requires staged learning or preparation.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: exposed author, learning stage, training phase, field-transition pathway, or SEIR state.
- Typical representation: multi-stage exposed compartment, latency chain, incubation-state diagram, or transition-rate cascade.
- Representation target: model adoption delays that are longer or more heterogeneous than a single exposed-to-active transition.
- Empirical signature: aggregate growth can be fit by alternatives that include several incubation classes before publication activity appears.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends the [scientific SEIR contact model](../methods/scientific_seir_contact_model.md) beyond a single exposed class.
- Refines [scientific apprenticeship latency](../measures/scientific_apprenticeship_latency.md) when training requires sequential coursework, lab setup, method transfer, or infrastructure access.
- Provides an explicit state-space option for [field-growth model identifiability](../validations/field_growth_model_identifiability.md).
- Links field growth to staged career and skill-acquisition representations.

## Operationalization

- Replace one exposed compartment with ordered exposed states, each with its own transition rate.
- Fit or constrain the latency chain using author histories, training records, publication lags, or expert historical evidence.
- Compare single-latency and serial-latency models against the same field-growth trajectory.
- Report whether the serial representation changes substantive conclusions about recruitment, contact, or saturation.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Bettencourt et al. (2008) states that their simple model belongs to a broader family that can include multiple latency classes.
- In the quantum-computing case, the authors note that models with several exposed classes and longer successive incubation times could fit the delayed takeoff, alongside time-varying contact-rate alternatives.
- This makes serial exposure classes a reusable representation for field-growth cases where a single incubation parameter hides staged preparation.

## Caveats

- Adding exposure classes can improve fit while worsening identifiability.
- The classes should correspond to plausible training or adoption stages, not only curve-fitting convenience.
- Aggregate author-count data rarely identify the number of latent stages without auxiliary evidence.
