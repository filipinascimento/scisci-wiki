# Isolated-school fragmentation regime

## Summary

Isolated-school fragmentation regime is a collaboration-network representation in which a field consists of many disconnected local schools rather than a single large connected community.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, collaboration network, team sequence, component, or school of thought.
- Typical representation: pre-percolation network with many small components and no dominant giant component.
- Representation target: describe a field where knowledge, methods, and norms remain localized in separate collaboration clusters.
- Empirical signature: the largest collaboration component remains small relative to the field, and repeated ties do not connect most incumbents into one network.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides the pre-transition counterpart to [collaboration percolation transition](../mechanisms/collaboration_percolation_transition.md).
- Gives [collaboration giant component](../measures/collaboration_giant_component.md) a qualitative interpretation when the giant component is absent or small.
- Contrasts with [invisible college](../mechanisms/invisible_college.md), where a field can be linked through a broad informal or collaboration network.
- Helps interpret team-assembly regimes that maintain local schools instead of field-wide integration.

## Operationalization

- Build a collaboration network from teams, authors, creators, or project participants.
- Compute connected components, largest-component size, clustering, and repeat-link rates.
- Locate the field relative to a percolation threshold or fitted team-assembly parameter space.
- Inspect whether components correspond to specialties, journals, institutions, genres, or schools of thought.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Guimera, Uzzi, Spiro, and Amaral (2005) describes collaboration regimes in which isolated clusters or schools of thought persist.
- Their team-assembly model shows that high repeat-collaboration propensity and insufficient incumbent mixing can keep a network fragmented.
- The paper contrasts this fragmented regime with fields where a giant component joins much of the collaboration network.

## Caveats

- Missing collaboration records can make a connected field look fragmented.
- Intellectual exchange can occur through citations, conferences, or informal ties even when coauthorship components are disconnected.
- Component fragmentation is not inherently bad; some fields may benefit from plural local schools.
