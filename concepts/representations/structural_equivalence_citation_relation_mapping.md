# Structural Equivalence Citation-Relation Mapping

## Summary

Structural equivalence citation-relation mapping represents co-citation, bibliographic coupling, and joint citing/cited profiles as special cases of structural equivalence over citation relations.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: article pair, citation relation, cited-by profile, citing profile, or joint citation-position profile.
- Typical representation: equivalence mapping from network relations to citation-similarity constructs.
- Mechanism or measurement target: positional similarity in citation neighborhoods.
- Empirical signature: two papers are similar when they share citing papers, shared references, or both.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects [co-citation](co_citation.md) to structural equivalence over cited-by relations.
- Connects [bibliographic coupling](bibliographic_coupling.md) to structural equivalence over citing relations.

## Operationalization

- Define whether the relation is cited-by, citing, or both.
- Build relation profiles for each paper over the chosen neighborhood.
- Compare profiles using exact structural equivalence, similarity, or a weighted approximation.
- Interpret the result as citation-position similarity rather than direct historical sequence.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hummon and Doreian (1989) explains structural equivalence as connection to the same other objects.
- They map structural equivalence over cited-by relations directly to co-citation.
- They map structural equivalence over citing relations to bibliographic coupling and note that both citing and cited relations can be analyzed together.

## Caveats

- Exact structural equivalence is rare in large noisy citation networks, so operational work often uses similarity approximations.
- The mapping captures positional similarity, not necessarily substantive agreement or direct influence.
- Results depend on citation-window, corpus-boundary, and edge-type definitions.
