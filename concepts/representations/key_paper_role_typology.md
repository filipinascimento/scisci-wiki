# Key-paper role typology

## Summary

Refines key-paper proxies by tagging highly cited or co-cited nodes as concepts, methods, experiments, formulas, theories, or other intellectual roles.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: highly cited or co-cited core paper.
- Typical representation: role label attached to a key paper in a specialty map.
- Representation target: semantic function of core papers rather than only their centrality.
- Empirical signature: co-cited core nodes play distinguishable roles in the citing community.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds role annotation to [key-paper concept proxy](key_paper_concept_proxy.md).
- Useful when co-cited core literature mixes theory, method, experimental result, and canonical review papers.
- Connects co-citation maps to citation contexts and research-area labeling.

## Operationalization

- Annotate co-cited core nodes using titles, abstracts, full text, citation contexts, and expert labels.
- Allow multiple role labels where a paper functions as both method and theory.
- Track role changes across citing communities or time slices.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Small (1973) says frequently cited papers may represent key concepts, methods, or experiments in a field.
- That statement motivates a role typology rather than a flat key-paper label.

## Caveats

- A single paper can play multiple roles, and role labels can shift across communities.
- Automated role classifiers need validation against domain reading.
