# Policy-attention hypothesis ladder

## Summary

Policy-attention hypothesis ladder is a representation that decomposes low policy-mention coverage into competing explanations such as source coverage, true relevance, citation-style omission, and weak researcher-policy interaction.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: policy mention, scientific paper, source family, field, or explanation class.
- Typical representation: ordered set of hypotheses explaining observed policy-mention sparsity.
- Representation target: keep measurement artifacts and substantive policy relevance hypotheses separate.
- Empirical signature: low coverage is not interpreted until alternative explanation rungs are assessed.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Organizes [policy attention coverage bias](../validations/policy_attention_coverage_bias.md).
- Links measurement artifacts to [policy citation style omission](../validations/policy_citation_style_omission.md).
- Connects substantive explanations to [research-policy interaction barrier](../mechanisms/research_policy_interaction_barrier.md).
- Helps interpret results from the [WoS-Altmetric policy mention panel](../datasets/wos_altmetric_policy_mention_panel.md).

## Operationalization

- Start from the observed policy-mention rate.
- Test whether DOI loss, source-list coverage, and citation-style omission can explain sparsity.
- Then evaluate field relevance and researcher-policy interaction hypotheses.
- Report which rungs remain plausible after available checks.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Haunschild and Bornmann (2017) lists multiple possible explanations for low policy-document mention rates.
- The paper separates data-source limitations from the possibility that few scientific papers are relevant to policy documents.
- This structure supports treating low policy attention as a hypothesis ladder rather than a single finding.

## Caveats

- Some rungs require qualitative policy-document reading or provider-internal data.
- Explanations can coexist; source bias and low true relevance are not mutually exclusive.
- The ladder is a diagnostic representation, not a causal identification strategy by itself.
