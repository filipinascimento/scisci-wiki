# Early-late citation-attention crossover

## Summary

Early-late citation-attention crossover represents citation trajectories in which newer papers receive more early attention while older cohorts retain more attention over longer horizons.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper cohort, annual citation trajectory, normalized citation curve, or field.
- Typical representation: cohort trajectory panel, early attention advantage, long-run attention reversal, or crossover curve.
- Representation target: show that citation attention is not uniformly higher for newer or older papers across all ages.
- Empirical signature: recent cohorts dominate in the first few post-publication years, but older cohorts can dominate at later ages.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a trajectory-shape motif to [peak-normalized citation trajectory](peak_normalized_citation_trajectory.md).
- Connects [citation aging and obsolescence](../mechanisms/citation_aging_obsolescence.md) with [research-front citation share](../measures/research_front_citation_share.md).
- Helps distinguish early visibility acceleration from long-run attention persistence.
- Supports field-comparative analysis of how discovery systems and publication growth reshape citation lifetimes.

## Operationalization

- Group papers by publication cohort and field.
- Normalize annual citation trajectories if comparing shapes rather than volumes.
- Plot citation attention by paper age and cohort.
- Identify ages where newer cohorts exceed older cohorts and ages where the ordering reverses.
- Interpret crossover timing with peak year, half-life, and publication-growth measures.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Parolo et al. (2015) states that citation trajectories for papers published in different years show systematic changes over time.
- The paper reports that new papers have higher citation rates for the first few years, whereas older papers have higher citation rates over longer periods.
- Parolo et al. note that irregularity in the tail can arise from heterogeneity in time to peak attention.

## Caveats

- Crossover shape depends on normalization and citation-window length.
- Right censoring can make recent cohorts appear to have weaker long-run attention.
- Cohort-level curves can hide topic-specific and paper-specific heterogeneity.
