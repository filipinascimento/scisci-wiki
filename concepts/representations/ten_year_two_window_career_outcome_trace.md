# Ten-year two-window career-outcome trace

## Summary

Ten-year two-window career-outcome trace represents post-treatment career outcomes in early and late five-year windows plus a full ten-year window.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, treatment cohort, post-event year, publication outcome, or citation outcome.
- Typical representation: outcome panels for years 1-5, 6-10, and 1-10.
- Representation target: distinguish persistent career effects from short-run artifacts.
- Empirical signature: the same outcome is reported for early, late, and cumulative windows.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [citation window selection](../methods/citation_window_selection.md) into career-event studies.
- Helps separate immediate funding or publication lags from longer-run career dynamics.
- Connects [scientific career impact](../mechanisms/scientific_career_impact.md) to time-window design.

## Operationalization

- Define an event year or treatment year.
- Compute outcomes for years 1-5, 6-10, and 1-10 after the event.
- Plot or tabulate each window for treated and comparison groups.
- Check whether conclusions depend on the chosen window.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Jones, and Wang (2019) reports publication and impact outcomes across 1-5, 6-10, and 1-10 year windows.
- The two-window representation shows that the near-miss advantage is not only a short-run fluctuation.
- It also helps separate funding dynamics from later career performance.

## Caveats

- Ten-year windows exclude very long-run career effects.
- Late-window outcomes can be conditioned on survival in the scientific system.
- Citation aging and field differences still require normalization.
