# Societal-impact institutional foundations

## Summary

Societal impact can be decomposed into epistemological, artefactual, and interactional-institutional foundations.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: impact trace, organization, artifact, partnership, knowledge claim, or assessment case.
- Typical representation: multi-label foundation categories for external impact evidence.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: which foundation of societal impact an indicator can observe.
- Empirical signature: impact evidence traces cluster by understanding, artifacts, or institutional interaction rather than one generic impact channel..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [target-oriented impact metrics](../measures/target_oriented_impact_metrics.md) with foundation labels.
- Useful for connecting policy mentions to broader societal-impact evidence.
- Links impact indicators to [research-object context graphs](research_object_context_graphs.md).

## Operationalization

- Classify impact traces by whether they reflect understanding, artifacts/instruments, or partnerships/organizations.
- Allow multi-label coding and document ambiguous cases.
- Compare which foundations are visible to policy mentions, patents, guidelines, or case studies.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Bornmann et al. (2016) names epistemological, artefactual, and interactional-institutional foundations as targets for additional impact indicators.
- This supports a representation that decomposes societal impact into foundations.

## Caveats

- One trace can serve multiple foundations, so coding needs multi-label rules.
- Some foundations are difficult to observe quantitatively.
