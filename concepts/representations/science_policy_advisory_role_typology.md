# Science-policy advisory role typology

## Summary

Science-policy interaction can be represented by advisory roles, not just mention counts.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: researcher, policy context, advisory interaction, citation function, or policy document.
- Typical representation: role classes such as pure scientist, science arbiter, issue advocate, and honest broker.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: types of researcher-policy interaction behind policy evidence traces.
- Empirical signature: policy mentions or acknowledgments can be interpreted differently depending on advisory role evidence..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds role-level interpretation to [policy-mention role reduction](../validations/policy_mention_role_reduction.md).
- Useful for connecting mentions to advisory participation and policy context.
- Complements [policy-author motivation surveys](../validations/policy_author_motivation_surveys.md).

## Operationalization

- Code policy contexts, advisory participation, acknowledgments, committee roles, and citation functions into role classes.
- Use full-text and external participation records where available.
- Keep role labels multi-label when one actor has several advisory functions.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Bornmann et al. (2016) summarizes Weiss and Pielke role typologies, including pure scientist, science arbiter, issue advocate, and honest broker.
- The discussion motivates role representation beyond raw mention counts.

## Caveats

- Roles are rarely identifiable from policy-reference metadata alone.
- Public advisory roles may not capture informal consultation or co-production.
