# Credit diversion sink typology

## Summary

Credit diversion sink typology classifies the works that receive explicit citations when foundational papers are mentioned but not cited.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: hidden citation, alternative cited work, foundational paper, topic, or citation path.
- Typical representation: sink categories such as reviews, books, applications, extensions, or related works.
- Measurement target: where explicit credit goes when foundational credit is hidden.
- Empirical signature: hidden-credit contexts often cite secondary or derivative works instead of the original foundational paper.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds recipient types to [citation diversion hierarchy](../mechanisms/citation_diversion_hierarchy.md).
- Complements path counts such as [hidden-citation indirect-path share](../measures/hidden_citation_indirect_path_share.md).
- Supports interpretation of [obliteration by incorporation](../mechanisms/obliteration_by_incorporation.md).
- Helps distinguish pure uncited use from redirected citation credit.

## Operationalization

- For papers with hidden citations, list frequently co-cited works.
- Determine whether alternative cited works are reviews, books, applications, extensions, datasets, tools, or other topic descendants.
- Compare sink types across topics and fields.
- Keep type labels separate from citation-path length.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Meng, Varol, and Barabasi (2024) asks whether hidden citations are pure untracked credit or diverted credit.
- The paper identifies frequently co-cited alternatives and gives examples where credit shifts to reviews, books, applications, or extensions.
- This typology clarifies the recipient side of hidden-credit mechanisms.

## Caveats

- Sink labels often require domain interpretation.
- A review or book may deserve citation for synthesis even if foundational credit is also missing.
- Sink typology depends on the citation context and selected foundational-paper set.
