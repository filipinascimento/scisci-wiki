# Nominal-Significance Finding Boundary

## Summary

Nominal-significance finding boundary represents the convention that a relationship becomes a reported research finding once it crosses a formal significance threshold, often `p < 0.05`.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: study, hypothesis test, claim, relationship, or published finding.
- Typical representation: binary positive-finding boundary based on formal statistical significance.
- Representation target: the claim-making convention that turns a test result into a finding.
- Empirical signature: papers treat threshold-crossing results as positive discoveries even when broader evidence remains weak.

## Operationalization

- Identify relationships labeled as findings because they meet a formal significance threshold.
- Compare threshold-positive claims with effect sizes, priors, power, and replication evidence.
- Track how binary finding labels shape abstracts, press releases, and meta-analytic inclusion.
- Separate nominal significance from evidential strength.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ioannidis (2005) criticizes claiming conclusive research findings from a single formally significant study.
- The paper notes the widespread practice of interpreting medical research articles primarily through p-values.
- Ioannidis defines research findings in the model as relationships reaching formal statistical significance.

## Caveats

- Not all fields use the same significance threshold or claim conventions.
- A nominally significant result can be useful as exploratory evidence without being conclusive.
- The motif describes a representation boundary, not a recommendation to rely on it.
