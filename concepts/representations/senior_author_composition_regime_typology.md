# Senior-author venue-composition regimes

## Summary

Journals can be grouped by trajectories of new, chaperoned, and established senior-author shares, such as balanced, incumbent-dominated, or access-tightening regimes.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: journal-year senior-author composition vector.
- Typical representation: clustered trajectories of new, chaperoned, and established senior-author shares.
- Representation target: venue regimes in authorship access and experience composition.
- Empirical signature: journals follow distinct temporal patterns in senior-author category shares.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Summarizes repeated [venue PI experience categories](../measures/venue_pi_experience_categories.md) over time.
- Useful for comparing venues and fields without reducing them to a single chaperone-effect ratio.
- Connects to [established PI share trajectory](../measures/established_pi_share_trajectory.md) and access tightening in high-impact journals.

## Operationalization

- Construct journal-year vectors for new, chaperoned, and established senior-author shares.
- Cluster vectors or trajectories by levels, slopes, and volatility.
- Label regimes descriptively and validate with null models or field-stratified panels.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sekara et al. (2018) contrasts journals such as NEJM, Physical Review D, and Nature in their category shares and temporal patterns.
- These differences support representing journals as composition regimes rather than a single universal access pattern.

## Caveats

- Regime labels are descriptive and should not be treated as causal explanations.
- Journal panels and field boundaries can shift observed regimes.
