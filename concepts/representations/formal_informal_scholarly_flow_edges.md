# Formal-informal scholarly flow edges

## Summary

Formal-informal scholarly flow edges represent science as a network in which papers, books, patents, software, people, ideas, tools, practices, and samples are connected by both documented and informal flows.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: artifact, scientist, institution, idea, tool, practice, sample, or flow edge.
- Typical representation: multiplex scholarly graph, research-object context graph, or multiscale science network.
- Representation target: include both formal traces and informal exchanges in the conceptual model of science.
- Empirical signature: formal records explain only part of the observed movement of information, materials, practices, and ideas.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [science as multiscale network](science_as_multiscale_network.md).
- Provides edge semantics for [multiplex scholarly graphs](multiplex_scholarly_graphs.md).
- Extends [research-object context graphs](research_object_context_graphs.md) beyond publication links.
- Clarifies why publication-only datasets can miss key channels of discovery and diffusion.

## Operationalization

- Distinguish formal edges such as citations, coauthorships, patents, and grants from informal edges such as conversations, tool sharing, samples, and tacit practice transfer.
- Record which edge types are observed directly and which are inferred.
- Use separate layers when formal and informal flows have different provenance or reliability.
- Treat missing informal edges as a data-quality caveat in causal and policy claims.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) describes science as a dynamic system connecting social structures, knowledge representations, and the natural world.
- The review says scientific knowledge is embodied in papers, books, patents, software, and other artifacts.
- It further states that social, conceptual, and material elements are connected through formal and informal flows of information, ideas, practices, tools, and samples.

## Caveats

- Informal flows are difficult to observe and can create hidden confounding.
- Formal traces may be easier to scale but can overrepresent published and institutionally legible work.
- Edge semantics should be documented before comparing networks across fields.
