# Institutional forecast decision contexts

## Summary

Science forecasts should be mapped to concrete decisions made by scientists, publishers, funders, hiring committees, and public-resource stewards.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: forecast, decision actor, decision object, intervention, or accountability horizon.
- Typical representation: actor-decision matrix for science-of-science prediction use.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: institutional context in which predictions are used.
- Empirical signature: the same predictive model has different consequences when used for topic choice, peer review, funding, hiring, or public-resource allocation..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [SciSci prediction domain map](../methods/scisci_prediction_domain_map.md) toward users and decisions.
- Pairs with [informal science forecast formalization](../methods/informal_science_forecast_formalization.md).
- Helps scope [automated predictive-evaluation safeguards](../validations/automated_predictive_evaluation_safeguards.md).

## Operationalization

- Annotate forecasts by decision actor, object, intervention, and accountability horizon.
- Separate descriptive prediction from decisions that allocate resources or status.
- Record which party bears false-positive and false-negative costs.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Clauset et al. (2017) lists topic choice, manuscript and proposal evaluation, faculty hiring, and public funding efficiency as distinct prediction settings.

## Caveats

- This overlaps with broad domain mapping but is narrower on who uses the prediction and what decision it supports.
- Decision context may change after a model is deployed.
