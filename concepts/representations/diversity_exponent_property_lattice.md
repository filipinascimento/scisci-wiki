# Diversity-exponent property lattice

## Summary

Diversity measures can be organized as a lattice of formulas that emphasize variety, balance, disparity, and exponent-like sensitivity in different ways.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: diversity formula, property, exponent, category distribution, or disparity relation.
- Typical representation: measure-family map relating diversity properties to formula parameters.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: structured comparison of diversity indices.
- Empirical signature: indices cluster by which diversity properties they emphasize and how strongly they weight common versus rare categories.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects diversity measure representation to [diversity measures](../measures/diversity_measures.md) and [rao stirling diversity](../measures/rao_stirling_diversity.md).
- Provides a reusable motif for comparing [diversity aggregation weight sensitivity](../validations/diversity_aggregation_weight_sensitivity.md) with adjacent science-of-science constructs.
- Should be cross-referenced when interpreting [category variety](../measures/category_variety.md) in linked scholarly data.

## Operationalization

- Map each candidate diversity index to the properties it includes: variety, balance, disparity, and sensitivity exponent.
- Compare whether two formulas differ only by parameter choice or by included property.
- Use the lattice to select measures matched to the science-policy question.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Stirling (2007) decomposes diversity into variety, balance, and disparity and discusses general forms for combining them.
- This supports a representation that organizes measure families by properties rather than treating each index as isolated.

## Caveats

- A property lattice simplifies mathematical differences and may hide implementation details.
- Formula families should still be tested on the actual category and distance data.
