# Regional elite remote-partnership maps

## Summary

Regional elite remote-partnership maps represent how institutions in a local research region increasingly connect to distant high-status partners, revealing status and geography together.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: focal region, university, institution pair, distance band, tier pair, or time period.
- Typical representation: regional map with highlighted elite institutions, local ties, remote ties, and changing collaboration shares.
- Representation target: whether collaboration changes are organized by geography, status, or their interaction.
- Empirical signature: top institutions in a region increase ties to geographically distant top institutions while other partnership types decline or grow more slowly.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Gives a spatial representation for [geographic reach-frequency decoupling](../mechanisms/geographic_reach_frequency_decoupling.md).
- Connects mapped collaboration geography to [status-assortative collaboration](../mechanisms/status_assortative_collaboration.md) and [elite-access stratification trend](../mechanisms/elite_access_stratification_trend.md).
- Complements large-scale [world city collaboration network](world_city_collaboration_network.md) views with institution-tier detail.
- Helps distinguish "nearby partner" mechanisms from "who the partner is" mechanisms in multi-university collaboration.

## Operationalization

- Choose focal research regions and map universities with their tier or prestige category.
- Link institution pairs by collaboration counts, changes in collaboration share, or observed-to-expected ratios.
- Separate local-region ties from remote ties and cross-tabulate them by status tier.
- Compare early and late periods to show whether remote elite partnerships grow while lower-tier or local-only ties decline.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Jones et al. (2008) describes supplemental regional maps for Boston, Chicago, Los Angeles, New York City, North Carolina, and San Francisco research centers.
- The paper reports that top schools increasingly collaborate with geographically distant top schools while other partnerships decline.
- Jones et al. interpret these maps as evidence that partner choice increasingly depends on who collaborators are rather than where they are.
- The maps complement the paper's national tier-mixing tables by making the status-geography interaction visible in specific regions.

## Caveats

- Regional maps are illustrative unless paired with formal uncertainty or null-model comparisons.
- Visual salience can overemphasize elite institutions and hide smaller but numerous lower-tier collaborations.
- Region definitions, campus geography, and distance thresholds affect interpretation.
- Map-based evidence should be linked back to tabular counts and field-normalized models.
