# Key-paper concept proxy

## Summary

Key-paper concept proxy is the practice of using frequently cited papers as observable stand-ins for the key concepts, methods, experiments, or ideas that organize a field.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: highly cited paper, cited-reference set, method paper, experimental report, theory paper, or specialty literature.
- Typical representation: ranked key-paper list, cited-paper network, co-citation map, or annotated reference core.
- Mechanism or measurement target: papers as measurable carriers of otherwise latent scientific ideas.
- Empirical signature: papers with high citation attention function as entry points for reconstructing a field's conceptual and methodological structure.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides the representation assumption behind document-level [co-citation](co_citation.md) and [specialty co-citation mapping](../methods/specialty_co_citation_mapping.md).
- Connects citation networks to intellectual histories by treating documents as proxies for ideas rather than only as publication events.
- Gives a practical sampling rule for building corpora around a research front when topic labels are incomplete or unstable.
- Supports [co-citation cycling seed expansion](../methods/co_citation_cycling_seed_expansion.md), where a highly cited paper is used as an entry point into a candidate specialty core.
- Helps bridge older citation-index workflows with later [science maps](science_maps.md), embeddings, and topic models.

## Operationalization

- Select a bounded field, specialty, or seed paper.
- Identify frequently cited candidate papers in the relevant citation index or full-reference corpus.
- Treat these papers as candidate proxies for key concepts, methods, or experiments.
- Validate the proxy interpretation through titles, abstracts, citation contexts, expert reading, and links to adjacent [co-cited core literature](co_cited_core_literature.md).
- Record [co-citation core sampling boundary](../validations/co_citation_core_sampling_boundary.md) assumptions when the proxy set is generated by one seed or one citation-index snapshot.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Small (1973) explicitly motivates co-citation mapping by assuming that frequently cited papers can represent the key concepts, methods, or experiments in a field.
- Small then uses co-citation patterns among these key papers to map relationships among the underlying ideas.
- Small's particle-physics example starts from a highly cited Gell-Mann paper and promotes frequently cited bibliography items into the candidate core, making the proxy role explicit.
- This supports a representation-level motif: the paper is not the concept itself, but it is a measurable trace that can anchor concept mapping when concepts are hard to index directly.

## Caveats

- Frequently cited papers can proxy methods, tools, disputes, reviews, or convenient citations rather than the precise concept of interest.
- The proxy can miss tacit knowledge, negative results, teaching influence, and uncited conceptual inheritance.
- Citation volume, database coverage, and language boundaries affect which papers become visible as "key" papers.
