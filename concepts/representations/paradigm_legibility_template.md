# Paradigm legibility template

## Summary

Paradigm legibility template is the representation of a scientific paradigm as a shared template that makes some questions, methods, evidence forms, and answers easier for evaluators to recognize as valid.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paradigm, evaluator community, proposal, method, question type, or evidence form.
- Typical representation: template of acceptable problem framings and solution formats.
- Representation target: explain why work near a field's established paradigm can be easier to judge than work that crosses or extends the frontier.
- Empirical signature: evaluator distance and novelty effects arise partly because proposals depart from familiar templates.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects [knowledge-frontier envelope](knowledge_frontier_envelope.md) to evaluator cognition.
- Distinguishes paradigm legibility from [paradigm-shift knowledge simplification](../mechanisms/paradigm_shift_knowledge_simplification.md), which is a historical simplification mechanism.
- Helps interpret [reviewer-proposal intellectual distance](../measures/reviewer_proposal_intellectual_distance.md) as a legibility problem rather than only a lack of technical expertise.
- Gives novelty penalties a representational layer: the issue may be whether evaluators can place the work inside a recognizable template.

## Operationalization

- Define the focal field's dominant questions, methods, vocabulary, and evidence norms before evaluation.
- Represent proposals by their distance from established terms, references, methods, or embeddings.
- Compare evaluation scores for proposals that fit versus stretch the template, controlling for proposal and evaluator fixed effects when possible.
- Use qualitative coding or controlled vocabularies to separate template departure from low quality.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Boudreau, Guinan, Lakhani, and Riedl (2016) links paradigms to the accepted questions, methods, and forms of answers that organize scientific evaluation.
- Their reviewer-proposal experiment treats greater intellectual distance and novelty as conditions where evaluators must look beyond familiar frontier templates.
- The paper's results imply that evaluators can systematically undervalue ideas that are less legible within their own paradigm-adjacent expertise.

## Caveats

- Paradigms are difficult to observe directly; operational measures usually approximate them with vocabulary, citations, methods, or expert coding.
- A proposal can be illegible because it is genuinely weak, not because it is valuable and unconventional.
- Templates differ across specialties and change over time, so static definitions can overstate evaluator consensus.
