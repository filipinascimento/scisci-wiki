# Policy-source typology cooperation proxy

## Summary

A transparent typology of policy-making bodies can act as a proxy map of where researcher-policy linkage is observable.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: policy source, organization type, sector, geography, or mentioned-paper count.
- Typical representation: source-type table with organizations, body type, sector, geography, and mention totals.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: where policy-document mentions can plausibly reveal researcher-policy cooperation.
- Empirical signature: source classes differ in tracked organizations and mentioned-paper totals..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [policy source list transparency](../validations/policy_source_list_transparency.md) with source-type representation.
- Useful for interpreting [policy-source mention concentration](../measures/policy_source_mention_concentration.md).
- Connects tracked policy bodies to [Altmetric policy source tracker](../datasets/altmetric_policy_source_tracker.md).

## Operationalization

- Classify tracked policy sources by body type, geography, sector, language, and source-list origin.
- Count sources and paper mentions per class and record crawlability.
- Use the typology to stratify coverage and cooperation claims.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Bornmann et al. (2016) proposes listing source types, counts of organizations, and mentioned-paper totals to show which policy bodies may cooperate with researchers.
- This supports source typology as a proxy for observable linkage contexts.

## Caveats

- Source-type mention counts conflate cooperation, crawlability, source-list choice, and citation culture.
- Many policy interactions leave no tracked document source.
