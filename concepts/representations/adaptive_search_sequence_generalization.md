# Adaptive-search sequence generalization

## Summary

Adaptive-search sequence generalization represents explore-then-exploit career dynamics as a broader adaptive-search pattern shared with foraging, cognitive search, bandits, brokerage-closure, and organizational cycles.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: career phase, search policy, topic state, network position, learning agent, or opportunity landscape.
- Typical representation: exploration state, exploitation state, policy transition, adaptive search, or sequence motif.
- Representation target: map scientific-career phase sequences onto general search models.
- Empirical signature: a career sequence can be represented as a transition from broad sampling to focused exploitation across comparable domains.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Broadens [exploration-exploitation career shifts](../mechanisms/exploration_exploitation_career_shifts.md).
- Connects career search to [knowledge-network experiment search](../mechanisms/knowledge_network_experiment_search.md) and [scientific problem choice](../mechanisms/scientific_problem_choice.md).
- Provides a representation for comparing career dynamics with non-scientific creative and organizational systems.

## Operationalization

- Encode career output sequences as search states and phase transitions.
- Compare entropy-based phase labels with bandit, foraging, brokerage-closure, or reinforcement-learning representations.
- Test whether transition timing predicts impact across domains.
- Use common state-space language for papers, artworks, films, patents, or projects.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Liu et al. (2021) explicitly relates explore-then-exploit hot-streak onset to broader adaptive-search systems including foraging, cognitive search, bandits, brokerage-closure dynamics, and business cycles.
- The paper's empirical analysis provides career-domain evidence for the sequence but treats the broader mapping as an interpretive generalization.
- This motif preserves the representation bridge for cross-domain modeling.

## Caveats

- General search analogies do not establish the same mechanisms across systems.
- Entropy-based career phases may not correspond to intentions or decision policies.
- Different domains have different opportunity landscapes and feedback delays.
