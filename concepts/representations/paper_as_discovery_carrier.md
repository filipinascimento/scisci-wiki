# Paper as discovery carrier

## Summary

Paper as discovery carrier is the representation of the individual paper as the minimal observable unit through which a scientific discovery enters citation, recognition, and impact dynamics.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, discovery claim, article cohort, citation trajectory, or recognition event.
- Typical representation: paper-level carrier, minimal discovery unit, or paper-centered impact history.
- Representation target: make discoveries measurable through published paper traces.
- Empirical signature: citation models fit individual paper histories rather than only aggregate journal or field distributions.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Grounds [citation trajectory models](../methods/citation_trajectory_models.md).
- Links paper-level discovery carriers to [paper fitness](../measures/paper_fitness.md).
- Provides the object encoded by [citation-parameter vector representation](citation_parameter_vector_representation.md).
- Separates paper-level analysis from author-level or journal-level indicators.

## Operationalization

- Treat each paper as the focal carrier of a discovery or contribution.
- Attach citation histories, metadata, fields, and venues to that paper.
- Fit paper-level models before aggregating to authors, journals, or fields.
- Preserve uncertainty when the same discovery is spread across several papers.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang et al. (2013) describes papers as minimal carriers of scientific discoveries.
- The paper moves from aggregate citation distributions to the temporal evolution of individual papers.
- It fits individual cumulative citation histories to recover paper-level impact parameters.

## Caveats

- Some discoveries are distributed across teams, instruments, datasets, or sequences of papers.
- Papers can contain multiple contributions with different recognition trajectories.
- Citation attention to a paper can represent method reuse, review function, controversy, or social visibility.
