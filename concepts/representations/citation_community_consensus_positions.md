# Citation-Community Consensus Positions

## Summary

Citation-community consensus positions represent clusters of mutually citing papers as epistemic camps that may share claims, positions, or specialized subtopics.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: citation community, paper cluster, author group, issue area, or subtopic.
- Typical representation: community detection on a citation network with semantic or issue labels.
- Representation target: the interpretive position of a citation cluster, not only its graph topology.
- Empirical signature: densely citing communities align with author groups holding common issue positions or working on a specialized subtopic.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds an interpretive layer to [citation networks](citation_networks.md).
- Connects [science maps](science_maps.md) and [co-citation](co_citation.md) to epistemic positions.
- Helps identify [field emergence](../mechanisms/field_emergence.md) and [research fronts](../mechanisms/research_fronts.md).
- Fits within [science as a multiscale network](science_as_multiscale_network.md).

## Operationalization

- Detect communities in direct citation, bibliographic coupling, or co-citation graphs.
- Label communities using title/abstract phrases, cited sources, or expert coding.
- Compare community labels with known scientific controversies, subtopics, or consensus positions.
- Track whether communities converge, split, or reinforce over time.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) states that citation-network methods identify communities of frequently citing publications.
- The review notes that such communities can correspond to author groups sharing positions on issues.
- It also describes communities as groups working on specialized subtopics.

## Caveats

- Citation communities can reflect methods, data, institutions, or journals rather than substantive positions.
- Community detection choices affect boundaries and labels.
- A cluster's consensus position should be validated with text, expert history, or citation-context evidence.
