# Early-career excellence strata

## Summary

Early-career excellence strata represent junior researchers by whether they are already high-performing on early institutional prestige, productivity, and citation dimensions.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: junior researcher, discipline-year cohort, early-career indicator, or mutually exclusive excellence group.
- Typical representation: binary membership across institutional prestige, productivity, and citation top-decile indicators.
- Representation target: early status and performance context before a later career exposure or outcome is evaluated.
- Empirical signature: researchers are assigned to strata such as none, I, P, C, IP, IC, PC, or IPC.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Separates top-coauthor effects for researchers who already have strong early performance signals from those who do not.
- Supports heterogeneous-effect analysis for [early-career sponsorship by top scientists](../mechanisms/early_career_sponsorship.md).
- Provides a compact representation for matching, regression controls, and policy interpretation.
- Links early institutional, productivity, and citation advantages to later [scientific career impact dynamics](../mechanisms/scientific_career_impact.md).

## Operationalization

- Define an early-career window, such as the first three career years.
- Compute institutional prestige, number of papers, and early citations for each junior researcher.
- Within each discipline and career-start year, mark whether the researcher is in the top 10% for each indicator.
- Assign each researcher to one of eight mutually exclusive strata: none, I, P, C, IP, IC, PC, or IPC.
- Compare later outcomes within strata by treatment exposure, such as early top-coauthor collaboration.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Li, Aste, Caccioli, and Livan (2019) splits junior researchers into eight groups based on top-decile status for institutional prestige, productivity, and citations in the first three career years.
- The paper compares authors against peers in the same discipline and career-start year.
- Li et al. report that early top-coauthor exposure matters most in lower early-excellence strata, especially for researchers who are not top decile on any of the three dimensions.
- For the no-top-decile group, early top-coauthor exposure increases the probability of becoming a top scientist from 15.7% to 27.2%.

## Caveats

- Top-decile thresholds are cohort-relative and may be unstable in small discipline-year cells.
- Early citations and productivity can reflect field tempo, database coverage, and lab/team norms.
- Strata are descriptive unless paired with a credible comparison design.
