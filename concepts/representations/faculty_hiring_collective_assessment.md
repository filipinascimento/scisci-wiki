# Faculty hiring collective assessment

## Summary

Faculty hiring collective assessment treats observed faculty placements as accumulated pairwise judgments made by departments about doctoral training, research promise, and institutional fit.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: faculty placement, doctoral institution, employing department, field, or hiring cohort.
- Typical representation: directed hiring edge interpreted as a revealed assessment from an employing unit to a training unit.
- Mechanism or measurement target: decentralized expert evaluation embedded in labor-market flows.
- Empirical signature: repeated placements from some doctoral origins to many employing departments, producing a consensus-like prestige signal.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides edge semantics for [faculty hiring networks](faculty_hiring_networks.md).
- Supports [faculty prestige-rank inference](../methods/faculty_prestige_rank_inference.md) by making placement direction an evaluation trace rather than only a mobility record.
- Helps interpret [institutional prestige hierarchy](../mechanisms/institutional_prestige_hierarchy.md) as an emergent collective-assessment structure.
- Connects labor-market outcomes to broader questions about [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md).

## Operationalization

- Build directed edges from doctoral institution to employing institution.
- Interpret the presence and volume of these edges as aggregate evidence that employing departments accepted the doctoral origin as a credible producer of faculty.
- Separate this interpretation from individual-level hiring causes such as topic fit, advisor reputation, geography, and candidate quality.
- Compare placement-derived rankings with rankings based on inputs, resources, reputation surveys, or citation indicators.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Clauset et al. (2015) frames faculty hiring as an extensive set of institutional judgments about faculty candidates and their doctoral training environments.
- The same paper argues that placement outcomes can reveal collective assessments of educational and research quality because hiring decisions are costly, public, and repeated across departments.
- The concept is validated indirectly when placement-derived prestige rankings align with external rank systems while also exposing hierarchy and inequality that input-based rankings can obscure.

## Caveats

- Hiring decisions are not pure assessments of institution quality; they also reflect networks, resources, subfield demand, geography, and discrimination.
- The edge interpretation is strongest in relatively closed doctoral ecosystems with broad coverage of both training and employing units.
- Treating placements as assessment traces can reproduce prestige bias if used normatively.
