# Topic-labeled coauthorship subcommunities

## Summary

Topic-labeled coauthorship subcommunities are partitions of a coauthorship graph interpreted against research topics, invisible colleges, or other intellectual groupings.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author, coauthorship edge, community, subfield, topic, or invisible college.
- Typical representation: community-colored coauthorship graph, topic-labeled cluster, or social-intellectual overlay.
- Representation target: connect collaboration structure to topical or intellectual organization.
- Empirical signature: network subcommunities correspond roughly to research topics or specialty groups.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [coauthorship networks](coauthorship_networks.md) from graph structure to interpreted community maps.
- Links to [invisible college](../mechanisms/invisible_college.md).
- Connects collaboration partitions to [science maps](science_maps.md).
- Provides a qualitative check on community-detection results.

## Operationalization

- Build a coauthorship network and detect communities or subcommunities.
- Assign topic labels using papers, keywords, affiliations, or expert interpretation.
- Compare social communities with citation, semantic, or field classifications.
- Report uncertainty when communities only approximately match topics.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Newman (2004) shows a small coauthorship network divided into subcommunities.
- The figure caption states that the subcommunities correspond roughly to topics of research.
- The conclusion suggests future tests for community structure or invisible colleges within coauthorship networks.

## Caveats

- Network communities can reflect institutions, methods, or data sources rather than topics.
- Topic labels can be subjective or unstable over time.
- Large multi-topic authors can bridge several communities and complicate labeling.
