# International policy-goal portfolio

## Summary

International policy-goal portfolio represents international collaboration policy as a bundle of political, developmental, security, capacity-building, and epistemic goals rather than a single collaboration-growth objective.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: policy program, collaboration goal, funding instrument, country, NGO, or international research network.
- Typical representation: portfolio of policy goals mapped to collaboration instruments.
- Representation target: keep distinct motivations for international collaboration visible in analysis and evaluation.
- Empirical signature: the same collaboration program is justified by multiple goals such as cost sharing, capacity building, goodwill, development, security, and large-scale research access.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [collaboration regime policy leverage](../methods/collaboration_regime_policy_leverage.md).
- Connects policy design to [international collaboration networks](international_collaboration_networks.md).
- Helps evaluate [constructed collaboration sustainability limit](../validations/constructed_collaboration_sustainability_limit.md) by asking which policy goal a constructed tie is meant to serve.
- Pairs with [permissive collaboration funding signal](../mechanisms/permissive_collaboration_funding_signal.md) when policy operates indirectly.

## Operationalization

- Code policy texts, program calls, and funding rules for stated goals.
- Separate political, developmental, security, capacity-building, and epistemic objectives.
- Link each goal to the intended collaboration mechanism and expected outcome.
- Avoid evaluating all programs only by international coauthorship counts.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wagner and Leydesdorff (2005) lists policy goals for international collaboration including cost savings, security, capacity building, goodwill, regional stability, development, and large-scale research.
- The paper also describes NGO and European Commission goals such as poverty alleviation, agricultural productivity, security, and intra-European networks.
- Wagner and Leydesdorff warn that older national or institution-bound policy models may miss opportunities in a global collaboration network.

## Caveats

- Policy goals can conflict; more collaboration is not always the correct outcome for every goal.
- Stated goals may differ from operational incentives.
- Portfolio coding requires program documents beyond coauthorship data.
