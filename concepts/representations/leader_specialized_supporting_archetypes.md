# Leader-specialized-supporting archetypes

## Summary

Leader-specialized-supporting archetypes represent scientists as career-stage role profiles that emphasize broad leadership contributions, specialized technical or analytic work, or lower-prevalence support roles.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, career stage, contribution-role vector, archetype label, or archetype mixture.
- Typical representation: categorical archetype assignment or mixture weights over leader, specialized, and supporting profiles.
- Representation target: division of scientific labor across careers.
- Empirical signature: archetype availability and prevalence vary by career stage, gender, productivity, and citation impact.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a compact representation for [task specialization](../mechanisms/task_specialization.md).
- Gives semantic labels to [robust archetypal contribution profiles](../methods/robust_archetypal_contribution_profiles.md).
- Links career-level roles to team-level hierarchy concepts such as [lead-role ratio](../measures/lead_role_ratio.md) and [team hierarchy and flatness](../mechanisms/team_hierarchy_flatness.md).
- Supports evaluation work under [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) by separating labor profiles from symmetric coauthorship counts.
- Feeds mechanism pages for [gendered contribution-archetype gap](../mechanisms/gendered_contribution_archetype_gap.md) and [supporting-archetype career attrition](../mechanisms/supporting_archetype_career_attrition.md).

## Operationalization

- Fit career-stage archetypes on contribution-role vectors or imputed contribution probabilities.
- Label high writing and conception/design profiles as leader, high experiment and analysis profiles as specialized, and generally low contribution-prevalence profiles as supporting.
- Allow the available archetype set to vary by career stage rather than forcing the same number of profiles everywhere.
- Analyze archetype prevalence by gender, field, career stage, productivity, and citation-impact outcomes.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Robinson-Garcia et al. (2020) identifies three archetypes from contribution data: Leader, Specialized, and Supporting.
- The Leader archetype has high coefficient values for most contributions except performing experiments, especially writing and conception/design.
- The Specialized archetype is characterized by high values for performing experiments and analyzing data, with a shift from experiments toward analysis across career stages.
- The Supporting archetype has generally low values for all contributorships and is described as the least discriminatory archetype.
- Robinson-Garcia et al. report two archetypes at junior stage, three at early- and mid-career stages, and two at late-career stage.
- The full text also reports gender differences in leader/specialized/supporting prevalence and career-stage progression differences by archetype, which are split into mechanism pages rather than treated as representation definitions.

## Caveats

- Supporting profiles may include hidden labor not captured by formal contribution statements.
- Archetype labels can be normative if interpreted as ranks rather than role profiles.
- Fields with different contribution vocabularies may produce different archetype structures.
