# Career-inequality linked evidence panel

## Summary

Career-inequality linked evidence panel is a data substrate for explaining inequality mechanisms by joining publication records, collaboration histories, grant applications, awards, hiring outcomes, and demographic or career-stage information.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, career stage, publication, grant, award, collaboration, hiring decision, or institution.
- Typical representation: linked longitudinal career panel with multiple evidence layers.
- Data target: distinguish productivity, collaboration, funding, hiring, and bias mechanisms in scientific inequality.
- Empirical signature: inequality patterns can be decomposed across outputs, opportunities, evaluations, and career transitions.

## Operationalization

- Link publication records, grant applications, awards, hiring data, affiliations, and collaboration histories at the researcher level.
- Preserve dates so career-stage, cohort, and sequence effects can be separated.
- Record demographic inference uncertainty and missing-data mechanisms.
- Compare observational patterns with experimental or quasi-experimental evidence where available.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) summarizes gender gaps in publications, collaborators, funding, and hiring.
- The review contrasts productivity and career-length explanations with experimental evidence of early-career bias.
- It argues that large-scale career datasets joining publication records, grant applications, and awards can clarify causes of inequality and motivate policy solutions.

## Caveats

- Linked panels can amplify privacy, demographic-inference, and selection-bias risks.
- Publication, grant, and award data are all conditioned on prior opportunity structures.
- Small-sample experimental evidence and large-scale observational evidence answer different questions and should not be conflated.
