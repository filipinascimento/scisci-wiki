# Multi-axis intersectional inequality panel

## Summary

Multi-axis intersectional inequality panel is an expanded data design that joins race, gender, socioeconomic status, disability, sexual orientation, attrition, career age, and institutional prestige.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author, paper, topic, institution, career event, or identity axis.
- Typical representation: enriched panel extending race-gender-topic data with additional identity and career dimensions.
- Dataset target: enable intersectional analyses beyond the limited demographic axes visible in bibliometric metadata.
- Empirical signature: multiple identity and career-status axes can be analyzed jointly with topics and impact.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [intersectional topic stratification](../mechanisms/intersectional_topic_stratification.md).
- Links bibliometric inequality panels to [faculty retention and attrition](../mechanisms/faculty_retention_attrition.md).
- Adds institutional context through [institutional prestige hierarchy](../mechanisms/institutional_prestige_hierarchy.md).
- Supports [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) by making missing axes visible.

## Operationalization

- Start from a paper-author-topic panel.
- Add career age, institution, prestige, attrition, and field histories.
- Include additional identity variables only when collected ethically and with appropriate governance.
- Record which axes are self-identified, inferred, missing, or not observable.
- Use the panel to test intersectional mechanisms, not only descriptive gaps.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Kozlowski et al. (2022) calls for future analyses adding dimensions such as socioeconomic status, disability, sexual orientation, career age, attrition, and institutional prestige.
- The paper notes that race-gender-topic data are only a partial view of intersectional inequality.
- This dataset motif captures the proposed next data layer for more complete mechanism tests.

## Caveats

- Additional identity data can raise privacy and governance risks.
- Many axes cannot be inferred responsibly from names or publications.
- Richer panels can still miss unobserved institutional mechanisms.
