# Promotion-tenure guideline baseline corpus

## Summary

Promotion-tenure guideline baseline corpus is a collection of public institutional assessment guidelines used to track criteria, compare reforms, and study diffusion of responsible assessment practices.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: promotion guideline, tenure policy, hiring rubric, department criteria document, institution, or revision date.
- Typical representation: document corpus with institution, department, policy date, criteria fields, and assessment indicators.
- Data target: observe what institutions publicly ask committees and candidates to value.
- Empirical signature: assessment reform is measured against a baseline of existing public criteria.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [research assessment proposal corpus](research_assessment_proposal_corpus.md) from reform proposals to institutional policy documents.
- Supports [transparent assessment-criteria publication](../methods/transparent_assessment_criteria_publication.md) and [assessment audit-feedback loops](../validations/assessment_audit_feedback_loops.md).
- Provides infrastructure for evaluating [responsible researcher assessment principles](../methods/responsible_researcher_assessment_principles.md) in real institutions.
- Enables before/after studies of [assessment reform-alignment precondition](../validations/assessment_reform_alignment_precondition.md).

## Operationalization

- Collect public promotion, tenure, hiring, and annual-review criteria from institutions and departments.
- Extract document date, decision level, assessment dimensions, indicators, required evidence, and revision history.
- Code references to journal impact factor, publications, openness, data sharing, mentoring, service, team science, and societal contribution.
- Preserve source URLs and archived copies for reproducibility.
- Use the baseline to compare future reforms, diffusion, and implementation fidelity.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Moher et al. (2018) recommends collecting and sharing institutional promotion and tenure guidelines to disseminate assessment innovations.
- The paper treats public guidelines as a practical evidence base for institutions trying to revise criteria.
- This motif turns those guidelines into a reusable dataset for science-of-science analysis.

## Caveats

- Public policy documents may differ from actual committee practice.
- Some institutions or departments may not publish guidelines, creating coverage bias.
- Guidelines need versioning because criteria can change without clear public archives.
