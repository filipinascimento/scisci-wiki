# Career metadata fusion

## Summary

Career metadata fusion combines publication, grant, award, affiliation, geographic, temporal, and demographic records to reconstruct scientific career trajectories and evaluate career-stage mechanisms.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, career-year, publication, grant application, award, institution, country, move, or evaluation event.
- Typical representation: longitudinal author panel, career trajectory table, linked administrative/publication record, or event-history dataset.
- Mechanism or measurement target: how career incentives, funding, mobility, inequality, reputation, and productivity interact over time.
- Empirical signature: career trajectories can be analyzed from multiple perspectives after person records are linked across sources.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides the data layer for [scientific career impact dynamics](../mechanisms/scientific_career_impact.md), mobility, funding, inequality, and evaluation studies.
- Complements [affiliation trajectory reconstruction](../methods/affiliation_trajectory_reconstruction.md) by adding grants, awards, productivity, and other career metadata.
- Supports [gender and race stratification](../mechanisms/gender_race_stratification.md), [NIH R01 award disparities](../validations/nih_r01_award_disparities.md), and [applicant bibliometric control vectors](../methods/applicant_bibliometric_control_vector.md).
- Depends on the broader [scholarly input-output data substrate](scholarly_input_output_data_substrate.md) and source-specific disambiguation.

## Operationalization

- Link person identifiers across publications, affiliations, grant applications, awards, funding histories, and institutional records.
- Construct career time from first publication, degree, first grant, first appointment, or another documented anchor.
- Attach career-stage measures, productivity histories, collaboration histories, institutional contexts, and policy exposure.
- Validate linkage with ORCID, CVs, administrative data, manual samples, or source-provided identifiers where available.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) states that career-dynamics research requires combining large repositories of high-resolution individual, geographic, and temporal metadata to build analyzable career trajectories.
- The review connects career metadata to individual incentives, institutional incentives, competition, funding schemes, contract structures, and unintended policy consequences.
- Fortunato et al. also argue that improved large-scale career datasets should leverage publication records, grant applications, and awards to understand inequality and inform policy solutions.

## Caveats

- Career data fusion can amplify author disambiguation, affiliation disambiguation, and missing-record errors.
- Administrative and demographic variables require privacy, consent, and governance review.
- Linked career panels can overrepresent visible, published, funded, or institutionally stable scientists.
