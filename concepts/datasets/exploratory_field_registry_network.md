# Exploratory Field Registry Network

## Summary

Exploratory field registry network is a proposed field-level infrastructure that records data collections or investigator networks to recover the testing denominator in hypothesis-generating research.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, data collection, investigator group, hypothesis-generating study, or registry.
- Typical representation: registry or network map of exploratory testing activity.
- Dataset target: the denominator of attempted tests and active investigators beyond published positive findings.
- Empirical signature: field-level registration makes isolated significant findings interpretable against outside testing activity.

## Operationalization

- Register exploratory data collections, investigator consortia, protocols, or analysis families at field level.
- Link later publications to the registered field activity.
- Track null, abandoned, and exploratory analyses where possible.
- Use the registry to interpret nominally significant claims against the larger testing environment.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ioannidis (2005) says conventional trial registration is useful but difficult for hypothesis-generating research.
- The paper proposes registration or networking of data collections or investigators within fields as a more feasible alternative.
- Ioannidis connects this need to the partial interpretability of single-study significance without knowledge of outside testing.

## Caveats

- Exploratory work can be too fluid for full hypothesis-by-hypothesis registration.
- Field registries may create administrative burden or strategic underreporting.
- Registry coverage must be audited before using it as a denominator.
