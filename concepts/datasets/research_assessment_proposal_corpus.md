# Research assessment proposal corpus

## Summary

The research assessment proposal corpus is a curated evidence set of manifestos, policy reports, journal proposals, metric proposals, and institutional experiments about how scientists should be assessed.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: proposal document, manifesto, policy report, journal recommendation, metric paper, institutional assessment scheme, or expert-panel output.
- Typical representation: annotated document table, proposal taxonomy, extracted problem/solution/limitation matrix, stakeholder map, or evidence-review corpus.
- Dataset target: collect reusable source material for studying assessment reform proposals and their implementation gaps.
- Empirical signature: records include proposal origin, stakeholder target, recommended criteria, unintended consequences, implementation detail, limitations, and evaluation evidence.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides source material for [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) and [responsible researcher assessment principles](../methods/responsible_researcher_assessment_principles.md).
- Separates proposal evidence from implementation evidence in [research assessment implementation experiments](../validations/research_assessment_implementation_experiments.md).
- Stores the document-corpus layer behind [responsible indicators for assessing scientists](../measures/responsible_indicators_for_assessing_scientists.md).
- Helps compare DORA, Leiden, Metric Tide, REWARD, REF, PQRST, RCR, journal-citation-distribution, peer-review-credit, and data-sharing-credit proposals.

## Operationalization

- Define inclusion criteria for assessment-reform documents and papers.
- Extract structured fields: proposal name, year, country or scope, target stakeholders, problem statement, proposed assessment behavior, implementation mechanism, limitations, and evidence status.
- Normalize recurring proposal types: anti-JIF guidance, qualitative portfolio review, responsible-metric principles, open-science rewards, article-level metrics, contribution assessment, societal-impact assessment, and implementation experiments.
- Link each proposal to downstream concept pages and to any later validation or adoption evidence.
- Version the corpus because proposal ecosystems change as institutions sign declarations, launch dashboards, or revise promotion criteria.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Moher et al. (2018) uses a selective review of 22 key documents plus an expert-panel workshop to synthesize assessment proposals for hiring, promotion, and tenure.
- The paper states that documents were selected by experts and searched for problems, unintended consequences, proposed solutions, and limitations.
- Table 1 organizes major international, individual, journal, and quantitative-metric proposals, including DORA, the Leiden Manifesto, Metric Tide, NAS, Nuffield, REWARD, REF, UMC Utrecht, PQRST, eLife, Nature, RCR, journal citation distributions, R-index, and S-index.
- Moher et al. explicitly note that most proposals were aspirational and often lacked details on implementation barriers, how committees should apply them, or how adoption should be evaluated.
- The corpus therefore supports a distinction between assessment-reform proposals and validated assessment interventions.

## Caveats

- Moher et al.'s corpus is selective rather than systematic and is concentrated in biomedical and adjacent assessment debates.
- Proposal documents vary in genre, from manifestos to metric papers to local institutional schemes, so they should not be treated as homogeneous observations.
- The presence of a proposal does not imply adoption, compliance, or effectiveness.
- Later updates should add newer national reforms, funder policies, institutional criteria, and empirical validations.
