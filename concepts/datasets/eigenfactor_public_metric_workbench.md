# Eigenfactor public metric workbench

## Summary

Eigenfactor public metric workbench is the public bundle of rankings, algorithms, maps, visual tools, correlations, and methods documentation that made the Eigenfactor metric family inspectable.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: metric platform, journal ranking product, public algorithm bundle, or bibliometric workbench.
- Typical representation: website, downloadable ranking table, algorithm description, visualization, or science map.
- Dataset target: expose a metric family as reusable infrastructure rather than only as article tables.
- Empirical signature: users can inspect rankings, method notes, correlations, and visual interfaces around the same metric system.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a concrete case for [transparent metric construction](../methods/transparent_metric_construction.md).
- Complements [metric accessibility and component disclosure](../methods/metric_accessibility_and_component_disclosure.md).

## Operationalization

- Archive the metric outputs, algorithm notes, and public documentation together.
- Record snapshot dates and product versions when rankings are reused.
- Preserve links among tables, maps, formulas, and explanatory material.
- Treat public visualizations as part of the evidence object when they shape interpretation.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from West et al. (2010) reports that rankings, algorithms, visual tools, and maps were freely available through the Eigenfactor Project.
- The article points readers to full Journal Citation Reports rankings online.
- Its notes refer to public methods pages and correlation views, making the workbench more than a static paper supplement.

## Caveats

- Public availability can change over time, so local snapshots and dates matter.
- A public workbench can still hide implementation details, cleaning rules, or provider constraints.
- Visual tools can guide interpretation as strongly as the underlying scores.
