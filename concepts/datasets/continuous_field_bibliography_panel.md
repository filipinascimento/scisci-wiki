# Continuous field-bibliography panel

## Summary

Continuous field-bibliography panel is a long, uninterrupted field bibliography that supports longitudinal coauthorship and productivity analysis beyond a short fixed observation window.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field bibliography, author, paper, collaboration tie, publication year, or longitudinal panel.
- Typical representation: multi-decade field panel, uninterrupted bibliography, or continuous collaboration history.
- Dataset target: observe long-run changes in collaboration norms, productivity, and network growth.
- Empirical signature: field records cover many decades without major gaps, enabling comparison of short-window and long-window collaboration statistics.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [coauthorship source-domain panels](coauthorship_source_domain_panels.md).
- Complements [collaboration fixed time window](../methods/collaboration_fixed_time_window.md), which is better for cross-field comparability.
- Supports [field author publication time series](field_author_publication_time_series.md).
- Provides evidence for [disciplinary collaboration norm shift](../mechanisms/disciplinary_collaboration_norm_shift.md).

## Operationalization

- Choose a field source with stable, long-run coverage.
- Preserve publication dates, authors, and source provenance for the full period.
- Analyze long-run trends separately from fixed-window cross-field comparisons.
- Document breaks, field-boundary changes, and source maintenance rules.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Newman (2004) describes a Mathematical Reviews collaboration network covering mathematics from 1940 to the paper's contemporary period without any break.
- The paper characterizes this mathematics source as especially complete and accurate among the networks studied.
- Newman contrasts the long mathematics panel with the shorter five-year Medline and physics panels.

## Caveats

- Long coverage can improve temporal analysis while complicating comparison with short-window panels.
- Field definitions and publication practices may shift over decades.
- Older records can have different author-name and affiliation quality than newer records.
