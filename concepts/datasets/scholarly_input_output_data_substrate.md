# Scholarly input-output data substrate

## Summary

Scholarly input-output data substrate is the linked empirical base of digital traces about research inputs, activities, outputs, and outcomes that makes large-scale science-of-science analysis possible.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, paper, grant, institution, collaboration, citation, patent, mobility event, field, or policy setting.
- Typical representation: multi-source data layer combining publication, funding, productivity, collaboration, citation, mobility, patent, and institutional records.
- Mechanism or measurement target: observation of science as a measurable system rather than only a historical or qualitative narrative.
- Empirical signature: multiple large data sources cover millions of scientific agents and outputs across fields, countries, and time.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides the empirical foundation for the [science of science framework](../methods/science_of_science_framework.md).
- Feeds [scholarly data lakes](scholarly_data_lakes.md), [multiplex scholarly graphs](../representations/multiplex_scholarly_graphs.md), and [scientific discovery prediction](../methods/scientific_discovery_prediction.md).
- Connects source-specific pages such as [Web of Science](web_of_science.md), [Scopus](scopus.md), [Google Scholar](google_scholar.md), [OpenAlex](openalex.md), [Dimensions](dimensions.md), and patent data layers.
- Supplies the data availability premise behind [career metadata fusion](career_metadata_fusion.md) and policy-facing evaluation studies.

## Operationalization

- Identify which input and output traces are needed for the research question: funding, projects, publications, citations, teams, institutions, fields, patents, mobility, or attention.
- Link records through stable identifiers, author and institution disambiguation, field classifications, and date fields.
- Record source coverage, access mode, snapshot date, and missing-data patterns.
- Keep source-specific provenance when combining proprietary indexes, open indexes, administrative records, patent data, and web/social traces.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) states that increasing digital data on scholarly inputs and outputs, including funding, productivity, collaboration, citations, and mobility, creates new opportunities to study the structure and evolution of science.
- The review identifies data availability as one of two drivers of modern science-of-science research.
- Fortunato et al. list Web of Science, Scopus, PubMed, Google Scholar, Microsoft Academic, the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office, and other sources as data substrates that cover millions of records across the world and many branches of science.
- The same review links these data to critical tests of generative models of science, institutions, and the scientific workforce.

## Caveats

- Data availability is not the same as complete, current, or unbiased coverage.
- Proprietary access, source-specific indexing rules, identifier gaps, and demographic or institutional missingness can shape results.
- Published-output data underrepresent failed, abandoned, or unpublished scientific attempts.
