# External-discipline citation fraction

## Summary

External-discipline citation fraction measures the share of a paper's citations that come from disciplines different from the paper's publication field, especially for testing cross-disciplinary awakening of sleeping beauties.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: cited paper, citing paper, publication field, citing field, citation edge, or high-B subset.
- Typical representation: external-citation share, same-field citation share, cross-disciplinary citation distribution, or high/medium/low-B comparison.
- Measurement target: whether attention to a paper comes from outside its field of origin.
- Empirical signature: high delayed-recognition papers receive a larger share of citations from external disciplines than lower-B comparison papers.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Operationalizes [cross-disciplinary awakening](../mechanisms/cross_disciplinary_awakening.md) as a citation-edge measure.
- Complements [sleeping-beauty field-origin profile](sleeping_beauty_field_origin_profile.md), which identifies where high-B papers were published.
- Uses [field classifications](field_classifications.md) to compare source fields and citing fields.
- Provides a mechanism-oriented validation for [sleeping-beauty detection](../methods/sleeping_beauty_detection.md) and [interdisciplinary citation delay](../mechanisms/interdisciplinary_citation_delay.md).

## Operationalization

- Assign cited and citing papers to field or journal categories using a documented classification.
- For each focal paper, count citations from fields that differ from the focal paper's publication field.
- Compare external-citation fractions across high-, medium-, and low-B paper groups, and around pre/post-awakening windows when possible.
- Report treatment of multidisciplinary journals, multiple categories, and field-category overlap.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ke et al. (2015) compares papers with high, medium, and low beauty coefficients by the fraction of citations received from disciplines different from their publication field.
- The paper reports that top sleeping beauties are more likely than lower-B papers to be cited across disciplines.
- Ke et al. interpret this as evidence that many awakenings involve discovery or reuse by communities outside the original publication field.

## Caveats

- Journal categories are imperfect proxies for intellectual communities.
- External citation can reflect methods reuse, review attention, database effects, or broad journal scope, not necessarily delayed conceptual recognition.
- Multiple-category and multidisciplinary journals need explicit counting rules.
