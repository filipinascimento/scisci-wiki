# Mobile scholar citation premium

## Summary

Mobile scholar citation premium measures the citation advantage associated with internationally mobile researchers relative to non-mobile researchers.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: researcher, region, country, mobility category, citation window, or cohort.
- Typical representation: citation-rate ratio, normalized citation difference, mobile-versus-non-mobile comparison, or regional mobility premium.
- Mechanism or measurement target: association between international mobility and later recognition, visibility, resources, selection, or network access.
- Empirical signature: mobile researchers receive higher normalized citation impact than non-mobile researchers within comparable regions or cohorts.

## Operationalization

- Classify researchers as mobile or non-mobile over a defined observation window.
- Normalize citations by field, year, document type, and citation window before aggregating to researcher level.
- Compare mobile and non-mobile citation rates within countries, regions, fields, career stages, and productivity bands.
- Report whether the comparison is descriptive or adjusted for selection, pre-move citation rates, career age, institution prestige, and productivity.
- Decompose the premium with [pre/post-move citation role decomposition](../methods/pre_post_move_citation_role_decomposition.md) when the question is where impact was produced or cultivated.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sugimoto et al. (2017) reports that mobile scholars have about 40% higher citation rates on average than non-mobile scholars.
- The paper states that the mobile-scholar advantage appears across regions, but the size varies widely.
- Sugimoto et al. report a 10.8% citation boost for mobile North American scholars and a 172.8% boost for Eastern European scholars.
- The paper also notes that mobile scholars are a minority but show the highest impact across the globe, making mobility restrictions relevant to scientific-system performance.
- A residual Sugimoto pass separates corridor-level high-impact mobility and impact-weighted policy leverage from the aggregate premium.

## Caveats

- The premium is not automatically causal; high-impact researchers may be more likely to receive mobility opportunities.
- Region, field, career stage, productivity, institution prestige, and publication coverage can change the measured premium.
- Citation advantage does not imply broader social value, equity, or contribution quality.
