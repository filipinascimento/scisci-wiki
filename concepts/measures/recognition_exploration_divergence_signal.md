# Recognition-exploration divergence signal

## Summary

High citations and prizes are associated with more diverse or bridging strategies than the modal biomedical search strategy.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, publication, prize, citation class, chemical pair, degree-distance profile, or strategy label.
- Typical representation: recognition-stratified strategy profile comparing modal and high-recognition work.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: association between recognition and exploratory or bridging research strategies.
- Empirical signature: recognized work shows broader strategy ranges and more disconnected-component bridging than modal publications..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines research-strategy measurement by linking it to [prize winner strategy enrichment](../validations/prize_winner_strategy_enrichment.md) and [research strategy surprisal](research_strategy_surprisal.md).
- Useful as a reusable distinction when [research strategy risk reward](../mechanisms/research_strategy_risk_reward.md) is analyzed in science-of-science data.
- Adds cross-links to [tail novelty](tail_novelty.md) so the motif is not read as an isolated result.

## Operationalization

- Compare degree-distance profiles of highly cited or prize-winning work with modal publication and patent strategies.
- Measure enrichment for disconnected-component bridging, distance, or low-degree combinations among recognized work.
- Treat recognition as an outcome stratum rather than proof of causal reward for risk.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Rzhetsky et al. (2015) reports that prize winners and highly cited scientists show broader strategy ranges, including more disconnected-component bridging.
- The motif provides a recognition-stratified signal of exploratory strategy value.

## Caveats

- Recognition is not the paper's main objective function and may reflect selection after success.
- Prizes and citations can reward status, topic visibility, or field size as well as exploration.
