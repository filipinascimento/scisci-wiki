# Team-size attention-delay curve

## Summary

Compares citation timing by team size after holding eventual citation volume approximately fixed, isolating whether smaller or more disruptive teams wait longer for recognition.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper age, team-size bin, disruption bin, and citation-count band.
- Typical representation: citation probability or cumulative citation curve by age within matched eventual-citation bands.
- Measurement target: recognition delay associated with team size or disruption.
- Empirical signature: small or disruptive teams accumulate citations more slowly among similarly cited eventual successes.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Quantifies the delay side of [small-team deep-search delay](../mechanisms/small_team_deep_search_delay.md).
- Connects team-size disruption to [delayed recognition](../mechanisms/delayed_recognition.md) and citation aging.
- Useful for assessing whether short-window indicators penalize disruptive small-team work.

## Operationalization

- Within citation-count bands, estimate citation probability by paper age and summarize with delay scores by team-size and disruption bins.
- Compare curves across field-year cohorts and team-size thresholds.
- Report how conditioning on eventual citations changes interpretation.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wu et al. (2019) analyzes Web of Science papers with 200-300 citations and finds longer citation delays for smaller and more disruptive teams.
- The analysis separates recognition timing from eventual citation count within a selected band.

## Caveats

- Conditioning on 200-300 citations selects successful papers and omits low-impact or uncited failures.
- Citation delay can also reflect field aging patterns and venue lags.
