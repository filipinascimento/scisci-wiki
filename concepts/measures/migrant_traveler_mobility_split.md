# Migrant-traveler mobility split

## Summary

Migrant-traveler mobility split distinguishes internationally mobile scientists who sever earlier country affiliations from those who retain origin-country ties while adding affiliations elsewhere.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, country affiliation sequence, mobility spell, or origin-destination path.
- Typical representation: non-mobile share, migrant share, traveler share, directional traveler share, non-directional traveler share, or mobility-category table.
- Mechanism or measurement target: whether international mobility is better described as brain drain, migration, or continuing brain circulation.
- Empirical signature: many mobile scholars retain origin affiliations, making one-way migration an incomplete description.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [international mobility typologies](international_mobility_typologies.md) into an operational split for publication-affiliation data.
- Helps interpret [scientific mobility](../mechanisms/scientific_mobility.md) without collapsing all multi-country affiliation histories into migration.
- Supplies categories for [brain circulation networks](../representations/brain_circulation_networks.md), [country mobility role typology](country_mobility_role_typology.md), and [mobility policy shock evaluation](../validations/mobility_policy_shock_evaluation.md).
- Helps measure policy consequences when border restrictions affect temporary affiliation chains, not only permanent moves.

## Operationalization

- Define a [country of scientific origin proxy](../methods/country_scientific_origin_proxy.md) for each scientist.
- Identify later publication years with affiliations outside the origin country.
- Classify migrants as scientists who move to another country and discontinue attachment to the previous country.
- Classify travelers as scientists who retain a footing in their origin country while adding international affiliations.
- Separate non-directional travelers when multiple country affiliations appear from the first publication year onward.
- Keep non-directional travelers separate from directed flows when building [regional mobility net balance](regional_mobility_net_balance.md) tables or [brain circulation networks](../representations/brain_circulation_networks.md).

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sugimoto et al. (2017) analyzes nearly 14 million papers and almost 16 million unique individuals who published from 2008 to 2015.
- The paper classifies about 96% of researchers as non-mobile and about 4%, more than 595,000 researchers, as mobile because they had more than one country affiliation during the study period.
- Sugimoto et al. report that migrants made up 27.3% of mobile researchers, or 162,519 researchers.
- Travelers made up 72.7% of mobile researchers, or 433,375 researchers, because they retained ties to their country of scientific origin while adding international affiliations.
- The paper further notes that nearly half of travelers are non-directional, with multiple affiliations in their first publication year that persist across publication years.

## Caveats

- Migrant and traveler labels depend on the origin proxy, publication window, and affiliation-reporting practices.
- Multi-affiliation can reflect visiting appointments, honorary ties, collaborations, remote work, or administrative affiliations rather than physical relocation.
- The split is descriptive and should not be interpreted as a causal effect of mobility without stronger designs.
