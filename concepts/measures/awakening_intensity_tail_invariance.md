# Awakening-intensity tail invariance

## Summary

Awakening-intensity tail invariance is the finding that high-awakening candidate counts decline very steeply and similarly across sleep-depth and sleep-duration conditions.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: awakening-intensity class, candidate count, tail slope, sleep-depth scenario, or sleep-duration scenario.
- Typical representation: power-law-like decline in candidate counts by awakening intensity across multiple panels.
- Measurement target: isolate the extreme-awakening tail from other sleep-threshold effects.
- Empirical signature: the high-awakening tail drops rapidly and shows little dependence on sleep length or depth.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [awakening probability function](awakening_probability_function.md) by isolating the awakening-intensity exponent.
- Connects threshold-based sleeping-beauty work to [continuous delayed-recognition spectrum](../mechanisms/continuous_delayed_recognition_spectrum.md).
- Gives [awakening-intensity panel plot](../representations/awakening_intensity_panel_plot.md) a quantitative interpretation.

## Operationalization

- Count candidates by awakening-intensity class under several sleep-depth and sleep-duration scenarios.
- Estimate or compare tail slopes across scenarios.
- Test whether the awakening-intensity effect changes after normalizing for publication volume.
- Report sparse-cell uncertainty for the highest intensity classes.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from van Raan (2004) derives a Grand Sleeping Beauty Equation with the steepest dependence on awakening intensity.
- The paper states that the probability of higher awakening intensities decreases extremely rapidly.
- It further reports that this high-intensity decline is independent of sleep length and sleep depth in the measured cases.

## Caveats

- Tail invariance is based on a specific threshold grid and corpus.
- Extreme awakening cells can contain very few cases.
- Later continuous measures may estimate delayed-recognition tails differently.
