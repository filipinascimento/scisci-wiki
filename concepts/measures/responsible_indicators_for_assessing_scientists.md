# Responsible indicators for assessing scientists

## Summary

Responsible indicators for assessing scientists are a proposed indicator family for evaluating faculty and researchers on behaviors that support reliable, reusable, socially useful science.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: individual scientist, faculty dossier, research group, department, institution, or funder assessment process.
- Typical representation: dashboard, indicator portfolio, CV supplement, audit report, or promotion-and-tenure evidence table.
- Measurement target: responsible research practice, openness, reproducibility, dissemination, peer-review service, and societal uptake.
- Empirical signature: indicators include registration, sharing, reproducible reporting, peer-review contributions, media or policy uptake, and data/software reuse rather than only publications and citations.

## Operationalization

- Define a dashboard with core indicators and optional institution-specific supplements.
- Candidate indicators from Moher et al. include study registration or registered reports, sharing results, reproducible research reporting, contributions to peer review, alternative media uptake, dataset sharing, and software sharing.
- Record source systems for each indicator, such as registries, repositories, journal badge systems, peer-review records, altmetric providers, ORCID records, or institutional CV systems.
- For each indicator, document ease of collection, field distribution, missingness, manipulability, and evidence that the indicator predicts better science.
- Pair dashboard values with qualitative portfolio review so indicators guide questions rather than replace judgment.
- State which indicators support [broad-output assessment portfolios](../methods/broad_output_assessment_portfolio.md), [team-science contribution assessment](../methods/team_science_contribution_assessment.md), or [mentoring-teaching-service assessment](../methods/mentoring_teaching_service_assessment.md).

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Moher et al. (2018) proposes "responsible indicators for assessing scientists" as alternatives or complements to traditional bibliometrics.
- The paper lists registration, registered reports, sharing of results, reproducible reporting, peer-review contributions, altmetrics, data sharing, and software sharing as candidate RIAS components.
- Moher et al. suggest a dashboard as an appealing implementation form across institutions, with supplemental indicators for local values or behaviors.
- The paper argues that RIAS must be studied for collection burden, distributions across fields and institutions, implementation systems, usefulness for evaluation, behavior-change effects, and gaming risk.
- Moher et al. also warn that evidence for many new responsible indicators is weaker than evidence for established bibliometric indicators, so institutions should experiment and validate rather than assume adoption is enough.

## Caveats

- New indicators can create new optimization targets, including symbolic compliance or low-quality openness.
- Some activities, such as mentoring, collegiality, community engagement, and reference letters, resist objective measurement.
- Dashboard use can privilege fields with mature registries or repositories unless missingness and field differences are handled explicitly.
- Responsible indicators are not inherently fair; they still need demographic, career-stage, field, and resource-access audits.
