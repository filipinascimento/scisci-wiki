# Collaboration clustering coefficient

## Summary

The collaboration clustering coefficient measures how often collaborators of a scientist also collaborate with one another, capturing local closure in coauthorship networks.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author node, field-level coauthorship graph, team, or time window.
- Typical representation: author-author graph projected from publication bylines.
- Mechanism or measurement target: local cohesion, transitivity, repeated social circles, and community closure.
- Empirical signature: triangles among coauthors occur more often than expected under a suitable random or degree-preserving null model.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Quantifies the local-cluster side of [small-world collaboration structure](../representations/small_world_collaboration_structure.md).
- Provides a measurable bridge between [coauthorship networks](../representations/coauthorship_networks.md), [invisible colleges](../mechanisms/invisible_college.md), and team formation.
- Links directly to [repeat incumbent collaboration fraction](repeat_incumbent_collaboration_fraction.md), which is one mechanism that can keep local coauthor circles closed.
- Helps distinguish globally connected fields from locally closed fields.
- Requires projection-aware interpretation through [team-size preserving clustering null](../methods/team_size_preserving_clustering_null.md), [shared-context collaboration clustering](../mechanisms/shared_context_collaboration_clustering.md), and field-specific caveats such as [biomedical lab-hierarchy clustering caveat](../validations/biomedical_lab_hierarchy_clustering_caveat.md).

## Operationalization

- Build an author-disambiguated coauthorship graph for a field and period.
- For each scientist, count how often pairs of their coauthors are also coauthors.
- Aggregate node-level clustering or compute a global transitivity measure.
- Compare against random graphs, configuration models, temporal nulls, or [team-size preserving clustering null](../methods/team_size_preserving_clustering_null.md) baselines that preserve team-size effects.
- Audit [large-team triangle inflation](../validations/large_team_triangle_inflation.md) before interpreting high clustering as social closure.
- Estimate [excess collaboration clustering](../validations/excess_collaboration_clustering.md) when the target is closure beyond simultaneous multi-author teams.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Newman (2001) reports clustering in scientific collaboration networks as part of the small-world pattern.
- Newman's PRE construction paper reports high clustering across source-domain panels and shows that only part of it is explained by triangles generated by papers with three or more authors.
- The same verified text points to shared journals, conferences, institutions, and biomedical laboratory hierarchy as alternative explanations for clustering differences once same-paper projection is accounted for.
- Verified full-text evidence from Newman (2004) defines clustering as the probability that two coauthors of a scientist have themselves coauthored a paper and reports strong field variation across biomedical research, physics, and mathematics.
- Newman (2004) also warns that multi-author papers mechanically create triangles, so clustering should be interpreted with team-size and temporal controls.
- Verified full-text evidence from Guimera et al. (2005) gives a formation mechanism for clustering-like structure: repeat incumbent collaborations and newcomer-incumbent mixing change whether a field forms isolated clusters or a large connected component.

## Caveats

- Large teams can inflate clustering without implying durable social closure.
- Author disambiguation errors create false triangles or split real ones.
- High clustering can indicate cohesion, but also exclusion or repeated closed teams.
