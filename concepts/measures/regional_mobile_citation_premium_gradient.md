# Regional mobile citation-premium gradient

## Summary

The citation premium associated with mobility varies sharply by region, so a single global mobile/non-mobile contrast can hide heterogeneous mobility advantages.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: region, mobile-scholar group, and non-mobile comparison group.
- Typical representation: region-specific ratio or difference in normalized citation impact for mobile versus non-mobile scholars.
- Measurement target: heterogeneity in the mobility citation premium.
- Empirical signature: mobile/non-mobile citation ratios differ substantially across regions.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [mobile scholar citation premium](mobile_scholar_citation_premium.md) with regional heterogeneity.
- Useful for comparing whether mobility advantages are concentrated in peripheral, central, or fast-growing regions.
- Connects route-specific impact to [route-specific high-impact mobility corridors](route_specific_high_impact_mobility_corridors.md).

## Operationalization

- Estimate normalized citation impact for mobile and non-mobile scholars by region, field, and cohort.
- Report ratios or marginal effects with uncertainty intervals and controls for career stage and field mix.
- Compare regional gradients with mobility-selection and destination-capacity measures.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sugimoto et al. (2017) reports that mobile/non-mobile citation premiums vary by region, with examples ranging from modest North American differences to much larger Eastern European contrasts.
- The result supports a gradient measure rather than a single global mobility premium.

## Caveats

- Selection into mobility, field composition, and destination prestige can confound regional comparisons.
- Large percentage premiums in low-baseline regions may reflect small denominators.
