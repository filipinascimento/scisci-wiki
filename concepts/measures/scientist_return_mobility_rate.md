# Scientist return mobility rate

## Summary

Scientist return mobility rate measures the share of scientists with international experience who have returned to their origin country, often paired with stated intentions to return.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: origin country, scientist with international experience, returnee, emigrant, or survey respondent.
- Typical representation: return rate, return-intention distribution, conditional return share, or country comparison table.
- Measurement target: whether international scientific mobility becomes permanent emigration, temporary training, or circular mobility.
- Empirical signature: high return rates indicate that many internationally experienced scientists are again located in their origin country at the observation date.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a circulation layer to [scientific emigration rate](scientific_emigration_rate.md).
- Helps distinguish one-way brain drain from [brain circulation networks](../representations/brain_circulation_networks.md).
- Supports policy analysis of return programs, job-market incentives, and family or lifestyle constraints.
- Complements [international mobility typologies](international_mobility_typologies.md) by turning returnees into a measurable category.

## Operationalization

- Identify scientists from an origin country using [country-at-18 origin proxy](../methods/country_at_18_origin_proxy.md) or another origin label.
- Identify respondents with international experience.
- Count those currently located in their origin country as returned.
- Divide by all origin-country scientists with international experience to estimate the return rate.
- For scientists still abroad, survey whether return is planned, conditional on jobs, part-time, delayed, or not expected.
- Record [job-conditioned return intention](job_conditioned_return_intention.md) separately from current return status so stated labor-market constraints are not treated as realized return.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Franzoni et al. (2012) reports international-experience and return-rate columns for scientists who lived in a core country at age 18.
- The paper reports that half or more of scientists from 13 of the 16 countries had international experience, with Switzerland at 78.4 percent and India at about three out of four.
- Return rates among those with international experience range from 92.0 percent for scientists from Japan to 47.1 percent for scientists from India.
- Franzoni et al. also find that return intentions vary by origin country; more than one in three emigrants from Sweden and Canada reported they would return, while fewer than one in five from the United Kingdom, Italy, Denmark, and Belgium did.
- The paper reports that job opportunities condition return for many emigrant scientists from the Netherlands, Japan, Italy, Spain, France, Germany, and Switzerland.
- The return-intention evidence motivates [attraction-return policy asymmetry](../mechanisms/attraction_return_policy_asymmetry.md), because the survey's outward-move motives and return motives have different dominant categories.

## Caveats

- Return rate is observed at one time point and does not reveal the duration or sequencing of mobility spells.
- Return intention is not realized return behavior.
- Return may reflect family, lifestyle, or visa constraints as well as scientific opportunity.
