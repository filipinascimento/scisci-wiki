# Faculty doctoral-origin composition

## Summary

Faculty doctoral-origin composition measures the distribution of faculty by doctoral training status, doctoral institution, and doctoral country or region, treating training origin as a structural attribute of academic labor markets.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: faculty member, doctoral institution, doctoral country, employing institution, field, domain, cohort, or faculty roster.
- Typical representation: share with U.S. doctorate, share with non-U.S. doctorate, no-doctorate share, origin-country distribution, domain-by-origin table, or doctoral-origin vector.
- Measurement target: how academic workforces are composed by training pathway and geographic origin of doctoral credentials.
- Empirical signature: fields and domains differ in doctoral credential requirements, international doctoral training shares, and concentration of non-U.S. doctoral origins.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides the denominator layer for [international-doctorate faculty attrition](../mechanisms/international_doctorate_attrition.md).
- Adds faculty-training composition to [faculty hiring networks](../representations/faculty_hiring_networks.md), [faculty retention and attrition](../mechanisms/faculty_retention_attrition.md), and [scientific mobility](../mechanisms/scientific_mobility.md).
- Helps distinguish country of doctorate from nationality, birthplace, immigration status, or current affiliation in mobility analyses.

## Operationalization

- Annotate each faculty member's highest degree, doctoral institution, doctoral country, and employing field or domain.
- Separate no-doctorate faculty, domestic doctorates, and international doctorates before estimating attrition or placement patterns.
- Group international doctorates by analytically justified country or region categories and report the grouping rule.
- Record missing doctorate information separately from true no-doctorate cases.
- Link origin composition to downstream outcomes such as retention, self-hiring, prestige rank, and production inequality only after checking field composition.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wapman et al. (2022) analyzes 295,089 U.S. tenure-track faculty and annotates doctoral university, doctoral country, faculty rank, and field/domain.
- Wapman et al. report that 92.7% of all faculty hold doctoral degrees, with domain variation: only 1% lack a doctorate in the Social Sciences compared with 19% in the Humanities.
- The paper reports that 11% of U.S. faculty have non-U.S. doctorates, ranging from 2% in Education to 19% in the Natural Sciences.
- Wapman et al. also show that internationally trained faculty are concentrated by origin, with 35.5% trained in the United Kingdom or Canada and 5.4% from Africa and the Americas excluding Canada.
- These measurements make doctoral-origin composition a prerequisite for interpreting [international-doctorate faculty attrition](../mechanisms/international_doctorate_attrition.md).

## Caveats

- Country of doctorate is not country of birth, citizenship, ethnicity, race, or immigration status.
- Doctoral-origin categories can hide institutional prestige, language, visa regime, and field-specific training differences.
- No-doctorate shares depend strongly on faculty definitions and whether arts, professional, clinical, or practice-based fields are included.
- Missing degree metadata should not be collapsed into no-doctorate or international-doctorate categories.
