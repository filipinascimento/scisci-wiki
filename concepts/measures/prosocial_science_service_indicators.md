# Prosocial science-service indicators

## Summary

Prosocial science-service indicators are measures intended to credit scientific service and community contributions such as data sharing, peer help, reviewing reliability, communication, teaching, and societal exchange.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, contribution, dataset, review activity, teaching effort, communication output, or service event.
- Typical representation: portfolio indicator, service trace, contribution score, or nonpublication performance dimension.
- Measurement target: productive scientific roles that are not captured by citation counts alone.
- Empirical signature: researchers can receive credit for valuable community functions even when those functions do not directly produce highly cited papers.

- Operationalizes [metric pluralism and productive specialization](../mechanisms/metric_pluralism_productive_specialization.md).

## Operationalization

- Identify service dimensions such as data sharing, peer help, review reliability, communication, teaching, or societal engagement.
- Define observable traces and their provenance for each dimension.
- Avoid aggregating unlike service roles into a single opaque score without interpretation.
- Validate whether indicators reward genuine service rather than easy-to-game visibility.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) argues that science needs communicators, teachers, detail-oriented experts, novel-question askers, and problem solvers.
- The review proposes broadening performance indicators beyond citation counts to include shared information, help offered to peers, and reviewer reliability.
- It warns that metric profusion requires understanding what each indicator captures and misses.

## Caveats

- Service indicators can become new targets for gaming or bureaucratic burden.
- Some prosocial contributions are private, informal, or difficult to measure fairly.
- Weighting service dimensions involves normative choices that should be explicit.
