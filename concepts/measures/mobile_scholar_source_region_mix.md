# Mobile scholar source-region mix

## Summary

Destination mobility profiles can be described by the regional composition of incoming mobile scholars, not only by net gain or loss.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: destination country, origin region, and mobile-scholar group.
- Typical representation: destination-by-source-region composition shares.
- Measurement target: where mobile scholars in a destination were previously anchored.
- Empirical signature: destinations differ in whether incoming mobile scholars originate primarily from Europe, North America, Asia, or other regions.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds composition detail to [regional mobility net balance](regional_mobility_net_balance.md).
- Useful for distinguishing hub countries with similar net inflows but different source-region dependencies.
- Connects to survey measures such as [immigrant source-country concentration](immigrant_source_country_concentration.md).

## Operationalization

- Classify mobile scholars by inferred origin or prior affiliation region and current destination.
- Compute source-region shares for each destination and stratify by field, career stage, and impact group.
- Compare composition with route-level mobility corridors and policy exposure.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sugimoto et al. (2017) reports regional source differences among mobile scholars, including European and North American origin patterns across destinations.
- This supports representing mobility profiles as compositions rather than only balances.

## Caveats

- Origin definitions based on first publication can differ from birthplace, training location, or citizenship.
- Composition shares can hide small absolute counts in low-volume destinations.
