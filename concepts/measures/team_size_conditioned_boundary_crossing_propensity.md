# Team-Size Conditioned Boundary-Crossing Propensity

## Summary

Team-size conditioned boundary-crossing propensity measures the likelihood that a team crosses university boundaries after holding team size fixed.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, team-size bin, field, domain, year, or institution set.
- Typical representation: probability of multi-university authorship conditional on number of authors.
- Measurement target: whether fields differ in cross-institutional collaboration beyond differences in team size.
- Empirical signature: one domain has a higher cross-university rate than another at the same author count.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [between-school collaboration share](between_school_collaboration_share.md).
- Separates general [mean team-size growth](mean_team_size_growth.md) from institutional boundary crossing.
- Helps interpret [authorship structure taxonomy](authorship_structure_taxonomy.md) across fields.

## Operationalization

- Bin papers by team size.
- Within each bin, compute the share of teams with authors at multiple universities.
- Compare fields, domains, and time periods within equivalent team-size bins.
- Contrast conditional propensities with aggregate multi-university shares.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Jones et al. (2008) reports that social scientists have a substantially higher propensity to engage in multi-university collaboration for any given team size.
- The paper gives an example: two-person social-science teams crossed universities nearly 40% of the time around 2000, compared with 18% in science and engineering.
- It also notes that larger teams are more common in science and engineering, offsetting this conditional difference in aggregate shares.

## Caveats

- Team size can reflect field norms, instrumentation, and labor organization.
- Conditional shares may still depend on institution type, geography, and subfield.
- Address data completeness can vary by field and period.
