# Supporting multi-contribution floor

## Summary

Supporting archetypes can be low-intensity without being single-task, because supporting researchers may still participate in multiple contribution types.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author-stage profile, Supporting archetype, number of contributions, role probability, or career stage.
- Typical representation: low-intensity multi-role contribution profile.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: task breadth inside supporting contribution archetypes.
- Empirical signature: support profiles show low role probabilities while retaining more than one contribution type in some stages.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines contribution-profile measurement by linking it to [leader specialized supporting archetypes](../representations/leader_specialized_supporting_archetypes.md) and [contribution role vectors](../representations/contribution_role_vectors.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [median stage role probability aggregation](../methods/median_stage_role_probability_aggregation.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [supporting archetype career attrition](../mechanisms/supporting_archetype_career_attrition.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Track NC and per-role median probabilities within Supporting profiles by career stage.
- Flag support profiles where all role probabilities are low but NC remains above one.
- Compare junior and late-career support profiles to identify different sources of multi-contribution breadth.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified local full text from Robinson-Garcia et al. (2020) describes Supporting as low on contributorship values while junior and late-career support still involve multiple contribution types.
- The motif prevents interpreting support profiles as purely single-task roles.

## Caveats

- NC is predicted for expanded WoS histories rather than directly observed in every paper.
- Low-intensity breadth can be a model artifact if contribution probabilities are poorly calibrated.
