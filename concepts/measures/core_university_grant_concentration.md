# Core-university grant concentration

## Summary

Status inequality can be represented as concentration of national grant resources in former Imperial or otherwise core universities.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: university, grant amount, top-seven share, top-twenty share, institutional status, or national funding distribution.
- Typical representation: institution-level grant concentration measure over prestige tiers.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: resource concentration component of institutional status.
- Empirical signature: core universities receive a disproportionate share of national competitive grant funding..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines funding-status measurement by linking it to [former imperial university core status proxy](former_imperial_university_core_status_proxy.md) and [funding model status contingency](../mechanisms/funding_model_status_contingency.md).
- Useful as a reusable distinction when [status conformity pressure](../mechanisms/status_conformity_pressure.md) is analyzed in science-of-science data.
- Adds cross-links to [status conditioned novelty tolerance](../mechanisms/status_conditioned_novelty_tolerance.md) so the motif is not read as an isolated result.

## Operationalization

- Compute grant shares held by top universities or historical elite categories.
- Use top-seven, top-twenty, or former-Imperial indicators as status-concentration anchors.
- Compare concentration in competitive grants with publication, faculty, or internal-funding distributions.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang et al. (2018) reports that Tokyo, the top seven, and the top twenty national universities account for large shares of Grants-in-Aid funding.
- The motif gives a funding-concentration layer to core-university status.

## Caveats

- Complements but should not duplicate former-Imperial university status proxies.
- Grant concentration may reflect size and field composition as well as prestige.
