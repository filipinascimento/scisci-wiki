# Intellectual rejuvenation decomposition

## Summary

Intellectual rejuvenation decomposition splits new field contributions into proximity, reference-source, reference-vintage, MeSH-vintage, and impact bins to test whether entrants refresh a field while still engaging its core topics.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: article, subfield, reference list, MeSH term combination, citation-impact bin, or field-year.
- Typical representation: separate outcome counts for proximate versus distant papers, within-field versus outside references, star versus non-star references, recent versus older references, and old versus new vocabulary.
- Measurement target: whether field growth reflects peripheral drift, mainstream renewal, new inspiration, or radical disruption.
- Empirical signature: additional post-shock papers remain close to the field but draw on newer or outside ideas and enter higher impact bins.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [outsider entry after star death](../mechanisms/outsider_entry_after_star_death.md) by asking what entrants bring into the field.
- Connects content-defined fields from [PMRA subfield delineation](../methods/pmra_subfield_delineation.md) with [vintage-specific citation-tail output](vintage_specific_citation_tail_output.md).
- Complements [MeSH keyword trajectory shift](mesh_keyword_trajectory_shift.md) when the question is field rejuvenation rather than a scientist's career shift.
- Helps distinguish small directional revolutions from wholesale disruption measured by the [disruption index](disruption_index.md).

## Operationalization

- Split field-year article counts by PMRA proximity to the source article.
- Split articles by whether they cite references inside the PMRA-defined field or exclusively outside it.
- Separately flag articles that cite the focal star versus articles that do not build directly on the star's work.
- Compare articles using recent versus older references and newer versus older MeSH term combinations.
- Pair these content splits with vintage-specific citation-tail bins to see whether refreshed work also attracts high long-run attention.
- Add disruption-bin checks such as [intermediate disruptiveness entry](../validations/intermediate_disruptiveness_entry.md) before describing renewal as radical disruption.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Azoulay, Fons-Rosen, and Graff Zivin (2019) finds that post-death non-collaborator contributions are disproportionately high-cited, with larger effects toward the citation tail.
- The paper shows that entrant contributions are more PMRA-proximate than PMRA-distant, ruling out a simple periphery-entry interpretation.
- Reference analyses indicate that growth comes mainly from articles that cite outside the focal PMRA subfield and do not build directly on the deceased star's work.
- Reference-vintage and MeSH-vintage splits show that new contributions use more recent science and newer vocabulary combinations.
- The authors describe the pattern as rejuvenation through many small permanent changes rather than radical disruption of the subfield.
- The disruption-bin version of that evidence is split out as [intermediate disruptiveness entry](../validations/intermediate_disruptiveness_entry.md).

## Caveats

- Citation-tail, reference-vintage, and MeSH-vintage bins are indirect indicators of intellectual value and direction.
- PMRA proximity is PubMed-specific and should be replaced or triangulated outside biomedical corpora.
- Newer references or MeSH combinations can reflect fieldwide database growth rather than substantive novelty unless controls are aligned in event time.
