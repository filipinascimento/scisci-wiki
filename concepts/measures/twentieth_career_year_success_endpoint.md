# Twentieth-Career-Year Success Endpoint

## Summary

Twentieth-career-year success endpoint measures long-run career impact by whether an author is top-cited, or by cumulative citations, around the twentieth year after career start.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author, career year, discipline, citation total, or top-status indicator.
- Typical representation: top-status indicator in career year 20 or citations accrued through 20 years.
- Measurement target: long-horizon academic career success.
- Empirical signature: early-career predictors are evaluated against a distant, fixed career endpoint.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Gives a long-run outcome for [top-cited scientist status](top_cited_scientist_status.md).
- Supports [top-coauthor regression predictor](../validations/top_coauthor_regression_predictor.md).
- Connects career prediction to [citation impact indicators](citation_impact_indicators.md).

## Operationalization

- Define career start as first publication year.
- Require enough observed career length to measure the twentieth year.
- Measure top-cited status in the twentieth career year, cumulative citations over 20 years, or both.
- Compare early-career predictors against this endpoint.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Li et al. (2019) reports the unconditional probability of being a top scientist in the twentieth career year.
- The paper repeatedly uses twentieth-career-year top status and first-20-year citation totals as long-term outcome variables.
- Regression analyses predict both 20-year citations and top-scientist status in the twentieth career year.

## Caveats

- The endpoint requires long-lived, observable careers and may exclude early exits.
- Twenty-year horizons can favor older cohorts and fields with stable citation coverage.
- Top-status endpoints still inherit citation-based evaluation limits.
