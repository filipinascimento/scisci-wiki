# Country mobility role typology

## Summary

Country mobility role typology classifies countries by how they produce, attract, cultivate, recruit, or incubate high-impact mobile researchers.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: country, region, mobile researcher, origin country, destination country, or citation-impact segment.
- Typical representation: producer, cultivator, recruiter, incubator, net-loss, net-gain, or bridge-role category.
- Mechanism or measurement target: national roles in global brain circulation and high-impact scholar flows.
- Empirical signature: countries differ in whether mobile scholars are highly cited before arrival, after arrival, before departure, or after departure.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Turns [brain circulation networks](../representations/brain_circulation_networks.md) into interpretable country-level roles.
- Refines [international mobility typologies](international_mobility_typologies.md) from individual mobility labels to country-system functions.
- Provides covariates for [mobility policy shock evaluation](../validations/mobility_policy_shock_evaluation.md) and science-workforce strategy.
- Helps avoid reducing mobility policy to net brain drain or gain alone.

## Operationalization

- Define mobile researcher cohorts and their [country of scientific origin proxy](../methods/country_scientific_origin_proxy.md).
- Compute normalized citation impact before and after moves or affiliation changes.
- Label countries or regions by whether they send out, receive, cultivate, or later benefit from high-impact mobile scholars.
- Report role definitions, time windows, field normalization, and whether labels are based on people, publications, or citation-weighted flows.
- Use [pre/post-move citation role decomposition](../methods/pre_post_move_citation_role_decomposition.md) when the role labels depend on citation timing around moves.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sugimoto et al. (2017) asks which countries are responsible for the production and cultivation of high-impact scholars by comparing citation scores for mobile researchers before and after their moves.
- The paper reports that countries in North America and Northern Europe act as strong producers by putting well-cited scholars into circulation before they move.
- Sugimoto et al. also describe the same countries as strong cultivators because they identify talent early and provide ground for high impact after scholars arrive.
- The paper characterizes Asian regions as strong recruiters of established, already well-cited scholars and Oceania as a notable incubator where scholars realize high potential after moving on.

## Caveats

- Country roles depend on the citation window, mobile cohort, field mix, and origin/destination definitions.
- Roles can change over time after policy shocks, funding changes, or database coverage shifts.
- A country-level role does not imply that all institutions or fields within that country behave similarly.
