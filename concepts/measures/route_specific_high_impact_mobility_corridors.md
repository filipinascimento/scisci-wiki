# Route-specific high-impact mobility corridors

## Summary

Route-specific high-impact mobility corridors measure whether particular origin-destination routes carry especially highly cited mobile scholars, rather than treating the mobility citation premium as one global average.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: mobile researcher, origin region, destination region, mobility corridor, citation score, or move spell.
- Typical representation: route-level citation premium, origin-destination impact table, high-impact corridor map, or citation-weighted mobility edge.
- Measurement target: route heterogeneity in the impact associated with international mobility.
- Empirical signature: some regional or country corridors show much higher pre- or post-move citation scores than the aggregate mobile-scholar average.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [mobile scholar citation premium](mobile_scholar_citation_premium.md) from an aggregate association to route-level heterogeneity.
- Adds impact weights to [brain circulation networks](../representations/brain_circulation_networks.md) and [regional mobility net balance](regional_mobility_net_balance.md).
- Provides a corridor-level input for [country mobility role typology](country_mobility_role_typology.md) and [pre/post-move citation role decomposition](../methods/pre_post_move_citation_role_decomposition.md).
- Helps policy analysis avoid assuming that all mobility routes have the same scientific value.

## Operationalization

- Reconstruct origin, destination, and intermediate affiliations for mobile scholars.
- Normalize citation impact by field, year, document type, and career stage where possible.
- Aggregate citation scores by origin-destination route, separating pre-move, during-move, and post-move periods when data permit.
- Compare route-level premiums with aggregate mobile/non-mobile differences and with route volume.
- Report multi-affiliation and return-mobility counting rules.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sugimoto et al. (2017) reports that the mobile-scholar citation advantage varies by region.
- The paper identifies specific high-impact route patterns, including movement involving North America, parts of Europe, Asia, and Oceania.
- Sugimoto et al. also show that citation impact depends on where scholars come from, where they stop, and where they go, supporting a corridor-specific rather than purely aggregate mobility measure.

## Caveats

- Route-level estimates can be noisy for low-volume corridors.
- Citation advantages may reflect selection, field mix, institutional prestige, language, or funding, not mobility alone.
- Multi-affiliation travelers can create routes that are not simple relocations.
