# International experience prevalence

## Summary

International experience prevalence measures the share of scientists from an origin country who have studied or worked internationally at some point, regardless of whether they are abroad or back home at the observation date.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: origin country, scientist, mobility history, field, or survey respondent.
- Typical representation: percentage with international experience, country comparison table, or origin-country mobility profile.
- Measurement target: breadth of international exposure in a national scientific workforce.
- Empirical signature: high prevalence indicates that international mobility is common even when current emigration rates are moderate.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a stock-of-experience measure to [scientific mobility](../mechanisms/scientific_mobility.md) beyond current-location counts.
- Separates temporary or circular mobility from one-time [scientific emigration rate](scientific_emigration_rate.md) and [scientist return mobility rate](scientist_return_mobility_rate.md).
- Provides a survey-based complement to affiliation-history motifs such as [brain circulation networks](../representations/brain_circulation_networks.md).
- Helps interpret whether a country with low current emigration has nevertheless exposed many scientists to foreign training or work settings.

## Operationalization

- Define an origin country with [country-at-18 origin proxy](../methods/country_at_18_origin_proxy.md), birth country, first publication country, or another documented baseline.
- Ask or infer whether each scientist has ever studied or worked outside the origin country.
- Divide the number of origin-country scientists with any international experience by the observed origin-country scientist count.
- Report the current-location split so internationally experienced scientists who returned are not conflated with those still abroad.
- Stratify by career stage, field, country, and survey-response weights when available.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Franzoni, Scellato, and Stephan (2012) reports international-experience shares for scientists who lived in one of the 16 core countries at age 18.
- The paper states that half or more of scientists from 13 of the 16 countries had international experience.
- Switzerland had the highest reported prevalence at 78.4 percent, and India had about three out of four scientists with international experience.
- Scientists from the United States had by far the lowest rate of international experience in the GlobSci table.
- The same table pairs international-experience prevalence with return rates, supporting separate measurement of exposure, current emigration, and return.

## Caveats

- International experience prevalence is not a flow rate and does not reveal timing, duration, destination quality, or number of mobility spells.
- Survey measures can miss short visits, informal appointments, remote affiliation, or experiences outside the sampled fields.
- High prevalence can reflect temporary training, career mobility, dual-career constraints, or structural lack of domestic opportunities.
