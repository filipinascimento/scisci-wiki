# Post-2004 IFBSCP acceleration

## Summary

Post-2004 IFBSCP acceleration measures the sharp rise after the early 2000s in mean IFBSCP and in the share of journals crossing high IFBSCP thresholds.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: journal-year, field-year, IFBSCP threshold, citation database snapshot, or journal population.
- Typical representation: interrupted trend, pre/post comparison, threshold-exceedance time series, or mean IFBSCP time series.
- Measurement target: population-level acceleration in impact-factor-window journal self-citation bias.
- Empirical signature: stable IFBSCP levels before the early 2000s followed by rising mean IFBSCP and rising high-threshold tail shares.

## Operationalization

- Compute IFBSCP for eligible journals over a long panel.
- Plot mean IFBSCP and threshold-exceedance shares before and after the early 2000s.
- Stratify by domain or field to identify whether the acceleration is broad or concentrated.
- Compare timing against external indicators of impact-factor salience, such as policy mentions, editorial discourse, or assessment rules.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Chorus and Waltman (2016) reports that mean IFBSCP and high-threshold IFBSCP shares were relatively stable from 1987 to about 2004 and then rose strongly.
- In their all-field panel, the share above 1.5 rises from about one third before 2004 to 44.6 percent in 2015, the share above 2 rises to 18.9 percent, and the share above 3 nearly triples to 5.6 percent.
- The paper links the timing to rising attention to the impact factor and treats the pattern as circumstantial evidence for increasingly pervasive journal self-citation malpractice, not as journal-specific proof.

## Caveats

- A pre/post pattern can reflect changes in journal coverage, field composition, or legitimate recent-self-citation mechanisms.
- Acceleration at the population level does not identify which individual journals acted strategically.
- Replication should use explicit database snapshots, journal inclusion rules, field strata, and baseline-window sensitivity checks.
