# Job-conditioned return intention

## Summary

Job-conditioned return intention measures the share of emigrant scientists who report that returning to their origin country depends on job opportunities.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: emigrant scientist, origin country, return-intention response, career stage, or mobility survey.
- Typical representation: response-category share, stacked return-intention bar, conditional-return rate, or country comparison.
- Measurement target: whether return mobility is constrained by origin-country labor-market opportunities.
- Empirical signature: many scientists answer that they may return only if suitable job opportunities are available.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a policy-sensitive subcategory to [scientist return mobility rate](scientist_return_mobility_rate.md).
- Links [scientific mobility motivation taxonomy](../methods/scientific_mobility_motivation_taxonomy.md) to origin-country labor-market capacity.
- Helps distinguish return intentions driven by family or personal reasons from those conditional on professional opportunity.
- Provides an interpretable survey outcome for [mobility policy shock evaluation](../validations/mobility_policy_shock_evaluation.md) and return-program design.

## Operationalization

- Identify scientists currently abroad relative to their origin proxy, such as [country-at-18 origin proxy](../methods/country_at_18_origin_proxy.md).
- Ask whether they may return to their origin country, with response categories such as yes, depends on job opportunities, part time or late-career, and no.
- Compute the share selecting the job-opportunity condition by origin country, field, and career stage.
- Compare job-conditioned intentions with observed return rates among scientists with [international experience prevalence](international_experience_prevalence.md).
- Treat stated intention as an outcome requiring follow-up, not as realized return.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Franzoni, Scellato, and Stephan (2012) reports return-intention responses for emigrant scientists by country of residence at age 18.
- The paper states that nearly one out of two emigrant scientists from the Netherlands and Japan see return as conditional on job opportunities.
- It also reports that about four out of ten emigrant scientists from Italy, Spain, France, Germany, and Switzerland indicate that return depends on job opportunities.
- The same text notes that Sweden, Brazil, and India place less emphasis on job prospects in possible return.
- These results separate job-conditioned return from a simple yes/no return intention.

## Caveats

- Return intention may not predict realized return without longitudinal follow-up.
- Job-opportunity responses can reflect field-specific labor markets, visa options, family constraints, and career stage.
- Country comparisons need comparable survey response rates and clear origin definitions.
