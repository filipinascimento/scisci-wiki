# Isolated regional collaboration components

## Summary

Some country clusters are internally linked yet weakly connected to the global collaboration core.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: country, regional component, country-pair tie, connected component, threshold, or cosine-intense dyad.
- Typical representation: thresholded connected-component profile of country collaboration networks.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: regional isolation inside international collaboration networks.
- Empirical signature: geographically close or regionally linked countries form components outside the main global core.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines international-collaboration measure by linking it to [regional collaboration hubs](regional_collaboration_hubs.md) and [country pair cosine intensity](country_pair_cosine_intensity.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [historical tie residual imprint](../validations/historical_tie_residual_imprint.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [country collaboration core](country_collaboration_core.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Compute connected components after a disclosed tie threshold.
- Record component size, regional makeup, and connection to the global core.
- Compare isolated components against cosine-intense dyads and historical-tie explanations.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified local full text from Wagner and Leydesdorff (2005) notes geographically close country pairs and Middle Eastern Arab-country groupings that can sit in otherwise disconnected networks.
- The motif captures regional collaboration that does not automatically integrate into the global core.

## Caveats

- Component isolation depends on thresholds, country coding, and source coverage.
- Small components can reflect data sparsity rather than durable collaboration regimes.
