# Interlayer presence bonus

## Summary

Some interlayer couplings should be interpreted as a bonus for being present across layers, distinct from a switching cost for traversing layers.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: entity, node-layer copy, identity coupling, centrality contribution, or presence indicator.
- Typical representation: identity-coupling parameter coded as bonus, neutral link, or traversal cost.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: multi-channel visibility and cross-layer participation in centrality measures.
- Empirical signature: entities present in more layers receive higher centrality or visibility after identity couplings are treated as bonuses.

## Operationalization

- Classify identity couplings as cost, bonus, or neutral for the mechanism at hand.
- Compare centrality rankings under bonus and cost interpretations.
- Control for data-source coverage before interpreting cross-layer presence as scholarly breadth.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from De Domenico et al. (2013) contrasts crossing costs with centrality settings where presence in multiple layers can generate a bonus.
- This distinction matters for authors or institutions visible in citations, collaborations, patents, grants, and policy documents.

## Caveats

- Presence bonuses can reward better data coverage rather than broader scholarly role.
- A bonus appropriate for visibility may be inappropriate for diffusion or traversal models.
