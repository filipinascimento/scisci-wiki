# Block research-allowance baseline

## Summary

The baseline internal block-funding environment can be measured through untied chair-level research allowances.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: university chair, internal allowance, MEXT block subsidy, project, research group, or funding source.
- Typical representation: funding-context measure for untied internal research support.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: baseline autonomy available before competitive project funding is added.
- Empirical signature: scientists in the studied system have an internal block-funding floor that shapes the meaning of competitive-funding shares..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines funding-source measurement by linking it to [block funding novelty buffer](../mechanisms/block_funding_novelty_buffer.md) and [competitive funding share threshold](competitive_funding_share_threshold.md).
- Useful as a reusable distinction when [fractional project funding attribution](fractional_project_funding_attribution.md) is analyzed in science-of-science data.
- Adds cross-links to [mixed funding within system comparison](../methods/mixed_funding_within_system_comparison.md) so the motif is not read as an isolated result.

## Operationalization

- Code institutional block funds as untied internal research support.
- Record typical annual chair-level allowance amounts and variance where available.
- Use the baseline allowance to contextualize competitive-funding ratios and mixed funding systems.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang et al. (2018) describes MEXT block subsidies and a standard chair-level research allowance around $25,000 +/- $10,000.
- The motif captures the funding environment behind the competitive-versus-block comparison.

## Caveats

- Close to block-funding novelty-buffer mechanisms; keep this as a funding-context measure.
- Allowance amounts may vary by institution, chair, field, and period.
