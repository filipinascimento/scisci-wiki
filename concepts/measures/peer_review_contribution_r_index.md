# Peer-review contribution R-index

## Summary

Peer-review contribution R-index is a proposed reviewer-credit metric that scores peer-review labor using disclosed reviewer activity, reviewed-paper word counts, journal impact factor weighting, and editor quality scores.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: reviewer, journal, reviewed manuscript, editor score, or annual review record.
- Typical representation: reviewer index computed from papers reviewed, manuscript length, journal IF, and editor-rated review quality.
- Measurement target: credit peer-review labor in researcher assessment.
- Empirical signature: peer-review service becomes citable or countable assessment evidence rather than invisible academic labor.

## Operationalization

- Have journals disclose annual reviewer lists and the number of papers reviewed by each reviewer.
- For each reviewed paper, combine manuscript word count with a journal-level impact-factor term.
- Weight the contribution by editor feedback on the quality of the review.
- Aggregate reviewed-paper contributions into an annual or career reviewer-credit score.
- Validate whether the index changes review participation, review quality, and review inequality.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Moher et al. (2018) reviews a proposed R-index intended to give academic recognition for peer review.
- The paper describes the formula as using disclosed reviewer lists, paper word counts, the square root of a journal's impact factor, and editor scores of review excellence.
- Moher et al. caution that the index is complex, relies on standardized inputs not currently gathered by journals, has not been applied across multiple journals, and partly relies on impact factor.

## Caveats

- The R-index could reproduce JIF dependence while trying to reduce JIF overuse.
- Editor quality scores may be inconsistent, biased, or unavailable.
- Counting review labor can incentivize quantity unless quality and conflict checks are strong.
