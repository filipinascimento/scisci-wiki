# Star subfield importance-commitment split

## Summary

Star subfield importance-commitment split distinguishes how important a star is to a local subfield from how important that subfield is within the star's own career portfolio.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: star scientist, subfield, star publication portfolio, local subfield publication share, or treatment-effect stratum.
- Typical representation: star importance to field, field importance to star, two-way split, or local dominance versus career commitment table.
- Measurement target: separate field dependence on a star from the star's personal commitment to that field.
- Empirical signature: post-star entry patterns differ when the star is locally dominant, personally committed, both, or neither.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [star-scientist entry deterrence](../mechanisms/star_scientist_entry_deterrence.md) by separating two forms of star prominence.
- Uses [deceased superstar life-scientist panel](../datasets/deceased_superstar_life_scientist_panel.md) data to interpret field-level heterogeneity.
- Complements [subfield coherence barrier measures](subfield_coherence_barrier_measures.md) and collaborator resource controls.
- Helps avoid equating a globally famous scientist with a locally field-dominant scientist.

## Operationalization

- Compute the star's share of publications or influence within the focal subfield.
- Compute the focal subfield's share of the star's own publication portfolio.
- Split or interact treatment effects by local importance and career commitment.
- Compare post-death collaborator, non-collaborator, and outsider outcomes across the resulting strata.
- Report whether star-authored papers are excluded from post-event field outcomes.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Azoulay, Fons-Rosen, and Graff Zivin (2019) separates a star's importance to the subfield from the subfield's importance to the star.
- The paper uses these measures to test whether non-collaborator entry depends on local dominance or on the star's own career commitment.
- This distinction clarifies whether the barrier is caused by field reliance on a luminary, the star's attention allocation, or both.
- It also helps explain why high star eminence is not a single-dimensional treatment intensity.

## Caveats

- Publication shares are only proxies for intellectual importance and personal commitment.
- A star can influence a subfield through training, data, methods, or institutions without a large publication share.
- Local importance can be endogenous to field size and field maturity.
- Career commitment can shift before death because of illness, age, or strategic repositioning.
