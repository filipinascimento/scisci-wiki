# Former Imperial university core-status proxy

## Summary

Former Imperial university core-status proxy measures Japanese institutional status by whether a researcher is affiliated with one of Japan's seven former Imperial universities.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: researcher, respondent author, affiliation, project, or publication.
- Typical representation: former-Imperial-university indicator, outside-top-seven indicator, or core/periphery institutional status flag.
- Measurement target: institutional prestige and resource position inside Japan's research system.
- Empirical signature: funding or novelty effects differ between researchers affiliated with the former Imperial universities and researchers outside that core.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Specializes [respondent-author status proxy](../methods/respondent_author_status_proxy.md) for the Japanese funding-system setting.
- Connects [institutional prestige hierarchy](../mechanisms/institutional_prestige_hierarchy.md) to [funding model status contingency](../mechanisms/funding_model_status_contingency.md).
- Provides a country-specific counterpart to broader prestige-rank measures, hiring-rank measures, and university-tier controls.
- Helps interpret [funding status-interaction tests](../validations/funding_status_interaction_test.md) without treating all institutional categories as universal.

## Operationalization

- Identify respondent or author affiliations at the relevant project/publication time.
- Code affiliation with one of Japan's seven former Imperial universities as core status, and outside that group as peripheral status.
- Use the indicator as a subgroup variable or interaction term in funding-outcome models.
- Report field, rank, gender, and career-stage controls where possible because institutional affiliation can absorb several forms of status.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Lee, and Walsh (2018) uses affiliation with Japan's seven former Imperial universities as a high-status institutional proxy.
- The paper notes that this institutional core receives a large share of Grants-in-Aid funding and codes outside-top-seven affiliation as a peripheral status category.
- The proxy is used alongside rank and gender to test whether competitive versus block funding has heterogeneous novelty associations.

## Caveats

- This is a country-specific prestige proxy and should not be copied to other systems without local institutional-history evidence.
- Former-Imperial affiliation can capture resources, selection, reputation, networks, and field composition.
- Affiliation at publication or survey time may not fully capture project-time institutional support.
