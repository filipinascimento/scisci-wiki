# Protected funding application substitution

## Summary

Protected funding application substitution measures whether scientists with long-horizon or person-centered support reduce applications to conventional project-grant systems.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist-year, funding-program participant, grant application, review panel, or funding portfolio.
- Typical representation: post-appointment grant-application count, R01 application ratio, conditional review score, or funding-source substitution measure.
- Measurement target: distinguish reduced dependence on conventional grants from worse treatment by conventional review panels.
- Empirical signature: protected funding recipients submit fewer conventional grant applications, and funding changes are decomposed into application behavior and review outcomes.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [funding autonomy and exploration](../mechanisms/funding_autonomy_exploration.md) by measuring whether protected autonomy changes interactions with conventional funding systems.
- Provides an input to [funding-program alternative-mechanism audit](../validations/funding_program_alternative_mechanism_audit.md), because lower conventional funding can reflect fewer applications rather than lower grant quality alone.
- Connects [HHMI-NIH incentive contrast](../methods/hhmi_nih_incentive_contrast.md) to grant-system behavior rather than publication outcomes only.
- Links proposal strategy, review outcomes, and exploratory science through [proposal-burden research-time crowding](../mechanisms/proposal_burden_research_time_crowding.md).

## Operationalization

- Count conventional grant applications before and after entry into a protected funding program.
- Compare treated scientists to matched or weighted controls with similar career stage, field, institution, and pre-treatment output.
- Decompose funding changes into application volume, conditional review scores, awards conditional on applying, and total funding.
- Track review-panel outcomes with measures such as [NIH peer-review percentile score](nih_peer_review_percentile_score.md).
- Interpret application substitution alongside [funding selection-treatment split](../methods/funding_selection_treatment_split.md) so reduced grant income is not mistaken for lower scientific quality.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Azoulay, Graff Zivin, and Manso (2009) states that their data can separate changes in NIH funding due to application behavior from changes due to NIH review-panel scoring.
- The paper reports that HHMI investigators submit roughly 60 percent as many R01 applications as comparable early-career-prize controls after appointment.
- Azoulay et al. also report that, conditional on applying, HHMI investigators receive worse NIH priority scores, which they interpret as evidence that conventional review can penalize exploratory work.
- This makes application substitution a necessary denominator check before interpreting post-treatment NIH funding as either crowd-out, rejection, or reduced need.

## Caveats

- Lower application volume can reflect resource substitution, time reallocation, strategic avoidance of hostile review, or lower need for bridge funding.
- Conditional review scores can be selected if only the most NIH-compatible projects are submitted after protected funding.
- Application-substitution measures should be paired with output, topic-shift, and funding-portfolio outcomes.
