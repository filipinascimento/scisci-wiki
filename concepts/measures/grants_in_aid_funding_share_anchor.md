# Grants-in-Aid funding-share anchor

## Summary

Japan's MEXT Grants-in-Aid system provides a source-specific anchor for the competitive-funding share in a mixed funding regime.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: research project, funding source, MEXT Grants-in-Aid, internal funds, competitive-fund ratio, or survey response.
- Typical representation: source-share measure for competitive Grants-in-Aid funding.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: empirical weight of competitive funding within Japanese university research.
- Empirical signature: Grants-in-Aid contributes a substantial but not dominant share of project funding in the surveyed system..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines competitive-funding measurement by linking it to [competitive funding share threshold](competitive_funding_share_threshold.md) and [grant competitiveness heterogeneity attenuation](../validations/grant_competitiveness_heterogeneity_attenuation.md).
- Useful as a reusable distinction when [fractional project funding attribution](fractional_project_funding_attribution.md) is analyzed in science-of-science data.
- Adds cross-links to [project funding source survey linkage](../datasets/project_funding_source_survey_linkage.md) so the motif is not read as an isolated result.

## Operationalization

- Use project funding shares to distinguish internal funds, Grants-in-Aid, other competitive funds, and noncompetitive grants.
- Report Grants-in-Aid share as a source-specific anchor rather than only a binary competitive indicator.
- Compare source shares across fields, ranks, and institution types.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang et al. (2018) reports internal funds as the largest source and Grants-in-Aid as the second-largest source, about one-third of project funds.
- The motif anchors mixed-funding measures in a recognizable national program.

## Caveats

- Near competitive-funding share thresholds; this concept is source-specific rather than a general mechanism.
- Survey-based project shares can differ from administrative award shares.
