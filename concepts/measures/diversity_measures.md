# Diversity measures

## Summary

Diversity measures quantify how a portfolio, paper, field, or reference set is distributed across categories while accounting for variety, balance, and disparity among those categories.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, reference list, team, institution, field, funding portfolio, technology portfolio, or science-map region.
- Typical representation: distribution over categories plus a distance or similarity matrix among categories.
- Mechanism or measurement target: breadth of options, evenness of allocation, and cognitive or structural distance among categories.
- Empirical signature: diversity changes when category count, category balance, or inter-category distance changes.

## Operationalization

- Assign items to categories such as fields, journals, topics, technology classes, or map regions.
- Estimate the share of items in each category and the disparity or distance between category pairs.
- Report decomposed components where possible: variety, balance, and disparity.
- Use a composite heuristic or Rao-Stirling-style diversity measure when the application requires a compact scalar.
- Audit component proxies, aggregation weights, and disparity assumptions with [diversity proxy-substitution risk](../validations/diversity_proxy_substitution_risk.md), [diversity aggregation-weight sensitivity](../validations/diversity_aggregation_weight_sensitivity.md), and [perspective-dependent disparity space](../representations/perspective_dependent_disparity_space.md).

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Stirling (2007) identifies three necessary but individually insufficient diversity properties: variety, balance, and disparity.
- Stirling argues for a general quantitative non-parametric diversity heuristic that can explore alternative perspectives, category definitions, and property weightings instead of treating diversity as one fixed formula.
- The subcomponents are now split as [category variety](category_variety.md), [distributional balance](distributional_balance.md), and [category disparity](category_disparity.md), with [Rao-Stirling diversity](rao_stirling_diversity.md) storing the compact share-weighted composite.
- The full text places diversity analysis in science and technology policy, research governance, innovation, and portfolio analysis, making it directly relevant to science-of-science evaluation and strategy.
- A later Stirling-focused pass separates the policy and appraisal layer into [science and technology diversity governance](../mechanisms/science_technology_diversity_governance.md), [diversity-as-option rhetoric risk](../validations/diversity_as_option_rhetoric_risk.md), [aggregation-accommodation-articulation challenge](../validations/aggregation_accommodation_articulation_challenge.md), [performance-space value representation](../representations/performance_space_value_representation.md), and [deliberative diversity sensitivity analysis](../methods/deliberative_diversity_sensitivity_analysis.md).
- A further residual pass adds scope conditions for using a general heuristic, boundaries between parametric and non-parametric approaches, taxonomy partition sensitivity, distance-geometry assumptions, and option-scale constraints in diversity-performance appraisal.
- Verified full-text evidence already integrated from Porter and Rafols (2009) and Wang, Thijs, and Glanzel (2015) shows how the same diversity components appear in bibliometric interdisciplinarity measurement.
- The more specific [interdisciplinarity dimensions](interdisciplinarity_dimensions.md) page keeps these components visible when they are used to study paper-level knowledge integration.

## Caveats

- Diversity scores are sensitive to the category system, distance matrix, and weighting of variety, balance, and disparity.
- A single scalar can hide whether diversity comes from many nearby categories, a balanced spread, or a few distant combinations.
- Diversity is not always beneficial by itself; interpretation depends on the evaluation goal and trade-offs.
- Before interpreting a score, check whether the taxonomy, distance geometry, and option constraints are part of the intended construct.
