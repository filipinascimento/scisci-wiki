# Citation-window impact split

## Summary

Citation-window impact split compares short-term and long-term citation outcomes to show whether a paper receives immediate attention, delayed influence, or both.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, team, author, field-year cohort, journal, or topic.
- Typical representation: early citation count, late citation count, window-specific percentile, or short-long citation contrast.
- Measurement target: timing of recognition rather than only total citation volume.
- Empirical signature: one mechanism or team form can increase short-term citations while another increases long-term influence.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Helps evaluate [team hierarchy and flatness](../mechanisms/team_hierarchy_flatness.md) without collapsing immediate attention and long-term influence.
- Links role-aware team science to [citation aging and obsolescence](../mechanisms/citation_aging_obsolescence.md), [long-term citation prediction](../methods/long_term_citation_prediction.md), and [responsible metrics](responsible_metrics.md).
- Pairs with [developmental index](developmental_index.md), because developmental work can receive rapid attention while flatter teams may show longer-run influence.
- Provides the citation-timing evidence for [team hierarchy credit-risk hedge](../mechanisms/team_hierarchy_credit_risk_hedge.md), where tall teams trade longer-run influence for more immediate attention.

## Operationalization

- Define explicit citation windows before analysis.
- Count or normalize citations in each window using field-year baselines where possible.
- Compare window-specific outcomes across team size, hierarchy, novelty, or policy treatments.
- Report whether the long-term window excludes the short-term period or uses cumulative citations.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Xu, Wu, and Evans (2022) measures short-term impact as citations within ten years and long-term impact as citations after twenty years.
- The paper reports that low-L-ratio teams receive more short-term citations, while high-L-ratio teams experience greater long-term influence.
- Figure 2 in the full text presents high-L-ratio teams as having more long-term impact but less short-term impact.

## Caveats

- Recent papers cannot yet support long-term windows without forecasting.
- Citation windows interact with field pace, publication year, and database coverage.
- A short-long split can miss noncitation influence, policy uptake, software adoption, or clinical translation.
