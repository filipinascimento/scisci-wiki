# Role-specific same-year productivity

## Summary

Team-flatness studies can measure same-year productivity separately for authors occupying lead and support roles.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author-paper role, focal publication year, author output count, lead role, support role, or L-ratio percentile.
- Typical representation: role-stratified same-year publication-count outcome.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: short-run productivity redistribution across lead and support roles.
- Empirical signature: lead-author and support-author productivity gradients differ as L-ratio changes.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines role-specific productivity measurement by linking it to [team hierarchy productivity redistribution](../mechanisms/team_hierarchy_productivity_redistribution.md) and [lead direct indirect support taxonomy](../representations/lead_direct_indirect_support_taxonomy.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [lead role ratio](lead_role_ratio.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [scientific career impact](../mechanisms/scientific_career_impact.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Assign lead and support roles at the author-paper level.
- Count each author's publications in the focal publication year.
- Aggregate productivity by role and L-ratio percentile with team-size and career-stage controls.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified local full text from Xu et al. (2022) defines productivity as same-year publication count and analyzes lead-author and support-author productivity separately.
- This makes productivity redistribution a role-specific outcome rather than a single team-level output measure.

## Caveats

- Same-year output may precede, follow, or be independent of the focal team structure.
- Publication count is an incomplete measure of research labor and opportunity.
