# Gendered discipline output sorting

## Summary

Gendered discipline output sorting measures how inferred gender representation varies across disciplines or specialties rather than only across countries or all science combined.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: discipline, specialty, subject category, field-year cell, or gendered fractional authorship aggregate.
- Typical representation: female-to-male authorship ratio by field, field-ranked parity table, or discipline heat map.
- Measurement target: field composition of gendered publication output.
- Empirical signature: some fields show near parity or female-majority authorship while others remain strongly male-dominated.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Splits [gendered fractional authorship ratio](gendered_fractional_authorship_ratio.md) into discipline and specialty cells.
- Provides the field layer for [gender parity pocket maps](../representations/gender_parity_pocket_maps.md).
- Helps separate geographic gender disparity from [field classifications](field_classifications.md) and country field-mix effects.
- Supplies descriptive evidence for [gender and race stratification](../mechanisms/gender_race_stratification.md) and follow-up studies on disciplinary cultures.

## Operationalization

- Assign papers to disciplines or specialties using a documented classification scheme.
- Infer or observe author gender and fractionalize gendered authorships within each paper.
- Aggregate female and male fractional authorships by field and compute field-level ratios or shares.
- Report whether papers in multiple fields are counted fractionally, wholly in each field, or assigned to one primary field.
- Pair the field table with coverage and minimum-size checks before interpreting extreme ratios.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Lariviere et al. (2013) aggregates gendered fractional authorships across disciplines and specialties in a 2008-2012 Web of Science corpus.
- The paper reports higher female representation in care-associated fields such as nursing, midwifery, education, social work, and librarianship.
- It also identifies strongly male-dominated areas including engineering, robotics, aeronautics, high-energy physics, mathematics, computer science, philosophy, and economics.
- The same evidence shows that disciplinary variation is large enough that country-level gender ratios should not be interpreted without a field-composition layer.

## Caveats

- Discipline labels are database-specific and can mix departments, journals, specialties, and intellectual topics.
- Field cells with low output can create unstable ratios.
- Gendered field sorting is descriptive unless paired with evidence on mechanisms, training pathways, hiring, seniority, or publication norms.
- Binary name-based gender assignment omits many identities and can have uneven coverage across countries and languages.
