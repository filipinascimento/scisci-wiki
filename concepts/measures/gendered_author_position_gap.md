# Gendered author-position gap

## Summary

Gendered author-position gap measures whether women and men appear at different rates in prominent byline positions, such as first, last, or sole author, rather than only in aggregate authorship counts.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, authorship, author position, inferred gender group, field, country, or collaboration type.
- Typical representation: female-to-male first-author ratio, female-to-male last-author ratio, sole-author gender ratio, or dominant-position share.
- Measurement target: gendered visibility, credit, leadership, and seniority encoded in byline positions.
- Empirical signature: the gender ratio in prominent positions differs from the ratio across all authorships.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Splits the author-position layer out of [gendered fractional authorship ratio](gendered_fractional_authorship_ratio.md).
- Supplies the position denominator for [lead-author gender citation gap](../mechanisms/lead_author_gender_citation_gap.md).
- Connects gender-disparity analysis to general [author-position citation components](author_position_citation_components.md) and [first-author publication frame](../methods/first_author_publication_frame.md) designs.
- Helps distinguish broad participation gaps from gaps in visible or leadership-coded authorship.

## Operationalization

- Assign inferred gender to authorships with a documented method such as [given-name gender assignment](../methods/given_name_gender_assignment.md).
- Identify first, last, and sole-authored papers, while marking fields where byline order is alphabetical or otherwise noncontributory.
- Compute female-to-male ratios or shares for each position category and compare them with all-authorship ratios.
- Stratify by field, country, collaboration type, and year when the data allow.
- Pair the measure with [gendered seniority-pipeline confound](../validations/gendered_seniority_pipeline_confound.md) before interpreting position differences as current bias alone.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Lariviere et al. (2013) reports that women are underrepresented not only in fractionalized authorships but also in first authorships.
- The paper states that for every article with a female first author, there are nearly two articles first-authored by men.
- Lariviere et al. also analyze sole, first, and last author positions when comparing gendered citation impact.
- The authors warn that author-position interpretation is limited by field-specific authorship norms, including alphabetical ordering.

## Caveats

- Author position has different meaning across fields, journals, and collaboration cultures.
- First or last authorship can proxy for contribution, seniority, or lab leadership, but it is not a universal role label.
- Inferred gender missingness can vary across fields and countries, changing the denominator.
- Position gaps can reflect seniority structure, collaboration access, funding, and field composition as well as direct discrimination.
