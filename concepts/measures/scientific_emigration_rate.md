# Scientific emigration rate

## Summary

Scientific emigration rate measures the proportion of scientists from an origin country who work or study outside that country.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: origin country, scientist, field, cohort, or survey population.
- Typical representation: percentage of origin-country scientists located abroad at a focal time.
- Measurement target: outward mobility or potential brain-drain pressure from an origin research system.
- Empirical signature: high rates indicate that many scientists whose origin is a country are currently active elsewhere.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Complements destination-side [foreign-born scientist share](foreign_born_scientist_share.md) with an origin-side measure.
- Supports [international mobility typologies](international_mobility_typologies.md) by distinguishing immigration and emigration views of the same flows.
- Helps parameterize [brain circulation networks](../representations/brain_circulation_networks.md) and country-level talent-flow models.
- Links to [scientist return mobility rate](scientist_return_mobility_rate.md) when outward mobility may be temporary, circular, or conditional on jobs.

## Operationalization

- Define each scientist's origin country using [country-at-18 origin proxy](../methods/country_at_18_origin_proxy.md), birth country, first publication country, or another documented origin measure.
- Determine current country of work, study, or affiliation.
- For each origin country, divide the number of origin-country scientists currently abroad by the total number of origin-country scientists observed.
- Apply survey weights or coverage corrections where response or database coverage varies.
- Report principal destination countries to separate broad emigration from dependence on one destination.
- Pair the origin-side rate with [scientific destination-hub dominance](scientific_destination_hub_dominance.md) when many origin countries send emigrant scientists to the same destination.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Franzoni et al. (2012) computes the proportion of scientists who lived in each core country at age 18 but were working or studying outside that country in 2011.
- The paper reports India as highest, with 39.8 percent of scientists from India working or studying abroad, followed by Switzerland at about one-third.
- The Netherlands and the United Kingdom are next, with about one-fourth of their scientists abroad.
- Japan has the lowest reported emigration rate at 3.1 percent, with the United States close behind at 5.0 percent.
- Franzoni et al. also report that the United States is the top destination for emigrants from 13 of the other 15 core countries and the second most likely destination for the remaining two.
- That destination concentration is split out as [scientific destination-hub dominance](scientific_destination_hub_dominance.md), because it is a separate structure from each origin country's emigration rate.

## Caveats

- A high emigration rate is not necessarily permanent brain drain; it may represent training, postdoctoral mobility, dual careers, or later return.
- Origin-country denominators depend on the sampled population and field coverage.
- Survey-based emigration rates can miss scientists from the origin country who no longer publish in sampled fields or journals.
