# Collaborator resource-control proxies

## Summary

Collaborator resource-control proxies measure whether a dominant scientist's collaborator network holds funding, editorial, or financial positions that can preserve incumbent influence after the focal scientist exits.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: star scientist, collaborator set, subfield, funding panel, editorial proxy, grant share, or barrier stratum.
- Typical representation: counts of collaborators in gatekeeping roles, collaborator editorial activity, or collaborator share of field funding.
- Measurement target: indirect control over field resources through the incumbent collaboration network.
- Empirical signature: outsider entry is weaker when collaborators hold more gatekeeping positions or control more field resources.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds an incumbent-network channel to [star-scientist entry deterrence](../mechanisms/star_scientist_entry_deterrence.md).
- Connects [invisible colleges](../mechanisms/invisible_college.md) and [peer review and gatekeeping](../mechanisms/peer_review_gatekeeping.md) to measurable field-entry barriers.
- Complements [subfield coherence barrier measures](subfield_coherence_barrier_measures.md), which capture intellectual and social closure rather than resource control.
- Provides a design template for studying editorial boards, grant panels, prize committees, or data-infrastructure steering groups.

## Operationalization

- Identify the focal scientist's pre-event collaborators.
- Count collaborators who served on funding panels in a window before the event.
- Use editorial publications, comments, or known board rosters as proxies for editorial influence when direct board data are unavailable.
- Compute the share of field funding or acknowledged grants associated with the collaborator network.
- Split fields by high and low collaborator resource control and compare outsider-entry responses.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Azoulay, Fons-Rosen, and Graff Zivin (2019) first rules out direct gatekeeping by stars: only three of 452 deceased stars sat on NIH study sections and three were journal editors in the five years before death.
- The paper then measures indirect control through collaborators' NIH study-section membership, editorial/comment publication activity, and field funding shares.
- Subfield expansion is more pronounced when stars have fewer collaborators in influential positions or when collaborators capture less field funding.
- The authors interpret these split-sample patterns as conditional correlations rather than randomized causal effects of resource control.
- Omitting the few stars with direct study-section or editor roles does not change the core findings.

## Caveats

- Editorial proxies can miss actual editorial board roles and overstate influence from invited comments.
- Funding-panel membership and funding share are only partial measures of field resource control.
- High collaborator funding share can also reflect expertise, infrastructure requirements, or field maturity rather than exclusionary control.
