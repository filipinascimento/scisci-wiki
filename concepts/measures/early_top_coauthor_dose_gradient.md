# Early Top-Coauthor Dose Gradient

## Summary

Early top-coauthor dose gradient measures whether later career success increases with the number of top-cited collaborators encountered in the early-career window.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: junior researcher, early-career paper, top coauthor, discipline, or cohort.
- Typical representation: no top coauthor, one top coauthor, multiple top coauthors.
- Measurement target: intensity of early elite-collaboration exposure.
- Empirical signature: later top-scientist probability rises from none to single to multiple early top-coauthor exposure.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [coauthor prestige exposure](coauthor_prestige_exposure.md) from a binary exposure into an intensity measure.
- Connects early exposure to [repeated top-coauthor access](../mechanisms/repeated_top_coauthor_access.md).
- Provides a quantitative input to [early-career sponsorship by top scientists](../mechanisms/early_career_sponsorship.md).

## Operationalization

- Define an early-career window, such as the first three years after first publication.
- Count unique top-cited coauthors in that window.
- Group junior researchers into none, one, or multiple exposure categories.
- Compare later career outcomes across exposure levels, controlling for baseline career indicators where possible.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Li et al. (2019) groups junior researchers by whether they had no, one, or multiple early top coauthors.
- The paper reports that the probability of becoming a top-cited scientist is higher for those with one top scientist and higher again for those with multiple top scientists.
- The figure caption frames this as a relationship between institutional prestige, early top-coauthor exposure, and later top-scientist probability.

## Caveats

- Dose can reflect selection into elite labs or projects rather than exposure alone.
- Counting unique top coauthors does not capture tie strength, contribution, or mentor role.
- Very high exposure may be confounded with institutional prestige and early productivity.
