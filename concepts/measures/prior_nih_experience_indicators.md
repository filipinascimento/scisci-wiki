# Prior NIH experience indicators

## Summary

Prior NIH experience indicators measure whether a grant applicant has already received NIH research or career awards, served in NIH review roles, or otherwise accumulated experience inside the NIH funding system.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: applicant, R01 application, prior award, review-service episode, or NIH experience history.
- Typical representation: binary or count indicators for prior NIH RPG awards, K awards, review committee service, and previous NIH exposure.
- Measurement target: accumulated system-specific knowledge, credibility, and familiarity with NIH proposal evaluation.
- Empirical signature: prior awards and review service predict later award probability, but their association can differ across demographic groups.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Gives a concrete administrative measure of cumulative advantage in [peer review and gatekeeping](../mechanisms/peer_review_gatekeeping.md).
- Supports [NIH R01 award disparities](../validations/nih_r01_award_disparities.md) by separating prior NIH-system experience from current application evaluation.
- Complements [NIH training support indicators](nih_training_support_indicators.md), which measure earlier training-pipeline support.
- Links to [scientific-resource cumulative advantage](../mechanisms/scientific_resource_cumulative_advantage.md) and [matthew effect](../mechanisms/matthew_effect.md).

## Operationalization

- Link applicants to prior NIH research project grants, career-development awards, and review committee service before the focal application date.
- Distinguish prior funding from prior reviewing, because one measures resource receipt and the other measures inside-system evaluation experience.
- Use lagged indicators or counts to avoid including current-cycle outcomes.
- Estimate whether the same prior-experience variables have similar associations with review and award outcomes across demographic groups.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ginther et al. (2011) includes prior NIH awards and NIH review committee service in models of R01 award probability.
- The paper reports that previous NIH research project or career awards and previous NIH review committee service are each associated with substantially higher R01 award probabilities in the reported models.
- The verified text also states that variables associated with higher award probability in the full sample do not always have the same association for Black applicants, especially for priority score receipt.
- This makes prior NIH experience both a control variable and a possible mechanism through which cumulative advantage enters the funding process.

## Caveats

- Prior NIH experience is itself produced by earlier selection and may mediate past inequality.
- Review service is not randomly assigned and likely reflects reputation, network position, and institutional access.
- Prior awards can proxy resources, preliminary data, lab stability, and grant-writing knowledge.
- These indicators should be measured before the focal application and should not be interpreted as pure applicant quality.
