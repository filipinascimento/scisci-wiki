# Biomedical need-resource misalignment

## Summary

Biomedical need-resource misalignment measures whether biomedical research funding and activity track actual health burdens or instead follow previous funding allocations, existing research capacity, or interest-group pressure.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: disease, health condition, funder program, research area, year, country, or funding portfolio.
- Typical representation: disease-burden-to-funding ratio, correlation between funding and burden, residual funding gap, or allocation-history dependence.
- Measurement target: alignment between societal health need and scientific resource allocation.
- Empirical signature: funding or publication activity correlates more strongly with past allocations or prior research volume than with disease burden.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a policy-facing measure for [funding-allocation design alternatives](../methods/funding_allocation_design_alternatives.md) and [societal-needs assessment alignment](../methods/societal_needs_assessment_alignment.md).
- Connects resource allocation to [responsible metrics](responsible_metrics.md), because productivity indicators can steer investment away from need.
- Helps evaluate whether funding agencies embedded in established paradigms can redirect portfolios without additional oversight, incentives, and feedback.
- Complements [scientific-resource cumulative advantage](../mechanisms/scientific_resource_cumulative_advantage.md) by focusing on topic and disease portfolios rather than institutions.

## Operationalization

- Define health need with disease burden metrics such as mortality, morbidity, DALYs, prevalence, costs, patient-reported burden, or public-health priority.
- Measure resource allocation with funding amounts, grants, publications, clinical trials, patents, or research workforce by disease area.
- Compare alignment against historical allocation, prior publication volume, advocacy intensity, commercial incentives, and field capacity.
- Report field and country scope, disease taxonomy, funder coverage, and lag between burden measurement and funding decision.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) states that U.S. biomedical resource allocation is more strongly correlated with previous allocations and research than with actual disease burden.
- The review frames this as a systemic misalignment between biomedical needs and resources.
- Fortunato et al. argue that this misalignment raises doubts about whether funding agencies embedded in established paradigms can steer science without additional oversight, incentives, and feedback.
- This makes need-resource alignment a science-policy measure, not only a health-economics statistic.

## Caveats

- Disease burden is not the only legitimate allocation criterion; tractability, neglectedness, scientific opportunity, equity, and public values also matter.
- Prior funding can create capacity that makes future research more effective, so historical dependence is not automatically waste.
- Need-resource comparisons depend on disease taxonomies and on how basic, translational, and cross-disease research are assigned.
