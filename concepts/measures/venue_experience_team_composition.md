# Venue-experience team composition

## Summary

A paper team can be described by the mix of members with no, junior, or senior prior experience in a target venue, not only by the last author's category.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper team within a venue history.
- Typical representation: team shares or counts by prior venue-experience role.
- Measurement target: distribution of venue know-how across the whole author team.
- Empirical signature: papers differ in whether venue experience is concentrated in the last author or distributed across coauthors.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Generalizes [venue PI experience categories](venue_pi_experience_categories.md) from last-author labels to team composition.
- Useful for studying how venue-specific knowledge is pooled, transferred, or concentrated within teams.
- Connects venue history to [newcomer-incumbent team mix](newcomer_incumbent_team_mix.md).

## Operationalization

- For each author on a paper, classify prior target-venue history as none, non-last-only, last-author, or mixed.
- Compute counts, shares, maximum prior experience, and role-weighted measures by author order.
- Compare models using last-author-only categories with models using full-team composition.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sekara et al. (2018) defines senior-author categories by prior same-journal histories and illustrates heterogeneous author histories within teams.
- The paper's design motivates extending venue-experience measurement to the whole team when author-order data permit.

## Caveats

- This is an extension beyond the paper's main senior-author-centered operationalization.
- Author-order conventions and equal-contribution statements can complicate role weighting.
