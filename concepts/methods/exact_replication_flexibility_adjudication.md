# Exact-replication flexibility adjudication

## Summary

Exact-replication flexibility adjudication is a method for resolving claims that depend on arbitrary analytic or design choices by requiring a direct replication with the relevant choices specified in advance.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: contested finding, reviewer request, replication protocol, confirmatory study, or decision letter.
- Typical representation: exact-replication protocol, preregistered analysis plan, robustness-decision tree, or reviewer adjudication note.
- Method target: turn an initially flexible or exploratory result into a prospective test without changing the design into a looser conceptual replication.
- Empirical signature: a result is accepted as provisional until a same-design replication reproduces it under pre-specified sampling, exclusion, covariate, condition, and outcome rules.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides an enforcement endpoint for [reviewer transparency enforcement](reviewer_transparency_enforcement.md) when author justifications for arbitrary choices are weak.
- Distinguishes direct confirmatory follow-up from conceptual replication, which can leave the original analytic flexibility unresolved.
- Links [researcher degrees of freedom](../validations/researcher_degrees_of_freedom.md) to practical review decisions: disclose, justify, test robustness, or replicate exactly.
- Supports policy experiments that compare acceptance, correction, and replication outcomes under different reviewer requirements.

## Operationalization

- Identify findings whose significance changes across reasonable exclusions, covariates, outcomes, stopping rules, or condition subsets.
- Ask authors to specify the exact design, sample rule, outcome, model, covariate set, exclusion rule, and comparison before a new data collection.
- Treat the follow-up as confirmatory only if those decisions are fixed before observing the new outcomes.
- Record whether exact-replication requests change editorial decisions, effect sizes, p-values, or later citation and correction trajectories.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Simmons et al. (2011) states that when justifications for arbitrary analytic decisions are not compelling, reviewers should require exact replications.
- The same paper argues that exact replications can confirm exploratory findings without pretending the original flexible analysis was prospectively specified.
- Simmons et al. contrast this remedy with conceptual replications, which can vary enough design and analytic choices that the original flexibility remains untested.
- The method is therefore a bridge between [exploratory-confirmatory disclosure boundary](../validations/exploratory_confirmatory_disclosure_boundary.md) and stricter preregistered or registered-report workflows.
- This contrast is split out as [conceptual replication flexibility leak](../validations/conceptual_replication_flexibility_leak.md), which marks the boundary where a conceptual follow-up no longer adjudicates the original analytic path.

## Caveats

- Exact replication can be costly, slow, or infeasible for rare populations, archival shocks, or one-time events.
- A successful exact replication does not validate every broader theory or conceptual variant.
- A failed exact replication can still reflect implementation differences, power, or context sensitivity.
- Review systems need criteria for when exact replication is proportionate rather than defaulting to infeasible demands.
