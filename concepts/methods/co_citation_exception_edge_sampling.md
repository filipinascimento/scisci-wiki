# Co-citation exception-edge sampling

## Summary

Uses the strongest and most discordant co-citation edges as interpretive cases, turning edge outliers into a qualitative validation sample.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: co-citation edge selected for interpretation.
- Typical representation: ranked high-weight or disagreement-edge sample for full-text review.
- Method target: qualitative explanation of edge strength or relation-type disagreement.
- Empirical signature: sampled edge cases reveal why a map links particular works.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [co-citation edge-context interpretation](co_citation_edge_context_interpretation.md) with a sampling rule.
- Useful for validating maps where only a subset of edges can be read closely.
- Connects ordinal edge weight to qualitative citation-context review.

## Operationalization

- Rank co-citation edges by weight and by disagreement with direct citation or bibliographic coupling.
- Sample high-weight and discordant edges for citation-context or full-text review.
- Code relation types such as shared method, theoretical transfer, shared problem, or canonical background.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Small (1973) highlights strongest and puzzling co-citation linkages and asks why those linkages are strong.
- The text uses edge outliers as interpretive evidence about the mapped specialty.

## Caveats

- Extreme edges are informative but not representative of the whole map.
- Manual explanation can overfit historically salient examples.
