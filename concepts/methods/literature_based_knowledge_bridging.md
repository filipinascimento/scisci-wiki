# Literature-based knowledge bridging

## Summary

Literature-based knowledge bridging is the search method of mining published knowledge to connect separated knowledge areas, exposing plausible relationships that ordinary local research strategies leave untested.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: knowledge cluster, concept pair, chemical pair, literature corpus, candidate bridge, or discovery workflow.
- Typical representation: disconnected or weakly connected clusters, cross-cluster candidate links, literature-mined hypotheses, or bridge-priority lists.
- Method target: identify nonlocal combinations that may be scientifically fruitful but are underexplored by routine search.
- Empirical signature: candidate links join established islands of knowledge and are rare relative to local consolidations or repeat work.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Turns [research strategy taxonomy](research_strategy_taxonomy.md) from a descriptive classification into a search intervention.
- Uses [map-equation knowledge clusters](map_equation_knowledge_clusters.md) or related community-detection methods to define knowledge islands and bridging opportunities.
- Provides a concrete response to [knowledge-use narrowing](../mechanisms/knowledge_use_narrowing.md) and the [mature-field exploration gap](../mechanisms/mature_field_exploration_gap.md).
- Connects library mining, ontologies, and science maps to [scientific problem choice](../mechanisms/scientific_problem_choice.md).
- Complements [co-citation secondary indexing](co_citation_secondary_indexing.md), because both use literature structure to reveal non-obvious conceptual relations.

## Operationalization

- Build a content-level knowledge network from entities, relationships, annotations, citations, or co-occurrences.
- Partition the network into local knowledge clusters or research fronts.
- Identify candidate links that connect clusters, disconnected components, or weakly connected regions.
- Filter candidates with domain constraints, evidence strength, novelty, feasibility, and potential downstream value.
- Compare literature-mined bridge candidates with observed researcher choices to estimate underexplored regions.
- Track whether proposed bridges later appear in papers, patents, clinical applications, or other validation corpora.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Foster, Rzhetsky, and Evans (2015) frames research strategies as structurally distinct contributions to a network of scientific knowledge, including bridges that connect distinct knowledge clusters.
- Foster et al. argue that biomedical chemistry contains expanding opportunities for new and bridging relationships, yet scientists increasingly concentrate on established and local knowledge.
- Their discussion states that science benefits when individuals overcome preferences for established islands of knowledge and links this to early breakthroughs in literature-based discovery.
- The same full text points to library mining, ontologies, and communities as a way to advance science by linking knowledge islands.

## Caveats

- Literature-mined bridge candidates can be biologically, technically, or theoretically impossible even when they look structurally promising.
- Published literature misses failed, negative, and proprietary experiments, so bridge opportunity estimates inherit publication bias.
- A bridge in one representation can be local in another; entity definitions and clustering choices must be documented.
- Search tools can overwhelm researchers with weak candidates unless paired with domain filtering and validation plans.
