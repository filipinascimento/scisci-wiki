# Indicator lifecycle review

## Summary

Indicator lifecycle review is the periodic scrutiny, revision, replacement, or retirement of research indicators as missions, data, incentives, and research systems change.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: indicator, dashboard, evaluation policy, ranking system, data provider, field, or assessment cycle.
- Typical representation: scheduled metric review, versioned indicator policy, retirement rule, post-implementation audit, or update log.
- Method target: prevent once-useful indicators from becoming stale, gamed, misaligned, or invalid.
- Empirical signature: indicator definitions and evaluation policies are revised after evidence on use, misuse, coverage, or mission change.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Makes [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) a recurring governance process rather than a one-time design choice.
- Complements [scholarly snapshot versioning](scholarly_snapshot_versioning.md) for the policy layer: both data and indicators age.
- Connects [indicator systemic effects](../mechanisms/indicator_systemic_effects.md) to concrete policy maintenance.
- Supports policy experiments in which evaluation reforms are monitored and updated.

## Operationalization

- Set review intervals for every consequential indicator.
- Monitor validity, coverage, gaming, field fit, mission alignment, and behavioral effects.
- Version indicator definitions and report changes to users.
- Retire or replace indicators when they no longer match the evaluation target or create unacceptable incentives.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hicks et al. (2015) says indicators should be scrutinized regularly and updated.
- Hicks et al. argue that research missions and assessment goals shift and that the research system co-evolves with indicators.
- The article notes that once-useful metrics can become inadequate and that new ones emerge.
- Hicks et al. use Australia's move from a simple publication-count formula to the Excellence in Research for Australia initiative as an example of policy revision after recognizing systemic effects.

## Caveats

- Frequent indicator changes can reduce comparability over time.
- Review processes can be captured by stakeholders who benefit from existing metrics.
- Replacement indicators should be evaluated before being installed as new targets.
