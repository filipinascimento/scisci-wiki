# Complete transparent reporting rewards

## Summary

Complete transparent reporting rewards are assessment criteria that give credit for making all research available, reporting it completely, and documenting methods and results regardless of whether findings are positive, null, or inconvenient.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: researcher, study, clinical trial, project portfolio, promotion dossier, journal, funder, or institution.
- Typical representation: reporting-compliance audit, registered-study completion record, preprint or repository deposit, reporting-guideline checklist, or CV field.
- Method target: convert complete dissemination and transparent reporting into rewarded scholarly behavior.
- Empirical signature: researchers receive assessment credit for registering, completing, depositing, publishing, and transparently reporting all studies rather than only high-impact positive findings.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Splits the reporting-specific principle out of [responsible researcher assessment principles](responsible_researcher_assessment_principles.md).
- Gives a promotion-and-tenure implementation layer for [publication bias and selective reporting](../mechanisms/publication_bias_selective_reporting.md).
- Connects [open science interventions](../validations/open_science_interventions.md), [registered-report in-principle acceptance](../validations/registered_report_in_principle_acceptance.md), and [registry-publication reporting-bias audit](registry_publication_reporting_bias_audit.md).
- Provides a concrete alternative to rewarding only publication counts, venue prestige, or citation impact.

## Operationalization

- Identify studies, trials, or projects that should have been registered, completed, reported, or deposited.
- Track registration numbers, registered-report participation, completed-study outputs, repository deposits, and reporting-guideline adherence.
- Include reporting evidence in CVs, annual reviews, grant reports, and promotion dossiers.
- Use audit-and-feedback tools where available, especially for clinical-trial registration and results reporting.
- Evaluate whether the reward policy reduces nonpublication, selective reporting, and incomplete dissemination.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Moher et al. (2018) identifies rewarding complete and transparent publication of all research as the third principle for assessing scientists.
- Table 2 states that reporting-bias and publication-bias problems motivate rewarding formal publication, preprints, repositories, and other ways of making research available.
- Moher et al. discuss reporting guidelines and registered reports as mechanisms for improving complete and transparent reporting.
- The paper recommends that funders and academic institutions reward registering planned studies and publishing or depositing completed studies.
- The implementation section proposes adding registration names and numbers, registered-report participation, and completed-study citations to researcher CVs.

## Caveats

- Counting deposits or registrations can reward formal compliance without high-quality reporting.
- Complete reporting may be hard to audit for nonclinical, exploratory, or long-running projects.
- Null and negative results need interpretable context, not just availability.
- Reward systems should avoid penalizing researchers for legitimate delays caused by privacy, safety, or participant-protection constraints.
