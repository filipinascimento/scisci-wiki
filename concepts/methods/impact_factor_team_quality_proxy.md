# Impact-factor team-quality proxy

## Summary

Impact-factor team-quality proxy is the method of using a journal's impact factor as a field-internal proxy for the typical quality or performance of teams publishing in that venue.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: journal, field, team, article set, or team-assembly parameter estimate.
- Typical representation: journal-level impact factor aligned with field-level collaboration parameters.
- Method target: approximate team output quality when article-level long-run impact is unavailable or when analysis is organized by venue.
- Empirical signature: collaboration regimes are compared across journals ranked by impact factor.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Supplies the performance proxy used in [team assembly performance gradient](../validations/team_assembly_performance_gradient.md).
- Connects [journal impact factor](../measures/journal_impact_factor.md) to team-assembly research.
- Lets [team assembly parameter space](team_assembly_parameter_space.md) be related to outcome differences across journals.
- Provides a compact way to compare collaboration patterns across venues within a scientific domain.

## Operationalization

- Assign each journal a contemporaneous or study-period impact factor.
- Estimate team-assembly parameters or network measures separately by journal or journal tier.
- Rank journals by impact factor and correlate the ranking with collaboration parameters.
- Report whether the proxy is field-normalized or restricted to a single domain.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Guimera et al. (2005) uses journal impact factor as a proxy for the quality or performance of teams in scientific journals.
- The paper compares journal-level team-assembly parameters with impact-factor rankings using rank correlations.
- This proxy supports their claim that teams combining incumbents and newcomers with moderate repeat collaboration tend to be associated with higher-impact venues.

## Caveats

- Impact factor is a journal-level average and can misrepresent individual article or team quality.
- Field, review style, article type, and citation culture can confound cross-journal comparisons.
- The proxy is more defensible for within-field ranking than for broad cross-field performance claims.
