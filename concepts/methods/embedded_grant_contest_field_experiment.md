# Embedded grant-contest field experiment

## Summary

A real, incentive-bearing grant contest can be used as the experimental setting for studying frontier proposal evaluation.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: grant contest, proposal, evaluator, random assignment, prize, or second-stage funding opportunity.
- Typical representation: lab-in-the-field proposal-review experiment embedded in an active funding process.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: real-stakes experimental evidence on resource allocation in science.
- Empirical signature: randomized evaluator-proposal assignments operate inside an actual proposal call with meaningful prizes and funding paths.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines science-policy experiment design by linking it to [biomedical frontier review experiment](../datasets/biomedical_frontier_review_experiment.md) and [randomized reviewer proposal assignment](randomized_reviewer_proposal_assignment.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [triple blind proposal review](triple_blind_proposal_review.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [low entry cost proposal capture](low_entry_cost_proposal_capture.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Embed random assignment and blinding inside an active grant call.
- Preserve real incentives such as prizes, public recognition, and downstream funding eligibility.
- Record evaluator-proposal panels, proposal formats, review scores, and allocation rules.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Boudreau et al. (2016) describes collaboration with grant administrators, 150 proposals, 142 evaluators, cash prizes, public recognition, and second-stage seed-grant opportunities.
- This makes the design an embedded field experiment rather than a survey vignette.

## Caveats

- Administrative realism improves external validity but ties the design to one institution and disease area.
- Experimental control remains limited by the active funding context.
