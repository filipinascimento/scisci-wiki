# Creative-output document-type filter

## Summary

Breakthrough-output measures can exclude reviews, editorials, letters, and very large-author papers to better isolate original research.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: publication, document type, author count, citation tail, review article, editorial, letter, or team paper.
- Typical representation: document-type and author-count filter applied before creative-output measurement.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: denominator construction for originality and citation-tail outcomes.
- Empirical signature: tail-output estimates shift when nonresearch and very large-team papers are excluded from the candidate set..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines creative-output denominator construction by linking it to [vintage specific citation tail output](../measures/vintage_specific_citation_tail_output.md) and [citation percentile indicators](../measures/citation_percentile_indicators.md).

## Operationalization

- Apply document-type exclusions before constructing citation-tail or creative-output measures.
- Set and justify author-count thresholds for excluding large-team papers.
- Report robustness with and without reviews, editorials, letters, and large-author papers.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Azoulay et al. (2009) excludes review articles, editorials, letters, and papers with more than 20 authors from key creative-output measures.
- The filter helps align citation-tail indicators with original research output.

## Caveats

- Exclusions can remove genuinely creative syntheses or large-team breakthroughs.
- Document-type metadata can be noisy across databases.
