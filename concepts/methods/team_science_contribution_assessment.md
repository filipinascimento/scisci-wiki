# Team-science contribution assessment

## Summary

Team-science contribution assessment evaluates the distinct roles scientists play in collaborative work rather than relying only on author position, paper counts, or undifferentiated coauthorship.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: team member, faculty dossier, author role, grant or protocol contribution, collaborative project, promotion committee, or research group.
- Typical representation: contribution statement, role rubric, team-science dossier, protocol-design evidence, grant-design evidence, or contribution-weighted portfolio.
- Method target: give fair credit for collaborative scientific labor, especially when essential contributions are not first-author or senior-author outputs.
- Empirical signature: committees recognize role-specific contributions to design, analysis, protocol development, grants, tools, mentoring, coordination, and implementation.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides an assessment-policy counterpart to [author contribution statements](../datasets/author_contribution_statements.md) and [contribution role vectors](../representations/contribution_role_vectors.md).
- Extends [collective credit allocation](../measures/collective_credit_allocation.md) from measurement to hiring, promotion, and tenure decisions.
- Addresses credit problems created by [team hierarchy credit-risk hedge](../mechanisms/team_hierarchy_credit_risk_hedge.md), [senior coauthor credit shadow](../mechanisms/senior_coauthor_credit_shadow.md), and author-order conventions.
- Complements [mentoring-teaching-service assessment](mentoring_teaching_service_assessment.md) for support, training, and coordination work inside teams.
- Links responsible assessment to the broader shift toward [coauthorship networks](../representations/coauthorship_networks.md) and team science.

## Operationalization

- Ask candidates to document role-specific contributions to selected collaborative outputs.
- Use contribution statements, CRediT-like roles, grant and protocol records, letters from collaborators, and project documentation where available.
- Distinguish intellectual, methodological, software, data, clinical, coordination, mentoring, and service contributions.
- Avoid treating first or senior authorship as the only evidence of leadership or contribution.
- Audit role-credit rules for field differences, team-size effects, gender or seniority bias, and compatibility with [broad-output assessment portfolios](broad_output_assessment_portfolio.md).

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Moher et al. (2018) reviews DORA recommendations that publishers identify different author contributions.
- The paper discusses a framework for rewarding biostatisticians' contributions to team science, including design of grant applications and research protocols as distinct scientific contributions.
- Moher et al. also describe teaching and service contributions in team settings, including mentoring during grant applications.
- Table 2 states that finding fair ways to reward team endeavors is critical because research is increasingly collaborative and ordinary bibliometrics cannot properly assess this work.
- The same discussion notes that some committees disregard work unless the candidate is first or senior author, while uncorrected citation metrics can overcredit superficial coauthorship.

## Caveats

- Role evidence can be self-reported or strategically framed.
- Contribution statements are unevenly available across journals, fields, and years.
- Fine-grained role credit can become administratively heavy and may be hard to compare across team sizes.
- Committees still need qualitative judgment because contribution labels do not automatically measure quality or independence.
