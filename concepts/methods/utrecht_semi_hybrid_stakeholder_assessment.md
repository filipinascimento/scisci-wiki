# Utrecht semi-hybrid stakeholder assessment

## Summary

Utrecht semi-hybrid stakeholder assessment combines classical bibliometrics, process indicators, peer review, and nonacademic stakeholder review when evaluating researchers, programs, or teams.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: researcher, research team, research program, institution, or assessment cycle.
- Typical representation: mixed indicator dashboard plus semi-qualitative review by peers and external stakeholders.
- Method target: evaluate research conduct, teamwork, leadership, societal interaction, and outputs together.
- Empirical signature: assessment records include process indicators and stakeholder input rather than only publication and citation histories.

## Operationalization

- Define process indicators for leadership, culture, teamwork, citizenship, and stakeholder interaction.
- Combine those indicators with past-performance bibliometrics rather than letting bibliometrics stand alone.
- Include peers and relevant stakeholders outside academia in semi-hybrid review.
- Evaluate both individual scientists and multidisciplinary research programs when the institution uses linked assessment systems.
- Track whether the assessment policy reshapes knowledge-production practices.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Moher et al. (2018) describes the Utrecht Medical Centre assessment system as mixing classical past-performance bibliometric measures with process indicators.
- The paper lists leadership, culture, teamwork, good behaviors, citizenship, and interactions with societal stakeholders as indicators in that system.
- Moher et al. state that the system includes semi-hybrid review procedures with peers and stakeholders from outside academia and is being evaluated with CWTS.

## Caveats

- Stakeholder inclusion can broaden relevance but can also introduce political, commercial, or local-interest bias.
- Process indicators need clear definitions or they become vague proxies for institutional fit.
- Mixed review systems should be evaluated for workload, equity, and effects on research practice.
