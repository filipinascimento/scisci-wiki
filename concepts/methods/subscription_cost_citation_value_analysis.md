# Subscription cost citation-value analysis

## Summary

Subscription cost citation-value analysis is a collection-management method that compares journal citation frequency or impact with subscription cost to support transparent library budget decisions.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: journal, subscription package, library collection, citation count, impact indicator, cost, or cost-benefit ratio.
- Typical representation: cost per citation, cost per impact point, citation-value table, or collection triage dashboard.
- Method target: align journal acquisition, cancellation, retention, or package negotiation with local or global citation-use evidence.
- Empirical signature: journals differ in citation value per subscription cost, and those differences inform collection decisions alongside field coverage and local needs.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Turns [journal citation frequency](../measures/journal_citation_frequency.md), [journal impact factor](../measures/journal_impact_factor.md), and [citation-based source selection](citation_based_source_selection.md) into a library decision workflow.
- Provides a historical predecessor to [Eigenfactor cost-effectiveness](../measures/eigenfactor_cost_effectiveness.md) and [Eigenfactor bundle influence](../measures/eigenfactor_bundle_influence.md).
- Links bibliometric indicators to infrastructure choices that shape access, citation data, and database coverage.
- Connects to [OA-adjusted cost per access](../measures/oa_adjusted_cost_per_access.md), which shifts the denominator from citation value to non-OA reader access.
- Supports [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) by treating citation value as one input rather than a complete collection policy.

## Operationalization

- Define the journal collection and subscription costs for a decision period.
- Compute citation frequency, impact, local use, or field-normalized influence for each journal.
- Calculate citation-value ratios such as citations per dollar or influence per dollar.
- Add constraints for field coverage, teaching needs, equity, language coverage, and local research priorities.
- Use the analysis for transparent budgeting, not automatic cancellation.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Garfield (1972) identifies collection management as an application of journal citation analysis.
- The paper states that citation frequency and impact factor should help determine the optimum makeup of special and general journal collections.
- Garfield explicitly proposes correlating citation frequency and impact data with subscription costs to provide a solid basis for cost-benefit analysis in subscription-budget management.
- The same passage links citation-age distributions to backfile, binding, and retention schedules, making collection management a multi-indicator use case.

## Caveats

- Citation value is not the same as local readership, teaching value, public-good value, or support for underrepresented fields.
- High-cost journals may be essential for small local communities even when global citation value is low.
- Package pricing, embargoes, open access, interlibrary loan, and local usage statistics should be included when available.
- Cost-benefit rankings can reinforce citation-rich fields unless coverage and equity constraints are explicit.
