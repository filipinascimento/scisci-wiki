# Known-false hypothesis demonstration

## Summary

Known-false hypothesis demonstration is a method that tests an intentionally false or implausible claim to show how flexible analysis can produce nominally significant results.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: demonstration study, implausible hypothesis, analytic flexibility route, p-value, or teaching example.
- Typical representation: intentionally absurd hypothesis test, redacted report, simulation, or side-by-side disclosure contrast.
- Method target: make false-positive mechanisms concrete by showing significance for a claim that should not be true.
- Empirical signature: a researcher can obtain a significant result for a known-false claim using undisclosed but apparently defensible analytic choices.

## Operationalization

- Choose a hypothesis that is implausible or known to be false in the study context.
- Collect or reuse data where multiple outcomes, subsets, covariates, stopping points, or exclusions are available.
- Analyze the data using choices that could be described as plausible if not fully disclosed.
- Present both the polished finding and the complete analytic-choice record.
- Use the exercise to evaluate reporting requirements or reviewer checks.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Simmons et al. (2011) uses "chronological rejuvenation" examples to show how flexibility can make people appear younger after listening to a song.
- The demonstration is powerful because the conclusion is substantively implausible even though the reported statistical result can look conventional.
- This motif differs from a pure simulation because the demonstration uses a recognizable empirical report structure.
- It supports disclosure rules by showing what the reader cannot infer from a clean p-value alone.

## Caveats

- A known-false demonstration is pedagogical; it does not estimate a field's actual false-positive rate by itself.
- The example depends on available researcher degrees of freedom and may not generalize to all designs.
- Demonstrations can be misunderstood as fraud examples, even when the point is that ordinary flexibility is enough.
