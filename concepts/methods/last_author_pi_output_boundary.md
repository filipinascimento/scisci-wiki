# Last-author PI output boundary

## Summary

In life-science bibliometrics, last-authored papers can be used to identify principal-investigator-led output for baseline comparisons.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, author position, principal investigator, publication cohort, or baseline output measure.
- Typical representation: authorship-role filter selecting last-author publications for PI output.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: field-specific authorship boundary in life-science career analyses.
- Empirical signature: baseline hit comparisons change when restricted to papers where the focal scientist is last author..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines career-output measurement by linking it to [publication author scaling exponent](../measures/publication_author_scaling_exponent.md) and [matched career counterfactuals](matched_career_counterfactuals.md).
- Useful as a reusable distinction when [citation percentile indicators](../measures/citation_percentile_indicators.md) is analyzed in science-of-science data.
- Adds cross-links to [conservative applicant publication matching bias](../validations/conservative_applicant_publication_matching_bias.md) so the motif is not read as an isolated result.

## Operationalization

- Restrict pre-appointment publication measures to papers where the focal scientist is last author when life-science norms support it.
- Compare all-author and last-author baseline measures for robustness.
- Document fields where last-author seniority is unreliable or absent.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Azoulay et al. (2009) focuses baseline hit comparisons on last-authored articles because that position identifies both treated and controls as principal investigators.
- The motif makes authorship-role filtering part of bibliometric design.

## Caveats

- Last-author norms are field-specific and may not transfer outside biomedical and life sciences.
- Large collaborations and alphabetical authoring can break the PI-role interpretation.
