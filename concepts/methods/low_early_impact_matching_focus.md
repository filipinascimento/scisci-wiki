# Low-Early-Impact Matching Focus

## Summary

Low-early-impact matching focus restricts matched career comparisons to junior researchers with low initial citation impact in order to test where elite collaboration can plausibly matter most.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: junior researcher, matched pair, early-career citation count, discipline, or career cohort.
- Typical representation: matched treatment-control sample restricted by early citation threshold.
- Method target: estimate top-coauthor advantage in a subgroup with greater room for sponsorship effects.
- Empirical signature: treated and control authors are balanced on early prestige, productivity, and citations, with low early citation counts.

## Operationalization

- Define early career as a fixed window after first publication.
- Set a low-impact threshold, such as no more than ten citations in the first three years.
- Match treated and control authors on institution prestige, productivity, and early citations.
- Estimate later impact differences within this restricted matched sample.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Li et al. (2019) performs matched-pair analysis among authors with low early career impact.
- The paper focuses on authors with no more than ten citations in the first three career years.
- The authors state this choice targets the group expected to benefit most from interaction with a top scientist.

## Caveats

- Threshold choice can affect which careers enter the analysis.
- Low early citations may reflect field citation tempo, not only early promise.
- Matching cannot remove unobserved selection into top-scientist coauthorship.
