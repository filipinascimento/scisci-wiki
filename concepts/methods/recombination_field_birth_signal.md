# Recombination Field-Birth Signal

## Summary

Recombination field-birth signal is a method for using changing novelty-conventionality profiles to detect shifts in research trajectories or the emergence of new fields.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, research trajectory, topic cluster, paper cohort, or citation community.
- Typical representation: time series of recombination profiles.
- Method target: early signs that a domain is reorganizing around new combinations of prior work.
- Empirical signature: persistent changes in tail novelty, conventional grounding, and combination-profile structure precede or accompany field formation.

## Operationalization

- Build yearly recombination profiles for papers or topics in a candidate area.
- Track shifts in tail novelty, median conventionality, and recurrent atypical pairings.
- Compare shifts with emerging venues, communities, keywords, funding programs, or citation clusters.
- Validate field-birth signals against expert histories or known field labels.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Uzzi et al. (2013) states that novelty and conventionality measures can inform how research trajectories shift and how new fields arise.
- The paper reports that novelty-conventionality regularities persist across fields and time, supporting longitudinal use.
- The method extends the paper's recombination profile from impact prediction toward field-evolution diagnosis.

## Caveats

- Field birth is a social and institutional process, not only a citation-combination signal.
- Early signals can be noisy when publication volumes are small.
- Recombination shifts may indicate transient fashion, review articles, or database coverage changes rather than field emergence.
