# Community-governed prediction tools

## Summary

Automated prediction systems used in science evaluation require scientific-community oversight, contestability, and transparent governance.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: prediction tool, governance process, model documentation, appeal path, or human decision maker.
- Typical representation: human-in-the-loop governance wrapper for predictive evaluation.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: community control and accountability around automated science prediction.
- Empirical signature: prediction tools have documented purpose, training data, review process, appeal path, and monitored failure modes..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Complements [automated predictive-evaluation safeguards](../validations/automated_predictive_evaluation_safeguards.md).
- Connects predictive tools to [transparent metric construction](transparent_metric_construction.md).
- Provides a contestability path for evaluated researchers.

## Operationalization

- Document model purpose, training data, review process, appeal path, and monitored failure modes.
- Keep humans responsible for consequential decisions.
- Make model evidence contestable to affected parties.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Clauset et al. (2017) argues that automated systems are likely to emerge and that the scientific community must guide their development and use under fairness, accountability, and transparency principles.

## Caveats

- This is close to predictive-evaluation safeguards but focuses on governance process and human oversight.
- Community oversight can be slow and may reproduce existing power structures.
