# Reviewer budget-blinding rule

## Summary

Scientific evaluation can be separated from budget allocation by instructing reviewers to ignore requested funding amounts.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: grant application, reviewer instruction, budget request, priority score, institute payline, or awarded dollars.
- Typical representation: two-stage review-allocation rule separating merit scoring from budget decisions.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: procedural separation between proposal quality evaluation and funding amount allocation.
- Empirical signature: review scores remain predictive after accounting for award dollars if budget-blind scoring is not the main channel..

## Operationalization

- Distinguish reviewer score generation from institute payline and budget allocation decisions.
- Record whether reviewers are told to ignore requested budgets or resource needs.
- Test whether awarded dollars explain score-outcome associations in funded-only designs.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Li and Agha (2015) says reviewers are asked to ignore budgetary issues when scoring scientific merit.
- The paper's robustness checks add awarded dollars and find little attenuation in the score-citation relationship.

## Caveats

- Budget blinding is procedural and may not remove indirect effects of requested scope on perceived feasibility.
- Institute-level paylines and later budget cuts still shape which scored applications become funded projects.
