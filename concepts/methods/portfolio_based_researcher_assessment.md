# Portfolio-based researcher assessment

## Summary

Portfolio-based researcher assessment evaluates an individual scientist through a qualitative reading of their work, expertise, activities, and influence rather than through a single author-level metric.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: individual researcher, candidate dossier, promotion case, grant applicant, or career portfolio.
- Typical representation: curated publication set, narrative CV, contribution statement, role evidence, field context, and contextualized metric appendix.
- Method target: prevent scalar indicators from substituting for judgment about what a researcher has actually contributed.
- Empirical signature: committee decisions cite multiple forms of evidence and explain why single-number metrics were insufficient.

- Links researcher assessment to [contribution role vectors](../representations/contribution_role_vectors.md), [author contribution statements](../datasets/author_contribution_statements.md), and [field-specific indicator suites](field_specific_indicator_suites.md).

- Contains more specific portfolio submotifs for [broad-output assessment portfolios](broad_output_assessment_portfolio.md), [team-science contribution assessment](team_science_contribution_assessment.md), and [mentoring-teaching-service assessment](mentoring_teaching_service_assessment.md).
- Separates named assessment artifacts for [ACUMEN/GEP performance portfolio](acumen_gep_performance_portfolio.md), [Utrecht semi-hybrid stakeholder assessment](utrecht_semi_hybrid_stakeholder_assessment.md), [peer-review contribution R-index](../measures/peer_review_contribution_r_index.md), [data reuse S-index](../measures/data_reuse_s_index.md), [open-access reward fee-support precondition](../validations/open_access_reward_fee_support_precondition.md), and [reproducibility impact letter](reproducibility_impact_letter.md).

## Operationalization

- Define the decision target: hiring, promotion, fellowship, prize, grant review, or annual evaluation.
- Ask the evaluated researcher to identify representative outputs, roles, field context, and non-publication contributions.
- Review the substance of selected works and documented contributions.
- Add metrics only as contextual evidence, with field, career-age, database, authorship, and self-citation caveats.
- Include evidence of openness, complete reporting, data/software sharing, peer-review service, mentoring, societal contribution, and role-specific team science when these are relevant to the decision.
- Route Moher-style principle evidence through linked criteria such as [societal-needs assessment alignment](societal_needs_assessment_alignment.md), [complete transparent reporting rewards](complete_transparent_reporting_rewards.md), [open research reward criteria](open_research_reward_criteria.md), and [intellectual-risk-taking assessment](intellectual_risk_taking_assessment.md).
- Record how qualitative judgment, portfolio evidence, and indicators were combined.
- Use [transparent assessment-criteria publication](transparent_assessment_criteria_publication.md) so committees do not shift portfolio weights after seeing candidates.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hicks et al. (2015) states that individual researchers should be assessed through qualitative judgment of their portfolio.
- Hicks et al. warn that h-index values rise with age, vary by field, and depend on the database used.
- The paper argues that reading and judging the researcher's work is more appropriate than relying on one number.
- Hicks et al. also recommend considering information about expertise, experience, activities, and influence when many researchers must be compared.
- Verified full-text evidence from Moher et al. (2018) applies portfolio-style assessment to hiring, promotion, and tenure, arguing that easy metrics such as grant totals, publication counts, citation counts, and JIF leave important research practices unassessed.
- Moher et al. synthesize assessment principles that broaden the portfolio to include societal contribution, responsible indicators, complete transparent reporting, open research, evidence-building on assessment, and intellectual risk-taking.
- The paper discusses CV and dossier changes, such as recording registration details, registered-report participation, completed-study publication, data/code/material sharing, and impact letters for reproducibility work.
- A later Moher pass separates ACUMEN/GEP, Utrecht, R-index, S-index, OA fee-support, and reproducibility impact-letter motifs from the broader portfolio model.

## Caveats

- Portfolio review can be opaque, status-biased, or labor-intensive if procedures are not explicit.
- Narrative dossiers can reward writing skill or institutional support unless reviewers use structured rubrics.
- A portfolio approach does not eliminate the need for data-quality checks, conflict-of-interest management, or field-specific calibration.
- Named assessment artifacts need implementation evidence before they are treated as validated evaluation instruments.
