# Responsible researcher assessment principles

## Summary

Responsible researcher assessment principles translate responsible-metrics ideas into hiring, promotion, tenure, grant-review, and annual-evaluation criteria for individual scientists.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, faculty candidate, promotion case, hiring dossier, grant applicant, department, or institution.
- Typical representation: principle set, evaluation rubric, portfolio checklist, assessment dashboard, or promotion-and-tenure policy.
- Method target: align researcher evaluation with robust, transparent, socially useful, and creative science rather than with easy scalar proxies.
- Empirical signature: assessment criteria explicitly reward open research, complete reporting, societal contribution, validated indicators, evidence on assessment reforms, and intellectual risk-taking.

## Operationalization

- State the decision context: hiring, promotion, tenure, grant review, award, or annual evaluation.
- Translate the six principle families into local criteria: [societal contribution](societal_needs_assessment_alignment.md), responsible indicators, [complete transparent reporting](complete_transparent_reporting_rewards.md), [open research](open_research_reward_criteria.md), [evidence-building for assessment](../validations/assessment_evidence_base_funding.md), and [support for out-of-the-box ideas](intellectual_risk_taking_assessment.md).
- Pair indicators with [portfolio-based researcher assessment](portfolio_based_researcher_assessment.md) so dossiers explain selected outputs, roles, openness, reproducibility, and non-publication contributions.
- Document which behaviors are rewarded, which weak proxies are de-emphasized, and how committees will audit adherence.
- Publish explicit criteria and role expectations so candidates know how outputs, service, team science, and responsible practices will be judged.
- Build in feedback loops: publish criteria, collect implementation data, revise indicators, and evaluate whether the policy changes scientific behavior.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Moher et al. (2018) reports an expert-panel workshop and selective review of 22 key documents on assessing scientists for hiring, promotion, and tenure.
- The paper synthesizes six principles: address societal needs, use responsible indicators, reward complete and transparent publication, reward open research, fund research on how to assess science and faculty, and value out-of-the-box ideas.
- The current wiki splits five principle-level motifs from that synthesis: [societal-needs assessment alignment](societal_needs_assessment_alignment.md), [complete transparent reporting rewards](complete_transparent_reporting_rewards.md), [open research reward criteria](open_research_reward_criteria.md), [assessment evidence-base funding](../validations/assessment_evidence_base_funding.md), and [intellectual-risk-taking assessment](intellectual_risk_taking_assessment.md).
- Moher et al. connect each principle to research and policy implications rather than treating the principles as only normative statements.
- The full text states that current assessment often emphasizes grant funding, publication counts, citation counts, and journal impact factor while underweighting reproducibility, transparency, dissemination, and societal contribution.
- Table 2 operationalizes the principles through candidate CV changes, dashboard indicators, audit and feedback, open research rewards, experimental evaluations, and longer-horizon support for creative work.

## Caveats

- Principles need local translation; a medical school, physics department, policy school, and national funder may need different evidence and weighting.
- New criteria can become targets, so they need [indicator systemic effects](../mechanisms/indicator_systemic_effects.md) audits.
- Responsible assessment can increase committee workload unless rubrics, dossiers, and evidence requirements are made explicit.
- Principle adoption is not implementation; institutions need monitoring, revision, and outcome evaluation.
