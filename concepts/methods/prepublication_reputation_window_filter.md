# Prepublication reputation-window filter

## Summary

Prepublication reputation-window filter restricts co-cited papers to those published after the focal collaboration to separate prior reputation from subsequent discovery credit.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: focal paper, co-cited paper, publication year, credit-share vector, or reputation correction.
- Typical representation: before-versus-after publication window in a co-citation credit analysis.
- Method target: distinguish credit due to a prior established reputation from credit generated by later related contributions.
- Empirical signature: credit shares change when pre-focal co-cited papers are excluded.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [credit-share evolution](../measures/credit_share_evolution.md) and [co-citation credit attribution](co_citation_credit_attribution.md).
- Helps interpret [Nobel-prize credit validation](../validations/nobel_prize_credit_validation.md) when senior coauthors have strong preexisting records.
- Connects citation-based credit to [reputation effects](../mechanisms/reputation_effects.md) and [senior coauthor credit shadow](../mechanisms/senior_coauthor_credit_shadow.md).

## Operationalization

- Identify the focal paper publication year.
- Separate co-cited papers published before and after the focal collaboration.
- Recompute credit shares with only post-focal co-cited papers.
- Compare the result with the full-window credit estimate.
- Interpret large differences as evidence that prior reputation or later discovery work is affecting perceived credit.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Shen and Barabasi (2014) uses a window that considers co-cited papers published after the focal Nobel-winning paper.
- The paper uses this filter to examine whether an established scientist's higher credit reflects prior reputation or independent contribution after the joint publication.
- Shen and Barabasi show that windowing can alter the interpretation of credit-share evolution among coauthors.

## Caveats

- Post-publication windows can remove genuinely relevant prior work.
- Later co-cited papers can themselves be influenced by Nobel recognition or other exogenous events.
- The filter estimates community perception under a chosen window, not actual contribution.
