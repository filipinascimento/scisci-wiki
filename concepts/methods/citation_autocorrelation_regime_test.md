# Citation autocorrelation regime test

## Summary

Exponential versus power-law citation decay can be interpreted through finite versus effectively unbounded autocorrelation regimes.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, post-peak trajectory, exponential fit, power-law fit, autocorrelation regime, or F-score.
- Typical representation: model comparison linked to finite-state and long-memory citation regimes.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: theoretical interpretation of citation-decay functional form.
- Empirical signature: finite-state ultrametric autocorrelation is associated with exponential behavior, while longer-memory regimes are associated with power-law behavior..

## Operationalization

- Estimate post-peak exponential and power-law decay fits.
- Relate exponential behavior to finite-state ultrametric autocorrelation.
- Treat power-law behavior as a possible signal of longer-range or unbounded memory.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Parolo et al. (2015) links ultrametric autocorrelation to exponential behavior for finite state spaces and power-law behavior otherwise, then compares the fits empirically.
- The motif bridges fit diagnostics with a theory of attention relaxation.

## Caveats

- This interpretation is partly theoretical and speculative.
- Fit superiority does not prove the underlying state-space mechanism.
