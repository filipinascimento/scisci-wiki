# Field-specific indicator suites

## Summary

Field-specific indicator suites let disciplines choose or weight indicators according to their publication, citation, language, venue, and output-format practices.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, department, portfolio, publication type, indicator suite, or evaluation panel.
- Typical representation: discipline-specific indicator menu, field-normalized dashboard, or field-selected evaluation rubric.
- Method target: avoid comparing fields with indicators that fit only some publication cultures.
- Empirical signature: books, conference papers, journal articles, national-language literature, and citation-density baselines receive field-appropriate treatment.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [field normalized citation impact](../measures/field_normalized_citation_impact.md) from citation scaling to broader indicator choice.
- Gives [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) a discipline-level implementation rule.
- Connects [reference set construction](reference_set_construction.md), [citation percentile indicators](../measures/citation_percentile_indicators.md), and [locally relevant research metrics](../validations/locally_relevant_research_metrics.md).
- Helps prevent evaluation systems from treating journal articles as the only valuable output format.

## Operationalization

- Identify field-specific output formats, language practices, citation densities, and relevant databases.
- Let fields select from an approved suite of indicators rather than applying one metric everywhere.
- Use percentile or other normalized indicators when citation rates differ strongly by field.
- Report which outputs are excluded by each database or indicator.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hicks et al. (2015) says evaluation should account for field variation in publication and citation practices.
- Hicks et al. argue that historians and social scientists need books and national-language literature counted, while computer scientists need conference papers counted.
- The article gives a concrete citation-density contrast: top journals in mathematics and cell biology have very different impact-factor scales.
- Hicks et al. recommend normalized indicators and describe percentile-based normalization as a robust option.

## Caveats

- Field-selected indicators can entrench disciplinary conservatism if they are not periodically reviewed.
- Field boundaries are fuzzy for interdisciplinary and emerging research.
- Indicator suites still need transparent construction and evaluated-party data verification.
