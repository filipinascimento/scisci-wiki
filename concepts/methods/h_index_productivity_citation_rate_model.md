# h-index productivity-citation rate model

## Summary

The h-index productivity-citation rate model derives h-index growth from a scientist's paper production rate and citation accrual rate.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author career, publication rate, citation rate, h-index, or career age.
- Typical method: express h growth as a function of papers per year and citations per paper per year.
- Method target: mechanistic interpretation of h-index trajectories and m-quotient values.
- Empirical signature: scientists with similar h can differ in the productivity and citation-rate components that generate it.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a generative layer beneath [h-index](../measures/h_index.md).
- Explains part of [m-quotient](../measures/m_quotient.md).
- Connects author metrics to [scientific career impact](../mechanisms/scientific_career_impact.md).
- Helps interpret later career-impact models such as [Q-model career impact prediction](q_model_career_prediction.md).

## Operationalization

- Estimate publication rate over scientific age.
- Estimate citation accrual rate for an author's papers.
- Relate these rates to expected h-index growth.
- Compare the model's implied h trajectory with observed h.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hirsch (2005) gives a simple model linking h to the number of papers produced per year and the rate at which those papers accumulate citations.
- The paper uses this model to interpret the m-quotient and differences among researchers.
- The model turns h-index from only a descriptive scalar into a career-rate decomposition.

## Caveats

- Citation accrual is not constant over careers or fields.
- The simple model abstracts away author position, team size, field growth, and citation aging.
- Later stochastic career models give richer uncertainty estimates.
