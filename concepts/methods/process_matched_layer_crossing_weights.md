# Process-matched layer-crossing weights

## Summary

Layer-crossing weights should be chosen to match the process being modeled, since movement between scholarly layers may be fast, slow, costly, or rewarding depending on the mechanism.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: interlayer edge, crossing weight, dynamic process, scholarly layer, or traversal event.
- Typical representation: process-specific interlayer weighting rule with sensitivity grid.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: dynamic interpretation of interlayer coupling strengths.
- Empirical signature: diffusion, attention, collaboration, and routing models produce different results under different crossing costs or bonuses.

## Operationalization

- Define the process before setting interlayer weights: diffusion, attention switching, collaboration formation, career mobility, or knowledge translation.
- Compare fast, slow, costly, and neutral crossing settings.
- Report which empirical conclusion is stable across plausible crossing-weight choices.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from De Domenico et al. (2013) notes that clustering weights can account for costs of crossing layers and that the cost depends on the dynamics and application.
- This motivates science-of-science sensitivity checks for citation-to-collaboration, paper-to-patent, and field-to-institution traversals.

## Caveats

- Crossing costs are often unobserved and can become researcher degrees of freedom.
- Process-matched weights should not be reused across mechanisms without justification.
