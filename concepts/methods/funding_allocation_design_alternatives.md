# Funding-allocation design alternatives

## Summary

Funding-allocation design alternatives are policy designs that allocate research resources through mechanisms other than conventional expert-panel proposal review.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: funding program, proposal, applicant, reviewer, allocation rule, institution, or research portfolio.
- Typical representation: funding lottery, person-directed funding, open online review, performance-measure allocation, peer-to-peer distribution, or hybrid review design.
- Mechanism or measurement target: how resource-allocation rules shape novelty, equity, efficiency, administrative burden, and scientific direction.
- Empirical signature: alternative funding rules are compared to conventional peer review on portfolio outcomes and unintended consequences.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Organizes policy-design options for [peer review and gatekeeping](../mechanisms/peer_review_gatekeeping.md).
- Provides a menu of interventions for [active science-policy experimentation](../validations/active_science_policy_experimentation.md) and [SciSci quasi-experimental policy evaluation](../validations/scisci_quasi_experimental_policy_evaluation.md).
- Connects to [funding autonomy and exploration](../mechanisms/funding_autonomy_exploration.md), [funding incentives and exploration](../mechanisms/funding_incentives_and_exploration.md), and grant-review validation motifs.
- Helps separate the design of a funding mechanism from the empirical question of whether it improves scientific outcomes.
- Uses [biomedical need-resource misalignment](../measures/biomedical_need_resource_misalignment.md) as one portfolio-alignment outcome for health-related funding programs.

## Operationalization

- Specify the allocation rule, eligibility rules, decision unit, budget granularity, and decision authority.
- Define comparison outcomes: novelty, disruption, replication, productivity, equity, administrative costs, career effects, field diversity, and long-run impact.
- Pilot the design through randomized, phased, threshold-based, or matched comparisons when feasible.
- Monitor gaming, concentration, fairness, applicant burden, and field-specific transfer failures.
- Add a scalability check for high-touch programs whose feedback, review, or legitimacy constraints may change when expanded.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) states that science funding allocation is a core issue for science-of-science policy.
- The review notes that current peer review is subject to biases and inconsistencies.
- Fortunato et al. list several proposed alternatives: random funding, person-directed funding without proposal preparation and review, opening proposal review to an online population, removing human reviewers through performance-measure allocation, and scientist crowd-funding.
- The review frames these as candidates for future science-of-science research rather than settled best practices.

## Caveats

- Alternative funding mechanisms can introduce new biases, strategic behavior, or inequities.
- Designs that work in one field, country, or career stage may not transfer elsewhere.
- Evaluation should separate applicant burden, review validity, funding treatment effects, and portfolio-level outcomes.
