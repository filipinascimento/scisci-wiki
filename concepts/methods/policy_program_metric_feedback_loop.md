# Policy-program metric feedback loop

## Summary

Policy-program metric feedback loop designs an evaluation program so repeated metrics track whether a policy, funding program, or institutional intervention changes research behavior over time.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: policy initiative, funded cohort, benchmark field, institution, program participant, or observation year.
- Typical representation: baseline benchmark, repeated indicator panel, intervention-monitoring dashboard, or before-after comparison.
- Method target: use science-of-science indicators as feedback for policy learning rather than only as descriptive statistics.
- Empirical signature: measured research practices are compared before and after a policy initiative or against benchmark communities.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Turns [interdisciplinarity benchmark field panels](../datasets/interdisciplinarity_benchmark_field_panel.md) into an evaluation reference.
- Connects [interdisciplinarity metric-map triangulation](../validations/interdisciplinarity_metric_map_triangulation.md) with policy learning.
- Uses [overlay opportunity scouting](overlay_opportunity_scouting.md) when metrics and maps identify possible cognitive complementarities.
- Generalizes to funding programs, institutional initiatives, research centers, and team-science interventions.

## Operationalization

- Define the policy intervention and the research behavior it is expected to change.
- Select benchmark fields, institutions, or cohorts for comparison.
- Measure the target behavior repeatedly using indicators and map overlays.
- Compare participant or program trajectories with external benchmarks.
- Feed the result back into program design, funding criteria, or evaluation strategy.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Porter and Rafols (2009) states that the National Academies Keck Futures Initiative needed interdisciplinarity measures to track the status and changes of a 15-year program.
- The paper describes evaluation at macro and micro levels, including national patterns and NAKFI seed-grant participants.
- The authors conclude that benchmarks and maps can support research intelligence and evaluation, including monitoring whether research practices change because of a policy initiative such as NAKFI.

## Caveats

- Metrics can change behavior in unintended ways when they become targets.
- Policy feedback requires a credible baseline and comparison group, not only post-intervention measurement.
- Indicators should be paired with qualitative assessment when the program goal is broad or exploratory.
