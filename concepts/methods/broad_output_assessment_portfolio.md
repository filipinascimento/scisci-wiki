# Broad-output assessment portfolio

## Summary

Broad-output assessment portfolios credit research outputs and outcomes beyond journal articles, including datasets, software, protocols, materials, peer-review contributions, clinical or policy outputs, and other reusable or socially valuable products.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: researcher, faculty dossier, research group, institution, funder, grant applicant, or output portfolio.
- Typical representation: output inventory, portfolio table, contribution dossier, CV supplement, repository-link appendix, or responsible-indicator dashboard.
- Method target: prevent article counts and venue prestige from defining the whole value of a scientist.
- Empirical signature: assessment dossiers include non-article outputs and explain their contribution, reuse, reliability, openness, or societal value.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [portfolio-based researcher assessment](portfolio_based_researcher_assessment.md) from paper selection to a wider output inventory.
- Operationalizes the DORA-style instruction to value all outputs and outcomes generated by research.
- Supplies dossier evidence for [open research reward criteria](open_research_reward_criteria.md), [complete transparent reporting rewards](complete_transparent_reporting_rewards.md), and [responsible indicators for assessing scientists](../measures/responsible_indicators_for_assessing_scientists.md).
- Links output valuation to [author contribution statements](../datasets/author_contribution_statements.md), [open access status](../datasets/open_access_status.md), and other reusable research-object records.
- Helps diagnose [assessment incentive misalignment](../mechanisms/assessment_incentive_misalignment.md) when valuable products are omitted from promotion criteria.

## Operationalization

- Define the output classes relevant to the field and mission: papers, datasets, code, software, protocols, materials, registered reports, preprints, clinical trial organization, peer review, policy documents, public engagement, or community tools.
- Require stable identifiers, repository links, licenses, documentation, and reuse or uptake evidence when available.
- Ask candidates to explain contribution and context rather than count outputs mechanically.
- Pair the output list with [transparent assessment-criteria publication](transparent_assessment_criteria_publication.md) so committees know how each output type is valued.
- Audit whether broader output credit changes behavior, workload, openness, and equity.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Moher et al. (2018) summarizes DORA recommendations that funders and institutions consider value from all outputs and outcomes generated by research.
- The paper argues that standard assessment often ignores practices that make research more open and available, including data sharing and education.
- Moher et al. identify responsible indicators such as registered reports, sharing results, peer-review contributions, alternative uptake, dataset sharing, and software sharing.
- Table 2 extends open-research rewards to data, protocols, software, code, materials, and other research tools.
- The same full text discusses portfolio designs that include expertise, outputs, impacts, narrative context, and influence rather than only article-level bibliometrics.

## Caveats

- Broad portfolios can become long and hard to compare unless criteria are explicit.
- Counting every output equally can reward quantity and compliance rather than quality, reuse, or contribution.
- Infrastructure availability differs by field, so repository-based evidence can create field and resource biases.
- Some outputs, such as community engagement or peer review, may be hard to verify without creating surveillance-heavy systems.
