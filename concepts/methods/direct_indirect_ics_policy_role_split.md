# Direct/indirect ICS policy-role split

## Summary

International collaboration policy can be separated into direct construction of collaboration and indirect incentive shaping.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: policy instrument, collaboration regime, funding rule, institution, incentive, or observed network outcome.
- Typical representation: typology of direct and indirect policy roles in international collaboration.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: policy-leverage modes for global collaboration systems.
- Empirical signature: direct megascience or coordinated policies differ from indirect attractiveness and incentive tools.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines international-collaboration policy method by linking it to [collaboration regime policy leverage](collaboration_regime_policy_leverage.md) and [collaboration organizing driver quadrants](../representations/collaboration_organizing_driver_quadrants.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [international policy goal portfolio](../representations/international_policy_goal_portfolio.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [permissive collaboration funding signal](../mechanisms/permissive_collaboration_funding_signal.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Classify policies as direct funding or institution-building versus indirect incentive and attractiveness tools.
- Map each policy mode to collaboration regime and observed network outcome.
- Record mixed cases where direct and indirect mechanisms operate together.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified local full text from Wagner and Leydesdorff (2005) distinguishes government's direct role in megascience/coordinated work from indirect influence in participatory research.
- The split makes policy leverage more specific than a generic collaboration-policy category.

## Caveats

- The categories are heuristic and not mutually exclusive.
- Observed network outcomes can lag or diffuse across policy instruments.
