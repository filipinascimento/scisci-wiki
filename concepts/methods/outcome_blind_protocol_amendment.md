# Outcome-blind protocol amendment

## Summary

Outcome-blind protocol amendment is the practice of documenting design or analysis-plan changes before outcomes are observed so the amendment preserves the inferential value of preregistration.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: preregistration, protocol amendment, analysis plan, outcome observation, or deviation report.
- Typical representation: timestamped amendment, blind-stage protocol update, deviation log, or outcome-known transparency note.
- Method target: distinguish amendments made before outcome knowledge from changes made after results are known.
- Empirical signature: amendments made before outcome observation retain stronger prediction status than post-outcome deviations.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [preregistration adherence and deviation audit](../validations/preregistration_adherence_deviation_audit.md) by separating amendment timing.
- Operationalizes the [prediction-postdiction boundary](../validations/prediction_postdiction_boundary.md) for studies where plans legitimately change.
- Links preregistration to real workflows where instruments fail, assumptions are violated, or analysis details need revision.
- Helps registered reports, registries, and reproducibility audits code deviations more precisely.

## Operationalization

- Record the original preregistration and its timestamp.
- When a plan must change, document the change, rationale, and whether relevant outcomes were already observed.
- Treat outcome-blind amendments as preserving more diagnosticity than outcome-informed deviations.
- Report post-outcome deviations transparently so readers can assess their effect on inference.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Nosek et al. (2018) states that deviations do not necessarily rule out effective prediction testing.
- The paper explains that if outcomes have not yet been observed, changes can be documented without undermining diagnosticity.
- Nosek et al. also state that even after data are observed, transparent reporting of changes and reasons still helps readers assess their impact.
- This supports amendment timing as a reusable method for interpreting preregistration deviations.

## Caveats

- Outcome-blindness can be partial if related variables or interim summaries are already known.
- Amendments can still weaken inference if they introduce flexibility or change the target hypothesis.
- A transparent post-outcome amendment is useful for assessment but should not be mislabeled as a preserved prediction.
