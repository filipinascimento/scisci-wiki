# ACUMEN/GEP performance portfolio

## Summary

ACUMEN/GEP performance portfolio is a structured researcher-assessment dossier that separates narrative, academic age, expertise, outputs, and influence while grounding evaluation in Good Evaluation Practices.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: researcher, promotion dossier, hiring dossier, evaluation portfolio, or institutional assessment policy.
- Typical representation: web or dossier portfolio with sections for narrative, academic age, expertise, outputs, and influence.
- Method target: broaden individual researcher assessment beyond mono-disciplinary bibliometric indicators.
- Empirical signature: reviewers inspect distinct evidence blocks rather than collapsing the case to publications, JIF, grants, or citations.

## Operationalization

- Define the GEP indicators being assessed, such as expertise, outputs, and impacts.
- Ask the researcher to supply separate evidence for narrative and academic age, expertise, output, and influence.
- Review each section with explicit criteria rather than allowing one high-status metric to dominate.
- Pair the narrative section with bias checks for self-marketing, institutional coaching, and uneven storytelling skill.
- Record which portfolio elements informed the decision.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Moher et al. (2018) summarizes the ACUMEN consortium as developing Good Evaluation Practice criteria and a prototype web-based performance portfolio.
- The paper states that GEP focuses on expertise, outputs, and impacts, and that the ACUMEN portfolio is divided into narrative and academic age, expertise, output, and influence sections.
- Moher et al. also warn that evidence-based narratives can encourage researchers to mold achievements into compelling stories, making narrative-bias validation necessary.

## Caveats

- A structured portfolio can still reward narrative skill and institutional support if reviewers do not use rubrics.
- The ACUMEN scaffold is an assessment design, not evidence that portfolio assessment improves research quality by itself.
- Academic-age and influence measures require field and career-stage context.
