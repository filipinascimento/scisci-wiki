# Selected-output portfolio review

## Summary

Selected-output portfolio review asks evaluated researchers to submit a small set of their most meaningful outputs, shifting assessment away from line-counting toward judged contribution.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: researcher, renewal dossier, promotion file, grant application, publication subset, or output portfolio.
- Typical representation: selected-paper packet, narrative portfolio, limited-output CV section, or top-output dossier.
- Method target: make reviewers attend to quality, meaning, and contribution rather than the total number of outputs.
- Empirical signature: assessment procedures cap the number of submitted outputs and ask reviewers to evaluate their substance and trajectory fit.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a concrete portfolio mechanism within the [HHMI-NIH incentive contrast](hhmi_nih_incentive_contrast.md).
- Connects [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) and [portfolio-based researcher assessment](portfolio_based_researcher_assessment.md) to funding renewal design.
- Differs from [broad-output assessment portfolio](broad_output_assessment_portfolio.md) by emphasizing a selected subset, not necessarily all output types.
- Helps evaluate whether review design rewards meaningful achievements rather than publication volume.

## Operationalization

- Set a maximum number of submitted outputs, such as five papers or a limited set of products.
- Ask the candidate to explain the importance, contribution, and relation to future plans for each output.
- Pair the output packet with a short research proposal or oral defense when trajectory judgment is needed.
- Compare selected-output review with exhaustive CV review on reviewer burden, perceived fairness, and incentives for quantity.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Azoulay, Graff Zivin, and Manso (2009) describes HHMI renewal packets that ask investigators to submit their five most notable papers from the past five years plus a short proposal.
- The authors emphasize that HHMI investigators publish many papers, so limiting the packet to five ensures that the renewal decision focuses on what investigators regard as their most meaningful achievements.
- The process contrasts with NIH grant review, where applications depend on a more exhaustive list of team accomplishments and predefined project detail.
- This selected-output design sits inside a broader high-autonomy funding regime, so its effect should not be interpreted separately from duration, feedback, and failure tolerance without additional evidence.

## Caveats

- A selected portfolio can invite strategic curation and may hide weak parts of a record.
- Reviewers still need field context to judge whether selected outputs are meaningful.
- The method is not equivalent to broad output credit unless non-article products are eligible and valued.
