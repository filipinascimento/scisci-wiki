# Overlay opportunity scouting

## Summary

Overlay opportunity scouting is the method of using science-map overlays to identify possible cross-disciplinary relationships, cognitive complementarities, and intervention targets.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, overlay node, subject-category cluster, distant knowledge area, or intervention target.
- Typical representation: science-map overlay used as a search surface for opportunities.
- Method target: turn mapping from descriptive measurement into exploratory strategy support.
- Empirical signature: new or distant overlay regions are interpreted as places to investigate emerging links or underused connections.

## Operationalization

- Build comparable overlays for a field, institution, portfolio, or time series.
- Identify new, growing, distant, or sparsely connected overlay regions.
- Pair map signals with domain review before proposing interventions.
- Track whether identified opportunities become sustained citation or collaboration links.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Porter and Rafols (2009) presents overlay maps as tools for visualizing and interpreting interdisciplinarity.
- The paper suggests maps can reveal changing cross-field relationships and possible integration opportunities.
- This method treats map overlays as exploratory instruments, not only retrospective summaries.

## Caveats

- Visual opportunities can reflect projection artifacts or classification noise.
- Map-identified opportunities need expert validation and temporal follow-up.
- Intervention targets can be constrained by funding, institutions, and field norms.
