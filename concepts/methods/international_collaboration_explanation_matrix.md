# International collaboration explanation matrix

## Summary

International collaboration explanation matrix is a diagnostic method for classifying proposed causes of international scientific collaboration before testing them against network evidence.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: explanation, collaboration trend, field, country network, or policy claim.
- Typical representation: a two-by-two matrix separating internal versus external factors and interconnection versus capacity-diffusion mechanisms.
- Mechanism or measurement target: organize candidate explanations before comparing them with observed collaboration structure.
- Empirical signature: some explanations match parts of the data, but no single legacy explanation accounts for rapid cross-field international collaboration growth.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a structured audit frame for [international collaboration networks](../representations/international_collaboration_networks.md).
- Separates volume changes such as [international coauthorship share](../measures/international_coauthorship_share.md) from structural changes such as [country collaboration core](../measures/country_collaboration_core.md) and [regional collaboration hubs](../measures/regional_collaboration_hubs.md).
- Helps decide whether observed collaboration growth is better explained by capacity, geography, policy, information technology, field organization, or [international collaboration preferential attachment](../mechanisms/international_collaboration_preferential_attachment.md).

## Operationalization

- List candidate explanations for a collaboration trend.
- Classify each explanation by whether it is internal or external to science.
- Classify each explanation by whether it concerns interconnection among scientists or diffusion of scientific capacity.
- Compare each explanation with observed changes in collaboration share, country cores, regional hubs, subfield networks, and field coverage.
- Keep explanations as partial mechanisms unless they match timing, field breadth, and network structure.
- Use targeted caveats such as [communication-technology initiation caveat](../validations/communication_technology_initiation_caveat.md) and [constructed collaboration sustainability limit](../validations/constructed_collaboration_sustainability_limit.md) when a candidate explanation is plausible but insufficient.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wagner and Leydesdorff (2005) reviews explanations for the growth of international collaboration in science.
- Their matrix includes historical and geographic ties, trade and communication technologies, disciplinary differentiation, megascience, professionalization, capacity-building, and center-periphery accounts.
- They then compare these explanations with 1990 and 2000 international coauthorship evidence and argue that the legacy explanations do not fully explain the rapid, cross-field growth of international collaboration.
- The paper uses this diagnostic gap to motivate subfield network analysis and a preferential-attachment explanation based on reputation and rewards.
- Wagner and Leydesdorff specifically argue that Internet communication is a facilitator rather than an initiation driver, and that constructed collaboration is sustainable only when it rests on strong local science and technical capability.

## Caveats

- A matrix can organize hypotheses but does not itself identify a causal mechanism.
- Explanations are not mutually exclusive; policy, capacity, geography, and self-organization can operate together.
- The categories should be updated for newer collaboration systems such as open data, platform-mediated teams, preprints, and remote instrumentation.
