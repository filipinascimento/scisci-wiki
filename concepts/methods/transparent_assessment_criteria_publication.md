# Transparent assessment-criteria publication

## Summary

Transparent assessment-criteria publication makes hiring, promotion, tenure, and grant-review criteria explicit enough that candidates, committees, and evaluators can see what is being rewarded before decisions are made.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: institution, department, promotion committee, funder, hiring process, assessment rubric, or faculty dossier.
- Typical representation: published criteria statement, rubric, promotion-and-tenure policy, committee guidance, baseline criteria inventory, or assessment-principles document.
- Method target: prevent implicit proxy use and make assessment reform observable.
- Empirical signature: institutions disclose the criteria used for academic appointment, promotion, tenure, or grant assessment, including the stated role of bibliometric indicators and responsible indicators.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Converts [responsible researcher assessment principles](responsible_researcher_assessment_principles.md) into an institutional implementation object.
- Supplies the baseline criteria inventory needed by [research assessment implementation experiments](../validations/research_assessment_implementation_experiments.md).
- Makes [assessment incentive misalignment](../mechanisms/assessment_incentive_misalignment.md) measurable by comparing stated values with stated reward criteria.
- Complements [mission-aligned indicator selection](mission_aligned_indicator_selection.md), because mission alignment cannot be audited if criteria are implicit.
- Provides an institutional counterpart to [metrics support qualitative assessment](metrics_support_qualitative_assessment.md).

## Operationalization

- Collect the current written and informal criteria used in hiring, promotion, tenure, and grant review.
- State how content review, portfolios, bibliometrics, responsible indicators, letters, teaching, mentoring, team science, open research, and societal contribution are weighed.
- Publish or internally disclose the criteria before they are used.
- Ask committees and affected researchers whether the criteria match institutional values.
- Use the criteria as a pre-reform baseline for later [assessment audit-feedback loops](../validations/assessment_audit_feedback_loops.md).

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Moher et al. (2018) notes that research assessment often defaults to easily measured proxies such as grant totals, publication counts, citations, and journal impact factor.
- The paper discusses research-assessment proposals that ask institutional leaders to state principles for assessment and ask recruitment or promotion panels to be explicit about appointment and promotion criteria.
- Moher et al. argue that identifying and collating current promotion-and-tenure criteria is essential baseline knowledge for evaluating later policy changes.
- Table 2 recommends that university leaders ask assessment committees to review and make current criteria available, then share the results with faculty and relevant communities.
- The same full text treats transparent criteria as an implementation prerequisite, because committees cannot monitor adherence to assessment reform if criteria are not specified.

## Caveats

- Published criteria can be symbolic if committees keep following informal prestige signals.
- Detailed criteria can become checklists that invite gaming or overload candidates with documentation demands.
- Criteria transparency does not ensure fairness; committees still need calibration, conflict-of-interest management, and outcome audits.
- Some criteria, such as collegiality or stakeholder letters, are difficult to specify without creating vague or biased evaluation hooks.
