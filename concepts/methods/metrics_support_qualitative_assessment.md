# Metrics support qualitative assessment

## Summary

Metrics support qualitative assessment when quantitative indicators are used as evidence for expert deliberation rather than as automatic substitutes for judgment.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: evaluation panel, researcher, paper, portfolio, institution, funding program, or indicator report.
- Typical representation: mixed evaluation dossier combining metrics, narrative evidence, peer review, and contextual explanation.
- Method target: keep indicators informative without ceding decisions to numbers.
- Empirical signature: decisions cite indicators as supporting evidence while preserving documented expert interpretation and responsibility.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a procedural foundation for [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md).
- Counters [data-led evaluation inversion](../mechanisms/data_led_evaluation_inversion.md), where indicators become de facto decision makers instead of evidence for accountable judgment.
- Connects [peer review gatekeeping](../mechanisms/peer_review_gatekeeping.md) with citation, altmetric, and portfolio evidence.
- Helps evaluation systems avoid treating [h-index](../measures/h_index.md), [journal impact factor](../measures/journal_impact_factor.md), or [citation impact indicators](../measures/citation_impact_indicators.md) as direct quality labels.
- Supports policy experiments that compare metric-only, peer-only, and mixed-evidence evaluation designs.
- Includes [novelty-aware review calibration](novelty_aware_review_calibration.md) when novelty indicators are supplied as context for expert deliberation rather than as automatic funding rules.
- Extends to [portfolio-based researcher assessment](portfolio_based_researcher_assessment.md) when the evaluation unit is an individual scientist.

## Operationalization

- Require evaluation reports to state what each indicator is intended to inform.
- Pair every metric table with qualitative review, field context, uncertainty, and limitations.
- Record when expert judgment diverges from indicator rankings and why.
- Audit whether decision makers use indicators as evidence or as binding thresholds.
- Treat quantitative indicators as more appropriate when the evaluation unit is too large for direct expert reading of each paper or small portfolio, and document that scope boundary.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hicks et al. (2015) states that quantitative evaluation should support qualitative expert assessment.
- Hicks et al. argue that metrics can challenge peer-review bias and facilitate deliberation, but indicators must not substitute for informed judgment.
- The same article places responsibility on assessors rather than on metric outputs, making accountability a core part of mixed evaluation.
- The principle gives science-of-science studies a suggested experiment: compare whether indicator-supported peer review improves transparency without creating metric-only decisions.
- Verified full-text evidence from West, Bergstrom, and Bergstrom (2010) gives an earlier journal-evaluation scope boundary: direct expert reading can work for a few papers, while broader comparison across journals and disciplines requires indicators that still need careful interpretation.

## Caveats

- Qualitative judgment can also be biased, opaque, or status-driven.
- Mixed evaluation can become performative if committees mechanically cite qualitative evidence after following metric rankings.
- The right balance depends on the evaluation unit, field, mission, and decision stakes.
