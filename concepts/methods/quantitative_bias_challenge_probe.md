# Quantitative bias-challenge probe

## Summary

Metrics can be used as deliberative probes that surface possible peer-review bias without becoming automatic decision rules.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: review decision, metric profile, panel judgment, deliberation note, or bias audit.
- Typical representation: expert-review comparison with contextual metric evidence.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: bias surfacing in qualitative assessment.
- Empirical signature: metric evidence challenges an expert judgment and triggers documented deliberation rather than automatic override..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Specifies a constructive use of [metrics support qualitative assessment](metrics_support_qualitative_assessment.md).
- Connects metric evidence to [peer review gatekeeping](../mechanisms/peer_review_gatekeeping.md).
- Provides a softer alternative to score-driven evaluation.

## Operationalization

- Compare expert judgments with contextual metric profiles.
- Document divergences for panel discussion.
- Require written rationale when metric evidence challenges or fails to change expert views.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hicks et al. (2015) states that quantitative evidence can challenge peer-review bias while responsibility remains with human assessors.

## Caveats

- Metric disagreement is not proof of peer-review bias.
- Panels may rationalize rather than genuinely review metric challenges.
