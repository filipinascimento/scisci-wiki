# Topic-network equity intervention portfolio

## Summary

Topic-network equity intervention portfolio combines support for historically underrecognized research topics with access interventions that bring marginalized scientists into high-prestige networks and topics.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: funding program, institution, topic portfolio, demographic group, prestige network, or evaluation system.
- Typical representation: paired funding and network-access intervention, topic-support allocation, mentorship/network program, or policy evaluation design.
- Method target: reduce both between-topic and within-topic inequality rather than treating topic diversification or network access as separate fixes.
- Empirical signature: intervention designs simultaneously track topic funding, topic citation density, network position, participation, and within-topic recognition.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a policy design response to [topic-choice privilege](../mechanisms/topic_choice_privilege.md).
- Addresses [between-topic citation disadvantage](../mechanisms/between_topic_citation_disadvantage.md) by supporting undervalued areas and [within-topic citation disadvantage](../mechanisms/within_topic_citation_disadvantage.md) by widening access to high-prestige networks.
- Targets [demographic knowledge-gap accumulation](../mechanisms/demographic_knowledge_gap_accumulation.md) by investing in topics linked to underrepresented communities.
- Connects equity policy to [funding allocation design alternatives](funding_allocation_design_alternatives.md), [institutional prestige hierarchy](../mechanisms/institutional_prestige_hierarchy.md), and [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md).

## Operationalization

- Identify topics where marginalized groups are overrepresented and where topic citation density or funding is low.
- Allocate targeted resources to historically underrepresented or socially important topics while avoiding stigma or isolation.
- Create access pathways into high-prestige networks, topics, mentorship, collaborations, and institutions.
- Evaluate outcomes with quasi-experimental or longitudinal designs that separate topic-support effects from network-access effects.
- Track publication output, citations, collaboration structure, retention, promotion, funding, and community relevance.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Kozlowski et al. (2022) recommends that scientific institutions recognize knowledge gaps related to author race and gender segregation and promote topics where gendered and racially minoritized authors are more present.
- The paper states that funding agencies can allocate increased funding to historically underrepresented areas and that funding affects productivity, impact, advancement, and visibility.
- Kozlowski et al. also recommend initiatives that provide marginalized populations access to high-prestige networks and topics.
- The authors frame the paired strategy as a way to reduce variance in impact across topics and reduce within-topic disparities at the intersection of race and gender.

## Caveats

- Topic support should not confine marginalized researchers to identity-linked topics.
- Prestige-network access can reproduce hierarchy if it only assimilates researchers into existing evaluation norms.
- Funding underrepresented topics should include community relevance and quality assessment, not only citation gains.
- Policy evaluations must guard against selection effects, institutional signaling, and delayed career outcomes.
