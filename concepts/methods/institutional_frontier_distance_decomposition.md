# Institutional frontier-distance decomposition

## Summary

Institutional frontier-distance decomposition separates whether institutional differences in science-technology proximity come from field composition or within-field positioning.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: institution type, paper, patent, field, technology class, or distance regression.
- Typical representation: regression decomposition with field/class controls and institution-type indicators.
- Method target: distinguish what institutions work on from how close their outputs are to the boundary within the same area.
- Empirical signature: institutional distance gaps split into composition and within-field components.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [distance-based basic-applied typology](distance_based_basic_applied_typology.md) for institutional comparisons.
- Adds an explanatory layer to [university-firm patent-paper handoff](../mechanisms/university_firm_patent_paper_handoff.md).
- Supports institutional uses of [science-technology distance](../measures/science_technology_distance.md).

## Operationalization

- Classify outputs by institutional type, such as university, government laboratory, or firm.
- Estimate distance differences with and without field or technology-class controls.
- Attribute the difference between specifications to field composition.
- Interpret remaining differences as within-field institutional positioning.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ahmadpoor and Jones (2017) compares universities, government laboratories, and firms by distance to the patent-paper boundary.
- The paper decomposes university-firm paper distance into about one-third field composition and two-thirds within-field institutional differences.
- It similarly decomposes university patent proximity to science into field-composition and within-field components.

## Caveats

- Institution-type matching must be accurate before decomposition is meaningful.
- Field controls can be too coarse to capture all topic composition.
- Decomposition is descriptive and does not prove why institutions choose different positions.
