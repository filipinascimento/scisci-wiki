# Science of science framework

## Summary

The science of science framework studies science itself as a data-rich system of people, institutions, ideas, artifacts, incentives, and policies.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, paper, project, institution, field, funding program, policy, or scientific system.
- Typical representation: integrated empirical framework combining scholarly data, networks, models, and policy evaluation.
- Mechanism or measurement target: conditions that shape discovery, creativity, collaboration, recognition, and scientific progress.
- Empirical signature: regularities across papers, careers, institutions, and fields can be measured, modeled, and tested against policy-relevant outcomes.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Serves as an umbrella motif tying together datasets, mechanisms, measures, representations, methods, and validations.
- Links [science as a multiscale network](../representations/science_as_multiscale_network.md) with predictive and causal tools.
- Provides the framing for [scientific discovery prediction](scientific_discovery_prediction.md), [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md), and policy design.
- Helps separate descriptive mapping from causal claims and intervention-ready evidence.

## Operationalization

- Assemble large-scale data on scholarly outputs, funding, careers, teams, citations, institutions, patents, topics, and mobility.
- Represent science through coupled networks, time series, text features, embeddings, and field classifications.
- Fit descriptive, predictive, generative, econometric, and simulation models to explain observed regularities.
- Evaluate policy relevance with experiments, quasi-experiments, robustness checks, and domain-specific interpretation.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) defines science of science as a quantitative effort to understand interactions among scientific agents across geographic and temporal scales.
- The review frames SciSci as combining natural, computational, and social science capabilities for empirical analysis and generative modeling of science, institutions, and the workforce.
- Fortunato et al. state that SciSci integrates scientometrics, sociology of science, innovation studies, network science, machine learning, econometrics, mathematical analysis, data visualization, and simulation.
- The same review makes policy design explicit: science-of-science models should support career paths, organizational evaluation, funding vehicles, frontier identification, and tools that can accelerate scientific progress.
- The review also supports split-out pages for [scholarly input-output data substrate](../datasets/scholarly_input_output_data_substrate.md), [career metadata fusion](../datasets/career_metadata_fusion.md), [metric pluralism and productive specialization](../mechanisms/metric_pluralism_productive_specialization.md), [funding-allocation design alternatives](funding_allocation_design_alternatives.md), and [mind-machine science partnerships](mind_machine_science_partnerships.md).
- Additional review-level guardrails now include [citation-count currency hierarchy](../mechanisms/citation_count_currency_hierarchy.md), [publication-conditioned SciSci blind spot](../validations/publication_conditioned_scisci_blind_spot.md), and [biomedical need-resource misalignment](../measures/biomedical_need_resource_misalignment.md).

## Caveats

- A field-level framework is not a substitute for evidence about a specific mechanism or intervention.
- Universal regularities can hide important field, country, culture, institution, and data-source differences.
- Predictive and evaluative tools can reshape incentives, so policy use needs causal validation and responsible-metrics guardrails.
