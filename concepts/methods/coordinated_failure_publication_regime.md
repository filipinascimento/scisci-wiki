# Coordinated failure-publication regime

## Summary

Coordinated failure-publication regime is a counterfactual search institution in which positive and negative findings are recorded and duplicate experiments are avoided.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: experiment, candidate edge, failed edge, duplicate test, registry, or field-level search policy.
- Typical representation: coordinated discovery simulation, negative-edge registry, or no-repeat experiment rule.
- Method target: estimate the discovery-speed gain from making failed attempts visible and reusable.
- Empirical signature: fewer duplicate or failed experiments are needed to discover the same portion of a target knowledge network.

## Operationalization

- Represent successful experiments as discovered edges and failed experiments as tested non-edges.
- Allow future search strategies to avoid previously tested failures and duplicate successes.
- Compare relative loss under ordinary unpublished-failure search versus coordinated publication of positive and negative findings.
- Evaluate whether coordination gains differ for random, observed, and optimized search strategies.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Rzhetsky et al. (2015) defines a coordinated discovery scenario in which scientists publish all findings, positive and negative, and do not repeat experiments.
- The paper analytically evaluates the random coordinated case and numerically estimates coordinated MEDLINE and optimal strategies on the sample network.
- Rzhetsky et al. report that coordinating research decreases discovery costs regardless of strategy.
- The article concludes that publication of failures substantially increases discovery speed in the modeled chemical-relationship network.

## Caveats

- A failure is useful only when methods, conditions, and quality are documented enough to be reusable.
- Avoiding all repeats can conflict with replication, robustness, safety, and translational validation.
- Coordination may require registries, standards, incentives, and access controls that differ by field.
