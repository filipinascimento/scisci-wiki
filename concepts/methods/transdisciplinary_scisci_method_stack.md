# Transdisciplinary SciSci method stack

## Summary

Transdisciplinary SciSci method stack is a study-design motif that combines theories and tools from scientometrics, sociology, innovation studies, econometrics, network science, machine learning, simulation, and mathematical analysis.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: science-of-science study, research design, data pipeline, or policy model.
- Typical representation: mixed-method stack, transdisciplinary toolkit, or integrated computational-social-science workflow.
- Method target: connect large-scale scholarly traces with theories of scientific behavior and policy-relevant inference.
- Empirical signature: a study uses multiple disciplinary inputs rather than a single bibliometric indicator or model family.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends the [science of science framework](science_of_science_framework.md) from broad agenda to concrete method repertoire.
- Supports [domain-specific SciSci studies](domain_specific_scisci_studies.md), where the stack is adapted to field-specific questions.
- Requires a [scholarly input-output data substrate](../datasets/scholarly_input_output_data_substrate.md).
- Connects descriptive mapping, causal modeling, and prediction inside one reusable workflow.

## Operationalization

- Specify which theoretical traditions and empirical tools are being combined.
- Join scholarly traces, institutional data, and career metadata when the question requires them.
- Use descriptive statistics and visualization for orientation, then network, econometric, machine-learning, or simulation tools for inference.
- Report which parts of the stack support description, prediction, causal inference, or policy design.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) says SciSci benefited from collaborations among natural, computational, and social scientists.
- The review states that SciSci integrates findings from scientometrics, sociology of science, and innovation studies.
- It also lists descriptive statistics, visualization, econometric methods, network science, machine learning, mathematical analysis, and agent-based modeling as part of the field's quantitative repertoire.

## Caveats

- Adding methods does not automatically improve inference; each layer needs a clear role.
- Method stacks can obscure assumptions if the components are not documented separately.
- Transdisciplinary designs need field expertise to avoid generic measurements that miss local scientific practice.
