# Mission-aligned indicator selection

## Summary

Mission-aligned indicator selection chooses evaluation indicators according to the stated goals of a researcher, group, institution, funder, or program.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: evaluation unit, mission statement, indicator, assessment goal, research portfolio, or policy target.
- Typical representation: indicator-to-goal mapping, evaluation rubric, mission-specific dashboard, or portfolio assessment plan.
- Method target: align measurement with the work being valued.
- Empirical signature: indicators differ across basic science, applied research, local engagement, teaching, policy, industry, or public missions.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Turns [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) from general principles into operational evaluation design.
- Helps explain why one-size-fits-all rankings can misvalue domain-specific and applied work.
- Supports [domain-specific SciSci studies](domain_specific_scisci_studies.md) by requiring indicators to match field and mission context.
- Provides a policy-design complement to [SciSci quasi-experimental policy evaluation](../validations/scisci_quasi_experimental_policy_evaluation.md).

## Operationalization

- State the mission or program goals before choosing indicators.
- Map each indicator to a goal and document what it cannot measure.
- Use different portfolios for research-frontier advancement, societal problem solving, education, clinical translation, policy contribution, and local engagement.
- Revisit the mapping when institutional goals or research missions change.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hicks et al. (2015) says performance should be measured against the research missions of the institution, group, or researcher.
- Hicks et al. argue that program goals should be stated at the start and that indicators should relate clearly to those goals.
- The article explicitly distinguishes frontier-advancing academic research from research aimed at societal problems, policy, industry, or public relevance.
- The full text concludes that no single evaluation model applies to all contexts.

## Caveats

- Mission language can be vague or strategically rewritten after results are known.
- Local mission alignment should not become an excuse for unvalidated indicators.
- Multiple missions can conflict, requiring explicit weighting or separate evaluation tracks.
