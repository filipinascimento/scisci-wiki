# Mixed-funding within-system comparison

## Summary

Mixed-funding within-system comparison studies multiple funding models inside one national or institutional science system so broad country context is held more constant than in cross-national comparisons.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: project, paper, researcher, funding source, institution, field, or national funding regime.
- Typical representation: within-country contrast between block funding, competitive project funding, and other funding categories.
- Method target: compare funding-model effects while reducing confounding from national evaluation culture, labor markets, and database coverage.
- Empirical signature: the same publication and author environment contains enough variation in funding models to estimate contrasts and interactions.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Gives a design foundation for [funding model status contingency](../mechanisms/funding_model_status_contingency.md).
- Works with [project funding-source survey linkage](../datasets/project_funding_source_survey_linkage.md), [competitive-funding share threshold](../measures/competitive_funding_share_threshold.md), and [funding definition sensitivity](../validations/funding_definition_sensitivity.md).
- Helps distinguish within-system funding mechanisms from broad national-system differences in [funding incentives and exploration](../mechanisms/funding_incentives_and_exploration.md).

## Operationalization

- Select a science system where more than one funding model contributes materially to research projects.
- Measure project funding sources directly or through harmonized administrative records.
- Compare outputs under competing funding types within fields, years, institutions, and researcher statuses.
- Treat other funders and mixed funding as explicit categories or sensitivity checks rather than forcing every project into one binary.
- Estimate interaction terms when the same funding model may affect high- and low-status researchers differently.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Lee, and Walsh (2018) uses Japan because competitive project grants and internal block funds both play substantial roles in the same national research system.
- The paper motivates Japan as a setting where block and competitive funding can be compared while avoiding a simple Germany-versus-United-States national contrast.
- Wang et al. use this within-system variation to evaluate average funding-model effects and status-contingent differences in publication novelty.

## Caveats

- Within-system comparisons still require controls for institutions, fields, and researcher selection into funding sources.
- A single national system can limit external validity when funding governance, career structure, or gender stratification is distinctive.
- Mixed funding sources can blur treatment labels unless fractional attribution and sensitivity checks are preserved.
