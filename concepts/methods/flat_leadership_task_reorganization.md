# Flat-leadership task reorganization

## Summary

Flat-leadership task reorganization is the intervention idea that a team can broaden leadership roles among existing members, rather than changing team size or age composition, to increase innovation potential and support career development.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: team, project, author-role assignment, lab management practice, grant team, or contribution plan.
- Typical representation: role-allocation protocol, leadership-rotation plan, contribution matrix, L-ratio target, or team-design intervention.
- Method target: redistribute conceptual, design, interpretation, writing, and coordination work without necessarily adding or removing team members.
- Empirical signature: a team with the same size and similar career-age composition shifts toward higher L-ratio and more shared leadership.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Turns [team hierarchy and flatness](../mechanisms/team_hierarchy_flatness.md) into a design and policy lever.
- Operationalizes a possible response to [team hierarchy productivity redistribution](../mechanisms/team_hierarchy_productivity_redistribution.md) and [team hierarchy credit-risk hedge](../mechanisms/team_hierarchy_credit_risk_hedge.md).
- Connects contribution-statement data to practical team governance, mentoring, and responsible evaluation.
- Provides a testable intervention for science-policy experiments on team design, training, and grant management.

## Operationalization

- Before a project begins, map key lead activities such as conception, design, interpretation, writing, supervision, and coordination.
- Assign or rotate these activities across multiple team members, including junior or support-position scientists where appropriate.
- Record expected roles in a contribution matrix and update deviations transparently.
- Measure resulting L-ratio, role-specific productivity, novelty, developmental index, and citation-window outcomes.
- Compare reorganized teams with similar teams that keep leadership concentrated.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Xu, Wu, and Evans (2022) argues that optimizing team innovation by changing size or age structure can require hiring or firing, but reorganizing tasks can broaden leadership without changing membership.
- The same full text suggests that leading roles can be extended across large teams and include younger scientists to maximize innovative potential.
- Xu et al. connect flatter teams with higher novelty, lower developmental index, greater long-term influence, and broader support-author productivity.
- The method is a suggested experiment inferred from their observational evidence rather than an experimentally validated intervention.

## Caveats

- Role redistribution can fail if technical expertise, authority, time, or accountability are not actually shared.
- Some high-stakes projects may require clear centralized decision rights.
- Experimental tests should avoid turning L-ratio into a box-checking metric detached from real intellectual leadership.
