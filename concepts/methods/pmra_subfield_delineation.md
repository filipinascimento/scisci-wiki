# PMRA subfield delineation

## Summary

PMRA subfield delineation uses the PubMed Related Citations Algorithm to construct biomedical research subfields around focal articles from title words, abstract words, and MeSH terms rather than from citation, collaboration, or journal-category links.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: focal article, related article, MeSH descriptor, title/abstract term, subfield, or star-linked article set.
- Typical representation: content-similarity neighborhood around one or more source articles, often with ordinal and cardinal relatedness scores.
- Method target: define local biomedical intellectual neighborhoods without using the social or citation relationships that may be outcomes.
- Empirical signature: subfield membership is generated by PubMed's related-articles function and can be aggregated to field-year panels.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Defines the subfield boundary for [superstar death event studies](superstar_death_event_study.md).
- Provides a controlled-vocabulary alternative to [topic models](topic_models.md), [semantic embeddings](../representations/semantic_embeddings.md), and citation-network clustering.
- Helps avoid endogeneity when collaboration or citation links are the primary outcomes of interest.
- Connects content-defined subfields to measures of direction change such as [MeSH keyword trajectory shift](../measures/mesh_keyword_trajectory_shift.md).

## Operationalization

- Identify focal source articles, such as articles where a star scientist appeared as last author in the five years before death.
- Query PubMed's related-articles feature for each source article and store related records, relatedness rank, and relatedness scores.
- Use MeSH descriptors, title words, and abstract words as the content signal, recognizing that MeSH terms are assigned by professional indexers while title and abstract words come from authors.
- Build subfield-year panels by aggregating related articles and their authors, references, citations, and grant acknowledgments.
- Check that PMRA cutoff rules and relatedness-score truncation do not differentially distort treated and control subfields.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Azoulay, Fons-Rosen, and Graff Zivin (2019) uses PMRA to define subfields around deceased biomedical star scientists' recent last-author papers.
- The paper emphasizes that PMRA relies heavily on MeSH and title/abstract words, but not on citation or collaboration linkages.
- Azoulay et al. use the method because citation and collaboration links are among the outcomes whose response to star death they want to measure.
- The full text reports robustness checks for PMRA cutoff rules and notes that treated/control comparisons reduce concern that idiosyncratic PMRA features drive the results.
- PMRA also gives the authors both ordinal and cardinal proximity measures, supporting subfield-coherence and within-field direction analyses.
- The same paper audits same-star subfield overlap, supporting the use of multiple PMRA-defined source-article neighborhoods as distinct field units.

## Caveats

- PMRA is biomedical and PubMed-specific; analogous methods in other fields need different vocabularies or embedding models.
- Title and abstract words can reflect author framing, aspirations, or strategic language.
- MeSH updates can lag emerging terminology, while title/abstract inputs can partially compensate for that lag.
- Related-article boundaries are algorithmic but not theory-free; cutoff and relatedness rules need sensitivity checks.
