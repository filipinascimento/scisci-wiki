# Generic trace proxy target operationalization

## Summary

Generic trace proxy target operationalization is the method of defining science-of-science prediction targets around measurable platform traces such as citations, publications, grants, prizes, careers, and productivity.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: prediction target, proxy trace, bibliographic platform, career event, grant record, prize, or publication outcome.
- Typical representation: target-construction table linking available traces to forecastable outcomes.
- Method target: make large-scale prediction feasible by converting broad scientific success questions into measurable proxy outcomes.
- Empirical signature: prediction studies focus on outcomes that can be observed consistently in large digital science data.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Bridges [predictive bibliographic platform traces](../datasets/predictive_bibliographic_platform_traces.md) and [scientific discovery prediction](scientific_discovery_prediction.md).
- Explains why [lagging-indicator prediction limits](../validations/lagging_indicator_prediction_limits.md) appear when proxy traces stand in for discovery, quality, or frontier movement.
- Helps compare generic platform proxies with [context-specific SciSci prediction data](../datasets/context_specific_scisci_prediction_data.md).
- Supports [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) by making proxy-target choices explicit.

## Operationalization

- Define the substantive construct, such as discovery value, impact, career success, or field emergence.
- Select measurable traces available before and after the prediction horizon.
- State what the proxy captures and what it omits.
- Benchmark proxy-based predictions against task-specific data or expert annotations where possible.
- Report whether proxy targets are being used for description, prediction, evaluation, or policy allocation.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Clauset et al. (2017) describes science as a vast ecosystem whose scale encourages focus on generic measurable quantities.
- The paper lists citations, production of new works, career trajectories, grant funding, scholarly prizes, and related traces as examples.
- Clauset et al. state that digital technology has made these data abundant and enabled computational tools for analyzing scientific activity.
- The same passage names platform sources such as Google Scholar, PubMed, Web of Science, JSTOR, ORCID, EasyChair, and altmetrics.

## Caveats

- Proxy targets can be easier to predict than the underlying scientific construct.
- Generic traces often lag frontier movement and can reinforce existing visibility.
- Different platforms encode different missingness, incentives, and governance constraints.
