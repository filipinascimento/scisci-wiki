# Reviewer transparency enforcement

## Summary

Reviewer transparency enforcement is a peer-review method in which reviewers and editors actively check disclosure requirements, accept transparent imperfections, and request robustness evidence when analytic choices could make a claim fragile.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: reviewer report, editorial decision, manuscript checklist, robustness request, or author response.
- Typical representation: reviewer checklist, disclosure-compliance table, decision letter, robustness appendix, or journal policy.
- Method target: convert transparency norms into enforceable review practice rather than leaving disclosure as an optional author preference.
- Empirical signature: reviewers ask for stopping rules, all variables, all conditions, exclusion sensitivity, covariate-free results, and alternative analyses before accepting a confirmatory claim.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Supplies the review-side counterpart to the [researcher-degrees disclosure checklist](researcher_degrees_disclosure_checklist.md).
- Gives [peer review and gatekeeping](../mechanisms/peer_review_gatekeeping.md) a constructive role in reducing [analytic flexibility and false-positive risk](../validations/analytic_flexibility_false_positives.md).
- Supports journal-policy audits that compare formal disclosure rules with actual reviewer requests and author compliance.
- Connects transparency interventions to incentives: authors face lower rewards for polished but undisclosed analytic selectivity when reviewers reward complete reporting.

## Operationalization

- Code reviewer reports for requests about stopping rules, sample-size rationales, all collected variables, all experimental conditions, exclusions, covariates, and robustness checks.
- Track whether editorial decisions condition acceptance on checklist completion or sensitivity analyses.
- Compare journals, fields, or policy periods by the share of manuscripts receiving disclosure-enforcement requests.
- Link reviewer requests to later corrections, replications, robustness outcomes, or post-publication critique.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Simmons et al. (2011) proposes reviewer guidelines alongside author requirements for preventing false-positive psychology.
- The reviewer guidelines ask reviewers to ensure authors follow disclosure requirements, tolerate imperfect but transparent results, and scrutinize whether findings depend on arbitrary analytic decisions.
- Simmons et al. argue that this enforcement changes incentives because omissions become consequential false statements rather than invisible flexibility.
- The same full text distinguishes reviewer enforcement from data/materials posting alone: reviewers should not need to reconstruct hidden flexibility from raw artifacts when a concise disclosure policy can expose it directly.

## Caveats

- Reviewer enforcement can increase review burden and may be uneven across journals, fields, and reviewer expertise.
- Checklist compliance does not prove that all undisclosed analyses are absent.
- The method does not solve whole-study file-drawer bias unless paired with broader registration, repository, or replication systems.
- Overly rigid enforcement can discourage legitimate exploratory work unless exploration is explicitly labeled and evaluated accordingly.
