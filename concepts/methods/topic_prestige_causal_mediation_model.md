# Topic-prestige causal-mediation model

## Summary

Topic-prestige causal-mediation model tests how topic choice and prestige markers jointly mediate systemic inequality mechanisms.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author, topic, institution, citation outcome, prestige marker, or identity group.
- Typical representation: mediation model with topic and prestige paths.
- Method target: move from descriptive association to mechanism tests for inequality in scientific impact.
- Empirical signature: identity-outcome gaps decompose into topic placement, prestige access, and residual components.

## Operationalization

- Measure topic portfolios, prestige markers, identity categories, and citation outcomes.
- Specify causal assumptions and temporal ordering.
- Estimate mediation through topics and prestige access.
- Test sensitivity to unobserved confounding and proxy error.
- Report residual gaps separately from explained components.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Kozlowski et al. (2022) calls for causal models that examine how topic choice and prestige indicators mediate systemic inequality.
- The paper frames its own results as large-scale descriptive evidence that motivates such causal work.
- This motif captures the suggested next methodological step beyond the paper's association maps.

## Caveats

- Topic choice and prestige are jointly determined and can be endogenous.
- Observational mediation needs strong assumptions.
- Identity proxy uncertainty can propagate through all modeled paths.
