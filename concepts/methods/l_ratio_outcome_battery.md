# L-ratio outcome battery

## Summary

A coordinated outcome battery tests team flatness against novelty, development, role-specific productivity, and citation timing in one design.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, author role, L-ratio, novelty score, development index, productivity count, or citation window.
- Typical representation: multi-outcome regression suite keyed to L-ratio.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: broad outcome profile of team flatness.
- Empirical signature: different outcomes show different signs or timing as L-ratio changes.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines flat-team outcome design by linking it to [lead role ratio](../measures/lead_role_ratio.md) and [developmental index](../measures/developmental_index.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [citation window impact split](../measures/citation_window_impact_split.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [team hierarchy productivity redistribution](../mechanisms/team_hierarchy_productivity_redistribution.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Regress each outcome on L-ratio with a common control set.
- Include novelty, developmental index, lead productivity, support productivity, short-term citations, and long-term citations.
- Compare signs, magnitudes, and uncertainty across outcomes rather than privileging one endpoint.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified local full text from Xu et al. (2022) evaluates L-ratio across novelty, developmental index, lead productivity, support productivity, short-term citations, and long-term citations.
- The battery makes flatness an outcome-profile question rather than a single-metric question.

## Caveats

- Multiple outcomes require multiplicity and dependence-aware interpretation.
- Shared controls do not make the outcomes causally independent.
