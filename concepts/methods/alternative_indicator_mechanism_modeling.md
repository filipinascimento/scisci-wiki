# Alternative-Indicator Mechanism Modeling

## Summary

Alternative-indicator mechanism modeling uses science-of-science models to explain what non-citation indicators capture, how they relate to citations, and how they can be manipulated.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, indicator, platform, field, reader event, download trace, or metric system.
- Typical representation: generative or diagnostic model of alternative-metric behavior.
- Method target: interpret non-citation indicators before using them in evaluation.
- Empirical signature: modeled distributions, cross-indicator relationships, and manipulation signatures explain observed alternative-metric patterns.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Operationalizes [metric pluralism and productive specialization](../mechanisms/metric_pluralism_productive_specialization.md).
- Supports [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) by clarifying indicator meaning.
- Connects [altmetrics](../datasets/altmetrics.md) and [social web non-citing reader traces](../mechanisms/social_web_non_citing_reader_traces.md).
- Helps detect [citation metric gaming](../validations/citation_metric_gaming.md) and related indicator manipulation.

## Operationalization

- Model distributions of downloads, reads, mentions, saves, or other non-citation events.
- Compare alternative indicators with citation-based metrics across fields and time windows.
- Identify anomalies consistent with gaming, bots, promotion campaigns, or platform artifacts.
- Document the mechanism an indicator plausibly captures before using it in assessment.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) argues that proliferating metrics require understanding what each captures and misses.
- The review says science of science can model mechanisms governing performance indicators.
- Fortunato et al. specifically mention download distributions, relationships to citation-based metrics, and manipulation recognition as targets for modeling.

## Caveats

- Alternative indicators often mix attention, access, platform design, and strategic behavior.
- Mechanism models need platform provenance and temporal coverage, not only aggregate counts.
- Good model fit does not imply that an indicator is suitable for reward allocation.
