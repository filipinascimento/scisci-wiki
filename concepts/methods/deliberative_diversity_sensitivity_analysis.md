# Deliberative diversity sensitivity analysis

## Summary

Deliberative diversity sensitivity analysis is a method for varying diversity weights, disparity assumptions, performance criteria, and interaction terms to support transparent policy deliberation rather than a single optimal diversity prescription.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: stakeholder perspective, research portfolio, technology mix, diversity weight, performance criterion, interaction assumption, or policy scenario.
- Typical representation: sensitivity grid, scenario portfolio frontier, perspective comparison, or deliberation map.
- Method target: reveal how portfolio recommendations change under contending assumptions about diversity and performance.
- Empirical signature: alternative perspectives produce different conditionally optimal portfolios, making disagreements explicit for deliberation.

## Operationalization

- Define the decision context, portfolio elements, performance criteria, diversity dimensions, and stakeholders.
- Elicit or specify multiple plausible disparity spaces, performance weights, interaction terms, and diversity-premium values.
- Compute portfolio summaries or frontiers for each perspective.
- Identify stable recommendations and assumption-sensitive reversals.
- Use the results as structured evidence for deliberation, documenting which value judgments drive disagreement.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Stirling (2007) states that the value of the system-level heuristic is not to derive an unconditional optimum.
- The paper instead uses sensitivity analysis to explore perspectives and assumptions about performance, interactions, disparity, component aggregation, and the performance-diversity trade-off.
- Stirling's habitat and energy examples show how varying the weight attached to diversity changes conditionally optimal portfolio composition.
- The full text frames this workflow as a basis for more effective and transparent deliberation between contending positions.

## Caveats

- Sensitivity analysis can map disagreement without resolving political or ethical conflicts.
- Results depend on the set of perspectives included; missing stakeholders can still bias the deliberation.
- Quantitative frontiers should be paired with uncertainty, feasibility, and governance constraints.
- The method is most useful when assumptions are archived and rerunnable.
