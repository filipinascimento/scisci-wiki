# Prospective evaluation-protocol precommitment

## Summary

Prospective evaluation-protocol precommitment is the method of stating evaluation goals, data rules, and indicator uses before the work being assessed is complete.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: evaluation protocol, program review, funding formula, data-construction rule, indicator suite, or assessment cycle.
- Typical representation: public protocol, preregistered assessment plan, indicator rulebook, or evaluation call document.
- Method target: reduce retrospective metric shopping and post hoc rule changes.
- Empirical signature: evaluators can point to a pre-existing protocol that defines goals, data sources, exclusions, normalizations, and indicator roles.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Combines [mission-aligned indicator selection](mission_aligned_indicator_selection.md) with [transparent metric construction](transparent_metric_construction.md).
- Gives [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) a timing rule: the protocol should exist before outcomes are known.
- Provides an evaluation analogue to open-science precommitment without requiring a randomized experiment.
- Helps distinguish legitimate [indicator lifecycle review](indicator_lifecycle_review.md) from opportunistic rule revision.

## Operationalization

- Publish the evaluation goals and intended indicator roles before the assessment period closes.
- Specify data sources, database construction rules, field classifications, and normalization procedures in advance.
- Record exceptions and revision triggers separately from the initial protocol.
- Archive protocol versions so evaluated units can inspect which rules applied to which cycle.
- Audit whether metric choices changed after preliminary rankings or score distributions were visible.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hicks et al. (2015) links responsible assessment to up-front goals and pre-set database construction rules.
- The Leiden Manifesto frames this as protection against evaluation systems that drift toward available indicators rather than declared purposes.
- The motif separates temporal commitment from transparency: a metric can be transparent but still selected after results are known.
- It also separates precommitment from rigidity because later revisions can be legitimate when they are documented and applied prospectively.

## Caveats

- Precommitment can freeze flawed rules if revision governance is weak.
- Some evaluation contexts require adaptation to unexpected output types or emerging research areas.
- Public protocols can invite gaming, so precommitment should be paired with plural indicators and qualitative review.
