# Large-scale concept testing

## Summary

Large-scale concept testing prioritizes adequately powered, low-bias studies for claims or conceptual families with already plausible pre-study odds, so negative results can refute broad areas rather than only narrow hypotheses.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: research question, concept family, large study, meta-analysis, field, or evidence-prioritization decision.
- Typical representation: priority list for large studies, concept-level trial, high-prior confirmation study, or low-bias evidence synthesis.
- Method target: spend expensive confirmatory evidence on questions where the post-study probability can become decisive.
- Empirical signature: large-scale tests are reserved for claims with substantial prior plausibility or broad conceptual consequences.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides an improvement strategy for [positive predictive value of findings](../measures/positive_predictive_value_findings.md).
- Connects [pre-study odds](../measures/pre_study_odds.md) to study-size and funding-prioritization decisions.
- Gives [negative findings evidence value](../mechanisms/negative_findings_evidence_value.md) a high-leverage role, because a well-powered negative can rule out a concept family.
- Helps distinguish useful confirmatory megastudies from large but narrow marketing-driven studies.

## Operationalization

- Estimate whether a research question has enough prior plausibility to justify a large confirmatory study.
- Prefer designs with low bias, high power, clear outcomes, and strong protocol discipline.
- Choose questions that test broad mechanisms or concept families where a negative result would be informative.
- Avoid using enormous sample sizes merely to detect trivial effects with little substantive meaning.
- Pair large-scale tests with preregistration, complete reporting, and transparent evidence synthesis.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ioannidis (2005) argues that large studies or low-bias meta-analyses can improve post-study probability by approaching an unknown gold standard.
- The paper says large-scale evidence should be targeted to research questions where pre-study probability is already considerably high.
- Ioannidis argues that large studies are especially useful when they test major concepts rather than narrow, specific questions.
- The same section warns that large studies chosen for narrow marketing promotion are often wasted and that extremely large studies can detect statistically significant but trivial effects.

## Caveats

- High pre-study odds are difficult to estimate and can be distorted by expert prejudice or field enthusiasm.
- Large studies can still be biased, underinterpreted, or overpowered for trivial effects.
- Concept-level testing should avoid prematurely closing fields where measurement or theory is still immature.
