# Two-phase hypothesis seed-grant design

## Summary

Two-phase hypothesis seed-grant design separates early screening of hypotheses and approaches from later eligibility for larger seed funding.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: proposal phase, hypothesis submission, review score, prize, seed grant, or follow-on funding pool.
- Typical representation: first-stage idea competition followed by second-stage seed-grant allocation.
- Method target: observe early frontier ideas at lower cost while preserving a path to material research resources.
- Empirical signature: initial winners receive recognition or eligibility advantages for later funding.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects proposal-level [peer review and gatekeeping](../mechanisms/peer_review_gatekeeping.md) to downstream resource allocation.
- Creates a setting for [rejected-proposal line attrition](../mechanisms/rejected_proposal_line_attrition.md) and [unobserved proposal-quality identification](../validations/unobserved_proposal_quality_identification.md).
- Works with [low-entry-cost proposal capture](low_entry_cost_proposal_capture.md) when researchers might otherwise avoid speculative submissions.

## Operationalization

- Define a short first-stage submission format for hypotheses or approaches.
- Review first-stage submissions under the experimental or administrative design.
- Use first-stage outcomes to assign prizes, invitations, or second-stage funding eligibility.
- Track later proposal continuation and output separately from first-stage score.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Boudreau et al. (2016) reports a two-phase grant process for endocrine-related disease research.
- The first phase solicited ideas and hypotheses, while winners gained a route into a later seed-grant pool.
- The design reduced the cost of early participation while preserving consequences for later funding allocation.

## Caveats

- First-stage results can become status signals that affect later review.
- Follow-on eligibility may mix scientific quality, compliance, and presentation skill.
- The second phase must be tracked explicitly before claims about funded outputs are made.
