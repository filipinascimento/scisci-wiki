# Creative-product phenotyping agenda

## Summary

Creative-product phenotyping agenda is the method motif that embeddings and representation-learning tools can be used to characterize creative outputs directly, beyond aligning them into career phases.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: creative product, paper, artwork, film, embedding vector, interpretable dimension, or career event.
- Typical representation: multimodal embedding, product phenotype, style dimension, topic dimension, cast-plot vector, or co-citation community.
- Method target: build validated representations that expose characteristics of creative products for career and impact analysis.
- Empirical signature: product-level features can be extracted and related to career events, phase shifts, or impact outcomes.

- Supports future analyses of novelty, style, topic, and recombination at the output level.

## Operationalization

- Build product embeddings from text, images, citations, cast networks, metadata, or multimodal sources.
- Validate embedding dimensions against human labels, expert taxonomies, or downstream prediction tasks.
- Extract interpretable phenotypes and relate them to career phase, collaboration, funding, and impact.
- Track representation drift across fields, media, and historical periods.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Liu et al. (2021) uses deep-learning and network-science representations of artworks, films, and scientific papers to study career phase shifts.
- The paper closes by suggesting that such representation techniques can probe characteristics of creative products and that future deep learning may add further creative dimensions.
- This motif captures the broader methodological agenda beyond the specific entropy pipeline.

## Caveats

- Embeddings can encode platform, language, and field biases.
- Product phenotypes need validation before interpretation.
- Predictive utility does not automatically imply meaningful creative dimensions.
