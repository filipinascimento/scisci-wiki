# Consultative perspective elicitation

## Summary

Diversity appraisal can elicit stakeholder or expert perspectives to define categories, disparity spaces, value dimensions, and sensitivity scenarios.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: stakeholder, expert, perspective, category definition, disparity judgment, or value weight.
- Typical representation: consultative protocol for eliciting appraisal perspectives before diversity measurement.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: participatory construction of diversity measures and sensitivity analyses.
- Empirical signature: diversity results are reported across elicited perspectives rather than one analyst-imposed category and weight system.

## Operationalization

- Identify relevant experts, stakeholders, or affected communities for the appraisal context.
- Elicit category definitions, disparity dimensions, value weights, and unacceptable aggregation choices.
- Report results as a sensitivity set across elicited perspectives.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Stirling (2007) emphasizes that diversity appraisal depends on perspectives, values, and category definitions.
- This motivates consultative elicitation as a method for constructing and stress-testing diversity measures.

## Caveats

- Consultation can be unevenly representative and requires transparent sampling.
- Elicited perspectives may conflict, requiring plural reporting rather than forced consensus.
