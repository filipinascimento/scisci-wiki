# Century-scale sleeping-beauty observation window

## Summary

Long historical citation windows are needed to observe decades-long sleeps and late awakenings.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: publication cohort, citation window, corpus span, sleep length, awakening time, or cohort age.
- Typical representation: long-window citation exposure design for delayed-recognition detection.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: observable horizon for century-scale sleeping-beauty events.
- Empirical signature: top delayed-recognition cases include papers with awakenings after multiple decades, requiring corpora older than ordinary impact windows..

## Operationalization

- Use publication and citation corpora spanning a century or more when looking for long sleeps.
- Report maximum observable sleep length and cohort-age exposure for each publication year.
- Separate delayed-recognition absence from insufficient observation horizon.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ke et al. (2015) uses APS and WoS records over more than a century and reports top cases awakening after roughly 50 to 100 years.
- The motif turns corpus time span into a measurement requirement for sleeping-beauty discovery.

## Caveats

- Older coverage and indexing vary by field and database.
- Long windows favor older cohorts unless age exposure is explicitly handled.
