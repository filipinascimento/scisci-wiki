# Dimension-targeted interdisciplinarity policy

## Summary

Dimension-targeted interdisciplinarity policy designs incentives around specific interdisciplinarity dimensions, such as variety, disparity, and balance, rather than treating interdisciplinarity as one undifferentiated goal.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: funding program, policy intervention, interdisciplinary team, research portfolio, or evaluation criterion.
- Typical representation: policy target by dimension, measurement plan, funding criterion, or theory-measure alignment table.
- Method target: align science policy with the specific interdisciplinary mechanism it wants to encourage.
- Empirical signature: policies distinguish increasing category variety, increasing distant integration, and changing balance across disciplines.

## Operationalization

- State whether the intervention targets variety, disparity, balance, or a combination.
- Choose measures that match the invoked theory and policy goal.
- Anticipate different consequences for citation impact, usefulness, novelty, and evaluation difficulty.
- Avoid rewarding one composite score when the desired behavior is dimension-specific.
- Test whether the intervention changes the targeted dimension without unintended shifts in other dimensions.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Thijs, and Glanzel (2015) argues that different interdisciplinarity measures should be used according to context because interdisciplinarity is multidimensional and its aspects have different consequences.
- The authors connect theories to dimensions: information processing emphasizes cognitive variety, while combinatorial novelty emphasizes disparity.
- Their policy discussion argues for a refined policy agenda, where effective policies require nuanced understanding of different dimensions and their consequences.
- They suggest encouraging knowledge integration across more disciplines, across more remote disciplines, or with caution around excessive disparity and balance depending on the policy goal.

## Caveats

- Citation impact is only one outcome; policies may also target usefulness, equity, resilience, or societal problem solving.
- Dimension-specific incentives can be gamed if metrics become targets.
- Some interdisciplinary value may require balanced integration even when citation counts penalize it.
