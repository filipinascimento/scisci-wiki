# Policy-relevance motivated corpus choice

## Summary

Corpus selection for policy-mention studies can be motivated by expected policy salience and tracked source coverage.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: topic corpus, policy-source list, expected policy salience, or paper denominator.
- Typical representation: pre-registered corpus rationale with source coverage and expected-density assumptions.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: why a policy-mention study chooses a topic corpus.
- Empirical signature: a corpus is selected because it is policy-facing and source coverage is expected to detect mentions..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds design rationale to [climate-change policy mention corpus](../datasets/climate_change_policy_mention_corpus.md).
- Useful before generalizing from high-policy-salience domains to all science.
- Connects topic selection to [policy-source corpus construction](policy_source_corpus_construction.md).

## Operationalization

- Pre-register why a topic corpus is expected to be policy-facing.
- Report tracked source coverage, expected-density assumptions, and source-list gaps.
- Compare high-salience corpora with lower-salience corpora before general claims.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Bornmann et al. (2016) chooses climate change because it is policy-relevant and because corresponding policy sites were continuously analyzed.
- This makes corpus choice part of the measurement design.

## Caveats

- Selecting a likely high-signal domain can bias impressions of policy-mention usefulness.
- Policy salience varies across countries and time.
