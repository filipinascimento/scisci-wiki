# General Diversity Heuristic Scope Trigger

## Summary

General diversity heuristic scope trigger identifies when to use a flexible diversity heuristic: specialist measures are contested, diversity is prominent but undefined, or multiple policy perspectives must be compared.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: diversity appraisal, policy portfolio, technology portfolio, research strategy, or science-governance debate.
- Typical representation: decision rule for choosing a general diversity heuristic over a single fixed index.
- Mechanism or measurement target: methodological scope condition for diversity measurement.
- Empirical signature: existing measures are challengeable or the debate invokes diversity without a settled analytic definition.

## Operationalization

- Check whether a field has a defensible specialist diversity model.
- Check whether policy actors use diversity without defining the property being measured.
- If either condition holds, use the heuristic to expose assumptions rather than imposing a single index.
- Report the scope trigger before presenting diversity scores or frontiers.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Stirling (2007) argues that a general diversity heuristic is useful when specialist understandings are circumscribed or challengeable.
- Stirling also identifies science and technology policy as a setting where diversity is prominent in discussion but often undefined or analytically neglected.
- Later in the paper, Stirling frames the heuristic as a flexible way to explore assumptions rather than as a single definitive diversity index.

## Caveats

- A scope trigger does not by itself define the diversity metric.
- Specialist parametric models should not be discarded when they are empirically or theoretically well grounded.
- The trigger is most useful when paired with transparent sensitivity analysis.
