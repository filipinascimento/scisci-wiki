# Scientific mobility motivation taxonomy

## Summary

Scientific mobility motivation taxonomy codes the stated reasons scientists give for moving internationally, separating career, team, institutional, network, lifestyle, family, and compensation motives.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: mobile scientist, survey response, origin-destination move, career stage, country, or field.
- Typical representation: ranked motivation items, Likert-scale response matrix, factor grouping, or motive-category profile.
- Method target: converting mobility reasons into comparable variables for science-workforce and policy analysis.
- Empirical signature: career prospects and research-team quality often outrank wages, benefits, family, and lifestyle motives for outward moves.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a mechanism layer to [scientific mobility](../mechanisms/scientific_mobility.md) and [scientific emigration rate](../measures/scientific_emigration_rate.md).
- Helps distinguish attraction policies that target research quality from return policies that depend on family or job-market conditions.
- Provides survey features for models of [brain circulation networks](../representations/brain_circulation_networks.md) and [mobility policy shock evaluation](../validations/mobility_policy_shock_evaluation.md).
- Connects individual decision motives to country-level mobility outcomes.

## Operationalization

- Ask mobile scientists to rate possible reasons for taking a postdoc, employment, or academic job abroad.
- Include career prospects, faculty/colleague/team quality, institutional prestige, international network expansion, infrastructure, research funds, wages, working conditions, fringe benefits, lifestyle, family or personal reasons, and origin-country job conditions.
- Rank or factor-analyze responses by country, field, career stage, and move type.
- Compare outbound mobility motives with return motives to test whether attraction and return use different policy levers.
- Keep motivation items linked to the [corresponding-author survey frame](corresponding_author_survey_frame.md) and [core-country scientist panel](../datasets/core_country_scientist_panel.md) so respondent scope is explicit.
- Treat [job-conditioned return intention](../measures/job_conditioned_return_intention.md) as a separate response class when return depends on origin-country labor-market opportunity.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Franzoni et al. (2012) asks immigrant scientists to evaluate 14 possible reasons for coming to work in their current country of residence.
- The paper reports little country variation: respondents in every country ranked opportunity to improve future career prospects and the presence of outstanding faculty, colleagues, or research team above all other reasons.
- Excellence or prestige of the foreign institution and opportunity to extend international networks tie for third place.
- Family reasons and fringe benefits are listed last for outward mobility, while personal or family reasons are the most important reported factor for return among those with international experience.
- Franzoni et al. conclude that policy levers matter for attracting scientists abroad, but play a smaller role in bringing migrants back except where return is conditional on job opportunities.
- This outward-versus-return contrast is split out as [attraction-return policy asymmetry](../mechanisms/attraction_return_policy_asymmetry.md).

## Caveats

- Motivation rankings are self-reported and may mix retrospective rationalization with actual decision drivers.
- Motivation categories can vary by career stage, family status, field, and visa regime.
- Stated reasons do not by themselves identify causal effects of policy or institutional quality.
