# Socioeconomic and cultural context alignment

## Summary

Indicator choice should align with the socioeconomic and cultural setting in which the research is produced and used.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: evaluation unit, mission, local context, indicator, research output, or stakeholder group.
- Typical representation: context statement mapped to indicators and expected research roles.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: context-sensitive indicator selection beyond formal mission matching.
- Empirical signature: evaluation criteria change after local cultural, socioeconomic, or mission context is explicitly considered..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [mission-aligned indicator selection](mission_aligned_indicator_selection.md).
- Connects [locally relevant research metrics](../validations/locally_relevant_research_metrics.md) to broader context documentation.
- Useful for responsible assessment across national or regional systems.

## Operationalization

- Require context statements for evaluation units.
- Map indicators to local socioeconomic and cultural goals.
- Audit whether chosen metrics penalize context-specific outputs.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hicks et al. (2015) says indicator choice and use should account for wider socioeconomic and cultural contexts and that no single evaluation model fits all settings.

## Caveats

- Context alignment can be abused as post hoc special pleading unless goals are stated before evaluation.
- Some evaluations legitimately compare across contexts but still need explicit normalization.
