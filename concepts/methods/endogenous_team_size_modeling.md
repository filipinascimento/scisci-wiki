# Endogenous team-size modeling

## Summary

Endogenous team-size modeling treats team size as an outcome generated by a collaboration model rather than as a fixed input to a model of team composition or performance.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: publication, team, author-count distribution, generative model, or collaboration process.
- Typical representation: generated team-size distribution, exogenous team-size parameter, endogenous author-count process, or held-out size-distribution fit.
- Method target: explain why teams of different sizes emerge and change over time.
- Empirical signature: a model is evaluated on whether it reproduces observed team-size distributions rather than only accepting team size as given.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [lead-author team choice model](lead_author_team_choice_model.md) from choice rules to generated team-size outcomes.
- Complements [team-size distribution reproduction](../validations/team_size_distribution_reproduction.md).
- Helps distinguish [team self-assembly models](team_self_assembly_models.md) from models that condition on observed team size.

## Operationalization

- Tag collaboration models by whether team size is exogenous or generated.
- Fit models to past team-size distributions and evaluate on held-out periods or fields.
- Compare generated distributions with observed low-k body, mode, and large-team tail.
- Report whether network structure and team-size distributions are both reproduced.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Milojevic (2014) contrasts prior team-assembly models that take team size as an input with a model that aims to explain the team-size distribution itself.
- The paper validates its model by comparing simulated and empirical team-size distributions over time.

## Caveats

- Conditioning on observed team size remains useful when the target is topology, performance, or role composition.
- Endogenous team-size models can fit distributions while missing communication or task-allocation mechanisms.
- Field boundaries and source coverage affect team-size distributions.
