# Low-entry-cost proposal capture

## Summary

Low-entry-cost proposal capture is a design motif in which short, standardized submissions make early-stage scientific ideas observable without requiring a full grant application.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: short proposal, applicant, entry cost, participation decision, or idea pool.
- Typical representation: page-limited submission with simplified requirements and standardized fields.
- Method target: increase proposal capture and make speculative ideas observable for review and study.
- Empirical signature: many early ideas can be collected before researchers incur full application costs.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Helps study [proposal-abundance evaluation bottleneck](../mechanisms/proposal_abundance_evaluation_bottleneck.md) without making the bottleneck a full-grant burden.
- Supports [single-author proposal standardization](single_author_proposal_standardization.md) by keeping proposal units simple.
- Makes [proposal format covariate control vector](proposal_format_covariate_control_vector.md) more feasible because proposal length and format are bounded.

## Operationalization

- Limit proposal length and required attachments.
- Standardize fields that describe the idea, approach, expected impact, and references.
- Record entry rules and participation incentives.
- Compare captured idea distributions with later full applications or funded projects.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Boudreau et al. (2016) describes a first-stage process designed to reduce submitter entry costs.
- The proposal format was short, averaging roughly six pages, and focused on approaches and hypotheses.
- The simplified submission structure supported individual-level linkage between proposal content, applicant attributes, and evaluator assignment.

## Caveats

- Low-cost proposals can be less developed than full applications.
- Short formats may favor concise, legible ideas over complex long-horizon work.
- Entry-cost reduction does not remove status, time, or opportunity-cost barriers for all scientists.
