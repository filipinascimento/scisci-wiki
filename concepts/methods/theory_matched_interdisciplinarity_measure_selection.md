# Theory-matched interdisciplinarity measure selection

## Summary

Theory-matched interdisciplinarity measure selection chooses an interdisciplinarity indicator according to the theory and research question being tested.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: research question, interdisciplinarity theory, indicator family, paper, or field.
- Typical method: map the focal theory to variety, balance, disparity, other-field reference share, or composite diversity measures.
- Measurement target: construct-valid alignment between an interdisciplinarity theory and the operational indicator.
- Empirical signature: cognitive-variety theories, balance theories, and combinatorial-novelty theories point to different measures.

## Operationalization

- State the theory before choosing the indicator.
- Use variety indicators when the theory concerns breadth of knowledge inputs.
- Use disparity indicators when the theory concerns cognitive distance or combinatorial novelty.
- Use balance indicators when the theory concerns evenness, coordination difficulty, or lack of a disciplinary core.
- Report why excluded indicators are not central to the focal theory.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Thijs, and Glanzel (2015) concludes that interdisciplinarity is abstract and multidimensional.
- The paper explicitly argues that indicators should be chosen according to the invoked theory and focal research question.
- It gives examples: information-processing perspectives emphasize cognitive variety, whereas combinatorial-novelty perspectives emphasize disparity.

## Caveats

- A single study can require more than one indicator when multiple mechanisms are plausible.
- Theory matching does not remove the need for robustness checks.
- Field taxonomies can still limit construct validity even when the indicator family is well chosen.
