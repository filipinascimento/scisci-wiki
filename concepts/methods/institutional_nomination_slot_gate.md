# Institutional nomination-slot gate

## Summary

HHMI selection is mediated by limited university nomination slots rather than direct self-application by all eligible scientists.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, university nomination slot, HHMI competition, self-selection channel, or selection-stage indicator.
- Typical representation: selection-stage design table distinguishing institutional nomination from direct application.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: sorting and self-selection audit in program comparisons.
- Empirical signature: limited nomination slots reduce direct applicant self-selection but introduce institutional gatekeeping..

## Operationalization

- Record whether candidates self-apply, are nominated, or face institutional slot limits.
- Use nomination-stage data to assess whether treatment groups are selected through PI initiative or institutional sorting.
- Compare nominated nonwinners with awardees when feasible.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Azoulay et al. (2009) uses HHMI's nomination process to argue that explorer-type PI self-selection is less plausible.
- The design shifts part of selection from individual application choice to university nomination.

## Caveats

- Universities may still select for unobserved explorer traits, institutional priorities, or prestige.
- Nomination slots weaken but do not eliminate sorting concerns.
