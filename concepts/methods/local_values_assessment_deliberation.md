# Local-values assessment deliberation

## Summary

Local-values assessment deliberation is the method of eliciting institutional, faculty, trainee, patient, public, or community values before translating responsible-assessment principles into local criteria.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: institution, department, stakeholder group, assessment criterion, deliberation process, or rubric revision.
- Typical representation: structured consultation, survey, workshop, Delphi process, or public values statement linked to assessment criteria.
- Method target: ensure that published criteria match what the local institution claims to value.
- Empirical signature: assessment rubrics cite stakeholder input and local mission rather than only inherited metrics.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Operationalizes [societal-needs assessment alignment](societal_needs_assessment_alignment.md) at the institutional level.
- Supports [transparent assessment-criteria publication](transparent_assessment_criteria_publication.md) by giving criteria an explicit local rationale.
- Extends [responsible researcher assessment principles](responsible_researcher_assessment_principles.md) beyond generic principle lists.
- Complements [metrics supporting qualitative assessment](metrics_support_qualitative_assessment.md) by specifying whose judgment and values are represented.

## Operationalization

- Identify relevant internal and external stakeholders for the decision context.
- Elicit values and priorities through meetings, surveys, workshops, or structured deliberation.
- Translate values into criteria, evidence types, and weights for assessment.
- Publish the resulting criteria and explain how stakeholder input shaped them.
- Revisit the deliberation periodically as institutional mission and research practice change.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Moher et al. (2018) recommends that institutions examine whether assessment criteria reflect what their communities value.
- The paper connects assessment reform to stakeholder deliberation, local context, and explicit criteria.
- This motif separates local value elicitation from the broader responsible-assessment principle set.

## Caveats

- Local values can reproduce local inequalities if marginalized groups are excluded.
- Deliberation needs to be tied to actual criteria and committee behavior.
- Stakeholder values may conflict, requiring transparent tradeoff decisions.
