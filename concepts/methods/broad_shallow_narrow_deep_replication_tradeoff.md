# Broad-shallow versus narrow-deep replication trade-off

## Summary

A replication program trades breadth across many findings against depth from multiple replications of the same finding.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: replication portfolio, focal finding, replication attempt, field estimate, or evidence depth.
- Typical representation: portfolio design axis contrasting many single replications with repeated replications of fewer claims.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: resource allocation in empirical reproducibility programs.
- Empirical signature: broad portfolios improve field coverage while narrow-deep portfolios improve claim-specific certainty.

## Operationalization

- Specify whether the project optimizes field-level coverage, claim-specific certainty, mechanism testing, or intervention evaluation.
- Track the number of distinct claims and number of replications per claim.
- Report how denominator interpretation changes under broad-shallow versus narrow-deep designs.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Open Science Collaboration (2015) ran one direct replication for many psychology studies and cautioned that a single replication does not provide definitive truth status for any individual finding.
- This design makes the breadth-depth trade-off explicit.

## Caveats

- Broad coverage can leave individual claims uncertain.
- Narrow depth can provide stronger claim-level evidence while losing representativeness across fields.
