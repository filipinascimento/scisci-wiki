# Knowledge-breadth idea-arrival function

## Summary

Jones formalizes individual idea production as a function of technological opportunity, crowding, and the innovator's breadth of expertise.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: innovator, idea arrival rate, application area, crowding, breadth, or technology state.
- Typical representation: inspiration or idea-arrival equation with opportunity, crowding, and breadth inputs.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: micro-foundation for innovation production under knowledge burden.
- Empirical signature: idea production depends jointly on opportunity, local crowding, and the breadth of knowledge held by the innovator.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines innovation production modeling by linking it to [breadth productivity tradeoff](../mechanisms/breadth_productivity_tradeoff.md) and [application area crowding arbitrage](../mechanisms/application_area_crowding_arbitrage.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [same cohort productivity equivalence](../mechanisms/same_cohort_productivity_equivalence.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [cohort productivity contribution aggregation](cohort_productivity_contribution_aggregation.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Specify idea arrival as a function of field opportunity, local specialist density, and breadth.
- Estimate invention rates per inventor using specialty density and breadth proxies where data allow.
- Use the function to simulate how increasing frontier depth changes individual and team productivity.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Jones (2009) defines an inspiration equation where idea arrivals depend on the technology state, application-area opportunity, crowding among nearby specialists, and breadth of knowledge.
- The equation is the model's bridge from knowledge burden to innovation organization.

## Caveats

- Breadth is latent in most empirical settings.
- The paper uses the function primarily as theory rather than a direct structural estimate.
