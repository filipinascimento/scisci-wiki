# Intellectual-risk-taking assessment

## Summary

Intellectual-risk-taking assessment gives researchers credit for pursuing creative, out-of-the-box, or blue-sky ideas whose value may not appear immediately in grants, publications, or citations.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: researcher, project portfolio, grant program, promotion case, early-career cohort, or funding scheme.
- Typical representation: risk-taking rubric, long-horizon portfolio review, exploratory-agenda support, blue-sky funding record, or midterm and long-term outcome evaluation.
- Method target: counter assessment systems that make researchers overly conservative by demanding early measurable success.
- Empirical signature: committees and funders explicitly value risky hypotheses, broad investigative agendas, and delayed-payoff work when judging careers or allocating support.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Splits the out-of-the-box principle out of [responsible researcher assessment principles](responsible_researcher_assessment_principles.md).
- Connects researcher assessment to [funding autonomy and exploration](../mechanisms/funding_autonomy_exploration.md), [risk-averse scientist selection](../mechanisms/risk_averse_scientist_selection.md), and [novelty penalty](../mechanisms/novelty_penalty.md).
- Provides an assessment counterpart to [scientific problem choice](../mechanisms/scientific_problem_choice.md) and [research strategy taxonomy](research_strategy_taxonomy.md).
- Helps design policies that support exploration without ignoring evidence quality and complete reporting.

## Operationalization

- Ask candidates or grantees to identify exploratory questions, risky hypotheses, and longer-horizon research agendas.
- Evaluate whether risk is intellectual and potentially valuable, not merely poorly planned or underpowered.
- Use longer observation windows and portfolio-level judgment rather than early publication or citation counts alone.
- Compare alternative funding and assessment models using midterm and long-term outcome indicators.
- Pair risk-taking credit with [complete transparent reporting rewards](complete_transparent_reporting_rewards.md) so failed or null exploratory work remains informative.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Moher et al. (2018) identifies valuing out-of-the-box ideas in promotion and tenure decisions as the sixth principle for assessing scientists.
- Table 2 reports participant interest in grant applications without specific aims and broader investigative agendas.
- The paper argues that different models for supporting out-of-the-box ideas should be evaluated comparatively with midterm and long-term outcome indicators.
- Moher et al. suggest stable longer-term funding, possibly four to eight years, across career stages as one support model.
- The discussion warns that pressure on young researchers to obtain funding early can produce conservatism that is hostile to groundbreaking work when researchers may be especially creative.

## Caveats

- Risk-taking should not become a vague label for weak design or missing evidence.
- Long-horizon evaluation can disadvantage evaluators who need timely decisions.
- Blue-sky support may be captured by already advantaged researchers unless access and selection are audited.
- Risk-oriented policies still need mechanisms for reporting failures and learning from them.
