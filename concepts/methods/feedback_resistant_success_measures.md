# Feedback-resistant success measures

## Summary

Feedback-resistant success measures are evaluation indicators and decision systems designed to reduce rich-get-richer amplification when past success is used to allocate future opportunity.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: metric, evaluation workflow, funding program, hiring process, publication gate, portfolio, or field.
- Typical representation: measure-design checklist, feedback-loop audit, portfolio outcome dashboard, or anti-amplification intervention.
- Method target: measure scientific success without letting the measure intensify inequality or suppress unpredictable discovery.
- Empirical signature: decisions based on the measure do not simply concentrate resources among already successful papers, scholars, institutions, or fields.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Converts [prediction feedback loops](../validations/prediction_feedback_loops.md) into a design target.
- Extends [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) and [indicator systemic effects](../mechanisms/indicator_systemic_effects.md) to predictive evaluation.
- Supports [automated predictive-evaluation safeguards](../validations/automated_predictive_evaluation_safeguards.md) when models use citations, publication counts, prizes, prestige, or prior funding.
- Pairs with [active science-policy experimentation](../validations/active_science_policy_experimentation.md) to test whether anti-feedback designs actually preserve discovery diversity.

## Operationalization

- Identify which inputs are themselves affected by prior evaluation decisions.
- Measure whether decisions concentrate attention, funding, hiring, or publication opportunities.
- Add uncertainty, diversity constraints, randomization, portfolio quotas, human review of novel cases, or delayed-outcome tracking where appropriate.
- Evaluate deployed measures by portfolio diversity, subgroup effects, novelty, delayed recognition, field emergence, and long-run impact rather than only near-term accuracy.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Clauset, Larremore, and Sinatra (2017) states that citations and publications are measures of past success with feedback loops that create rich-get-richer dynamics.
- The paper warns that allocating future opportunities from recent-success markers can create dramatic inequalities and self-fulfilling predictions.
- Clauset et al. explicitly call for measures of success and evaluation systems that are less prone to feedback loops.
- The same essay connects this design problem to preserving innovation, avoiding inequality amplification, and supporting potentially fundamental but unpredictable advances.

## Caveats

- A metric can be feedback-resistant in one setting and feedback-amplifying in another.
- Anti-feedback design can trade off with accountability, efficiency, or short-term predictability.
- Randomization and diversity constraints require transparent governance and domain-specific legitimacy.
