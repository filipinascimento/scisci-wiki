# Single-author proposal standardization

## Summary

Single-author proposal standardization is a review-study design that restricts proposal submissions to a standardized first-stage format and a single named submitter, making proposal content and applicant-level metrics easier to compare.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: grant proposal, applicant, first-stage idea submission, review score, or proposal metadata record.
- Typical representation: standardized proposal template, single-author eligibility rule, simplified submission requirements, or author-proposal one-to-one key.
- Method target: reduce variation in proposal packaging and coauthor attribution when studying evaluation behavior.
- Empirical signature: each proposal can be linked to one submitter's characteristics and a common set of proposal features.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Strengthens [randomized reviewer-proposal assignment](randomized_reviewer_proposal_assignment.md) by reducing author-team ambiguity.
- Supports [proposal-evaluator fixed-effect identification](proposal_evaluator_fixed_effect_identification.md) and applicant-level controls in [peer review and gatekeeping](../mechanisms/peer_review_gatekeeping.md).
- Provides cleaner inputs for [proposal MeSH novelty percentile](../measures/proposal_mesh_novelty_percentile.md) and [reviewer-proposal intellectual distance](../measures/reviewer_proposal_intellectual_distance.md).
- Defines a design feature of the [biomedical frontier-review experiment](../datasets/biomedical_frontier_review_experiment.md).

## Operationalization

- Use a common proposal template and require the same core content fields across submissions.
- Restrict the analyzed stage to ideas, hypotheses, or short proposals before extensive team-building and formatting differences arise.
- Require a single accountable submitter when applicant-level controls are central to the design.
- Record the proposal length, references, figures, topic tags, and other formatting variables as controls.
- Treat the restriction as part of the estimand, because multi-author and full-application stages may behave differently.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Boudreau et al. (2016) focuses on the first stage of a grant process where ideas and new hypotheses were solicited and evaluated.
- The paper states that this stage allowed the authors to standardize proposal format and content.
- Boudreau et al. simplified submission requirements so that the analyzed proposals could be restricted to single-author submissions.
- The design let the study associate each proposal with fine-grained metrics for individual submitters and evaluators.

## Caveats

- Single-author standardization improves measurement but may exclude collaborative proposals and team-science dynamics.
- First-stage idea reviews may not generalize to full applications with budgets, preliminary data, and multi-investigator teams.
- A common format can still leave differences in writing skill, field norms, and preliminary evidence.
