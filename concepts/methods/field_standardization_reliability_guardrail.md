# Field Standardization Reliability Guardrail

## Summary

Field standardization reliability guardrail is the use of agreed designs, outcomes, reporting practices, and analytic routines to reduce flexibility-driven false findings.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, research design, outcome set, analysis protocol, or reporting standard.
- Typical representation: field-level standards that constrain analytic and reporting flexibility.
- Method target: improve the reliability of findings by reducing discretionary transformations of negative results into positive ones.
- Empirical signature: fields with standardized conduct and outcomes produce more credible findings than fields with experimental or flexible methods.

## Operationalization

- Document whether a field has common outcome definitions, reporting standards, and analytic routines.
- Compare finding credibility across standardized and flexible design settings.
- Track adherence to standards and deviations from them.
- Combine standardization with preregistration, reporting checklists, and replication audits.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ioannidis (2005) states that flexibility in designs, definitions, outcomes, and analyses lowers the likelihood that findings are true.
- The paper describes standardized conduct and reporting as likely to increase the proportion of true findings.
- Ioannidis points to agreed outcomes and stereotyped methods as reliability-enhancing examples.

## Caveats

- Standardization can suppress innovation or fail to fit heterogeneous contexts.
- Shared standards can still encode biased or weak assumptions.
- Reliability gains require adherence, not only written guidelines.
