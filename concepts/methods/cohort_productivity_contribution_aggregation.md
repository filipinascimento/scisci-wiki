# Cohort productivity contribution aggregation

## Summary

Aggregate innovation growth can be modeled by summing productivity contributions from living innovator cohorts weighted by cohort size and breadth.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: innovator cohort, R&D labor force, cohort breadth, aggregate productivity contribution, or growth rate.
- Typical representation: cohort-decomposed aggregation of living innovators' idea contributions.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: macro aggregation of micro innovation under knowledge burden.
- Empirical signature: aggregate productivity growth is represented as the cohort-weighted sum of living innovators' expected contributions.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines innovation growth modeling by linking it to [same cohort productivity equivalence](../mechanisms/same_cohort_productivity_equivalence.md) and [knowledge breadth idea arrival function](knowledge_breadth_idea_arrival_function.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [knowledge burden scale effects constraint](../mechanisms/knowledge_burden_scale_effects_constraint.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [productivity age policy causal ambiguity](../validations/productivity_age_policy_causal_ambiguity.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Decompose R&D labor into living innovator cohorts.
- Estimate cohort-specific breadth or training burden where possible.
- Aggregate productivity growth as active R&D labor times average cohort contribution.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Jones (2009) aggregates living cohorts' idea contributions in the steady-state growth proof and shows cohort breadth differences determine contribution differences across cohorts.
- The method links individual training burden to aggregate growth implications.

## Caveats

- The aggregation depends on balanced-growth assumptions.
- It abstracts from heterogeneous idea size and institutional allocation differences.
