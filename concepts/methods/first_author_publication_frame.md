# First-author publication frame

## Summary

First-author publication frame restricts a bibliometric analysis to papers where the focal scientist appears as first author, using author position as a proxy for primary contribution and visibility.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, first author, author position, demographic group, field, topic, or citation outcome.
- Typical representation: first-author-only publication panel, first-author topic portfolio, or first-author citation comparison.
- Method target: align publication records with the author role most likely to represent project leadership or visible intellectual contribution.
- Empirical signature: conclusions are drawn from first-authored papers rather than all coauthored papers, corresponding-author records, or full author lists.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Defines the authorship scope for [intersectional topic stratification](../mechanisms/intersectional_topic_stratification.md) when topic choice is attributed to the author most visibly tied to a paper.
- Complements [author-position citation components](../measures/author_position_citation_components.md), which separate impact by authorship role rather than filtering a study frame.
- Makes [name-based demographic inference](name_based_demographic_inference.md) and [probabilistic demographic fractional counting](probabilistic_demographic_fractional_counting.md) more interpretable by reducing ambiguity about which coauthor's inferred attributes are analyzed.
- Provides a narrower alternative to all-author panels in [paper-author-affiliation linkages](../representations/paper_author_affiliation_linkages.md).
- Serves as the authorship frame for the [WoS US first-author intersectional topic panel](../datasets/wos_us_first_author_intersectional_topic_panel.md).

## Operationalization

- Start from paper-author records with ordered authorship information.
- Keep papers whose first author can be linked to the demographic, institution, field, or career attribute under study.
- Define the institutional or national scope from the first author's affiliation when the research question is author-centered.
- Report how fields with noncontributory alphabetical ordering, last-author seniority, or equal-contribution notes may affect interpretation.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Kozlowski et al. (2022) examines 5,431,451 Web of Science articles with 1,609,107 distinct U.S.-affiliated first authors from 2008 to 2019.
- The paper focuses on first authors because they are generally the authors who contributed most to an article and because the first name is highly visible in bibliographic references.
- Kozlowski et al. use the first-author frame to link inferred race, inferred gender, topic distributions, and field-normalized citation outcomes.
- This frame is central to their claim about topic choice: demographic-topic associations are interpreted through the author position most plausibly connected to topic selection.

## Caveats

- First-author conventions differ by field and can be alphabetical, coequal, or junior-author oriented.
- Last authors, corresponding authors, and middle authors can shape topic choice, resources, supervision, and citations even when they are outside the first-author frame.
- Demographic analysis based on first authors should not be generalized to the entire author team without a separate team-level design.
