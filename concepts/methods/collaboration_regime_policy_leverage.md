# Collaboration Regime Policy Leverage

## Summary

Collaboration regime policy leverage maps collaboration regimes to policy instruments, distinguishing direct link-building for coordinated or megascience work from indirect incentive design for self-organized regimes.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: collaboration regime, policy instrument, funding program, international collaboration strategy, or field network.
- Typical representation: regime-to-policy-lever mapping.
- Mechanism or measurement target: how policy can influence collaboration without assuming all regimes respond to the same intervention.
- Empirical signature: direct government link-building fits some regimes, while bottom-up regimes require incentives, meeting opportunities, and attractive research opportunities.

## Operationalization

- Classify the collaboration setting into a regime such as megascience, coordinated research, participatory research, or self-organized research.
- Identify whether policy can directly build links or mainly shape incentives and opportunities.
- Align funding, infrastructure, travel, meeting, and reward instruments with the regime.
- Evaluate whether the intervention changes durable collaboration structure rather than only short-run coauthorship counts.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wagner and Leydesdorff (2005) argues that preferential-attachment network structure overtook older organizational drivers in international collaboration during the 1990s.
- The paper warns that policies mimicking older national or top-down models may fail to exploit global research opportunities.
- Wagner and Leydesdorff distinguish cases where government directly builds links through funding and institution building from cases where government has an indirect role by creating attractive researchers, opportunities, and incentives.
- A residual Wagner pass separates policy-goal representation and indirect funding signals into [international policy-goal portfolio](../representations/international_policy_goal_portfolio.md) and [permissive collaboration funding signal](../mechanisms/permissive_collaboration_funding_signal.md).

## Caveats

- Regimes can overlap within a field or project portfolio.
- Policy leverage may vary by country capacity, funding system, and field norms.
- Durable collaboration effects require follow-up beyond program participation counts.
