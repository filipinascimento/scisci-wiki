# Fresh collaborator hot-streak onset

## Summary

Fresh collaborator hot-streak onset is the pattern that scientists add new collaborators around the transition into a hot streak, alongside a shift from small-team exploration to larger-team exploitation.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, hot-streak onset, paper team, new collaborator, team-size trajectory, or collaboration network.
- Typical representation: new-collaborator count around onset, team-size phase trajectory, observed-versus-randomized collaborator change, or collaboration-refresh indicator.
- Mechanism: new collaborators can help scale, refine, or exploit a promising direction after a period of smaller-team exploration.
- Empirical signature: collaboration structure changes near hot-streak onset, with new coauthors appearing as the creator enters the focused high-impact interval.

## Operationalization

- Identify each scientist's hot-streak onset and align papers around the event.
- Track coauthors before, during, and after onset.
- Count new collaborators who had not appeared in the scientist's prior coauthor history.
- Compare observed new-collaborator rates with randomized onset baselines.
- Separate collaborator novelty from raw team size, field, career stage, publication year, and self-citation controls.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Liu et al. (2021) reports that scientists explore with small teams before a hot streak and exploit with large teams after the hot streak begins.
- The paper further reports that hot-streak onset appears to mark an increase in new collaborators.
- Liu et al. connect this result to prior findings on the advantages of fresh teams while emphasizing that the collaboration analysis is applicable only to scientific careers.
- The study reports robustness across disciplines, self-citation adjustment, publication year, research field, and career-stage controls.

## Caveats

- New collaborator timing is observational and can reflect funding, lab growth, institutional moves, or topic shifts.
- Team roles vary, so new coauthors do not automatically imply new intellectual input.
- The analysis is domain-specific to science because teams are not defined comparably in art and film careers.
- Collaborator refresh should not be used as a prescriptive evaluation target without causal evidence.
