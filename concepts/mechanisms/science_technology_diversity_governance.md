# Science and technology diversity governance

## Summary

Science and technology diversity governance is the mechanism by which maintaining diverse research strategies, publics, disciplines, technologies, and institutions can support rigor, creativity, resilience, innovation, and pluralism under uncertainty.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: research portfolio, technology pathway, governance process, stakeholder forum, funding program, institution, or policy mix.
- Typical representation: diversity-governance rationale, plural portfolio design, opened-up policy process, or strategy-mix argument.
- Mechanism: diverse perspectives and options hedge ignorance, reduce lock-in, enable learning across programs, and accommodate otherwise conflicting interests.
- Empirical signature: policy arguments explicitly value plurality, alternative pathways, and cross-perspective learning rather than only current best-performing options.

## Operationalization

- Identify which diversity dimension is being governed: disciplines, publics, technologies, institutions, funding mechanisms, research strategies, or evaluation criteria.
- Measure portfolio spread using [category variety](../measures/category_variety.md), [distributional balance](../measures/distributional_balance.md), [category disparity](../measures/category_disparity.md), or a composite measure.
- Record the claimed governance benefit: rigor, creativity, innovation, resilience, precaution, flexibility, lock-in mitigation, or social accommodation.
- Compare the diversity-governance rationale against costs and performance trade-offs.
- Audit whether decision processes include the publics, disciplines, or interests named in the diversity rationale.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Stirling (2007) places diversity at the center of science and technology policy, research strategy, risk regulation, energy policy, innovation, and sustainable development.
- The paper argues that diverse disciplinary perspectives can enhance rigor and creativity, while diverse research portfolios offer flexibility and learning under uncertain future progress.
- Stirling also frames diverse technology strategies as a resource pool that can provide flexibility and resilience in the face of ignorance and surprise.
- The full text treats diversity as both an input and output of governance: a mix of strategies informed by varied perspectives can help accommodate otherwise irreconcilable social interests and values.

## Caveats

- Governance diversity can become rhetorical if the relevant diversity dimension and mechanism are not specified.
- More diversity can increase coordination costs or reduce economies of scale.
- Diverse representation in a process does not guarantee influence unless agenda-setting, evidence weighting, and decision authority are also examined.
- A diversity-governance rationale should be paired with explicit performance, equity, and accountability criteria.
