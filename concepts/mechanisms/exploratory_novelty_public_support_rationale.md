# Exploratory novelty public-support rationale

## Summary

Novel work has portfolio value because it combines high upside, higher failure risk, and downstream spillovers that private or short-window evaluation may underprovide.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: funded portfolio, novel paper class, funding design, or downstream impact trace.
- Typical representation: novelty class by citation variance, top-tail outcomes, and follow-on big-hit stimulation.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: why public or patient funding may support exploratory novelty despite short-run penalties.
- Empirical signature: novel work shows high variance, long-run or indirect upside, and underrewarding by short-window metrics..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects [novelty impact dispersion](../measures/novelty_impact_dispersion.md) to [funding incentives and exploration](funding_incentives_and_exploration.md).
- Useful for policy arguments around research portfolios and risk tolerance.
- Links novelty penalties to [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md).

## Operationalization

- Compare funded portfolios on novelty class, citation variance, top-tail outcomes, low-tail outcomes, and cited-by-big-hit outcomes.
- Stratify by funding design, field, and time horizon.
- Pair bibliometric outcomes with grant selection and review data where possible.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang et al. (2017) links high-risk, high-gain novelty to Arrow-style public support and reports both higher variance and follow-on big-hit stimulation.
- The result frames novelty as portfolio value under imperfect private incentives.

## Caveats

- Wang et al. motivate the rationale but do not causally estimate a public-funding treatment.
- Novelty can be risky without being valuable.
