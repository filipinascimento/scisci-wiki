# Core Journal Current-Awareness Scanning

## Summary

Core journal current-awareness scanning is the routine in which experts briefly browse a small set of core journals to maintain awareness before deeper search, reading, or citation selection.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: researcher, journal set, browsing routine, awareness practice, or discovery channel.
- Typical representation: behavioral mechanism for exposure to recent field content.
- Mechanism: repeated scanning of a small trusted journal set shapes what scholars notice before targeted search.
- Empirical signature: experts report or exhibit browsing of a small number of core journals as a current-awareness practice.

## Operationalization

- Identify the journal set a scholar or field routinely scans.
- Measure browsing, table-of-contents alerts, click logs, or self-reported current-awareness practices.
- Separate current-awareness scanning from topic search and citation-link following.
- Test whether shifts away from scanning change citation breadth, recency, or concentration.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Evans (2008) summarizes library-use research showing that publishing scientists and scholars commonly browse or briefly scan a small number of core journals.
- Evans describes this practice as a way to build awareness of current research before articles are discovered, printed, and read in depth.
- The paper places this practice alongside topic search and online citation-link following as one of the main discovery routines.

## Caveats

- Core-journal scanning can reinforce elite venues and field boundaries.
- Online table-of-contents browsing may preserve part of the practice even when print use declines.
- The relevant core set can vary strongly by field, career stage, and institution.
