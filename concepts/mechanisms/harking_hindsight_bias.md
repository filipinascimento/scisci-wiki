# HARKing and hindsight bias

## Summary

HARKing and hindsight bias describe the mechanism by which explanations generated after seeing data are remembered, framed, or reported as if they had been predicted in advance.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: hypothesis, result, analysis path, manuscript claim, narrative explanation, or preregistered prediction.
- Typical representation: prediction-postdiction label, preregistration comparison, claim provenance table, or exploratory-versus-confirmatory audit.
- Mechanism: observed outcomes shape interpretation and memory, making post hoc explanations feel prospectively predicted.
- Empirical signature: vague hypotheses, many possible supportive outcomes, or analyses that appear confirmatory only after outcome-dependent specification.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a cognitive mechanism beneath the [prediction-postdiction boundary](../validations/prediction_postdiction_boundary.md).
- Links [researcher degrees of freedom](../validations/researcher_degrees_of_freedom.md) to ordinary reasoning biases rather than only deliberate misconduct.
- Explains why [preregistration and registered reports](../validations/preregistration_registered_reports.md) can improve interpretability by preserving temporal order.
- Connects [selective reporting and publication bias](publication_bias_selective_reporting.md) to manuscript narratives.
- Motivates the [preregistration specificity gradient](../validations/preregistration_specificity_gradient.md), because vague predictions leave room for hindsight-compatible interpretations.

## Operationalization

- Compare final hypotheses, outcome choices, covariates, and exclusion criteria with time-stamped plans.
- Code claims as predicted, postdicted, transparent deviation, or unclear.
- Identify vague predictions broad enough to rationalize many possible outcomes.
- Audit whether exploratory findings are explicitly labeled as generative and followed by independent prediction tests.
- Use interviews, lab records, preregistrations, or protocol histories when available to reconstruct claim timing.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Nosek et al. (2018) identifies post hoc theorizing or hypothesizing after results are known as a circular-reasoning problem.
- The same full text defines hindsight bias as seeing outcomes as more predictable after they occur than before they were observed.
- Nosek et al. give examples in which a broad prediction, covariate set, exclusion rule, or narrative can be rationalized after seeing results.
- They frame preregistration as a way to make the timing of hypotheses and analyses inspectable before outcome observation changes memory or reasoning.

## Caveats

- Postdiction is useful for discovery when it is labeled as exploratory.
- HARKing can be unintentional because memory and interpretation are outcome-sensitive.
- A preregistration can still be too vague to constrain hindsight rationalization.
- Specificity and timing both matter: a time-stamped but broad prediction can still be hindsight-compatible.
