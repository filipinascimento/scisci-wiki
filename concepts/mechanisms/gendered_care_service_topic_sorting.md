# Gendered care-service topic sorting

## Summary

Gendered care-service topic sorting is the concentration of women authors in topics linked to care, service, family, learning, and gender identity.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: topic, author demographic group, gendered labor domain, or topic portfolio.
- Typical representation: overrepresentation of women in care and service topic clusters.
- Mechanism target: how social roles and field structures shape scientific problem choice.
- Empirical signature: feminized topics are disproportionately about education, care, family, nursing, pregnancy, or gendered identity.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Specializes [topic-identity homophily](topic_identity_homophily.md).
- Connects [gendered discipline output sorting](../measures/gendered_discipline_output_sorting.md) to within-field topic choice.
- Adds content detail to [intersectional topic stratification](intersectional_topic_stratification.md).
- Links to [topic-choice privilege](topic_choice_privilege.md) when care-service topics carry different citation or prestige returns.

## Operationalization

- Infer author demographic groups and document-topic mixtures.
- Identify topics with high women-author participation.
- Label topics through top words and representative papers.
- Compare citation density and institutional prestige for care-service topics.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Kozlowski et al. (2022) reports feminized topics involving families, learning, LGBT studies, nursing, pregnancy, and education.
- The paper links these topic patterns to traditional gender roles and gendered labor.
- This mechanism connects demographic representation to the shape of the knowledge base.

## Caveats

- Topic labels can reproduce stereotypes if not carefully validated.
- Sorting can reflect constraints, interest, opportunity, or social need.
- The mechanism should not imply that women authors are naturally tied to care topics.
