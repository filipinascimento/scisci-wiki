# Proposal commitment creativity constraint

## Summary

Proposal commitment creativity constraint is the mechanism by which competitive project grants can reduce post-award exploration when researchers feel locked into promised aims, deliverables, and future track-record expectations.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: grant proposal, funded project, researcher, award condition, milestone, output, or follow-on proposal.
- Typical representation: post-award treatment effect, constrained aim changes, reduced topic deviation, or administrative lock-in.
- Mechanism: competition rewards detailed commitments before work begins, and awardees may avoid creative departures that could threaten compliance or future funding.
- Empirical signature: competitively funded projects become less novel than comparable flexible-funding projects, especially for researchers with weaker bargaining power.

## Operationalization

- Compare novelty, topic shifts, method shifts, or reference-combination distance before and after competitive awards.
- Separate selection into funding from treatment after funding using proposal histories, timing, status strata, or project process-change measures.
- Measure rigidity through promised aims, budget restrictions, reporting obligations, award duration, or perceived obligation to deliver the proposed plan.
- Test whether the constraint is stronger for junior, female, peripheral, or low-resource researchers.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Lee, and Walsh (2018) distinguishes selection effects from treatment effects in competitive project funding.
- The paper argues that competitive funding can constrain research autonomy after award because researchers must execute promised projects, satisfy funder restrictions, and build a record for future competition.
- Wang et al. report evidence consistent with lower-status researchers becoming less novel under competitive funding, making the commitment constraint part of the status-contingent funding mechanism.

## Caveats

- Reduced novelty after award can reflect selection, topic feasibility, team composition, or reporting incentives rather than commitment lock-in alone.
- Some grant programs explicitly encourage exploratory deviations or adaptive milestones, so the mechanism is not universal.
- Novelty is only one creative-output dimension; constrained projects may still produce reliable, cumulative, or socially valuable work.
