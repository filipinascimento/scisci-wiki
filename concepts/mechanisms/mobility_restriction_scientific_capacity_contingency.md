# Mobility restriction scientific capacity contingency

## Summary

Mobility restriction scientific capacity contingency is the mechanism by which countries receiving redirected talent from restrictive mobility hubs benefit only if they have enough scientific capacity to cultivate that talent.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: destination country, redirected researcher flow, scientific capacity, policy shock, or training system.
- Typical representation: redirection path, capacity indicator, cultivation outcome, or destination-readiness comparison.
- Mechanism or measurement target: whether displaced mobility flows become productive scientific capacity in substitute destinations.
- Empirical signature: redirected applications or affiliation flows translate into durable output and impact only in destinations with sufficient institutions, funding, training, and collaboration infrastructure.

## Operationalization

- Identify central-country restrictions that plausibly redirect students, postdocs, faculty, or collaborators.
- Measure alternative destinations' scientific capacity using research funding, doctoral training capacity, institutional quality, field infrastructure, publication volume, and collaboration access.
- Compare redirected demand signals such as applications with realized affiliations, publications, retention, citations, and return flows.
- Estimate whether destinations cultivate incoming talent or merely absorb short-term applications without durable scientific output.
- Model heterogeneous treatment effects by field, career stage, institutional tier, and existing international network position.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sugimoto et al. (2017) argues that isolationism in central countries can benefit other competitive nations by redirecting talent.
- The paper uses increased graduate-student applications to Canada after a change in the US administration as an example of a possible destination benefit.
- It immediately qualifies this possibility by warning that if adequate scientific capacity does not exist in those other countries, global talent will not be cultivated to the same extent.
- This mechanism converts mobility displacement into a capacity-contingent outcome rather than a simple gain for substitute destinations.

## Caveats

- Application increases are weak evidence for realized mobility, productivity, or retention.
- Capacity can be field-specific: a country may absorb talent in one domain but not another.
- Redirected talent can crowd out local researchers or change domestic training opportunities.
- Capacity can itself respond to policy shocks through funding, institutional expansion, or international partnerships.
