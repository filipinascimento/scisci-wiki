# Cross-disciplinary awakening

## Summary

Cross-disciplinary awakening occurs when a dormant paper becomes influential after a different field or community discovers and reuses it.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: sleeping-beauty paper, citing paper, source field, citing field, awakening time, or co-citation neighborhood.
- Typical representation: pre/post-awakening citing-field mix, cross-disciplinary citation share, co-citation trigger map, or keyword shift.
- Mechanism: a contribution is premature or peripheral in its original field but becomes useful when another field develops tools, questions, or terminology that make it salient.
- Empirical signature: high-B papers receive a large share of citations from disciplines different from the publication field, especially after awakening.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a mechanism candidate for [delayed recognition](delayed_recognition.md) and [sleeping beauty](sleeping_beauty.md).
- Links [interdisciplinary citation delay](interdisciplinary_citation_delay.md), [field emergence](field_emergence.md), and [research fronts](research_fronts.md).
- Turns [awakening time](../measures/awakening_time.md) into a point for comparing pre- and post-awakening citing communities.
- Uses [external-discipline citation fraction](../measures/external_discipline_citation_fraction.md) as an operational measure and [multidisciplinary venue delayed recognition](multidisciplinary_venue_delayed_recognition.md) as a venue-level mechanism candidate.

## Operationalization

- Identify high-B papers and their awakening times.
- Assign source and citing papers to fields, journals, topics, or communities.
- Compare citing-field composition before and after awakening.
- Use co-citation and keyword shifts to identify candidate trigger papers or communities.
- Validate with full-text citation contexts where possible.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ke et al. (2015) reports that top sleeping beauties often achieve delayed exceptional importance in disciplines different from those where they were originally published.
- In the WoS analysis, Ke et al. compare high-, medium-, and low-B papers by the fraction of citations received from other disciplines.
- The paper reports that top sleeping beauties are clearly different: for about 80% of top SBs, at least 75% of citations are interdisciplinary.
- Ke et al. illustrate trigger analysis with Garfield's 1955 citation-index paper and Zachary's 1977 karate-club paper, both awakened through later communities and topics.
- The Garfield case is split as an [author-mediated awakening bridge](author_mediated_awakening_bridge.md), because later same-author articles bridged the dormant paper to HITS and impact-factor-debate literatures.
- The Zachary case is split as [benchmark-mediated rediscovery](benchmark_mediated_rediscovery.md), because a later network-science method paper adopted the dormant social network as a paradigmatic benchmark.
- The narrower method motif [awakening co-citation keyword diagnosis](../methods/awakening_cocitation_keyword_diagnosis.md) captures this pre/post-awakening co-citation and title-keyword workflow.
- A residual Ke pass separates the measurable external-field citation share from the mechanism-level claim and adds multidisciplinary venues as a possible origin setting for delayed recognition.

## Caveats

- Journal category is an imperfect proxy for disciplinary community.
- Cross-disciplinary citation can reflect methodological reuse, review attention, or metric fashion, not only substantive delayed recognition.
- The mechanism needs case-level citation-context checks before making strong claims about why awakening occurred.
