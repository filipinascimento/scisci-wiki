# Scientific-capacity network entry threshold

## Summary

Scientific-capacity network entry threshold is the mechanism by which national research capacity enables countries to enter international collaboration networks but does not by itself explain collaboration growth.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: country, scientific capacity, collaboration edge, field network, or international coauthorship share.
- Typical representation: capacity threshold plus residual network dynamics.
- Mechanism: countries need sufficient scientists, infrastructure, and output to become plausible collaborators, but partner choice then follows reputation, field, and network incentives.
- Empirical signature: capacity expansion predicts entry for developing systems but cannot explain rapid growth among already advanced systems.

## Operationalization

- Measure country scientific capacity with publication output, R&D staff, funding, infrastructure, or field-specific presence.
- Track when countries first form durable international coauthorship ties.
- Separate entry into the network from growth in tie volume or centrality.
- Test whether capacity explains residual collaboration after field and reputation dynamics are modeled.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wagner and Leydesdorff (2005) treats rising scientific capacity as one candidate explanation for international collaboration growth.
- The paper argues that capacity helps explain developing-country entry but cannot fully explain advanced-country willingness to collaborate.
- Wagner and Leydesdorff call for further tests of whether collaboration dynamics differ by national capacity.

## Caveats

- Capacity metrics can be endogenous to international collaboration.
- Country-level capacity can miss institutional islands of excellence or weakness.
- Entry thresholds differ by field, facility needs, language, and funding regime.
