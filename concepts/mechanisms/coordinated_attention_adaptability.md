# Coordinated attention adaptability

## Summary

Coordinated attention adaptability is the mechanism by which flatter or more synchronously communicating teams adapt better under uncertainty, complexity, and change.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: team, communication pattern, contribution role, task, or project.
- Typical representation: team flatness, synchronized communication, shared attention, and adaptive performance.
- Mechanism: symmetric coordination lets more team members monitor and respond to changing problem states.
- Empirical signature: flatter teams are associated with higher novelty or longer-term influence in uncertain scientific tasks.

## Operationalization

- Measure flatness with contribution roles, lead-role ratios, or communication symmetry.
- Compare outcomes under tasks with different uncertainty or complexity.
- Estimate whether flatter teams have higher novelty, disruption, or long-run impact after accounting for team size.
- Treat coordinated attention as a hypothesized mechanism unless direct communication data are observed.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Xu, Wu, and Evans (2022) cites organizational evidence that flatter teams and synchronous communication can improve problem solving and other complex tasks.
- The paper frames coordinated attention as one reason flat teams may adapt to uncertainty and change.
- Their empirical results link higher L-ratio teams to more novel papers and greater long-term influence.

## Caveats

- Contribution flatness is only an indirect proxy for real-time coordinated attention.
- Hierarchy may still be efficient for routine, modular, or time-critical tasks.
- Observational role data cannot fully separate team design from project selection.
