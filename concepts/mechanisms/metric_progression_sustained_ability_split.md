# Metric progression versus sustained-ability split

## Summary

Separates career-progress metrics that grow with time from a more stationary inferred parameter meant to capture sustained impact tendency.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: career metric, author trajectory, cumulative indicator, or Q estimate.
- Typical representation: decomposition of cumulative indicators into productivity volume and stationary impact tendency.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: difference between time-growing career metrics and stable inferred ability parameters.
- Empirical signature: h-index and cumulative citations grow over time while inferred Q remains comparatively stable..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Clarifies how [individual Q parameter](../measures/individual_q_parameter.md) differs from [h-index](../measures/h_index.md) and cumulative citation indicators.
- Useful when comparing career-stage metrics with sustained-impact models.
- Connects career prediction to [career prediction nonstationarity caution](../validations/career_prediction_nonstationarity_caution.md).

## Operationalization

- Decompose h-index, cumulative citations, and productivity trajectories into Q-like ability plus output volume.
- Report how age and productivity drive time-growing indicators.
- Avoid interpreting stability as intrinsic or fair without confound checks.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sinatra et al. (2016) contrasts time-growing indicators with stable Q estimates.
- The contrast motivates a split between metric progression and sustained-impact tendency.

## Caveats

- Stable does not mean intrinsic, fair, or unconfounded.
- Q may absorb resources, prestige, topic visibility, and collaboration context.
