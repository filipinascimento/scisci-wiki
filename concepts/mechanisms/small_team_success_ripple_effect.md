# Small-team success ripple effect

## Summary

Small-team success ripple effect describes how disruptive or delayed-recognition work from small teams can become the foundation for later large-team development and high-impact follow-on work.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: small-team focal work, later citing work, citing team size, citation delay, or high-impact predecessor.
- Typical representation: time series of later citations from larger teams to high-impact small-team papers.
- Mechanism: small teams open or preserve deep-search directions that larger teams later scale, consolidate, or develop.
- Empirical signature: successful small-team work receives increasing uptake by larger teams after a delay.

## Operationalization

- Identify high-impact or high-delay small-team papers.
- Track future citations to those papers by citing-team size and year.
- Compare later large-team uptake against matched small-team or average-predecessor baselines.
- Distinguish delayed recognition from immediate large-team adoption.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wu et al. (2019) reports that successful small-team work can become a source for later large-team success.
- Extended Data Fig. 8 tracks increasing large-team citations to high-impact, high-delay small-team papers.
- The mechanism supports a division of labor in which small teams disrupt and large teams later develop or scale the resulting directions.

## Caveats

- Later large-team uptake does not prove that the small-team work caused the later research direction.
- Citation delay, field growth, and database coverage can shape the apparent ripple.
- The effect should be distinguished from generic preferential attachment to highly cited predecessors.
