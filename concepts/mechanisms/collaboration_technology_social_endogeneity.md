# Collaboration Technology-Social Network Endogeneity

## Summary

Collaboration technology-social network endogeneity is the possibility that collaboration tools and social networks co-evolve with partnership demand rather than simply causing new collaborations.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, collaboration technology, institutional partnership, time period, or collaboration network.
- Typical representation: trend break test, pre/post technology comparison, or co-evolution narrative.
- Mechanism: communication technologies may support collaboration while also being adopted because collaboration demand is already rising.
- Empirical signature: cross-institutional collaboration grows smoothly before a major technology shock or resumes its prior growth rate afterward.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Interprets [communication-technology jump test](../validations/communication_technology_jump_test.md).
- Complements [communication-technology initiation caveat](../validations/communication_technology_initiation_caveat.md).
- Helps avoid over-attributing collaboration growth to lower communication costs.

## Operationalization

- Identify plausible technology-shock periods.
- Compare collaboration trends before, during, and after the shock.
- Test whether adoption timing coincides with a discontinuity or with continuation of prior trends.
- Consider partnership demand, specialization, funding, and institutional strategy as co-drivers.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Jones et al. (2008) finds an unusual science-and-engineering jump around 1998 but continued growth at the pre-1998 rate afterward.
- The paper states that multi-university collaboration growth substantially predates recent communication technologies.
- The authors suggest that collaboration technologies and social networks may be endogenous outcomes of rising interest in research partnerships.

## Caveats

- Smooth aggregate trends do not rule out technology effects in specific fields or teams.
- Technology may affect collaboration quality, speed, or scope without changing the overall trend slope.
- Adoption and collaboration data need comparable timing resolution.
