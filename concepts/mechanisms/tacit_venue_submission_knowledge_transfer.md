# Tacit venue submission knowledge transfer

## Summary

Tacit venue submission knowledge transfer is the mechanism by which junior experience publishing in a specific venue teaches otherwise invisible practices for framing, packaging, and negotiating a later senior-author submission to that venue.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, journal, author-venue history, submission process, mentor-protege tie, or senior-author transition.
- Typical representation: prior nonlast publication as exposure to venue-specific tacit knowledge.
- Mechanism: early participation in a successful venue submission transmits knowledge about message framing, cover letters, supporting information, editorial interaction, and venue idiosyncrasies.
- Empirical signature: authors with prior nonlast experience in a journal later appear as senior authors in that journal more often than expected from author-order null models.

## Operationalization

- Identify prior nonlast authorship in the same journal before a later last-author appearance.
- Treat that prior accepted publication as exposure to venue-specific submission norms.
- Compare later senior-author transitions across authors with and without such exposure.
- Use field and author-order robustness checks to avoid interpreting arbitrary byline order as tacit-knowledge transfer.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sekara et al. (2018) states that chaperoned PIs previously went through the intensive process of preparing a manuscript for a high-impact journal.
- The paper names tacit knowledge about framing the message, cover-letter tone, supporting-information structure, and interaction with editors as examples of invisible submission knowledge.
- Sekara et al. argue that having experienced the whole publication process once increases the chance of publishing in similar journals again because the author knows the venue's idiosyncrasies.
- The discussion frames the effect as knowledge needed to write for certain journals, especially high-impact interdisciplinary journals.

## Caveats

- Prior nonlast publication is only a proxy for learning; it can also reflect selection, topic fit, lab resources, or senior-author reputation.
- The measure observes accepted papers, not rejected submissions or informal mentoring.
- Tacit knowledge may transfer across similar venues even when the operational measure is journal-specific.
