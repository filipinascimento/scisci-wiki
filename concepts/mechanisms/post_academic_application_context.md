# Post-Academic Application-Context Pressure

## Summary

Post-academic application-context pressure is the mechanism by which funders and evaluation systems push researchers to specify expected economic or societal outcomes alongside scientific excellence.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: funding call, research proposal, evaluation system, institution, or field.
- Typical representation: application-context demand attached to funding and assessment.
- Mechanism: competition for project-dependent funds shifts attention toward user relevance, social robustness, and broader impact claims.
- Empirical signature: proposals and evaluations require explicit broader-impact plans or societal-impact evidence.

## Operationalization

- Code funding calls, proposal forms, and assessment systems for required impact statements.
- Track whether application-context requirements change project selection, topics, or reporting.
- Compare fields and countries with different broader-impact regimes.
- Separate ex ante impact promises from ex post auditable external impact.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Bornmann et al. (2016) describes post-academic science as marked by increasing competition for project-dependent research funds.
- The paper states that applicants are increasingly forced to specify expected economic and societal outcomes.
- Bornmann et al. connect this shift to the context of application becoming relevant for funding alongside excellence and originality.

## Caveats

- Application-context pressure can produce useful relevance, strategic rhetoric, or distorted research priorities.
- Requirements may differ sharply across national systems and funders.
- Stated impact plans should not be treated as achieved societal impact.
