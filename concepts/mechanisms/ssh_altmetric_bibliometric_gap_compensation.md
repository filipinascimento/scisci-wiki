# SSH altmetric bibliometric-gap compensation

## Summary

SSH altmetric bibliometric-gap compensation is the mechanism by which altmetrics may be especially useful in social sciences and humanities because traditional citation analysis covers those fields less well.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, social-science or humanities publication, altmetric source, citation indicator, or evaluation use case.
- Typical representation: field-specific complementarity argument, altmetric-density versus citation-density comparison, or SSH evaluation channel map.
- Mechanism: online attention traces may capture cultural, societal, or public-facing attention that citation databases underrepresent in SSH fields.
- Empirical signature: SSH publications show relatively high altmetric activity and similar altmetric density to citation density, unlike fields where citations dominate.

## Operationalization

- Compare altmetric presence, altmetric density, citation presence, and citation density by broad field.
- Separate social sciences and humanities from biomedical, natural-science, engineering, and mathematics/computer-science fields.
- Test whether altmetric events in SSH sources correspond to public, cultural, educational, or policy uses.
- Treat the compensation claim as a hypothesis requiring source-specific and qualitative validation.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Costas et al. (2015) reports higher altmetric activity for social sciences and humanities and a similar altmetric density to citation density in those fields.
- The paper argues that altmetrics could add value for humanities and social sciences, which are traditionally less well represented by citation analysis.
- Costas et al. connect this possibility to social or cultural aspects of scientific work and call for further research to confirm the advantage.

## Caveats

- Higher altmetric activity does not by itself prove better evaluation validity.
- SSH altmetric events may still reflect platform, language, country, or provider-coverage biases.
- Field-specific complementarity must be validated against the intended evaluation construct.
