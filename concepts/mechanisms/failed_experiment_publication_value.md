# Failed-experiment publication value

## Summary

Failed-experiment publication value is the idea that negative or unsuccessful experiments can improve collective discovery by revealing which candidate relationships do not hold.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: experiment, failed edge test, negative result, research program, or knowledge network.
- Typical representation: published failures, negative edges, failed-search records, or reduced duplicate testing.
- Mechanism: failures provide information that prevents repeated unsuccessful search and guides future exploration.
- Empirical signature: search simulations or empirical fields discover new relationships faster when failed attempts are recorded and reusable.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects [publication bias and selective reporting](publication_bias_selective_reporting.md) to collective search efficiency.
- Provides a policy lever for [knowledge-network experiment search](knowledge_network_experiment_search.md): publish negative results, not only successful links.
- The policy counterfactual is operationalized as [coordinated failure-publication regime](../methods/coordinated_failure_publication_regime.md).
- Helps separate individual publication incentives from field-level discovery speed.
- Complements [unpublished-failure censoring](../validations/unpublished_failure_censoring.md), which marks the inferential problem caused when failed projects are absent from observed publication data.

## Operationalization

- Represent failed experiments as tested non-edges or negative labels in a knowledge graph.
- Compare discovery speed when failures are hidden versus when failures are recorded and affect later choices.
- Track duplicate attempts, avoided search cost, and changes in [knowledge-network search efficiency](../measures/knowledge_network_search_efficiency.md).
- Validate with registries, null-result journals, preclinical datasets, or field-specific negative-result archives when available.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Rzhetsky et al. (2015) models successful experiments as published edges and failed experiments as tested relationships that are not realized in the empirical network.
- The paper notes that publications overwhelmingly document successful experiments, so published data underrepresent risky but unsuccessful choices.
- Rzhetsky et al. report that a more complete record of failures would deepen understanding of research behavior and improve efficiency.
- Their conclusion states that publication of failures substantially increases the speed of discovery in their knowledge-network search model.
- The coordinated regime combines failure publication with avoiding duplicate experiments.

## Caveats

- Failed experiments are not all equally informative; design quality, power, measurement error, and contextual assumptions matter.
- Publishing all failures can increase noise unless metadata and methods are sufficiently structured.
- Incentives for failure publication may differ across high-cost experiments, computational screens, clinical trials, and exploratory laboratory work.
