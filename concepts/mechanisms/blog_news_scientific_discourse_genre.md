# Blog-news scientific discourse genre

## Summary

Blog-news scientific discourse genre is the mechanism that treats blog and news mentions as a distinct discourse and dissemination layer rather than as generic social-media activity.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: blog mention, news mention, paper, media outlet, scientific blog, or dissemination chain.
- Typical representation: source-factor component, blog-news source class, media-discourse channel, or cross-platform dissemination role.
- Mechanism: blogs and news can amplify, contextualize, and translate scholarly work, often interacting with high-impact journals and other social-media channels.
- Empirical signature: blog and news mentions load together as a distinct factor and correlate differently with citations and journal indicators than Twitter-like sources.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Interprets [blog and news mention counts](../measures/blog_and_news_mention_counts.md) as a discourse channel, not only a count.
- Provides mechanism content for [altmetric source factor structure](../representations/altmetric_source_factor_structure.md).
- Links [altmetric audience-filtering hypothesis](altmetric_audience_filtering_hypothesis.md) to source-specific audiences and dissemination roles.
- Helps avoid collapsing news, blogs, Twitter, Facebook, Mendeley, and policy mentions into one attention mechanism.

## Operationalization

- Keep blog and news mentions source-specific in altmetric data models.
- Test whether blogs and news form a factor separate from other social-media channels.
- Code content, actor type, outlet type, and whether the mention contextualizes, reviews, critiques, or merely links the paper.
- Compare citation and journal-indicator association separately for blog/news sources and for fast social-media sources.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Costas et al. (2015) finds that blogs and news form a separate factor from Twitter, Facebook, and Google+ mentions.
- The paper interprets blogs and news as a different impact flavor and later notes that scientific blogs can be a genre of scientific output sharing characteristics with scientific discourse.
- Costas et al. also report that blogs and news show relatively stronger citation correlations than other altmetric measures, and that bloggers can disseminate information across more than one social medium.

## Caveats

- Blog and news sources vary widely in expertise, editorial standards, audience, and motivation.
- Stronger citation correlation can reflect journal or topic selection rather than discourse quality.
- Provider source lists and language coverage can bias which blogs and news outlets are visible.
