# OA mandate diffusion pressure

## Summary

Funder and institutional mandates operate as policy pressure that shifts open-access prevalence and route mix.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: publication, funder, institution, mandate, field, or publication year.
- Typical representation: mandate exposure table linked to article-level OA status and route labels.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: policy pressure on OA adoption and route composition.
- Empirical signature: OA rates or route shares change after funder or institutional mandates become active..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects [funding acknowledgments](../datasets/funding_acknowledgments.md) and grant-publication links to [open access prevalence measurement](../measures/open_access_prevalence_measurement.md).
- Useful for separating voluntary OA uptake from mandate-driven diffusion.
- Adds a policy mechanism to [OA growth route decomposition](../measures/oa_growth_route_decomposition.md).

## Operationalization

- Link funder mandates to publication-level OA status by funder, field, mandate start date, and publication date.
- Estimate route-specific shifts before and after mandate exposure with funder and field controls.
- Distinguish policy compliance from repository backfill and publisher route changes.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Piwowar et al. (2018) lists NIH, Gates, European Commission, NSF, and Wellcome mandates as signs that OA diffusion is becoming required.
- The paper's prevalence and route estimates provide the outcome layer for studying mandate pressure.

## Caveats

- Mandates are endogenous to funder prestige, field norms, and repository infrastructure.
- Acknowledgment data and grant-publication linkages can be incomplete.
