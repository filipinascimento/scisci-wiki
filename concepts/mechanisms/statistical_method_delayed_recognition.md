# Statistical-method delayed recognition

## Summary

Statistical methods can sleep for decades until later scientific communities need the technique, making methods canonization a recurring delayed-recognition pathway.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: method paper, later citing field, and citation-context function.
- Typical representation: delayed-recognition trajectory plus method-use coding in citing papers.
- Mechanism: later methodological demand, software adoption, or cross-field diffusion awakens an initially underused technique.
- Empirical signature: high-B statistical papers later cited as standard methods, estimators, tests, or algorithms.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Links [sleeping beauty](sleeping_beauty.md) to methodological reuse and [cross-disciplinary awakening](cross_disciplinary_awakening.md).
- Useful for distinguishing rediscovered findings from later uptake of reusable tools.
- Connects delayed recognition to [obliteration by incorporation](obliteration_by_incorporation.md) when methods become standard.

## Operationalization

- Identify high-B papers whose later citations use the work as a method, estimator, algorithm, test, or software foundation.
- Code citation functions and citing-field shifts before and after awakening.
- Track textbook, software, and benchmark adoption where bibliographic citations alone are incomplete.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ke et al. (2015) highlights delayed-recognition examples in statistics, including principal component analysis and the Wilson interval, and notes other statistical methods in supplementary lists.
- These examples support method canonization as a concrete sleeping-beauty mechanism.

## Caveats

- Method uptake can reflect field growth, software packaging, or teaching diffusion rather than rediscovery of the original argument.
- Standard methods may be undercited once incorporated into routine practice.
