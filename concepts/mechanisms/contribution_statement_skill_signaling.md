# Contribution-statement skill signaling

## Summary

Contribution-statement skill signaling is the mechanism by which standardized contribution reports communicate not only credit shares but also the skills and roles individual contributors performed.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: contribution statement, contributor role, author, paper, journal policy, or skill category.
- Typical representation: role taxonomy, contributor-skill vector, standardized contribution label, or credit-and-skill signal.
- Mechanism: required contribution reporting turns invisible labor into structured signals that readers, evaluators, and models can interpret.
- Empirical signature: author-level contribution categories can be used to infer lead/support roles and skill profiles.

## Operationalization

- Parse contribution statements into standardized activities or taxonomies.
- Map activities to skill families, role clusters, or lead/support labels.
- Use contributor-level vectors to compare teams, career trajectories, and evaluation outcomes.
- Preserve journal policy, reporting mandate, and contribution taxonomy version.
- Audit whether reported skills differ by field, seniority, gender, institution, or team size.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Xu, Wu, and Evans (2022) states that open-science calls for transparent and equitable credit have increased mandatory contribution reporting in high-profile journals.
- The same passage says reporting has become more standardized to reflect researcher contribution and signal contributor skills.
- Their study uses contributor-level information to analyze how individual contribution hierarchy relates to the character of scientific advance.

## Caveats

- Contribution statements are self-reported and may reflect negotiation, norms, or policy compliance.
- Skill signaling can become evaluative surveillance if used without context.
- Standardized taxonomies may miss informal mentoring, conceptual steering, or infrastructure labor.
