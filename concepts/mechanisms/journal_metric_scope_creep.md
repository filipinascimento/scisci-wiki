# Journal metric scope creep

## Summary

Journal metric scope creep is the mechanism by which journal indicators drift from source-selection and collection-management tools into high-stakes hiring, promotion, advertising, ranking, and funding signals.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: journal metric, institution, hiring committee, promotion process, publisher, or ranking system.
- Typical representation: metric-use pathway from local informational tool to broad evaluative proxy.
- Mechanism: convenient and visible journal metrics are repurposed beyond their original scope.
- Empirical signature: journal-level indicators are used to evaluate articles, authors, departments, or institutional prestige.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Links [journal impact factor](../measures/journal_impact_factor.md) to assessment incentives.
- Connects with [assessment convenience-proxy drift](assessment_convenience_proxy_drift.md).
- Provides a journal-specific pathway for indicator systemic effects.
- Helps explain why responsible-metrics guidance emphasizes fit between metric and evaluative question.

## Operationalization

- Identify where a journal metric is used outside journal selection or journal comparison.
- Classify uses by stakes: advertising, library decisions, hiring, promotion, rankings, or funding.
- Compare the metric's unit of measurement with the unit being evaluated.
- Track whether metric use changes behavior by authors, journals, publishers, or institutions.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from West, Bergstrom, and Bergstrom (2010) describes how journal metrics are used in advertising, library decisions, rankings, and academic evaluation.
- The paper warns that metrics can be misused when the evaluation target differs from the metric's intended unit.
- This motivates question-specific selection among Eigenfactor, Article Influence, and cost-effectiveness measures.

## Caveats

- Some journal metrics remain useful when the question is genuinely journal-level.
- Scope creep depends on governance and assessment culture, not the metric alone.
- Metric misuse can coexist with transparent and appropriate metric use.
