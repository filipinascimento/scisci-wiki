# International-doctorate faculty attrition

## Summary

International-doctorate faculty attrition describes differential departure risk among U.S. faculty by country or region of doctoral training.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: faculty member, doctoral country group, U.S. employing institution, field, domain, annual roster transition, or attrition event.
- Typical representation: annual per-capita attrition risk, relative attrition ratio, country-of-doctorate strata, or logistic attrition model.
- Mechanism: immigration constraints, labor-market integration, institutional support, career opportunity, and credential recognition can shape retention after U.S. faculty placement.
- Empirical signature: faculty trained in some non-U.S. doctoral systems leave U.S. tenure-track rosters at higher rates than U.S.-trained peers.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Splits a specific retention mechanism from broader [faculty retention and attrition](faculty_retention_attrition.md).
- Uses [faculty doctoral-origin composition](../measures/faculty_doctoral_origin_composition.md) as the composition baseline before comparing attrition rates.
- Connects international training pathways to [scientific mobility](scientific_mobility.md), [international mobility typologies](../measures/international_mobility_typologies.md), and [institutional prestige hierarchy](institutional_prestige_hierarchy.md).
- Helps audit whether faculty-market diversity is shaped by post-hiring retention, not only hiring.
- Provides one observed attrition channel in [faculty hiring-attrition dynamic equilibrium](faculty_hiring_attrition_dynamic_equilibrium.md), where stable non-U.S. doctorate shares can coexist with elevated departure risk.

## Operationalization

- Annotate faculty doctoral institution and country.
- Group doctoral origins into analytically justified country or region categories.
- Use annual faculty rosters to identify retention and attrition events.
- Estimate relative attrition risk by doctoral origin while stratifying or controlling by field, domain, faculty rank, and employer type.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wapman et al. (2022) finds that 11% of U.S. faculty have non-U.S. doctorates, with large variation by domain.
- The paper reports that internationally trained faculty primarily receive training from a limited range of regions, including a large share from the United Kingdom or Canada.
- Wapman et al. estimate annual per-capita attrition risk for faculty trained in the United States, Canada/United Kingdom, and all other countries.
- They report that faculty with doctorates from Canada and the United Kingdom leave at rates statistically indistinguishable from U.S.-trained faculty across fields and domains, while faculty with doctorates from all other countries leave at markedly higher rates overall, across all domains, and in 39 of 107 fields.
- Wapman et al. connect this differential attrition with stable group proportions over time when motivating [faculty hiring-attrition dynamic equilibrium](faculty_hiring_attrition_dynamic_equilibrium.md).

## Caveats

- Roster attrition can mean retirement, migration, non-academic employment, administrative changes, or data coverage loss.
- Country of doctorate is not the same as nationality, birthplace, race, ethnicity, or immigration status.
- Observed differences require causal designs before being attributed to discrimination, visa constraints, selection, or job preferences.
