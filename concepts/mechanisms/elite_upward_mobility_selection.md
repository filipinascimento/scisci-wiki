# Elite upward mobility selection

## Summary

Elite upward mobility selection is the mechanism that moves into elite institutions may select scientists who were already high-performing before the move, limiting average post-move impact gains.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: career move, origin institution, destination institution, scientist, rank difference, or post-move impact change.
- Typical representation: rank-difference performance curve, pre/post citation-change comparison, selection caveat, or upward-move subgroup audit.
- Mechanism: elite destinations admit or recruit scientists with strong pre-move trajectories, so destination prestige and prior performance are intertwined.
- Empirical signature: upward moves into elite institutions show little or no average post-move impact gain despite the destination's higher rank.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [institutional mobility stratification](institutional_mobility_stratification.md) by separating selection into elite destinations from causal destination effects.
- Qualifies [rank-difference performance curve](../measures/rank_difference_performance_curve.md) and [post-move citation-change score](../measures/post_move_citation_change_score.md).
- Connects mobility studies to [reputation effects](reputation_effects.md), [scientific career impact](scientific_career_impact.md), and cumulative-advantage interpretations.
- Suggests that policy claims about moving scientists into elite institutions need counterfactual designs, not only before/after citation comparisons.

## Operationalization

- Identify moves from lower-ranked institutions into higher-ranked or elite institutions.
- Measure pre-move performance, post-move performance, rank difference, career age, and coauthor network context.
- Compare observed post-move change with matched nonmovers, peer movers, or event-study baselines.
- Test whether upward movers already had stronger pre-move trajectories than comparable scientists who did not move.
- Report selection and environment interpretations separately.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Deville et al. (2014) finds that moving to lower-ranked institutions is associated with a decline in average impact.
- In contrast, the same paper reports that moving from lower-ranked locations to elite institutions shows no average performance change.
- Deville et al. suggest that scientists who make large upward jumps may already have had excellent performance at their previous institutions, so the move does not further affect their impact.
- This supports a selection-sensitive interpretation of upward elite mobility.

## Caveats

- No average gain does not rule out gains for some subgroups or outcomes other than citations.
- Elite mobility selection can coexist with resource, network, or prestige benefits that are delayed or field-specific.
- Strong causal claims require matched designs or quasi-experiments beyond descriptive mobility curves.
