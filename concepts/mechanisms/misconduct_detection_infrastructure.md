# Misconduct detection infrastructure

## Summary

Misconduct detection infrastructure is the institutional, journal, reader, reviewer, journalist, and watchdog apparatus that turns hidden or suspected research misconduct into documented findings, notices, and retractions.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: oversight agency, institutional investigation, whistleblower channel, reviewer report, journal process, watchdog record, news report, or detected paper.
- Typical representation: detection pathway, source-provenance table, oversight timeline, or cause-coding evidence stack.
- Mechanism target: explain why observed misconduct retractions depend on monitoring and reporting capacity as much as underlying misconduct rates.
- Empirical signature: changes in oversight institutions, whistleblower protection, investigation practices, or watchdog visibility coincide with changes in detected misconduct.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a mechanism for interpreting [fraud retraction-rate trends](../measures/fraud_retraction_rate_trend.md) without assuming that detection and prevalence move together.
- Underlies [retraction source reconciliation](../methods/retraction_source_reconciliation.md), [retraction notice transparency](../validations/retraction_notice_transparency.md), and [retraction cascades](retraction_cascades.md).
- Links post-publication correction to [peer review and gatekeeping](peer_review_gatekeeping.md), [open science interventions](../validations/open_science_interventions.md), and [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md).
- Makes journalists, watchdogs, institutions, and national agencies part of the science-of-science data-generating process.

## Operationalization

- Record how each suspected or confirmed case was detected: journal notice, institutional finding, Office of Research Integrity report, whistleblower, reviewer, reader, Retraction Watch, news report, or author clarification.
- Separate detection date, investigation date, publication date, expression-of-concern date, and retraction date.
- Compare cause-specific trends before and after changes in oversight infrastructure while accounting for publication volume and retraction lag.
- Preserve source provenance so downstream cause labels can be audited.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fang, Steen, and Casadevall (2012) argues that the first discernible increase in retractions followed the formation of the Office of Scientific Integrity and passage of the Whistleblower Protection Act in 1989.
- The same full text states that identifying and documenting misconduct requires vigilance by reviewers, editors, readers, institutions, government agencies, and journalists.
- Fang et al. use Office of Research Integrity reports, Retraction Watch, news media, and other public records to reclassify many cases beyond what journal notices alone showed.
- The paper describes author-level investigations that can reveal multiple older fraudulent articles, creating [retraction cascades](retraction_cascades.md).

## Caveats

- Strong detection infrastructure can make a field look worse by increasing visible retractions.
- Detection sources differ by country, institution, language, legal regime, and media attention.
- Observed trends should distinguish detection capacity from underlying misconduct prevalence.
