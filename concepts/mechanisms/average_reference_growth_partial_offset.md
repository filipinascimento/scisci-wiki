# Average reference-growth partial offset

## Summary

Longer reference lists may partly offset publication growth, but not enough to remove attention competition.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: publication year, average references per paper, publication growth, attention pool, cited paper, or citation opportunity.
- Typical representation: mechanism balancing growing paper supply against growing reference-list length.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: partial compensation for attention competition.
- Empirical signature: reference-list expansion can increase citing capacity while publication growth still accelerates forgetting..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines attention-decay mechanism by linking it to [publication growth attention competition](publication_growth_attention_competition.md) and [reference age attention diversion](reference_age_attention_diversion.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [reference list length growth confound](../validations/reference_list_length_growth_confound.md) is interpreted from citation histories.
- Adds cross-links to [digital discovery processing capacity limit](../validations/digital_discovery_processing_capacity_limit.md) so delayed-recognition and ranking motifs stay connected.

## Operationalization

- Model publication growth alongside average references per paper.
- Test whether reference-list expansion offsets the growing pool of candidate cited papers.
- Separate citation capacity growth from reader attention limits.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Parolo et al. (2015) notes that rising publication volume increases filtering pressure and says this may be partly compensated by more references, but publication growth still affects attention decay.
- The motif captures a qualitative offset mechanism inside the attention-economy argument.

## Caveats

- The paper discusses this mechanism qualitatively rather than estimating a reference-list offset parameter.
- More references can redistribute citation attention without improving reading capacity.
