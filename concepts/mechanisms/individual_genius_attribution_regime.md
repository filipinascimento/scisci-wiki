# Individual-genius attribution regime

## Summary

Individual-genius attribution regime is the recognition culture that frames scientific discovery through named individuals, prizes, and canonical heroic contributors.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: award, discovery narrative, scientist, solo author, team paper, or public attribution.
- Typical representation: individual-centered discovery history, prize list, named-law attribution, or heroic biography.
- Mechanism: credit systems make individual creators salient, producing a baseline expectation that team dominance can overturn.
- Empirical signature: discovery narratives and reward systems emphasize individuals even when production is increasingly team based.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Explains the recognition baseline behind [solo-to-team impact reversal](../validations/solo_to_team_impact_reversal.md).
- Connects team dominance to Mertonian [Matthew effect](matthew_effect.md) mechanisms.
- Helps interpret [team high-citation tail dominance](team_high_citation_tail_dominance.md) as a challenge to individualist credit culture.
- Provides a cultural contrast with [humanities solo-authorship persistence](../validations/humanities_solo_authorship_persistence.md).

## Operationalization

- Compare attribution language in textbooks, awards, press releases, and citation contexts with actual authorship team structure.
- Measure whether highly cited or prize-recognized discoveries are narrated through individuals despite team production.
- Track field differences in named-individual versus team-centered credit conventions.
- Use award rules and public communication as evidence of attribution regimes.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wuchty et al. (2007) opens by contrasting the tradition of scientific discoveries attributed to individuals with the empirical rise of teams.
- The paper uses this individualist baseline to motivate testing whether teams now dominate production and impact.
- This motif is separate from team-authorship share because it concerns the cultural framing of credit.
- It also links team science to broader reward-system and recognition concepts.

## Caveats

- Individual recognition can still be appropriate for some discoveries or leadership roles.
- Team-authored work can contain unequal contributions, so team attribution is not automatically fairer.
- Public attribution may lag behind production practices.
