# Claim-level retraction utility

## Summary

Claim-level retraction utility is the mechanism by which readers continue to use a retracted article because they treat some claims or historical facts as useful even after other claims are invalidated.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: retracted article, cited claim, invalidated result, residual historical fact, citation context, or post-retraction use.
- Typical representation: claim-level coding of why a retracted work is cited after retraction.
- Mechanism target: post-retraction citation can persist when invalidation is partial, ambiguous, or claim-specific.
- Empirical signature: citations continue to a retracted paper for a narrower claim than the one that caused retraction.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a mechanism for [post-retraction citation persistence](../measures/post_retraction_citation_persistence.md).
- Refines [continued access to retracted articles](../methods/continued_access_retracted_articles.md) by distinguishing access for historical or residual use from endorsement of invalid claims.
- Complements [retraction status-marking failure](../validations/retraction_status_marking_failure.md): persistence can reflect both marking failures and claim-level ambiguity.
- Helps citation-context studies avoid classifying all post-retraction citations as equally problematic.

## Operationalization

- Extract post-retraction citation contexts and classify the cited claim.
- Compare the cited claim with the stated reason for retraction.
- Code whether the citation is endorsing, critical, historical, methodological, or claim-limited.
- Distinguish full invalidation from partial correction, expression of concern, and unresolved warning states.
- Combine claim-level coding with status-marking audits.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fang, Steen, and Casadevall (2012) notes that some retracted articles continue to be cited after retraction.
- The paper discusses a case where readers may still cite an article for an initial identification claim even though the article was retracted over concerns about another property.
- This supports a claim-level interpretation of some post-retraction use, not merely ignorance of retraction status.

## Caveats

- Claim-level residual utility should not excuse missing retraction warnings or careless citation.
- Clinical, safety, or public-risk claims may require stricter treatment than historical or methodological citations.
- Detailed citation-context coding is labor-intensive and may require expert judgment.
