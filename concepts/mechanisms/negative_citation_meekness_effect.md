# Negative citation meekness effect

## Summary

Negative citation meekness effect is the mechanism by which authors avoid or soften explicit criticism of prior work, making negative citation functions rare and often indirectly expressed.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: citation context, weakness label, negative contrast label, author stance, or citation-function corpus.
- Typical representation: low frequency of explicit weakness or negative contrast labels, cautious wording, or neutralized critique.
- Mechanism: social and political costs of criticizing prior work encourage authors to understate or avoid negative citation roles.
- Empirical signature: explicit negative citation classes are rare relative to neutral, use, support, and nonnegative contrast labels.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Explains part of [citation-function class imbalance](../validations/citation_function_class_imbalance.md), especially the rarity of weakness and negative-contrast labels.
- Adds a mechanism layer to [citation function taxonomies](../representations/citation_function_taxonomies.md) and [citation sentiment-role projection](../methods/citation_sentiment_role_projection.md).
- Warns [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) against assuming that absence of explicit criticism means absence of disagreement.
- Connects citation-function analysis to scientific norms around politeness, priority, and critique.
- Helps interpret why [explicit citation-evidence annotation](../methods/explicit_citation_evidence_annotation.md) can under-label negative relationships.

## Operationalization

- Measure the frequency of weakness and negative-contrast labels in an explicitly annotated citation-function corpus.
- Compare negative-role rates across fields, venues, career stages, and review genres.
- Inspect whether criticism appears as hedging, neutral contrast, method limitation, or omission rather than direct weakness claims.
- Pair explicit labels with broader discourse analysis when the research question concerns disagreement or controversy.
- Treat rare negative labels as under-observed social signals rather than automatically as rare disagreements.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Teufel et al. (2006) reports that clearly negative citation categories are relatively infrequent in their annotated computational-linguistics corpus.
- The paper cites prior arguments that negational citations can be politically dangerous and states that its data show ample evidence of a meekness effect.
- Teufel et al. also note that authors may avoid admitting or stressing that they use somebody else's method, creating difficult distinctions between similarity and use.
- The mechanism helps explain why citation-function annotation must include neutral and insufficient-evidence labels while still preserving explicit weakness when it appears.

## Caveats

- Low negative-label frequency can also reflect corpus composition, field norms, annotation rules, or insufficient context.
- A softened citation can still communicate substantial disagreement to insiders.
- Meekness is difficult to validate without interviews, peer-review history, or field-specific discourse knowledge.
