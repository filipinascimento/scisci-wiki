# Review-Synthesis Policy Advantage

## Summary

Review-synthesis policy advantage is the mechanism by which review papers are more likely to be used in policy documents because they condense many primary studies into a usable synthesis.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: review paper, policy document, topic corpus, or research synthesis.
- Typical representation: overrepresentation of reviews among policy-mentioned papers.
- Mechanism: reviews reduce the search and interpretation burden for policy authors by summarizing connected lines of evidence.
- Empirical signature: reviews appear more often in policy-cited subsets than in the underlying research corpus.

## Operationalization

- Classify policy-mentioned and non-mentioned papers by document type.
- Compare review-paper shares in the policy-mentioned subset versus the base corpus.
- Test whether review overrepresentation persists after controlling for journal, age, field, and citation impact.
- Analyze policy mention contexts to determine whether synthesis is the reason for use.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Bornmann et al. (2016) reports that review papers are overrepresented among climate-change papers mentioned in policy documents.
- The paper reports a higher review share in the policy-mentioned subset than in the full climate-change corpus.
- Bornmann et al. explain that reviews summarize research lines, reducing the work needed by policy-document authors.

## Caveats

- Review overrepresentation can reflect journal prestige, older publication age, or broad topical relevance.
- Reviews can carry selective interpretations or consensus bias.
- Policy authors may cite reviews for authority rather than evidence synthesis.
