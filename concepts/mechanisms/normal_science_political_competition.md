# Normal-science political competition

## Summary

Normal-science political competition is the mechanism in which scientists compete for influence, credit, and agenda control inside an otherwise cumulative field, so field direction can change through incumbent rivalry rather than only through paradigm shifts.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, subfield, incumbent group, entrant group, research agenda, or intellectual neighborhood.
- Typical representation: crowded problem space with positions held by incumbents and potential challengers.
- Mechanism: when a research area is already organized around strong incumbents, entrants face social, reputational, and strategic costs even if the relevant ideas are scientifically legitimate.
- Empirical signature: entry, topic choice, or citation direction changes after an incumbent constraint weakens, without requiring a full Kuhnian revolution.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Gives [scientific problem choice](scientific_problem_choice.md) a competitive field-politics channel.
- Connects [research strategy risk-reward](research_strategy_risk_reward.md) to incumbent control over promising but crowded questions.
- Provides a higher-level framing for [star-scientist entry deterrence](star_scientist_entry_deterrence.md) and [outsider entry after star death](outsider_entry_after_star_death.md).
- Helps interpret field renewal as a normal-science process, not only a paradigm-shift process.

## Operationalization

- Define incumbent scientists, collaborator circles, and potential entrant pools within a field or PMRA subfield.
- Track entry, publication share, references, or topic movement before and after a change in incumbent presence.
- Separate ordinary field growth from changes in who is willing to enter or redirect work.
- Compare effects across fields with different levels of incumbent eminence, social coherence, or resource control.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Azoulay, Fons-Rosen, and Graff Zivin (2019) frames progress within normal science as a political competition over ideas and scientific space.
- The paper contrasts this mechanism with rare paradigm-scale revolutions, arguing that incumbents can shape whether outside ideas enter a field.
- Their death-shock design shows that non-collaborator entry and field redirection can rise after a star scientist's death, consistent with field politics being embedded in normal scientific advance.

## Caveats

- Political competition does not imply misconduct or explicit exclusion.
- The same incumbents may be both productive field builders and barriers to later renewal.
- Entry changes can also reflect topic maturation, funding shifts, or measurement artifacts in field boundaries.
