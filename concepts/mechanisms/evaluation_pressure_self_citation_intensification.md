# Evaluation pressure self-citation intensification

## Summary

Evaluation pressure self-citation intensification is the mechanism that the growing use of citation counts in hiring, funding, and assessment can make self-citation a more consequential self-promotion behavior.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author, self-citation, evaluation system, citation metric, hiring or funding decision, or incentive environment.
- Typical representation: incentive mechanism, assessment-pressure narrative, self-citation trend, or metric-aware behavior model.
- Mechanism: when citation metrics carry career consequences, authors have stronger incentives to cite their own prior work and to increase the visibility of their research stream.
- Empirical signature: self-citation behavior is interpreted in relation to evaluation systems, not only as reference-list composition.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects [citation metric gaming](../validations/citation_metric_gaming.md) to ordinary self-promotion incentives short of explicit manipulation.
- Provides an evaluation-system mechanism for [gendered self-citation gap](gendered_self_citation_gap.md).
- Links self-citation behavior to [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) and [citation impact indicators](../measures/citation_impact_indicators.md).
- Helps explain why self-citation reporting matters for assessment workflows.

## Operationalization

- Identify the evaluation contexts where citation counts are used.
- Compare self-citation behavior across time, fields, or institutions with different metric salience.
- Track whether platforms expose self-citations separately from total citations.
- Distinguish self-citation as legitimate scholarly continuity from metric-oriented inflation.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from King et al. (2017) frames citation counts as increasingly relevant to professional evaluation.
- The paper argues that self-citations can contribute to cumulative citation advantage and should be considered in evaluation contexts.
- This motif is distinct from citation gaming because it describes incentive pressure and self-promotion behavior without assuming misconduct.
- It is also distinct from the gendered gap itself because the mechanism concerns metric salience.

## Caveats

- Self-citations can be substantively necessary when research programs build cumulatively.
- Evaluation pressure is difficult to isolate from field norms, productivity, and collaboration structure.
- Different databases expose self-citation information unevenly.
