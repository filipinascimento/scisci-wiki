# National policy enabler/inhibitor role

## Summary

National policy can enable or block global knowledge flows and actor linkages without fully determining international collaboration.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: national policy, funding eligibility, travel rule, affiliation rule, IP rule, knowledge flow, or collaboration tie.
- Typical representation: policy gate profile over global-local knowledge-flow channels.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: national policy mediation of international collaboration flows.
- Empirical signature: collaboration flows change under permissive versus restrictive national policy regimes.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines international-collaboration mechanism by linking it to [local global knowledge bridge](local_global_knowledge_bridge.md) and [knowledge creation exploitation location decoupling](knowledge_creation_exploitation_location_decoupling.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [collaboration regime policy leverage](../methods/collaboration_regime_policy_leverage.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [national funding boundary friction](national_funding_boundary_friction.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Code funding eligibility, travel, affiliation, IP, and data-sharing rules.
- Compare collaboration-flow changes under permissive versus restrictive regimes.
- Link policy gates to global-local bridges rather than treating policy as the only driver.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified local full text from Wagner and Leydesdorff (2005) says the national policy structure becomes either an enabler or inhibitor of knowledge flows and actor linkages.
- The motif makes national policy a gate in a global communication system.

## Caveats

- Policy effects are hard to isolate from capacity, language, geography, and field mix.
- A policy can enable flows without creating them directly.
