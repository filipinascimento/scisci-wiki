# Contextual-importance shift signal

## Summary

Awakening can mark a change in why a community finds an old paper relevant.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: sleeping paper, citing paper, title term, citation context, co-cited neighbor, awakening period, or topic label.
- Typical representation: before-after semantic comparison of citing contexts around awakening.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: semantic mechanism behind delayed recognition.
- Empirical signature: post-awakening citations use different terms or contexts than earlier citations..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines awakening mechanism by linking it to [awakening cocitation keyword diagnosis](../methods/awakening_cocitation_keyword_diagnosis.md) and [author mediated awakening bridge](author_mediated_awakening_bridge.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [citation context windows](../representations/citation_context_windows.md) is interpreted from citation histories.
- Adds cross-links to [journal impact factor](../measures/journal_impact_factor.md) so delayed-recognition and ranking motifs stay connected.

## Operationalization

- Split citing papers before and after awakening.
- Compare title terms, citation contexts, co-cited neighbors, or topical labels across the split.
- Use the shift to infer whether the old paper acquired a new role rather than merely more attention.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ke et al. (2015) says the Garfield case shows post-awakening citing-title keywords changing, with impact-factor language becoming salient after 2000.
- The motif treats awakening as a change in interpretive context, not just citation volume.

## Caveats

- The cited example is a case study in the main text.
- Title keywords are a crude proxy for intellectual context and should be paired with citation contexts when available.
