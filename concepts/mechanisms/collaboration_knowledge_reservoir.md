# Collaboration knowledge reservoir

## Summary

Collaboration knowledge reservoir is the mechanism by which a field's collaboration network stores access to skills, perspectives, and prior knowledge that teams can draw on through their members' network positions.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: team, collaborator, field-level collaboration network, prior tie, component, or knowledge pool.
- Typical representation: collaborator neighborhoods, component membership, disparate collaborator sets, or network-embedded access to field knowledge.
- Mechanism: teams inherit access to distributed knowledge through members' past collaborators and positions in the broader network.
- Empirical signature: teams whose members connect to larger and more diverse neighborhoods have broader potential access to field resources.

## Operationalization

- Build a time-resolved collaboration network before each focal team forms.
- Measure each team member's prior collaborator set, component membership, brokerage position, or neighborhood diversity.
- Aggregate member-level network access into team-level reservoir measures.
- Compare teams with overlapping prior neighborhoods to teams whose members bring disparate collaborator sets.
- Test whether reservoir breadth predicts novelty, impact, field connectivity, or downstream collaboration.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Guimera et al. (2005) states that the collaboration network is the medium in which future collaborations develop and acts as a storehouse for the pool of knowledge created within a field.
- The paper argues that how team members are embedded in the larger network affects how they access field knowledge.
- Guimera et al. state that individuals with large but disparate collaborator sets are more likely to draw from a diverse reservoir of knowledge.
- The same mechanism supports their claim that team organization within the larger network affects the likelihood of breakthroughs in a field.

## Caveats

- Coauthorship neighborhoods are imperfect proxies for actual knowledge access.
- Large components can contain disconnected specialties in practice even when graph paths exist.
- Reservoir access can be constrained by institutions, status, geography, language, funding, or informal exclusion.
