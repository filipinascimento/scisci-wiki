# Team coordination-loss counterhypothesis

## Summary

Team coordination-loss counterhypothesis is the expectation that groups can underperform individuals because communication, coordination, and social-network costs offset the benefits of pooled expertise.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: team, solo author, coordination cost, communication network, field, or impact outcome.
- Typical representation: counterhypothesis paired with team-versus-solo impact comparisons.
- Mechanism: adding collaborators increases expertise but also creates coordination overhead, social loafing risk, and communication loss.
- Empirical signature: if the counterhypothesis holds, larger or team-authored work should show lower impact after comparable opportunity controls.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides the opposing mechanism that makes [team impact advantage](team_impact_advantage.md) a real empirical test.
- Links team-size claims to [fixed-size team impact premium](../measures/fixed_size_team_impact_premium.md).
- Helps interpret [team high-citation tail dominance](team_high_citation_tail_dominance.md) as evidence against a simple coordination-loss view.
- Connects team-performance models to [team assembly mechanisms](team_assembly_mechanisms.md).

## Operationalization

- Specify a counterfactual prediction for solo versus team outputs before measuring impact.
- Compare team and solo papers within field, year, document type, and citation window.
- Test whether larger teams lose impact after controlling for expertise, field growth, and self-citations.
- Separate coordination-loss hypotheses from authorship-norm artifacts and field-composition changes.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wuchty et al. (2007) motivates the team-dominance test by noting arguments that teams can suffer from coordination and social-network losses.
- The paper then tests whether the empirical record instead shows increasing team production and impact.
- This motif is not the team-advantage result; it is the countermechanism that makes the result informative.
- Keeping it explicit helps later studies design tests where team benefits and team costs are both modeled.

## Caveats

- Coordination losses can coexist with average team impact advantage, especially in very large or poorly structured teams.
- Citation impact may miss coordination costs borne by participants.
- The counterhypothesis may be stronger for creative search than for development, measurement, or infrastructure-heavy work.
