# Late-career secondary support shift

## Summary

Some senior scientists appear to move from earlier leadership profiles into late-career support roles while remaining active across multiple contribution types.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author career stage, archetype transition, leader profile, supporting profile, contribution count, or author position.
- Typical representation: stage-to-stage archetype transition pattern with late support profiles.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: late-career movement from leadership to secondary contribution roles.
- Empirical signature: authors with earlier leader profiles appear as supporting contributors in later career stages.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines contribution-career mechanism by linking it to [leader specialized supporting archetypes](../representations/leader_specialized_supporting_archetypes.md) and [contribution archetype career progression](contribution_archetype_career_progression.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [asymmetric leader archetype mobility](asymmetric_leader_archetype_mobility.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [supporting archetype career attrition](supporting_archetype_career_attrition.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Estimate transitions from early or mid-career Leader profiles to late-career Supporting profiles.
- Compare late-career Supporting role counts and author-position distributions against late-career Leader profiles.
- Separate cohort composition from within-author transition where longitudinal histories allow.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified local full text from Robinson-Garcia et al. (2020) reports that many late-career researchers are Supporting despite earlier Leader profiles and suggests some senior scientists leave leadership to younger colleagues.
- The motif captures a possible leadership handoff mechanism in contribution careers.

## Caveats

- Generational composition and survivor selection limit causal interpretation.
- Late-career support may reflect collaboration choice, role labeling, or publication selection.
