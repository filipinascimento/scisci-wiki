# Annual peer-feedback meetings

## Summary

Regular investigator meetings can provide critique, ideas, encouragement, and collaboration channels that support exploratory research.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: investigator meeting, peer network, collaboration, critique, topic pivot, or funding program.
- Typical representation: program-network channel linking convenings to collaboration and topic shifts.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: social feedback mechanism inside elite funding programs.
- Empirical signature: formal meetings expose funded scientists to peer critique and collaboration opportunities outside their local institutions..

- Refines funding-program social mechanism by linking it to [review feedback exploration scaffold](review_feedback_exploration_scaffold.md) and [citation collaboration flow coupling](citation_collaboration_flow_coupling.md).

## Operationalization

- Track formal convenings, attendance, peer-network density, subsequent collaborations, and topic pivots after meetings.
- Compare funded programs with and without recurring scientific meetings.
- Distinguish meeting-induced collaboration from preexisting network position.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Azoulay et al. (2009) notes that HHMI investigators participate in annual science meetings that expose them to critique, ideas, and potential collaborations.
- The motif separates program social infrastructure from pure money or autonomy.

## Caveats

- Meeting effects can be confounded with HHMI status, lab quality, and elite network access.
- Conferences can reinforce conformity as well as exploration.
