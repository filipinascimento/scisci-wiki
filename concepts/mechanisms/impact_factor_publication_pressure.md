# Impact-factor publication pressure

## Summary

Impact-factor publication pressure is the incentive for researchers, supervisors, institutions, or governments to prioritize publication in high-impact-factor journals as a career or resource target.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: researcher, trainee, supervisor, department, university, funding policy, or journal venue.
- Typical representation: publication-target rule, bonus policy, promotion criterion, mentoring expectation, or high-impact-journal count.
- Mechanism or measurement target: venue-level citation averages becoming career and funding incentives.
- Empirical signature: researchers are encouraged or rewarded for publishing in journals above a specified impact-factor threshold regardless of article-level content.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a behavioral mechanism behind [journal impact factor assessment misuse](../validations/journal_impact_factor_assessment_misuse.md).
- Connects [journal impact factor](../measures/journal_impact_factor.md) to [assessment incentive misalignment](assessment_incentive_misalignment.md) and [indicator systemic effects](indicator_systemic_effects.md).
- Has an editorial-side counterpart in [editorial impact-factor pressure channel](editorial_impact_factor_pressure_channel.md).
- Helps explain venue-prestige pressure on early-career researchers, PhD students, and hiring or promotion candidates.
- Supplies a policy target for DORA-style assessment reform and portfolio-based researcher review.

## Operationalization

- Identify policies, mentoring advice, promotion templates, or bonuses that reference high-impact journals or impact-factor thresholds.
- Track whether such rules change venue choice, publication timing, collaboration behavior, or risk-taking.
- Compare article-level evidence with journal-level targets to detect venue-prestige substitution.
- Audit whether formal JIF bans reduce informal high-impact-journal language in CVs, letters, and committee reports.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hicks et al. (2015) reports promotion decisions based on high-impact-journal publication counts and supervisors asking PhD students to publish in high-impact journals before they are ready.
- The article also notes university funding or bonus systems that reward publication in journals above specific impact-factor thresholds.
- Hicks et al.'s impact-factor figure frames rising attention to the journal impact factor as a crisis in research evaluation.
- These examples show the mechanism by which a journal-level average becomes a behavioral target for individual scientists.

## Caveats

- Venue choice can still matter for audience, peer-review fit, and field visibility.
- Not every preference for selective venues is impact-factor pressure; the diagnostic issue is whether JIF or journal rank substitutes for article-level evaluation.
- Pressure can persist culturally even after formal policies remove JIF language.
