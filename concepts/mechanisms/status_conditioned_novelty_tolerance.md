# Status-conditioned novelty tolerance

## Summary

Status-conditioned novelty tolerance is the mechanism in which evaluators treat novel or unorthodox proposals as more credible when they are attached to high-status applicants, institutions, or records.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: proposal, reviewer panel, applicant, institution, funding decision, or publication outcome.
- Typical representation: novelty score, applicant-status indicator, review outcome, funding outcome, and novelty-by-status interaction.
- Mechanism: status provides credibility, reputational insurance, or benefit of the doubt that makes reviewers more willing to endorse unconventional work.
- Empirical signature: the penalty or discount for novelty is weaker for high-status applicants than for low-status applicants.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [status authority evaluation bias](status_authority_evaluation_bias.md) and [right-tail novelty review discount](right_tail_novelty_review_discount.md) by combining them into an interaction mechanism.
- Provides an evaluative channel for [funding model status contingency](funding_model_status_contingency.md).
- Connects [novelty penalty](novelty_penalty.md), [peer review gatekeeping](peer_review_gatekeeping.md), and [institutional prestige hierarchy](institutional_prestige_hierarchy.md).
- Helps interpret why competitive funding can appear creativity-enhancing on average while disadvantaging lower-status researchers.

## Operationalization

- Measure proposal or output novelty using reference-combination, topic, semantic, or classification-based measures.
- Code applicant status through rank, gender, institutional tier, prior funding, prior citations, or local prestige proxies such as [former Imperial university core-status proxy](../measures/former_imperial_university_core_status_proxy.md).
- Estimate interactions between novelty and status in review scores, funding decisions, or post-award creative output.
- Compare selection effects with post-award treatment effects where project histories allow.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Lee, and Walsh (2018) argues that status can make unorthodox or risky ideas easier to defend in peer review.
- Their Japanese funding evidence shows negative competitive-funding interactions for lower-status categories, especially junior and female researchers, while high-status researchers benefit more from competitive funding.
- The mechanism is consistent with the paper's split between selection into competitive grants and process changes after funding.

## Caveats

- Status-conditioned tolerance is an inferred mechanism unless proposal texts, review scores, and panel deliberation data are directly observed.
- Status may correlate with proposal quality, resources, team composition, and field placement.
- Effects can differ across funding systems and review criteria.
