# Prestige-core norm diffusion

## Summary

Prestige-core norm diffusion is the mechanism by which centrally located elite institutions can spread research agendas, collaboration norms, and departmental practices through doctoral placement networks.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: institution, prestige core, doctoral placement edge, shortest path, faculty hiring network, research norm, or agenda signal.
- Typical representation: core-to-periphery placement flow, centrality-by-prestige curve, elite-origin share, shortest-path tree, or norm-diffusion pathway.
- Mechanism: doctoral training imprints interests and norms, and elite institutions are closer to the rest of the hiring network, so their practices can spread more easily than peripheral innovations.
- Empirical signature: high-prestige institutions are both highly central and internally supplied by other elite institutions, giving core-origin ideas shorter paths through the field.

## Operationalization

- Infer prestige ranks and centrality measures in a field-specific faculty hiring network.
- Identify core institutions and measure their shortest-path distance to other institutions.
- Track topics, methods, collaboration norms, publication venues, or hiring practices from doctoral origin through faculty placements.
- Compare diffusion speed and reach for ideas originating in core versus peripheral institutions.
- Control for merit, resources, field size, geography, and publication visibility where possible.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Clauset, Arbesman, and Larremore (2015) reports that standard network centrality measures correlate strongly with prestige rank in faculty hiring networks.
- The paper states that harmonic centrality increases smoothly with prestige, meaning high-prestige institutions are separated from all others by fewer intermediaries than low-prestige institutions.
- Clauset et al. argue that because research interests, collaboration networks, and academic norms are often cemented during doctoral training, central high-prestige institutions can influence research agendas, communities, and departmental norms throughout a discipline.
- They further argue that ideas originating in the high-prestige core can spread more easily regardless of merit, while ideas from low-prestige institutions must pass through more intermediaries.

## Caveats

- Placement-network centrality is an opportunity channel, not direct proof of norm adoption.
- Topic diffusion can also occur through papers, conferences, funding, mobility, and online communication.
- Core-origin ideas may spread because of resources, quality, visibility, or status; these mechanisms need separate identification.
