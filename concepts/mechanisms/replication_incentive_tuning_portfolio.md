# Replication incentive-tuning portfolio

## Summary

Replication, openness, publication, and funding incentives can be tuned as a portfolio to shift the balance between innovation and verification.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: incentive policy, replication credit, publication norm, funding program, or verification activity.
- Typical representation: portfolio of incentive levers for replication, transparency, and confirmatory evidence.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: system-level incentives for reliable cumulative science.
- Empirical signature: changes in credit, funding, or publication rules alter the share of resources devoted to verification and transparent reporting.

## Operationalization

- List incentive levers including replication funding, registered reports, data/code requirements, negative-results venues, and evaluation credit.
- Track verification outputs and innovation outputs before and after incentive changes.
- Monitor unintended effects such as excessive conservatism or box-checking compliance.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Open Science Collaboration (2015) interprets reproducibility shortfalls as a systemic issue involving incentives for innovation, publication, and verification.
- The discussion motivates incentive changes rather than only individual-level correction.

## Caveats

- Incentive portfolios can shift behavior without guaranteeing improved truth discovery.
- Overweighting verification can reduce exploratory work if not balanced carefully.
