# Mobile elite policy leverage

## Summary

Mobile elite policy leverage is the mechanism by which a small internationally mobile minority can have outsized consequences for national and global science because mobile scholars are disproportionately high impact.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: mobile scholar, high-impact researcher, country, policy shock, citation premium, or mobility-restricted group.
- Typical representation: mobile share, citation-premium ratio, high-impact mobile fraction, policy-exposure scenario, or restricted-elite loss estimate.
- Mechanism: policy changes that affect a small mobile population can still alter scientific capacity if that population contains many high-impact scholars.
- Empirical signature: mobile scholars are numerically rare but show higher normalized citation impact than non-mobile counterparts.

## Operationalization

- Estimate the share of researchers classified as internationally mobile in a defined corpus and window.
- Estimate their normalized citation premium relative to non-mobile researchers by region, field, and career stage.
- Identify policy-exposed mobile groups and compute potential losses under restricted-flow scenarios.
- Compare headcount exposure with impact-weighted exposure.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sugimoto et al. (2017) reports that about four percent of researchers in their corpus were internationally mobile.
- The same paper reports that mobile scholars had about 40 percent higher citation rates on average than non-mobile scholars, with large regional variation.
- Sugimoto et al. conclude that mobile scholars are a minority but show the highest impact globally, making restrictions on their movement scientifically consequential.

## Caveats

- High impact may select researchers into mobility rather than result from mobility.
- Citation impact is only one dimension of scientific value and may privilege fields, languages, or institutions with higher citation density.
- Policy leverage estimates require careful treatment of destination capacity, substitution, and delayed effects.
