# Prestige-venue author specialization

## Summary

Prestige-venue author specialization is the mechanism in which some scientists or teams learn to repeatedly write, frame, and submit work for particular disciplinary or high-impact generalist venues.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author, senior author, journal, paper, team, or venue history.
- Typical representation: repeated same-venue senior authorship, venue-specific prior-publication history, generalist-journal specialization, or field-specific incumbent share.
- Mechanism: authors accumulate venue-specific tacit knowledge, editorial fit, narrative style, and review expectations.
- Empirical signature: some venues show growing or persistent dominance by established same-venue senior authors, while other venues show different new-author patterns.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [venue chaperone effect](venue_chaperone_effect.md) by separating author specialization from junior-to-senior apprenticeship.
- Connects [new PI access tightening in high-impact journals](new_pi_access_tightening_in_high_impact_journals.md), [tacit venue submission knowledge transfer](tacit_venue_submission_knowledge_transfer.md), and [peer review gatekeeping](peer_review_gatekeeping.md).
- Helps explain why venue access can be a learned production style rather than only a quality threshold.
- Uses [established PI share trajectory](../measures/established_pi_share_trajectory.md) as one measurable signature.

## Operationalization

- Build journal-specific author-order histories.
- Track whether last authors have previously published in the same venue as last authors, non-last authors, or neither.
- Compare repeat-senior-author shares across venues, fields, and time.
- Interpret repeated venue authorship alongside citation impact, author order norms, and accepted-publication denominator limits.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sekara et al. (2018) contrasts Physical Review D's growing repeated-PI pattern with Nature's shift toward authors specializing in high-impact general-audience journals.
- The paper's chaperone framework shows that venue access can reflect prior author-venue experience rather than only one-paper quality.
- This page keeps the mechanism separate from the chaperone ratio, which measures non-last-to-last transitions.

## Caveats

- Repeated venue authorship can reflect true field specialization, editorial fit, selection, reputation, or author-side submission strategy.
- Accepted publications do not reveal unsuccessful submissions.
- The mechanism should be interpreted with field-specific author-order norms.
