# Inspiration-Implementation Split

## Summary

Inspiration-implementation split is the mechanism by which idea origination and idea execution require different knowledge capacities, making teamwork necessary even when inspiration is individual.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: innovation, inventor, team, project, or application area.
- Typical representation: two-stage process separating idea arrival from implementation coverage.
- Mechanism: individuals may generate ideas within narrow expertise, but implementation requires pooling complementary foundational knowledge.
- Empirical signature: inventions or discoveries have identifiable originators plus additional specialists needed to make the idea work.

## Operationalization

- Separate roles or contributions into idea origination and implementation work.
- Measure whether implementation requires expertise outside the originator's own specialty.
- Compare single-originator and multi-specialist invention histories.
- Test whether deeper fields show more frequent separation between inspiration and implementation.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Jones (2009) uses the microprocessor as an example where one researcher helped originate the architecture while another had the working-chip design expertise.
- The paper describes the microprocessor as one person's inspiration but several people's invention.
- Jones formalizes this distinction with separate inspiration and implementation stages in the model.

## Caveats

- Historical accounts may over-assign inspiration to visible individuals and undercount distributed idea formation.
- Implementation can feed back into inspiration rather than following it cleanly.
- Contribution statements, patents, and biographies differ in how they reveal the split.
