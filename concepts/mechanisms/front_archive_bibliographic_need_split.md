# Front-archive bibliographic need split

## Summary

Front-archive bibliographic need split is the mechanism by which citation networks create two distinct information needs: rapid alerting to the active research front and slower organization of the accumulated archive.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: literature, research front, archive, subject classification, alerting service, or citation index.
- Typical representation: paired current-awareness and archival-search workflows.
- Mechanism: fast-moving front literature requires immediate filtering, while older literature requires durable taxonomy and retrieval structure.
- Empirical signature: the same citation system supports both front surveillance and historical or subject-based archive navigation.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [research fronts](research_fronts.md) by separating front-facing current-awareness needs from archive-facing classification needs.
- Places [citation-index alerting service](../methods/citation_index_alerting_service.md) inside a broader bibliographic-use typology.
- Links matrix views such as [citation topography strips](../representations/citation_topography_strips.md) to practical information needs.
- Helps distinguish rapid citation-front dynamics from stable [taxonomic subject knowledge parcels](../representations/taxonomic_subject_knowledge_parcels.md).

## Operationalization

- Classify bibliographic tools by whether they optimize current-front alerting, archival retrieval, or both.
- Compare citation-index alerts against subject-classification searches for the same query domain.
- Track whether users seek recent connected work or stable older bodies of knowledge.
- Use citation matrix density, citation age, and subject-classification stability as diagnostic signals.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Price (1965) distinguishes the needs of the active research front from broader archival and taxonomic organization.
- Price describes citation indexing as especially useful for following the tight, current, selected part of the literature.
- The same discussion contrasts this with established subject organization through books, classifications, and taxonomic parcels.
- The motif is broader than the alerting-service page because it names the split between two bibliographic regimes.

## Caveats

- Many fields need both regimes at once; a sharp front/archive split is an ideal type.
- Review articles, handbooks, and databases can bridge front and archive roles.
- Digital search systems can blur the distinction by mixing recency, citation links, and subject terms.
