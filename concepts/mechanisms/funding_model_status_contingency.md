# Funding model status contingency

## Summary

Funding model status contingency is the motif that the effect of competitive versus block funding on novelty depends on researcher status, rank, gender, institutional position, and autonomy.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: funded project, publication, grant, scientist, institution, or funding system.
- Typical representation: competitive-funding indicator, block-funding indicator, novelty score, researcher-status interaction, or selection-versus-treatment decomposition.
- Mechanism: competitive funding can select high-novelty projects for high-status researchers while increasing conformity pressure or selection barriers for low-status researchers.
- Empirical signature: funding-model effects on novelty vary by rank, gender, institutional prestige, or other status proxies.

## Operationalization

- Link project-level funding source to publication outputs and classify funding as competitive project funding or internal block funding.
- Use [project funding-source survey linkage](../datasets/project_funding_source_survey_linkage.md) and a [competitive-funding share threshold](../measures/competitive_funding_share_threshold.md) when acknowledgments cannot capture block funding reliably.
- Measure novelty using reference-combination, topic, semantic, or field-classification methods.
- Estimate interactions between funding type and status variables such as rank, gender, institutional tier, prior reputation, or career stage using [funding status-interaction tests](../validations/funding_status_interaction_test.md).
- Make the status proxy explicit, for example with [respondent-author status proxy](../methods/respondent_author_status_proxy.md), and test whether the mechanism is better described as [status conformity pressure](status_conformity_pressure.md).
- Where possible, decompose whether differences arise from selection into funding versus treatment after funding.
- Interpret within-system funding contrasts through [mixed-funding within-system comparison](../methods/mixed_funding_within_system_comparison.md), and audit coarse grant categories with [grant competitiveness heterogeneity attenuation](../validations/grant_competitiveness_heterogeneity_attenuation.md).

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Lee, and Walsh (2018) combines a survey of Japanese scientists with Web of Science publication outputs to compare competitive project funding and internal block funding.
- The paper reports that competitively funded projects have higher novelty on average than block-funded projects in this Japanese setting.
- The same full text reports negative interactions between competitive funding and low-status categories, especially junior and female researchers: the positive novelty association of competitive funding holds mainly for high-status researchers.
- Wang et al. further report evidence consistent with both selection and treatment mechanisms for junior researchers: competitive selection is less receptive to novel ideas from junior researchers, and junior researchers become less novel after receiving competitive funds.
- The paper's survey design and process-change split are separated here as reusable motifs for project funding linkage, competitive-funding share measurement, funding status-interaction testing, and [funding selection-treatment splits](../methods/funding_selection_treatment_split.md).
- The same paper distinguishes the post-award [proposal commitment creativity constraint](proposal_commitment_creativity_constraint.md) from selection into competitive funding and uses subgroup interactions as an [interaction-effect alternative-explanation stress test](../validations/interaction_effect_alternative_explanation_stress_test.md).
- The same revisited evidence also supports policy-level framing through the new-public-management funding shift, status-conditioned tolerance for novelty, feasibility and preliminary-data screening barriers, and the former-Imperial-university proxy for Japan's institutional core.

## Caveats

- Evidence comes from Japan's mixed funding system and may not generalize directly to other national systems.
- Status categories are proxies and can mix rank, gender, institutional resources, reputation, and autonomy.
- Publication novelty is only one output dimension; productivity, training, infrastructure, and social value may respond differently.
