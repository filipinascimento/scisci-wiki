# High-disruption tail conservation

## Summary

High-disruption tail conservation is the pattern that the absolute number of highly disruptive papers and patents can remain roughly stable even while average disruptiveness declines.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: highly disruptive paper, highly disruptive patent, field-year cohort, technology category, or right-tail CD5 bin.
- Typical representation: yearly counts of works above CD5 thresholds, often separated from the much larger body of moderately disruptive or consolidating works.
- Mechanism or conclusion: frontier-rerouting work may have a relatively stable production rate even as total publication and patent output expands.
- Empirical signature: flat or weakly changing counts in high-CD5 bins despite large growth in total papers and patents.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Qualifies [disruptiveness decline over time](disruptiveness_decline.md) by separating prevalence from absolute count.
- Helps interpret apparent tension between aggregate slowing and visible breakthroughs.
- Provides a policy-relevant target for studying whether high-disruption production has a carrying capacity or responds to field, funding, and institutional conditions.

## Operationalization

- Choose a disruption score and threshold, such as CD5 greater than a positive cutoff.
- Count focal papers or patents above the threshold by year and field.
- Compare absolute counts, shares, and field composition over time.
- Track churn in the fields or technology categories responsible for right-tail disruptive works.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Park, Leahey, and Funk (2023) reports that despite large increases in scientific productivity, the number of papers and patents in the far-right CD5 tail remains nearly constant over time.
- The paper describes this as conservation of the absolute number of highly disruptive papers and patents.
- Park et al. report that this stability holds despite churn in the underlying scientific fields and technology categories responsible for producing highly disruptive works.
- Their discussion presents two possible interpretations: a fixed carrying capacity for highly disruptive work, or a policy-responsive process shaped by funder and scientist interests and the ripeness of fields.

## Caveats

- A stable absolute count can still imply a declining share of disruptive work when total output grows.
- Threshold choices can change which works are counted as high-disruption.
- The mechanism behind the stable tail is not identified by the count pattern alone.
