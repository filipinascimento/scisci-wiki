# Competency-independent technological effects

## Summary

Competency-independent technological effects are trajectory changes whose consequences propagate through technology networks, not only through whether incumbent organizations' capabilities are enhanced or destroyed.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: invention, patent, technology component, incumbent capability, or technology trajectory.
- Typical representation: network effect on predecessor and successor use, separated from firm-level competency categories.
- Mechanism: inventions rewire the use of prior components and subsequent combinations across a technology network.
- Empirical signature: a technology can consolidate or destabilize predecessor streams even when organizational capability effects are not directly measured.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [disruption and consolidation](disruption_consolidation.md) beyond organization-centered theories of technological discontinuity.
- Motivates measuring change with citation-network structure rather than only firm performance or incumbent survival.
- Provides a mechanism-level interpretation for [continuous disruption-consolidation scale](../methods/continuous_disruption_consolidation_scale.md).
- Helps connect patent-level science-of-science measures to technology-policy questions about public funding and commercialization.

## Operationalization

- Represent focal inventions and their predecessors as a citation or knowledge network.
- Track later inventions' reliance on the focal invention, the predecessors, or both.
- Interpret consolidation or destabilization as a network-level effect before mapping it onto firms, universities, or policy regimes.
- Compare organizational predictors, such as commercial engagement or federal support, after the network effect is estimated.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Funk and Owen-Smith (2017) builds on competency-enhancing and competency-destroying technology theories but separates network effects from organizations' competencies.
- The paper argues that inventions' implications can be felt through incumbent capabilities, yet their effects ripple more broadly through networks of prior and subsequent technologies.
- This mechanism explains why the same patent-citation data can be analyzed at the focal-patent level and then aggregated into [university patent portfolio disruption](../measures/university_patent_portfolio_disruption.md).
- Their commercialization and federal-funding models show how organizational settings can predict different network-level effects after those effects are measured.

## Caveats

- Competency-independent does not mean organization-irrelevant; it means the measured effect is first defined in the technology network.
- Patent citations only partially observe technological relationships and may miss tacit or non-patented components.
- Mapping network effects back to organizational consequences requires separate validation.
