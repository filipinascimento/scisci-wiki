# Rejected-proposal line attrition

## Summary

Rejected-proposal line attrition is the mechanism by which unfunded proposals and their associated research lines are discontinued, making review decisions shocks to future knowledge production.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: grant proposal, research line, applicant, funding decision, follow-on output, or abandoned project.
- Typical representation: rejected-proposal tracking, continuation/discontinuation indicator, later publication or grant record, and funding outcome.
- Mechanism: resource denial, reviewer signal, opportunity cost, and strategic redirection can terminate research lines that might otherwise have developed.
- Empirical signature: a nontrivial share of rejected proposals has no detectable follow-on research activity, even after accounting for proposal quality.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Gives [peer review and gatekeeping](peer_review_gatekeeping.md) a downstream attrition consequence.
- Extends [funded-only peer-review validation](../validations/funded_only_peer_review_validation.md), which cannot observe the full rejected-proposal counterfactual.
- Connects to [unobserved proposal-quality identification](../validations/unobserved_proposal_quality_identification.md) because discontinuation may reflect either low quality or lost opportunity.
- Motivates [funding threshold quasi-experiments](../methods/funding_threshold_quasi_experiments.md) when near-cutoff funding decisions can be tracked.

## Operationalization

- Link proposal records to later publications, follow-on grants, patents, preprints, clinical trials, or topic-continuation traces.
- Define a research-line continuation window and topic-matching rule before inspecting outcomes.
- Compare rejected proposals with near-funded proposals or funded proposals with similar review scores.
- Distinguish true discontinuation from output migration, delayed publication, unfunded continuation, or missing data.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Boudreau, Guinan, Lakhani, and Riedl (2016) frames scientific peer review as a competitive resource-allocation process where NIH and NSF fund only a minority of applications.
- The paper cites prior evidence that between one-third and one-half of rejected project proposals and associated research lines are subsequently discontinued by their authors.
- Boudreau et al. emphasize that rejected proposals may be lower quality, but unexplained variation and low interrater reliability make rejection a noisy and consequential gatekeeping event.
- This mechanism therefore connects proposal evaluation to missing or foreclosed knowledge, not only to the quality of funded projects.

## Caveats

- Discontinuation can be appropriate if a proposal is infeasible, redundant, or low quality.
- Tracking rejected lines requires access to confidential proposal text and reliable downstream matching.
- Later output absence can reflect publication lag, topic relabeling, team movement, or unobserved non-public outputs.
