# Scientific ecosystem diversity

## Summary

Scientific ecosystem diversity treats the research system as an adaptive ecosystem whose long-run discovery capacity depends on maintaining varied scientist types, strategies, fields, and institutions.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientific ecosystem, funder portfolio, institution, field, scientist type, research strategy, or training lineage.
- Typical representation: portfolio diversity, strategy mix, field diversity, demographic and institutional diversity, or niche-structure map.
- Mechanism or measurement target: diversity provides resilience against unpredictable discoveries and changing scientific opportunities.
- Empirical signature: systems with broader strategy and researcher diversity are less dependent on any one prediction model, metric, field, or dominant institution.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Links [diversity measures](../measures/diversity_measures.md) to discovery-policy design rather than only interdisciplinarity measurement.
- Provides a counterweight to [predictable-discovery selection pressure](predictable_discovery_selection_pressure.md) and [risk-averse scientist selection](risk_averse_scientist_selection.md).
- Supports policy portfolios that include focused mission work, exploratory projects, contrarian work, tool building, and field-bridging research.
- Turns uncertainty about individual discoveries into an argument for maintaining a diverse discovery ecosystem.

## Operationalization

- Define the ecosystem level: funder portfolio, university, country, field, journal system, or scientific workforce.
- Measure strategy diversity, field spread, team-size diversity, demographic and institutional representation, funding-instrument mix, and support for high-variance projects.
- Track how evaluation regimes change the portfolio over time.
- Test whether [feedback-resistant success measures](../methods/feedback_resistant_success_measures.md) preserve ecosystem diversity better than raw past-success indicators.
- Evaluate whether portfolio diversity predicts resilience, field emergence, high-disruption tail output, delayed-recognition uptake, or broad societal benefits.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Clauset, Larremore, and Sinatra (2017) explicitly compares science to an ecosystem that adapts to changing incentives and requirements.
- The paper argues that a reliable engine for discoveries may be to cultivate and maintain a healthy ecosystem of scientists rather than focus on predicting individual discoveries.
- Clauset et al. call for policies that cultivate a diverse scientific ecosystem, including different scientist types and research styles.
- The same essay connects this diversity argument to the unpredictability of some discoveries and to the risk that predictive evaluation narrows innovation.
- Verified full-text evidence from Stirling (2007) adds a governance mechanism for this portfolio logic: diversity in science and technology can hedge ignorance, mitigate lock-in, foster innovation, and accommodate plural perspectives.

## Caveats

- Ecosystem diversity is a portfolio-level construct and should not be reduced to a single diversity score.
- Different diversity dimensions can conflict: field diversity, demographic equity, institutional spread, strategy diversity, and reliability incentives need separate measurement.
- Maintaining diversity still requires accountability and evidence about which interventions preserve useful variety.
