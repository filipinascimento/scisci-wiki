# Early-impact longevity feedback probe

## Summary

Treats early paper impact as a possible feedback signal for career continuation and later productivity.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: early-career author, first-paper impact, later productivity, or career continuation.
- Typical representation: early-impact predictor of later publication persistence.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: feedback from early recognition into career longevity.
- Empirical signature: authors with higher early impact continue publishing longer or more productively after controls..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects [citation productivity feedback](citation_productivity_feedback.md) to career-impact modeling.
- Useful for distinguishing Q-like sustained impact from cumulative advantage feedback.
- Links early citations to [impact-productivity coevolution boundary](../validations/impact_productivity_coevolution_boundary.md).

## Operationalization

- Use first-10-paper average impact to predict later publication persistence, controlling for field, cohort, venue, and prior productivity.
- Compare citation feedback with institution, collaborator, and funding data where available.
- Avoid causal claims without designs that separate selection from feedback.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sinatra et al. (2016) probes whether early paper impact predicts career longevity or later productivity.
- The analysis motivates early impact as a possible feedback signal.

## Caveats

- The association may reflect selection, resources, or topic visibility rather than citation feedback.
- Early citation windows can be short and field-dependent.
