# Prestige refutation suppression

## Summary

Prestige refutation suppression is the mechanism by which high-status investigators or groups can make it harder for contradictory findings to pass review or gain attention.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: refuting manuscript, peer-review process, high-status investigator, entrenched claim, or field consensus.
- Typical representation: gatekeeping pathway where status affects whether negative or contradictory evidence is published.
- Mechanism target: false or exaggerated claims can persist when refutation is socially or institutionally disadvantaged.
- Empirical signature: refuting findings face higher barriers when they challenge powerful authors, influential groups, or dominant theory.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects [interest and prejudice bias barrier](interest_prejudice_bias_barrier.md) to [peer review and gatekeeping](peer_review_gatekeeping.md).
- Provides a mechanism for [selective reporting and publication bias](publication_bias_selective_reporting.md) beyond author-level reporting decisions.
- Links to [status-authority evaluation bias](status_authority_evaluation_bias.md), especially when prestige shapes which claims receive deference.
- Helps explain why replication and refutation may need protected publication formats.

## Operationalization

- Identify refutation attempts and the status of the investigators or claims being challenged.
- Compare review outcomes, publication delays, framing, and citation attention for refuting versus confirmatory work.
- Track whether refutations are published in lower-visibility venues or after longer delays.
- Analyze reviewer or editor conflicts when available.
- Pair with preregistration, registered reports, or replication formats that reduce outcome-based gatekeeping.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ioannidis (2005) argues that powerful investigators can suppress contrary results through peer review, helping false findings persist.
- The same discussion places this mechanism among nonfinancial interests, prejudice, and field-level bias.
- This page turns that mechanism into a reusable motif connecting credibility models to social hierarchy.

## Caveats

- Direct evidence is difficult because review decisions and reviewer identities are often confidential.
- Strong claims can survive refutation attempts for substantive reasons, not only prestige.
- Status can increase scrutiny as well as deference, depending on field and claim.
