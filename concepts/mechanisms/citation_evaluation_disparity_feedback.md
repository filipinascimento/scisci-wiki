# Citation-evaluation disparity feedback

## Summary

Citation-evaluation disparity feedback is the mechanism by which citation-based assessment can amplify demographic disparities when the citation indicators used for evaluation already reflect unequal authorship positions, collaboration access, field placement, or recognition.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, author group, citation metric, evaluation process, hiring or promotion decision, funding allocation, or collaboration opportunity.
- Typical representation: citation disadvantage feeding into evaluation, evaluation feeding into resources, and resources feeding into future output and impact.
- Mechanism: metric-mediated cumulative disadvantage.
- Empirical signature: groups with lower citation exposure face evaluation penalties that can reduce future opportunities and reinforce the original gap.

## Operationalization

- Estimate demographic gaps in citation indicators after field, year, document type, author position, and collaboration controls.
- Identify where the same indicators enter hiring, promotion, funding, salary, or ranking decisions.
- Test whether citation-based decisions predict later resources, collaboration access, publication output, or citation impact differently by group.
- Compare evaluation systems before and after metric-audit, narrative-CV, or responsible-assessment interventions.
- Treat observed feedback as a system-level process, not as a statement about individual paper quality.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Lariviere et al. (2013) reports that papers with women in dominant author positions receive fewer citations than those with men in the same positions in the most productive countries.
- The paper argues that this disadvantage is accentuated because women's publication portfolios are more domestic and therefore benefit less from the citation premium associated with international collaboration.
- Lariviere et al. explicitly warn that because citations play a central part in researcher evaluation, this situation can worsen gender disparities.
- The paper frames the result as evidence that barriers to women in science remain widespread despite policies intended to level the playing field.

## Caveats

- Citation disadvantage can reflect many channels, including field composition, seniority, collaboration access, topic choice, journal placement, bias, and source coverage.
- Feedback claims require longitudinal evidence about evaluation decisions and subsequent opportunities.
- Field-normalized citations reduce some confounding but do not make citation indicators demographically neutral.
- Interventions must avoid obscuring real inequities by simply removing demographic auditing.
