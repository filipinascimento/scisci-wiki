# Asymmetric leader-archetype mobility

## Summary

Asymmetric leader-archetype mobility is the career-stage pattern in which scientists with leader contribution profiles can move across role archetypes more readily than specialized or supporting scientists can move into later senior stages.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, career stage, contribution archetype, transition between archetypes, or survival to later publication stages.
- Typical representation: archetype transition matrix, Sankey diagram, or stage-to-stage progression rate.
- Mechanism: leader-profile access provides versatility and progression opportunities, while specialized or supporting profiles face narrower advancement paths.
- Empirical signature: transition probabilities are asymmetric, with more shifts from leader to other profiles than from specialized or supporting profiles into senior leader stages.

## Operationalization

- Assign each scientist to contribution archetypes within career stages.
- Build transition counts or probabilities between archetypes and stages.
- Compare progression rates from junior to early-career, early-career to mid-career, and mid-career to late-career stages.
- Test whether mobility is directional, especially whether specialized or supporting profiles move into leader and senior stages less often.
- Stratify by gender, field, cohort, and data coverage when sample size permits.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Robinson-Garcia et al. (2020) reports that 84% of early-career Leader scientists reach mid-career, compared with 30% of Specialized and 16% of Supporting scientists.
- From mid-career to late-career, 37% of Leader-profile scientists progress, compared with 1% of Specialized and 2% of Supporting authors.
- The paper states that shifts across archetypes are more likely at earlier career stages and from Leader to other archetypes, but not vice versa.
- In the discussion, Robinson-Garcia et al. describe the Leader archetype as highly versatile and note that most Specialized or Supporting scientists in the dataset do not progress to more senior stages.

## Caveats

- Stage progression is conditioned on remaining visible in publication data.
- Archetype transitions can reflect institutional position, funding, team composition, or selection rather than individual preference.
- Mobility estimates depend on the career-stage definitions and the accuracy of imputed contribution profiles.
