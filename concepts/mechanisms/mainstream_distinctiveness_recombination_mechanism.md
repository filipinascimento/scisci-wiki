# Mainstream-distinctiveness recombination mechanism

## Summary

Mainstream-distinctiveness recombination mechanism explains high-impact work as a combination of recognizable mainstream grounding with a distinctive tail of uncommon knowledge pairings.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, reference-combination profile, research trajectory, field audience, or impact outcome.
- Typical representation: high median conventionality plus high tail novelty, mainstream trajectory with rare left-tail combinations, or two-dimensional recombination strategy.
- Mechanism: conventional grounding makes a paper legible and relevant to an active research audience, while rare combinations provide distinctiveness.
- Empirical signature: impact is elevated when high tail novelty is embedded in substantial conventionality.

## Uses in Science of Science

## Operationalization

- Measure the focal paper's central tendency of conventional combinations and its rare-tail combinations.
- Identify papers that jointly have high conventionality and high tail novelty.
- Compare impact rates for this joint strategy against one-dimensional novelty or conventionality profiles.
- Interpret the result with field controls and candidate audience or legibility mechanisms.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Uzzi et al. (2013) states that high-impact work is sharply elevated when combinations of prior work are anchored in substantial conventionality while mixing in a left tail of rarely seen combinations.
- The authors suggest that the pattern may reflect advantages of being within the mainstream of a research trajectory, where scientists are focused, while remaining distinctive in creativity.
- This gives a mechanism for why high novelty alone and high conventionality alone can underperform their joint combination.

## Caveats

- The mechanism is inferred from reference-combination and impact patterns, not directly observed audience cognition.
- Mainstream grounding can become conformity if it lacks a distinctive component.
- Field maturity and journal granularity can affect both conventionality and apparent distinctiveness.
