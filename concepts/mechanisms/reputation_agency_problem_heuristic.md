# Reputation agency-problem heuristic

## Summary

Reputation agency-problem heuristic is the mechanism motif that reputation serves as a shortcut for evaluating scientific work when quality, effort, and future value are only partly observable.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, author, evaluator, attention allocation, peer-review setting, or search interface.
- Typical representation: reputation cue, information asymmetry, status signal, quality heuristic, or agency problem.
- Mechanism target: explain why evaluator attention can attach to authors as well as papers.
- Empirical signature: reputation has stronger effects in settings where direct quality assessment is costly or incomplete.

## Uses in Science of Science

## Operationalization

- Compare reputation-cue effects in information-poor and information-rich evaluation settings.
- Use blinded, partially blinded, or information-reduced designs where available.
- Interact author reputation with paper age, venue, field, and availability of independent quality signals.
- Distinguish reputation as information from reputation as bias.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Petersen et al. (2014) describes reputation as a social construct that helps evaluation under incomplete systemic information.
- The paper frames reputation signaling as a response to agency problems and information asymmetries in scientific reward systems.
- The empirical model then estimates how author reputation modifies citation rates for papers at different citation levels.

## Caveats

- Reputation can encode accumulated evidence of quality as well as status bias.
- Citation outcomes are only one expression of the heuristic.
- Direct quality information, field norms, and venue selection can mediate or confound observed reputation effects.
