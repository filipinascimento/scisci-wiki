# Community self-filtering into generalist venues

## Summary

Fields may differ in whether their communities reserve broad elite venues for exceptional work, changing observed new-PI shares without directly observing rejected submissions.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, journal, submission culture, or accepted-paper pool.
- Typical representation: accepted-paper category mix interpreted against field norms and venue prestige.
- Mechanism: community norms and self-selection shape who attempts broad high-impact venues.
- Empirical signature: low new-PI share in a venue may reflect selective submission behavior as well as editorial gatekeeping.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a community-level mechanism to [venue chaperone effect](venue_chaperone_effect.md).
- Connects accepted-publication patterns to [peer review gatekeeping](peer_review_gatekeeping.md) and elite venue signaling.
- Useful when submission data are missing but field-specific publication cultures differ strongly.

## Operationalization

- Compare accepted-paper category mixes with any available submission pools, topic extremity, impact, and field norms.
- Where rejected submissions are unavailable, state that the estimate concerns accepted publications rather than attempts.
- Look for field differences in whether broad elite venues are treated as exceptional outlets.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sekara et al. (2018) discusses NEJM and high-impact venue patterns with possible field-level explanations for new-PI access.
- The paper frames community behavior as a possible explanation rather than directly measuring submission self-selection.

## Caveats

- Without submissions, self-filtering and editorial selection are difficult to separate.
- The same observed pattern can reflect reputation, topic fit, mentorship, or review selectivity.
