# Self-serving analytic ambiguity

## Summary

Self-serving analytic ambiguity is the mechanism by which ambiguous but defensible analytic choices are interpreted in ways that favor statistically significant results without requiring deliberate fraud.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: analytic decision, researcher, ambiguous preprocessing rule, covariate choice, exclusion rule, outcome transformation, or reporting choice.
- Typical representation: ambiguity-plus-motivation mechanism, justifiable-choice path, motivated reasoning audit, or degrees-of-freedom decision tree.
- Mechanism: researchers facing multiple plausible analysis choices can sincerely conclude that the choice yielding significance is the appropriate one.
- Empirical signature: unpublished analytic alternatives exist, the selected path is defensible, and the chosen path aligns with the desired statistically significant result.

## Operationalization

- Identify analytic decisions with multiple defensible options.
- Record whether decisions were specified before data inspection.
- Compare reported results with alternative justified specifications.
- Audit whether the selected path is consistently significance-favoring across studies.
- Encourage disclosure or preregistration rather than assuming malicious intent.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Simmons et al. (2011) attributes false-positive flexibility to ambiguity in analytic decisions and researchers' desire to find statistically significant results.
- The paper argues that people are self-serving in interpreting ambiguous information and adept at reaching justifiable conclusions aligned with their desires.
- Simmons et al. give reaction-time outlier handling as an example where many defensible but inconsistent choices exist.
- The authors emphasize that the behavior is not necessarily malicious but can still inflate false-positive findings.

## Caveats

- Self-serving ambiguity is hard to prove in individual cases without preregistration, audit trails, or disclosed analytic alternatives.
- Some flexible choices are scientifically justified and improve measurement.
- The mechanism can coexist with deliberate misconduct, but it does not require it.
- Transparency can expose flexibility while still leaving judgment about which analysis is substantively best.
