# Preliminary-evidence lock-in

## Summary

Review demands for preliminary results can push researchers toward already-developed, safer projects.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: proposal, preliminary result, resubmission, project maturity, topic continuity, or review criterion.
- Typical representation: proposal-maturity measure tracking preliminary evidence and continuity with prior work.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: safe-project bias induced by preliminary-data requirements.
- Empirical signature: higher preliminary-evidence expectations make less-developed exploratory ideas harder to propose..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines proposal-risk mechanism by linking it to [peer review gatekeeping](peer_review_gatekeeping.md) and [nih study section review process](../methods/nih_study_section_review_process.md).
- Useful as a reusable distinction when [proposal burden research time crowding](proposal_burden_research_time_crowding.md) is analyzed in science-of-science data.
- Adds cross-links to [novelty underinvestment selection pathway](../consequences/novelty_underinvestment_selection_pathway.md) so the motif is not read as an isolated result.

## Operationalization

- Measure preliminary-data requirements, resubmission counts, proposal maturity, and topic continuity from prior work.
- Compare applications requiring extensive preliminary data with programs that fund investigators or broad agendas.
- Track whether proposal text narrows toward already feasible lines over revision cycles.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Azoulay et al. (2009) links NIH study-section risk aversion and preliminary-evidence demands to safer avenues and less exploratory research.
- The motif provides a concrete pathway from review procedure to project lock-in.

## Caveats

- The evidence is partly institutional description and researcher complaint rather than a standalone causal estimate.
- Preliminary evidence can also improve feasibility and reduce waste.
