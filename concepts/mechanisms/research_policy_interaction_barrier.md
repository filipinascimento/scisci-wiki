# Research-policy interaction barrier

## Summary

Research-policy interaction barrier is the mechanism by which weak contact between researchers and policymakers reduces observed policy use or formal policy-document mentions of scientific work.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: researcher, policymaker, boundary organization, policy document, field, topic, or evidence pathway.
- Typical representation: low mention rate, interview evidence, missing citation pathway, weak network tie, or absent boundary-spanning activity.
- Mechanism: scientific evidence may not enter policy documents because actors are disconnected, use different communication formats, or work on different timelines.
- Empirical signature: policy-relevant research has low tracked policy-document mention rates even after accounting for source-list and DOI coverage.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a non-metadata explanation for low [policy mention coverage rate](../measures/policy_mention_coverage_rate.md).
- Complements [policy citation-style omission](../validations/policy_citation_style_omission.md), which focuses on citation practices inside policy documents.
- Links [public and policy attention](public_policy_attention.md) to mechanisms of knowledge transfer and boundary work.
- Warns against interpreting non-mentions as no societal value when researcher-policy interaction is weak or indirect.

## Operationalization

- Pair policy-mention data with surveys, interviews, collaboration records, advisory roles, and case studies.
- Distinguish source-coverage failures from genuine weak interaction between research and policy communities.
- Inspect whether papers influence intermediaries, guidelines, reports, or professional bodies that may not be tracked as policy sources.
- Compare fields with similar source coverage but different boundary-organization and policy-network structures.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Haunschild and Bornmann (2017) lists barriers and low interaction levels between researchers and policymakers as a possible reason for low policy-document mention coverage.
- The paper also notes that policy documents may draw on broad bodies of work without formally mentioning every paper, so weak observed mention rates can reflect interaction and communication structures rather than absence of relevance.
- This mechanism should therefore be tested with policy-process evidence rather than inferred from altmetric zeros alone.

## Caveats

- Low interaction is one possible explanation among several, including source coverage, DOI denominator loss, non-scholarly citation styles, and true lack of policy relevance.
- Interaction can be indirect through reports, professional associations, or boundary organizations that are not visible in paper-level policy mentions.
- Evidence of interaction does not guarantee policy influence; it only narrows the path from research to policy attention.
