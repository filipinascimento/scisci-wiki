# Assessment-scale threshold for metrics

## Summary

Assessment-scale threshold for metrics is the principle that quantitative indicators become most defensible when the evaluation task is too large for direct expert reading, while individual papers or small portfolios require qualitative assessment.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: evaluation task, paper set, journal set, portfolio, or assessment system.
- Typical representation: scale threshold, infeasibility boundary, or quantitative-assessment trigger.
- Mechanism target: the shift from expert reading to indicators as the number of objects grows.
- Empirical signature: metric use is justified by scope, but article-level judgments remain unreliable when projected from aggregate indicators.

## Operationalization

- Define the number of papers, journals, people, or institutions being assessed.
- Identify whether careful reading or specialist consultation is feasible at that scale.
- Use metrics for screening, mapping, or large-scale comparison only when the unit and scale match.
- Require qualitative review when a decision concerns an individual paper or small portfolio.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from West, Bergstrom, and Bergstrom (2010) contrasts careful reading of an individual article with the need for quantitative tools when evaluating large collections.
- The same paper warns that aggregate journal-level metrics are poorly suited to individual-paper judgments.
- Its note on skewed within-journal citation distributions gives the empirical reason why journal averages fail at small assessment scales.

## Caveats

- Large scale does not make any metric valid by itself.
- Small-scale qualitative assessment can still be biased or inconsistent.
- The scale threshold should be paired with construct validity, transparency, and error auditing.
