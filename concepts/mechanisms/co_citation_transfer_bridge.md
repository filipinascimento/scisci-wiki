# Co-citation transfer bridge

## Summary

A strong co-citation tie can mark a transfer bridge where one work applies another's idea, formula, or method into a new theoretical setting.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: high co-citation document pair and its citation contexts.
- Typical representation: co-citation edge annotated as method transfer, theoretical extension, shared problem, or background canon.
- Mechanism: later citing work binds two prior works because one transfers or repurposes the other.
- Empirical signature: high co-citation with a readable context showing applied or theoretical transfer.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Turns [co-citation edge-context interpretation](../methods/co_citation_edge_context_interpretation.md) into a mechanism label.
- Useful for edges with high co-citation but low bibliographic coupling.
- Connects co-citation maps to [research fronts](research_fronts.md) and citation-context functions.

## Operationalization

- Identify high co-citation and low bibliographic-coupling pairs.
- Read citing contexts or full text to code whether the relation is method transfer, theory extension, shared problem, controversy, or canon packaging.
- Use multiple coders or examples for high-stakes mechanism claims.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Small (1973) explains a strong Lovelace/Veneziano tie by noting that Lovelace used the Veneziano formula in an unexpected theoretical direction.
- The explanation supports transfer as a specific relation behind a co-citation edge.

## Caveats

- Bridge coding requires domain reading and can be hard to scale automatically.
- Strong co-citation can also reflect review packaging or shared canonical status.
