# Novel-positive-clean result incentive

## Summary

Novel-positive-clean result incentive is the reward mechanism in which career, editorial, and attention systems favor findings that look new, statistically positive, and narratively tidy, even though the underlying research process often produces null, mixed, or ambiguous outcomes.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: manuscript, result, hypothesis test, journal decision, grant review, hiring case, or promotion dossier.
- Typical representation: reward target portfolio, publication-selection model, result-direction audit, novelty filter, or narrative-cleanliness code.
- Mechanism: incentives make positive and clean claims more valuable to scientists than equally informative null, redundant, or exception-rich evidence.
- Empirical signature: overrepresentation of positive claims, reluctance to report failed or mixed tests, paper narratives that hide uncertainty, and stronger visibility for apparently novel findings.

## Operationalization

- Code manuscripts or grant outputs by result direction, novelty claim, and narrative complexity.
- Compare visibility, publication probability, citation, or reward outcomes for positive/clean claims versus null, mixed, replication, or exception-rich claims.
- Audit whether preregistered null or ambiguous outcomes disappear from final reports.
- Measure institutional incentives that explicitly or implicitly reward high-impact, surprising, or positive findings.
- Treat novelty, positivity, and narrative cleanliness as distinct dimensions rather than one generic publication-bias label.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Nosek et al. (2018) describes a reward pattern in which novel results, positive results, and clean stories are valued more than redundant, negative, uncertain, or exception-rich findings.
- Nosek et al. argue that these valued outcomes are rare in ordinary empirical work, creating a conflict between incentives for publishability and incentives for accuracy.
- The same full text connects the conflict to selective analysis, rationalization, and reporting when the boundary between prediction and postdiction is not clear.
- The mechanism helps interpret why preregistration and registered reports try to move evaluation earlier, toward question and design quality rather than the appeal of the realized result.

## Caveats

- Novel, positive, and clean findings are not automatically unreliable; the risk comes from systematic selection on these attributes.
- Incentives can operate through journals, funders, hiring committees, informal prestige, or researcher self-selection.
- The mechanism overlaps with publication bias but is useful as a separate concept because it names the reward targets that make selective reporting attractive.
