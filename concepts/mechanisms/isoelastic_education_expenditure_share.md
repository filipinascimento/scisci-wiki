# Isoelastic education expenditure share

## Summary

In Jones's model, optimal education cost is a constant fraction of innovator lifetime income because breadth has isoelastic benefits and costs.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: innovator, education cost, lifetime income, breadth parameter, or model equilibrium.
- Typical representation: theoretical education-to-income ratio from an isoelastic breadth-choice model.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: model mechanism behind cross-field entry-age invariance.
- Empirical signature: optimal education expenditure remains a constant income share under the model's isoelastic assumptions.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines knowledge-burden theory by linking it to [cross field age invariance puzzle](../validations/cross_field_age_invariance_puzzle.md) and [application area crowding arbitrage](application_area_crowding_arbitrage.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [breadth productivity tradeoff](breadth_productivity_tradeoff.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [knowledge breadth idea arrival function](../methods/knowledge_breadth_idea_arrival_function.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Model education cost relative to expected innovator income.
- Estimate or simulate comparative statics for breadth cost elasticity and breadth productivity elasticity.
- Use the ratio to interpret why cross-field first-invention age may remain similar despite field depth differences.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Jones (2009) derives a constant education-to-income ratio from the breadth first-order condition and links it to Cobb-Douglas-style expenditure shares.
- The result helps explain cross-sectional education equalization in the model.

## Caveats

- The mechanism is model-specific and sensitive to the isoelastic functional-form assumption.
- Empirical education costs and innovator incomes are difficult to measure directly.
