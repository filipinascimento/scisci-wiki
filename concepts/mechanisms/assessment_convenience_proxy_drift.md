# Assessment convenience-proxy drift

## Summary

Assessment convenience-proxy drift is the mechanism by which time-pressed committees substitute easy-to-count indicators for harder-to-observe qualities such as rigor, openness, mentoring, and societal value.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: committee, candidate dossier, evaluation criterion, quantitative proxy, promotion rule, or institutional assessment system.
- Typical representation: proxy substitution pathway from local decision constraints to metric-heavy assessment.
- Mechanism target: convenience changes what is rewarded, even when committees endorse broader values.
- Empirical signature: assessment practice emphasizes publication counts, funding, citations, or journal prestige because they are readily available.

## Operationalization

- Compare stated assessment values with the evidence actually requested in dossiers and committee rubrics.
- Identify which criteria are easy to count and which require qualitative or portfolio review.
- Audit time, staffing, training, and data constraints that make proxy substitution likely.
- Track whether proxy-heavy criteria displace openness, complete reporting, mentoring, or societal contribution.
- Redesign dossiers to make valued but harder-to-count contributions visible.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Moher et al. (2018) states that researcher assessment often focuses on what is easily determined and measurable.
- The paper argues that current systems often emphasize publication counts, grant income, citation counts, and journal impact factor while underweighting responsible practices and societal contribution.
- This motif treats convenience as a mechanism linking committee constraints to misaligned incentives.

## Caveats

- Quantitative indicators can be useful when validated and interpreted in context.
- Convenience-proxy drift can occur even without bad intent.
- Portfolio approaches can create new burdens or new proxies unless implementation is audited.
