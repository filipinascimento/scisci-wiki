# Large-team resource treadmill

## Summary

Large teams may require continuous funding, staffing, and reputational success, making failure more costly and encouraging developmental work with nearer-term payoff.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: large team, lab, grant portfolio, or infrastructure project.
- Typical representation: resource-dependence proxy by team size and disruption.
- Mechanism: ongoing costs and reputational stakes push large teams toward safer developmental opportunities.
- Empirical signature: resource-intensive teams favor recent, popular, or lower-disruption references after controls.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Links [team size and disruption](team_size_disruption.md) to funding and institutional maintenance pressures.
- Connects team scale to [funding incentives and exploration](funding_incentives_and_exploration.md).
- Useful for studying big-science infrastructure and large-lab topic choice.

## Operationalization

- Use funding volume, renewal dependence, infrastructure cost, staff count, lab size, and grant duration as resource-treadmill proxies.
- Test whether these proxies predict recent-reference use, lower disruption, or safer topic choice.
- Compare teams with similar size but different funding or infrastructure dependence.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wu et al. (2019) argues that large teams need ongoing funding and success to pay the bills and are more sensitive to reputation and support losses.
- This provides a resource-dependence mechanism for developmental orientation.

## Caveats

- Resource dependence can reflect genuinely large-scale tasks, not only conservatism.
- Funding data are often incomplete and may be endogenous to project ambition.
