# Same-cohort productivity equivalence

## Summary

In equilibrium, innovators from the same cohort contribute at the same expected productivity rate across application areas despite different field opportunities and burdens.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: innovator cohort, application area, expected income, education choice, or productivity contribution.
- Typical representation: equilibrium parity condition across application areas for the same cohort.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: allocation equilibrium in the knowledge-burden model.
- Empirical signature: expected gross innovation income and productivity contribution equalize across application areas within a cohort.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines knowledge-burden theory by linking it to [isoelastic education expenditure share](isoelastic_education_expenditure_share.md) and [application area crowding arbitrage](application_area_crowding_arbitrage.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [knowledge breadth idea arrival function](../methods/knowledge_breadth_idea_arrival_function.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [cohort productivity contribution aggregation](../methods/cohort_productivity_contribution_aggregation.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Build cohort-by-field fixed-effect tests of inventor output or income where data allow.
- Compare field-adjusted productivity contributions within the same cohort.
- Use the condition as a theoretical benchmark for interpreting field crowding and opportunity arbitrage.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Jones (2009) derives that equal income and equal education within a cohort imply equivalent expected gross innovation income and productivity contribution across application areas.
- The result is an equilibrium property of the theoretical model.

## Caveats

- The motif is theoretical and not directly estimated in the paper.
- Real fields can have frictions, rents, and institutional barriers that prevent equalization.
