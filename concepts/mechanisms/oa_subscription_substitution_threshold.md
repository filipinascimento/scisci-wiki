# OA subscription substitution threshold

## Summary

Once enough reader-demand literature is legally OA, libraries may gain bargaining or cancellation leverage over subscription packages.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: library, subscription package, article demand event, OA availability, or cancellation decision.
- Typical representation: share of demanded literature legally available outside subscriptions.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: threshold where OA availability substitutes for subscription access.
- Empirical signature: higher OA availability in usage logs predicts package cancellation, bargaining leverage, or lower OA-adjusted cost per access..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects [OA-adjusted cost per access](../measures/oa_adjusted_cost_per_access.md) to subscription decision models.
- Useful for studying Big Deal cancellation and library access strategy.
- Links reader-experienced OA to institutional purchasing decisions.

## Operationalization

- Combine institutional usage logs, package holdings, OA availability, resolver success, and cancellation outcomes.
- Estimate substitution thresholds by field, publisher, and user group.
- Distinguish frictionless legal access from merely available but hard-to-find copies.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Piwowar et al. (2018) argues that roughly half of reader-interest literature being OA may affect Big Deal cancellation decisions and discusses OA-adjusted cost per access.
- This frames reader-demand OA availability as a possible subscription substitute.

## Caveats

- Availability is not the same as frictionless access, preservation, license rights, or faculty tolerance for loss.
- Cancellation decisions also depend on budgets, negotiations, and campus politics.
