# Proposal-abundance evaluation bottleneck

## Summary

Proposal-abundance evaluation bottleneck is the mechanism by which large pools of ideas or applications make expert filtering scarce, consequential, and vulnerable to imperfect review.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: proposal pool, grant program, crowdsourcing platform, reviewer panel, funding decision, or idea pipeline.
- Typical representation: number of submitted ideas, reviewer capacity, review load, funding rate, evaluation time, and filtering stage.
- Mechanism: when proposal supply grows faster than evaluative capacity, institutions rely on compressed expert judgment, scoring heuristics, triage, or automated filters.
- Empirical signature: many proposals compete for limited funding or attention, making small review differences consequential for downstream research lines.

## Operationalization

- Record proposal volume, reviewer counts, reviews per proposal, reviews per reviewer, funding rates, and triage stages.
- Estimate how reviewer load changes score reliability, variance, and reviewer agreement.
- Compare manual review with staged triage, lotteries, prediction support, or portfolio rules.
- Track whether bottleneck pressure increases reliance on novelty penalties, prestige cues, or standardized metrics.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Boudreau, Guinan, Lakhani, and Riedl (2016) opens by framing project selection as a major managerial and scientific challenge.
- The paper notes that NIH and NSF evaluate more than 89,000 research applications annually with tens of thousands of experts.
- It also links the challenge to ideation platforms, contests, crowdsourcing, and crowdfunding, which generate large numbers of proposals requiring selection.
- Boudreau et al. use this bottleneck context to motivate why intellectual distance, novelty, and expert-evaluation biases matter for resource allocation.

## Caveats

- High proposal volume does not by itself imply poor review; some systems may scale reviewer pools or stage screening effectively.
- Bottleneck pressure can be reduced by better program design, pre-registration of criteria, lotteries near thresholds, or portfolio allocation.
- Automated filters can relieve load but introduce feedback loops and fairness problems.
