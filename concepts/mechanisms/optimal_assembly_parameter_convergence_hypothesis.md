# Optimal assembly-parameter convergence hypothesis

## Summary

Optimal assembly-parameter convergence hypothesis is the proposition that different creative or scientific fields may converge toward a similar high-performing region of team-assembly parameters.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, journal, creative domain, team-assembly parameter set, or performance tier.
- Typical representation: domains located in a shared parameter space with high-performing cases near a common region.
- Mechanism: successful fields balance incumbent expertise, newcomer injection, and repeat collaboration in ways that support both coordination and novelty.
- Empirical signature: high-performing journals or fields cluster near similar values of incumbent participation and repeat-collaboration propensity.

## Operationalization

- Estimate team-assembly parameters for multiple fields, journals, or creative domains.
- Attach a performance proxy such as journal impact, awards, citations, or expert ranking.
- Test whether high-performing units occupy a common region of the parameter space.
- Identify exceptions where field-specific production constraints shift the apparent optimum.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Guimera et al. (2005) compares Broadway musicals and scientific journals in the same team-assembly parameter space.
- The paper reports that higher-performing scientific journals tend to combine incumbents and newcomers while avoiding excessive repeat collaboration.
- It suggests that several creative fields may be organized by similar expertise-diversity tradeoffs, while noting exceptions such as astronomy.

## Caveats

- Convergence can be induced by shared measurement choices or common publication norms.
- Field-specific equipment, collaboration cost, author-order conventions, and team-size constraints can move the optimum.
- Performance proxies such as impact factor may not capture all dimensions of field success.
