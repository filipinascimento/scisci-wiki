# Conventional Display Strategy

## Summary

Conventional display strategy is the practice of presenting novel ideas through familiar frames, examples, or reference combinations so that audiences can recognize and evaluate them.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, argument, invention, claim, or presentation strategy.
- Typical representation: strategic conventional wrapper around atypical content.
- Mechanism: familiar frames lower communication costs and help audiences absorb unconventional combinations.
- Empirical signature: high novelty is paired with high conventionality, familiar analogies, or established reference anchors.

## Operationalization

- Identify papers with atypical tail combinations and high median conventionality.
- Code whether introductions, reference lists, or venue choices emphasize familiar frames.
- Compare impact, review success, or diffusion speed for novel work with versus without conventional anchors.
- Distinguish strategic display from genuinely conventional problem choice.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Uzzi et al. (2013) says scientists may intentionally display conventionality when communicating novel ideas.
- The paper uses Newton and Darwin as examples of presenting new insights through accepted frames.
- Uzzi et al. generalize the mechanism beyond science with examples of innovations that retain familiar design cues.

## Caveats

- Conventional display can obscure the novelty of an underlying contribution.
- The strategy may be unavailable for ideas that lack accepted anchors.
- It can be confused with low novelty unless the atypical content is measured separately.
