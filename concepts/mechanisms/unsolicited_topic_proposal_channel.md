# Unsolicited-topic proposal channel

## Summary

Investigator-initiated proposal entry gives researchers a bottom-up route for topic choice alongside targeted funder requests.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: grant application, solicitation, request for proposals, investigator-initiated topic, institute priority, or research line.
- Typical representation: proposal-entry channel distinguishing solicited and unsolicited scientific topics.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: bottom-up topic selection in grant-funded science.
- Empirical signature: researchers can submit open-topic proposals even when funders also issue targeted priority calls..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines grant topic choice by linking it to [scientific problem choice](scientific_problem_choice.md) and [funding incentives and exploration](funding_incentives_and_exploration.md).
- Useful as a reusable distinction when [funding autonomy exploration](funding_autonomy_exploration.md) is analyzed in science-of-science data.
- Adds cross-links to [proposal commitment creativity constraint](proposal_commitment_creativity_constraint.md) so the motif is not read as an isolated result.

## Operationalization

- Classify applications by solicited priority-area calls versus unsolicited investigator-initiated submissions.
- Compare topic novelty, field entry, and funding odds across proposal-entry channels.
- Model funder steering separately from researcher-initiated topic choice.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Li and Agha (2015) notes that NIH requests can identify priorities but investigators may also submit unsolicited topics.
- The motif connects peer-review designs to scientific problem-choice channels.

## Caveats

- Li and Agha do not estimate separate effects for solicited and unsolicited applications.
- Unsolicited proposals can still be shaped by implicit funder priorities and review expectations.
