# Generalist-Journal Policy Visibility

## Summary

Generalist-journal policy visibility is the mechanism by which papers in broad, prestigious journals become more visible and usable to policy-document authors.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: journal, policy-mentioned paper, topic corpus, or policy document.
- Typical representation: overrepresentation of generalist high-prestige journals among policy-mentioned papers.
- Mechanism: multidisciplinary scope, general framing, and journal visibility increase the chance that policy authors encounter and cite a paper.
- Empirical signature: journals such as *Nature* and *Science* are disproportionately represented among policy-mentioned papers.

## Operationalization

- Compare journal distributions for policy-mentioned papers and the base topic corpus.
- Control for paper age, citation impact, document type, and topic.
- Test whether generalist journal visibility predicts policy mentions independently of scholarly citations.
- Inspect policy contexts to distinguish journal visibility from content relevance.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Bornmann et al. (2016) reports that *Nature* and *Science* are comparatively often mentioned by policy documents.
- The paper attributes this to their multidisciplinary orientation and the general character of many papers.
- Bornmann et al. also report that several of the most policy-mentioned climate-change papers were published in these journals.

## Caveats

- Journal effects can be confounded with article quality, topic importance, press coverage, and review status.
- Generalist visibility does not imply greater policy relevance or quality.
- The mechanism may vary by policy domain and language coverage.
