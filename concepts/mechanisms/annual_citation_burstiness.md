# Annual citation burstiness

## Summary

Annual citation burstiness is the mechanism that heavy citation to a paper can arrive in irregular yearly bursts, making single-year citation incidence an unstable proxy for durable influence.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, citation year, annual citation count, citation burst, or citation trajectory.
- Typical representation: year-to-year volatility in received citations, burst flags, or deviation from smooth aging curves.
- Mechanism: attention can concentrate temporarily around methods, controversies, reviews, discoveries, or research-front activity.
- Empirical signature: papers heavily cited in one year are not necessarily heavily cited in adjacent years.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a temporal caveat to [annual citation-incidence distribution](../measures/annual_citation_incidence_distribution.md).
- Motivates longitudinal [citation trajectory models](../methods/citation_trajectory_models.md) rather than single-year impact snapshots.
- Connects citation counting to [citation aging and obsolescence](citation_aging_obsolescence.md), [sleeping beauty](sleeping_beauty.md), and [delayed recognition](delayed_recognition.md).
- Helps interpret early citation-alert systems and research-front monitoring.

## Operationalization

- Build annual citation histories for paper cohorts.
- Measure burstiness with year-to-year variance, maximum-to-mean ratios, burst detection, or deviations from fitted aging curves.
- Compare burstiness across fields, document types, citation tails, and research-front membership.
- Separate short bursts from durable high-citation plateaus and delayed-recognition awakenings.
- Avoid classifying classics from a single annual citation spike without follow-up.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Price (1965) notes that annual citation counts for individual cited papers vary from year to year.
- Price states that heavy citation appears in capricious bursts, while still conjecturing population-level statistical regularity.
- The paper uses this observation to caution that a paper not cited in one year may be cited in the next, and a heavily cited paper may not remain heavily cited.
- Price links the issue to the possibility of identifying classics and superclassics automatically from citation-index procedures, but calls for more work on persistence.

## Caveats

- Burstiness can reflect measurement windows, database updates, review articles, controversies, or source expansion rather than sudden intellectual importance.
- Some papers have genuine long-run plateaus rather than bursts.
- Annual counts should be paired with citation-context or field-front evidence before interpreting the source of a burst.
