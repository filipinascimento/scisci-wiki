# Online search-browse substitution

## Summary

Online search-browse substitution is the mechanism by which researchers replace print browsing, table-of-contents scanning, and local shelf perusal with keyword search across large digital archives.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: researcher, search session, paper, journal-year, archive interface, or field.
- Typical representation: shift in discovery mode from browsing to search and link following.
- Mechanism: search interfaces make targeted retrieval efficient but reduce incidental exposure to adjacent, older, or weakly indexed work.
- Empirical signature: deeper digital availability coincides with more recent references, fewer distinct cited sources, and stronger concentration.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Splits the behavioral interface mechanism out of [electronic access citation narrowing](electronic_access_citation_narrowing.md).
- Gives a mechanism for [knowledge-use narrowing](knowledge_use_narrowing.md) that does not require the total knowledge stock to shrink.
- Connects digital-library design, search ranking, and citation behavior.
- Provides the behavioral route through which [search-result reputation reinforcement](search_result_reputation_reinforcement.md) can affect attention.
- Splits into interface and archive-representation motifs for [search-first interface affordance](search_first_interface_affordance.md) and [undifferentiated archive search](../representations/undifferentiated_archive_search.md).
- Motivates interface experiments that compare search-first, browse-first, and mixed discovery defaults.

## Operationalization

- Measure search and browsing behavior with platform logs, clickstreams, download paths, reference-manager traces, surveys, or experimental interface assignments.
- Compare reference age, source breadth, citation concentration, and topic diversity across access modes.
- Separate keyword search, within-journal browsing, table-of-contents scanning, citation following, and recommendation-system exposure.
- In bibliometric panels, use archive availability as a proxy for the broader shift from print browsing toward online search.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Evans (2008) reports that the percentage of papers read as a result of browsing had dropped and been replaced by online searches, especially for productive scientists and scholars.
- Evans contrasts print holdings, where table-of-contents lists and physical arrangement encourage browsing, with online archives where searching the full archive is prominent and more frequently used.
- The paper uses citation-index and Fulltext Sources Online data to show that as journal issues came online, researchers cited more recent articles, fewer journals and articles, and more concentrated sources.
- Evans interprets this as a shift from browsing in print to searching online, allowing researchers to avoid older and marginally related literature.

## Caveats

- Search can broaden discovery when ranking, query expansion, and recommendation tools intentionally expose diverse or older work.
- Availability is a proxy for behavior unless paired with logs or direct observation.
- Browsing and searching coexist, so the relevant contrast is often the share of discovery routed through each mode.
