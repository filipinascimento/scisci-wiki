# Scientific visibility destabilizing patent portfolios

## Summary

Scientific visibility destabilizing patent portfolios is the mechanism that university article impact, not only publication volume, can be associated with patent portfolios that destabilize existing technology streams.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: university, article portfolio, patent portfolio, citation impact, or disruption score.
- Typical representation: university-level regression, article-count and article-citation covariates, average CD, impact-scaled CD, or destabilizing citation totals.
- Mechanism: visible research capacity may expose technology-transfer pipelines to more frontier knowledge and increase the chance of trajectory-breaking patents.
- Empirical signature: article impact measures predict destabilizing patent portfolio measures after accounting for patenting volume and other institutional covariates.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a science-capacity channel to [university patent portfolio disruption](../measures/university_patent_portfolio_disruption.md).
- Separates article volume from [citation impact indicators](../measures/citation_impact_indicators.md) when explaining downstream technological change.
- Complements [federal funding destabilizing patents](federal_funding_destabilizing_patents.md), because funding and publication visibility are different institutional inputs.
- Provides a translational case where scientific attention can be tied to technological direction, not only scholarly visibility.

## Operationalization

- Build institutional article and patent portfolios over aligned time windows.
- Measure article volume and citation impact separately.
- Aggregate patent disruption with average CD, impact-scaled CD, or destabilizing and consolidating citation totals.
- Include institutional and technology-class controls when comparing universities.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Funk and Owen-Smith (2017) models university patent portfolios alongside university publication and citation indicators.
- The paper distinguishes the predictive role of article counts from article impact when explaining destabilizing patent portfolios.
- The result supports treating scientific visibility as a possible source of technological trajectory change rather than only as an evaluation signal.
- This motif is distinct from portfolio disruption measurement because it concerns a hypothesized institutional input to those portfolio outcomes.

## Caveats

- University-level publication impact can proxy for resources, prestige, field mix, or lab scale.
- Patent portfolio disruption may reflect technology-transfer strategy rather than only scientific visibility.
- Institutional aggregation can hide department-level or inventor-level mechanisms.
