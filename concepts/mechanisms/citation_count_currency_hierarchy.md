# Citation-count currency hierarchy

## Summary

Citation-count currency hierarchy is the mechanism by which treating citations as the dominant currency of scientific value creates rank hierarchies, rich-get-richer feedback, and pressure toward already visible ideas.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, researcher, institution, field, metric system, evaluation rule, or attention market.
- Typical representation: citation-ranked hierarchy, citation-count reward signal, evaluation currency, or feedback loop from citations to resources and visibility.
- Mechanism target: how one-dimensional citation valuation shapes search, evaluation, and diffusion of ideas.
- Empirical signature: citation-rich papers, authors, or paradigms receive more attention and resources, while new or low-status ideas face diffusion barriers.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects [citation impact indicators](../measures/citation_impact_indicators.md) to [Matthew effect](matthew_effect.md), [preferential attachment](preferential_attachment.md), and [cumulative advantage](cumulative_advantage.md).
- Provides the problem statement that motivates [metric pluralism and productive specialization](metric_pluralism_productive_specialization.md).
- Helps explain why [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) should treat citations as partial attention signals rather than a universal quality currency.
- Supports policy audits of whether citation-ranked funding, hiring, recommendation, or dashboard systems suppress new ideas from junior scientists or paradigm outsiders.

## Operationalization

- Identify places where citation counts or citation-derived ranks drive search, funding, hiring, promotion, journal prestige, or institutional resource allocation.
- Estimate whether citation-rich entities receive disproportionate later visibility, resources, or opportunities after relevant controls.
- Compare one-dimensional citation rankings with plural contribution profiles, novelty indicators, replication value, data sharing, peer support, and societal-use measures.
- Audit subgroup and topic effects: junior scientists, lower-prestige institutions, interdisciplinary work, and nonmainstream paradigms may be most exposed to hierarchy lock-in.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) describes science as often behaving like an economic system with a one-dimensional currency of citation counts.
- The review states that this currency creates a hierarchical system in which rich-get-richer dynamics suppress the spread of new ideas.
- Fortunato et al. identify ideas from junior scientists and ideas outside field-supported paradigms as especially vulnerable to this suppression.
- The same passage argues for broadening performance indicators and modeling what each indicator captures to avoid misuse.

## Caveats

- Citations are useful traces of attention and influence; the problem is treating them as a complete currency.
- Citation hierarchies can reflect quality, visibility, field size, age, social status, and database coverage simultaneously.
- Replacing one citation metric with a composite score can reproduce the same hierarchy if the composite is not theory-grounded.
