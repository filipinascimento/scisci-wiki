# Author preemptive journal self-citation

## Summary

Author preemptive journal self-citation is the mechanism in which authors add recent citations to the target journal before submission because they expect those citations to improve editorial reception.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: submitting author, target journal, reference list, editorial review process, or journal-year citation trace.
- Typical representation: added target-journal references, survey reports, submission-version comparison, or unusually recent same-journal citation concentration.
- Mechanism: authors adapt reference lists to perceived or anticipated editorial incentives tied to journal self-citation and impact-factor improvement.
- Empirical signature: manuscripts submitted to a journal include extra recent citations to that journal, especially to papers in the impact-factor window, without corresponding intellectual need.

- Complements [coercive-citation survey linkage](../validations/coercive_citation_survey_linkage.md), where author reports reveal pressure or perceived pressure to add journal-specific citations.

## Operationalization

- Compare submitted, revised, and accepted reference lists when version histories are available.
- Identify references added after editorial interaction or immediately before submission that cite recent papers from the target journal.
- Combine bibliometric screens such as IFBSCP with survey, correspondence, or manuscript-history evidence.
- Treat the mechanism as suspected only when the added citations lack topical relevance or cluster around the impact-factor window.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Chorus and Waltman (2016) states that authors may strategically add references to recent papers in the target journal to improve the chance of surviving editorial review.
- The paper also notes that authors may respond to anticipated coercive citation practices, so the behavior can occur even before an explicit editorial request.
- Chorus and Waltman connect this mechanism to high IFBSCP values but warn that citation traces alone do not prove unethical behavior.

## Caveats

- Added same-journal citations can be legitimate when recent papers are substantively relevant.
- Citation traces rarely reveal whether a citation was author-initiated, editor-requested, or independently necessary.
- Surveys and manuscript-version evidence may be biased toward reported or remembered coercion cases.
