# Continuous delayed-recognition spectrum

## Summary

The continuous delayed-recognition spectrum is the finding that sleeping beauties are not a separate rare class but the high end of a broad distribution of delayed citation recognition.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, beauty coefficient, citation history, field cohort, or database.
- Typical representation: survival distribution of beauty coefficients, ranked delayed-recognition scores, or heavy-tailed citation-trajectory spectrum.
- Mechanism: delayed recognition varies by degree because papers differ continuously in sleep duration, awakening intensity, field readiness, and cross-disciplinary rediscovery.
- Empirical signature: no natural cutoff separates ordinary papers from sleeping beauties.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Reframes [sleeping beauty](sleeping_beauty.md) from an exceptional anecdote into a continuous citation-dynamics motif.
- Warns against hard thresholds in [sleeping-beauty detection](../methods/sleeping_beauty_detection.md).
- Links delayed recognition to [citation aging and obsolescence](citation_aging_obsolescence.md), [citation window selection](../methods/citation_window_selection.md), and [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md).

## Operationalization

- Compute [beauty coefficient](../measures/beauty_coefficient.md) for a broad paper corpus.
- Plot the survival distribution across all papers, preferably by dataset, field, and publication cohort.
- Check whether a natural mode or cutoff separates sleeping beauties from other trajectories.
- Treat high-score cases as extreme points on a continuum unless additional historical evidence supports a discrete class.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ke et al. (2015) reports heterogeneous but continuous beauty-coefficient distributions in APS and WoS data.
- The paper states that the distributions span several orders of magnitude and have no clear demarcation between sleeping beauties and normal papers.
- Ke et al. report that APS and WoS distributions are remarkably similar except for the higher WoS cutoff, and that delayed recognition occurs over a wide continuous range.
- The discussion concludes that dormant-then-fast-growth papers are not exceptional outliers but extreme cases in heterogeneous continuous distributions.

## Caveats

- Continuous distribution evidence does not by itself explain why individual papers awaken.
- Cross-field and cross-age comparisons can be biased by different citation densities and observation windows.
- A continuous score still requires thresholds when building candidate lists for manual follow-up.
