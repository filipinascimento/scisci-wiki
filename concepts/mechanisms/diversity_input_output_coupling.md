# Diversity input-output coupling

## Summary

Diversity can be studied both in the inputs to science and technology systems and in their outputs, with governance choices coupling the two.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: research portfolio, input option, output trajectory, governance intervention, or evaluation criterion.
- Typical representation: input-output diversity model linking portfolio composition to downstream outcomes.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: how diversity in resources, perspectives, or options affects output diversity.
- Empirical signature: policy choices that alter input diversity change output variety, balance, or disparity over time.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects diversity governance mechanism to [science technology diversity governance](science_technology_diversity_governance.md) and [diversity measures](../measures/diversity_measures.md).

## Operationalization

- Separate input diversity in funding, teams, methods, and fields from output diversity in publications, technologies, or policy options.
- Measure whether changes in input composition precede downstream output diversity changes.
- Use sensitivity analysis to show which diversity dimension is being governed.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Stirling (2007) treats diversity as relevant to science, technology, and society across appraisal and governance contexts.
- The paper links diversity analysis to choices about both portfolios of options and their consequences.

## Caveats

- Input-output coupling is often delayed and mediated by institutions.
- Higher input diversity does not automatically produce desirable output diversity.
