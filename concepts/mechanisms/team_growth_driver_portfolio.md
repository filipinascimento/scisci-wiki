# Team-growth driver portfolio

## Summary

Team-growth driver portfolio frames rising teamwork as the combined result of several candidate drivers, including capital intensity, researcher population growth, knowledge growth and specialization, lower communication costs, and changing authorship norms.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, subfield, domain, output type, year, institution, or collaboration regime.
- Typical representation: driver-audit table, mechanism diagram, multivariate field-year panel, or residual explanation comparison.
- Mechanism: multiple forces can increase the need for collaboration or reduce its cost, so teamwork growth should be evaluated as a portfolio of candidate drivers.
- Empirical signature: no single driver explains the cross-field and cross-domain pattern; different validations rule out or support different parts of the portfolio.

## Operationalization

- Build a field-year or subfield-year panel with teamwork outcomes, citation outcomes, field size, and field taxonomy.
- Add proxies for capital intensity, equipment scale, project complexity, researcher population, knowledge depth, communication infrastructure, and authorship norms where available.
- Estimate how much of teamwork growth each proxy explains, then inspect residual trends.
- Use cross-domain contrasts, event-window checks, and robustness tests rather than relying on one regression alone.
- Treat discussion-layer mechanisms as hypotheses until validated with direct measures.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wuchty, Jones, and Uzzi (2007) lists several candidate explanations for the shift to teamwork: capital intensity in laboratory sciences, growth in the number of researchers, finer divisions of labor, knowledge growth and specialization, declining communication costs, and shifting authorship norms.
- The same paper reports evidence that limits single-driver explanations: teamwork grows in social sciences and mathematics, field-size growth explains only a limited share of team-size growth, and the results hold across fields with diverse authorship norms.
- Wuchty et al. therefore motivate a portfolio view in which capital intensity may matter in some domains, while specialization, communication, population, and norms may matter elsewhere.
- Verified full-text evidence from Jones (2009) gives a later mechanism-level development for one part of this portfolio: accumulated knowledge can push innovators toward longer training, narrower specialization, and larger teams.

## Caveats

- A portfolio framing is not itself causal identification; each driver needs its own measurement and validation design.
- Driver proxies are correlated: field size, capital intensity, knowledge depth, funding, and institutional scale can move together.
- The relative importance of drivers may differ across papers, patents, software, datasets, humanities outputs, and policy-facing research.
- Authorship norms can mimic collaboration growth even when underlying work organization changes less.
