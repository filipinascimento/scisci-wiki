# Generalized self-promotion sanction channel

## Summary

Generalized self-promotion sanction channel is the mechanism that social penalties for self-promotion can suppress self-citation behavior for some groups across fields, rather than only inside male-dominated specialties.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author, self-citation, demographic group, field, self-promotion behavior, or sanction norm.
- Typical representation: mechanism statement, field-composition contrast, backlash caveat, or gendered behavior model.
- Mechanism: if self-promotion is sanctioned differently across groups, authors may avoid citing their own prior work even when the citation is relevant.
- Empirical signature: lower self-citation appears broadly across fields and is not fully explained by field gender composition.

## Operationalization

- Compare self-citation gaps within fields and across fields with different gender composition.
- Examine whether self-promotion norms or sanctions are measured directly through surveys or experiments.
- Separate the sanction channel from publication-opportunity and specialization channels.
- Interpret policy interventions in light of possible backlash.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from King et al. (2017) discusses social penalties for women's self-promotion as a possible explanation for lower self-citation.
- The paper reports that the self-citation gap appears broadly, not only in fields with the lowest representation of women.
- This motif is distinct from the intervention-backlash page because it describes a mechanism for observed behavior rather than a policy risk alone.
- It is also distinct from opportunity-set confounding because it concerns norms around citing one's own work.

## Caveats

- The sanction channel is difficult to prove using citation traces alone.
- Lower self-citation can also reflect productivity, specialization, authorship role, or field norms.
- The mechanism should not be used to prescribe more self-citation without considering equity and evaluation design.
