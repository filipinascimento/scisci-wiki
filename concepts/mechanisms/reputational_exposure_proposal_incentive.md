# Reputational-exposure proposal incentive

## Summary

Reputational-exposure proposal incentive is the mechanism by which public recognition, elite attention, and visibility motivate participation in scientific proposal competitions.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: applicant, proposal winner, prize, public ceremony, media exposure, or funding competition.
- Typical representation: nonmonetary reward channel alongside money or seed funding.
- Mechanism: recognition and audience access increase the expected payoff of submitting a proposal.
- Empirical signature: proposal competitions offer public awards, elite endorsement, or visibility in addition to funding.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Links [peer review and gatekeeping](peer_review_gatekeeping.md) to recognition systems, not only to resource allocation.
- Extends [attention inequality](attention_inequality.md) into proposal-stage competition.
- Connects with [prize-winner authority expansion](../methods/prize_winner_authority_expansion.md) when awards create later influence or access.

## Operationalization

- Record monetary and nonmonetary awards attached to proposal outcomes.
- Track whether winners receive public recognition, elite audiences, media coverage, or follow-on review advantages.
- Compare participation and later outputs with competitions lacking visible recognition.
- Distinguish recognition incentives from funding amount and review score.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Boudreau et al. (2016) reports that winners received monetary prizes and a route into a seed-grant stage.
- Awards were publicly conferred by the medical-school dean in front of peers, White House staff, and media.
- The design therefore offered visibility and prestige in addition to direct funding opportunities.

## Caveats

- Recognition incentives can bias participation toward scientists who value or can afford public visibility.
- Public awards can become status signals that influence later evaluation.
- Recognition and funding effects should be measured separately when possible.
