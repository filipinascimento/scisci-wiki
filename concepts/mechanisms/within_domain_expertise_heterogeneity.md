# Within-domain expertise heterogeneity

## Summary

Within-domain expertise heterogeneity is the mechanism by which scientists in the same broad domain still differ enough in training, methods, and exposure that they evaluate the same proposal from different intellectual distances.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: evaluator, proposal, disease area, specialty, vocabulary, method, or prior publication portfolio.
- Typical representation: distance variation inside a nominally common field.
- Mechanism: shared domain membership does not eliminate cognitive distance because scientists specialize in different literatures and methodological repertoires.
- Empirical signature: evaluator-proposal distance predicts scores even when all evaluators are credible members of the relevant biomedical or scientific area.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [reviewer-proposal intellectual distance](../measures/reviewer_proposal_intellectual_distance.md) by explaining why distance exists within a domain.
- Connects peer review to [burden of knowledge](burden_of_knowledge.md): specialization creates both expertise and local blind spots.
- Helps interpret [closest-expert review penalty](closest_expert_review_penalty.md) as a within-field effect rather than a simple outsider-insider contrast.
- Supports review designs that deliberately sample evaluator distance.

## Operationalization

- Represent each evaluator's prior work with controlled vocabulary terms, references, topics, or embeddings.
- Represent proposals in the same intellectual space.
- Compute evaluator-proposal distance while holding the broad field or disease area fixed.
- Test whether distance effects remain after evaluator, proposal, seniority, and domain controls.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Boudreau et al. (2016) emphasizes that even biomedical scientists recruited around the same disease areas varied in expertise and exposure.
- The experiment uses evaluator publication histories and proposal MeSH terms to estimate distance inside the relevant domain.
- The paper finds that more distant evaluators give higher scores to more novel proposals, indicating that within-domain heterogeneity matters for resource allocation.

## Caveats

- Distance can proxy for evaluator taste, methodological preference, or strategic conflict rather than only expertise heterogeneity.
- Very distant evaluators may lack minimum competence to assess feasibility.
- Broad-domain labels can hide institutional, subfield, and training differences that require separate controls.
