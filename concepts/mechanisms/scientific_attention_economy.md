# Scientific attention economy

## Summary

Scientific attention economy frames publications, citations, and citation lifetimes as signals in a scarce attention system where recognition and reputation are allocated through scholarly notice.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, citation, author, journal, attention lifetime, or recognition outcome.
- Typical representation: attention currency, citation-lifetime distribution, recognition market, or attention-allocation mechanism.
- Mechanism: scientists seek attention by producing new knowledge, and citation attention becomes a reward signal that feeds reputation and career outcomes.
- Empirical signature: citation counts and decay patterns are interpreted as the visibility and lifetime of scholarly attention.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects [attention inequality](attention_inequality.md), [citation aging and obsolescence](citation_aging_obsolescence.md), and [publication growth attention competition](publication_growth_attention_competition.md).
- Provides the mechanism frame behind [time-to-peak attention](../measures/time_to_peak_attention.md) and [paper attention half-life](../measures/paper_attention_half_life.md).
- Links citation dynamics to recognition, reputation, promotion, and priority claiming.
- Helps compare citation attention with altmetric and online attention traces without treating all sources as equivalent.

## Operationalization

- Model citation receipt as attention allocation over time.
- Measure how quickly papers attract attention, when attention peaks, and how long it persists.
- Compare attention lifetimes across fields, cohorts, topics, and publication-volume regimes.
- Interpret attention metrics together with reputation and reward mechanisms rather than only as neutral impact counts.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Parolo et al. (2015) states that scientific publishing records progress, claims priority, exposes results for checking, and attracts attention from scientists working on related problems.
- The paper describes attention, measured by the number and lifetime of citations, as the main currency of the scientific community and a basis for promotion and reputation.
- Parolo et al. explicitly invoke an attention economy in science in which producers of new knowledge seek attention and are rewarded by citations from peers.

## Caveats

- Citation attention can reflect controversy, convenience, field size, or methods reuse rather than intellectual endorsement.
- Attention economy framing can overstate strategic behavior if used without qualitative or institutional context.
- Citation lifetime is only one attention channel and should not be collapsed with public or policy attention.
