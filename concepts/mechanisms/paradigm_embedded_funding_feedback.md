# Paradigm-embedded funding feedback

## Summary

Paradigm-embedded funding feedback is the mechanism by which funding agencies and review systems rooted in incumbent scientific paradigms tend to reproduce prior priorities unless counterweighted by oversight, incentives, and feedback.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: funding agency, review panel, research field, resource-allocation system, or disease area.
- Typical representation: feedback loop between prior funding, incumbent knowledge, reviewer expertise, and future funding.
- Mechanism: established paradigms shape both perceived merit and resource allocation, reinforcing what is already visible.
- Empirical signature: allocations track previous research and prior allocations more strongly than external need or unexplored opportunity.

## Operationalization

- Measure correspondence among disease burden, prior funding, publication volume, and new allocations.
- Identify whether reviewers and agency leaders are embedded in the fields being evaluated.
- Test whether oversight, innovation incentives, or feedback mechanisms change allocation patterns.
- Separate deliberate strategic priorities from inertia generated by incumbent paradigms.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) argues that risky projects and unexplored hypotheses may need proactive sponsorship.
- The review reports that U.S. biomedical resources are more strongly correlated with prior allocations and research than with actual disease burden.
- It warns that agencies run by scientists embedded in established paradigms may struggle to influence science without additional oversight, incentives, and feedback.

## Caveats

- Alignment with previous allocations can sometimes reflect real cumulative expertise or infrastructure needs.
- Disease burden is only one possible external need metric.
- Feedback mechanisms must avoid simply replacing one entrenched priority system with another.
