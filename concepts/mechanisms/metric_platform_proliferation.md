# Metric platform proliferation

## Summary

Metric platform proliferation is the expansion of web databases, analytics products, and profile tools that make bibliometric indicators easy to produce and therefore easier to overuse.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: citation index, analytics platform, researcher-profile tool, institutional dashboard, or altmetric provider.
- Typical representation: platform timeline, data-access stack, analytics-product ecosystem, or dashboard dependency map.
- Mechanism or measurement target: lowered technical barriers to metric production and wider organizational adoption of indicators.
- Empirical signature: evaluation practices move from specialist analyses toward routine platform-generated comparisons of researchers, groups, institutions, or journals.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Gives an infrastructure mechanism behind the growth of [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) as a policy concern.
- Connects [bibliometric data access modes](../methods/bibliometric_data_access_modes.md) to assessment behavior: interface access and analytics products shape what indicators are easy to use.
- Links database infrastructure such as [Web of Science](../datasets/web_of_science.md), [Scopus](../datasets/scopus.md), [Google Scholar](../datasets/google_scholar.md), and [altmetrics](../datasets/altmetrics.md) to downstream evaluation systems.
- Splits Web of Science product layers into [WoS analytics product integrations](../datasets/wos_analytics_product_integrations.md) when Journal Citation Reports, Essential Science Indicators, or InCites supply the metric surface.
- Helps explain why transparent methods and evaluated-party verification become harder when indicators are generated by multiple platforms.

## Operationalization

- Record which platforms, dashboards, APIs, or profile tools supply each indicator in an evaluation system.
- Track launch dates, institutional uptake, data sources, formula versions, and accessible comparison units.
- Compare decisions before and after platform adoption to test whether metrics became more routine or consequential.
- Audit whether users understand platform coverage, source inclusion, author disambiguation, update cadence, and formula assumptions.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hicks et al. (2015) contrasts the pre-2000 Science Citation Index on CD-ROM, used by specialists, with later web-accessible citation databases.
- The article notes the arrival of Web of Science as an integrated web platform, competing citation indices such as Scopus and Google Scholar, and analytics tools such as InCites, SciVal, and Publish or Perish.
- Hicks et al. also note the growth of social-usage and online-comment metrics through services such as F1000Prime, Mendeley, and Altmetric.com.
- The same full text frames this proliferation as part of the shift toward routine metric-reliant research evaluation.

## Caveats

- Platform access can improve transparency and reproducibility when methods are well documented.
- Proliferation is not inherently misuse; the risk arises when easy comparison outruns validation and contextual interpretation.
- Platform timelines and coverage are unstable, so studies should record exact versions, snapshots, and access routes.
