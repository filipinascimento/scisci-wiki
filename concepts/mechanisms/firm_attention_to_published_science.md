# Firm attention to published science

## Summary

Firm attention to published science is the mechanism by which patent citations to papers reveal which external scientific discoveries firms notice and draw into inventive activity.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: firm, patent, cited scientific paper, assignee, field, or time period.
- Typical representation: firm-paper citation links, firm-level science reliance counts, or technology-to-science attention profiles.
- Mechanism target: corporate monitoring and appropriation of published science.
- Empirical signature: firm patents cite papers produced outside the firm, especially as internal R&D boundaries shift.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Interprets [patent scientific reliance count](../measures/patent_scientific_reliance_count.md) as attention to published science.
- Provides an organizational mechanism behind [scientific knowledge application index](../measures/scientific_knowledge_application_index.md).
- Connects to [organizational science-based patent value premium](organizational_science_based_patent_value_premium.md).
- Complements [university-firm patent-paper handoff](university_firm_patent_paper_handoff.md) by emphasizing firm-side uptake.

## Operationalization

- Link firm-assigned patents to cited scientific papers.
- Separate firm-authored, university-authored, and other external papers when author-affiliation data permit.
- Aggregate patent-paper citations by firm, technology class, field, and year.
- Compare firm attention to internal publication activity, R&D decline, or open-innovation measures.
- Validate with assignee names, applicant/examiner source roles, and confidence thresholds.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Marx and Fuegi (2020) argues that public PCS links enable research on which scientific discoveries firms rely on.
- The paper highlights the relevance of PCS as corporate internal research declines and firms increasingly look outward for scientific input.
- This mechanism turns patent-paper edges into a firm attention measure rather than only a science-to-technology citation count.
- It remains a documented-reference signal, not direct observation of firm search behavior.

## Caveats

- Patent citations can be added by attorneys or examiners and may not reflect firm scientists' attention.
- Firm-name disambiguation and mergers can distort firm-level profiles.
- Attention to science does not imply successful commercialization.
