# Gendered faculty placement gap

## Summary

Gendered faculty placement gap describes differences in prestige-rank movement, self-hiring, or placement outcomes between men and women within faculty hiring markets.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: faculty placement, doctoral institution, employing institution, inferred prestige rank, field, gender group, or prestige tier.
- Typical representation: rank-change distribution by gender, median placement difference, upward/downward mobility share by gender, self-hire share by gender, or field-level significance test.
- Mechanism: gender can interact with prestige hierarchy so similar doctoral origins do not produce equivalent placement outcomes.
- Empirical signature: gender differences are field- and prestige-tier-specific rather than uniform across all academic labor markets.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Splits a faculty-market pathway from broad [gender and race stratification](gender_race_stratification.md).
- Extends [faculty placement rank change](../measures/faculty_placement_rank_change.md) and [prestige-constrained faculty mobility](prestige_constrained_faculty_mobility.md) with subgroup comparisons.
- Connects [faculty gender demographic turnover](faculty_gender_demographic_turnover.md) to placement-side inequalities before attrition and retention operate.
- Provides a fairness lens for [faculty placement predictive validity](../validations/faculty_placement_predictive_validity.md) and rank-based evaluations.

## Operationalization

- Infer or import field-specific prestige ranks for doctoral and employing institutions.
- Compute rank-change distributions separately by gender, field, domain, cohort, and doctoral prestige tier.
- Test differences in median placement, upward/downward mobility shares, or self-hiring rates.
- Interpret observed gaps alongside gender annotation coverage, field size, rank uncertainty, and multiple-testing correction.
- Separate placement gaps from later [faculty retention and attrition](faculty_retention_attrition.md).

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Clauset et al. (2015) reports that male and female faculty have similar but not equivalent rank-change distributions in computer science, business, and history.
- Clauset et al. find that women's median rank change is 23-38 ranks compared with 21-35 for men, with the largest differences among graduates of the top 15% of computer science and business units.
- The paper reports that median placement for elite women in those fields is 12-18% worse than for men from the same institutions, while history shows no median-placement gender difference.
- Verified full-text evidence from Wapman et al. (2022) finds no significant mobility difference between men and women in 82 of 107 fields, but in the 25 fields with significant differences, women are less likely to move down the prestige hierarchy and more likely to be self-hires.
- Wapman et al. also report that new and existing faculty are more likely to be men as prestige increases in academia overall, suggesting interaction between prestige and gendered hiring or retention.

## Caveats

- Gender annotations can be missing, inferred, or binary; subgroup analysis can erase nonbinary identities.
- Field-specific sample sizes and multiple testing strongly affect which gaps are detectable.
- Placement gaps do not by themselves identify mechanisms such as bias, preferences, subfield mix, family constraints, or search timing.
- Prestige rank differences are not equivalent to research quality, job satisfaction, or long-run career outcomes.
