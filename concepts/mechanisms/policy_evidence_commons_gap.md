# Policy evidence-commons gap

## Summary

Policy evidence-commons gap is the mechanism by which policy documents draw on accumulated bodies of research, making single-paper policy mentions undercount contribution to policy knowledge.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: policy document, evidence base, paper, field, review, guideline, or policy citation.
- Typical representation: gap between paper-level mentions and body-of-evidence influence.
- Mechanism: policy use often aggregates many studies into a shared evidence commons rather than citing every contributing paper.
- Empirical signature: policy documents cite or mention only a small subset of the research base they rely on.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Explains why [policy citation-style omission](../validations/policy_citation_style_omission.md) can be structural rather than accidental.
- Links policy metrics to [research-policy interaction barrier](research_policy_interaction_barrier.md).
- Gives [policy attention hypothesis ladder](../representations/policy_attention_hypothesis_ladder.md) a mechanism for weak paper-level signals.
- Helps prevent policy mentions from being interpreted as complete traces of policy influence.

## Operationalization

- Compare policy mentions with systematic reviews, guidelines, or evidence syntheses in the same policy area.
- Estimate how many relevant research papers are uncited or unmentioned despite being part of the evidence base.
- Use topic or review-level aggregation when paper-level mentions are too sparse.
- Separate direct cited influence from indirect evidence-commons contribution.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Haunschild and Bornmann (2017) notes that a body of research can be important for policy even when individual papers are not mentioned.
- The paper uses this point to caution against overinterpreting sparse paper-level policy mentions.
- This motif is distinct from DOI denominator loss because it concerns policy evidence aggregation rather than identifier matching.
- It supports aggregated field, topic, or review-level policy-impact analysis.

## Caveats

- Evidence-commons contribution is hard to verify without substantive policy-domain review.
- Aggregation can hide which papers were actually influential.
- Policy actors may use research indirectly through intermediaries, syntheses, or expert committees.
