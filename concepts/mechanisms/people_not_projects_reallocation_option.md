# People-not-projects reallocation option

## Summary

Funding investigators rather than fixed projects creates an option to abandon unfruitful paths and redirect effort quickly.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: funding award, investigator, project commitment, topic pivot, budget flexibility, or failed research line.
- Typical representation: real-option representation of within-award reallocation rights.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: exploratory pivot capacity under person-level funding.
- Empirical signature: person-level funding permits faster abandonment and reallocation than project-locked funding contracts..

## Operationalization

- Compare contractual unit of selection: person, laboratory, broad program, or specific project.
- Measure ex post topic shifts, within-award budget flexibility, and speed of pivot after failed research lines.
- Separate flexible reallocation rights from prestige, budget size, and selection into elite programs.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Azoulay et al. (2009) says HHMI investigators can redirect effort away from avenues that do not bear fruit, while NIH proposals map experiments far in advance.
- The motif captures the real-option value of people-not-projects funding.

## Caveats

- Close to broad autonomy motifs; its residual value is the explicit reallocation mechanism.
- Reallocation rights can be difficult to observe without project histories or topic trajectories.
