# Topic-identity homophily

## Summary

Topic-identity homophily is the pattern that scientists' social identities are associated with the topics they study, so demographic composition can shape the knowledge portfolio of science.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, first author, topic, field, demographic group, paper, or topic portfolio.
- Typical representation: group-topic overrepresentation matrix, [race-gender topic portfolio map](../representations/race_gender_topic_portfolio_maps.md), or topic concentration statistic.
- Mechanism: lived experience, community relevance, mentorship, institutional access, funding priorities, exclusion, and identity-linked opportunity structures shape problem choice.
- Empirical signature: some demographic groups are overrepresented in specific topics and underrepresented in others relative to their overall author share.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [scientific problem choice](scientific_problem_choice.md) by adding demographic and intersectional structure.
- Provides a mechanism for [intersectional topic stratification](intersectional_topic_stratification.md) before citation outcomes are considered.
- Connects diversity in the scientific workforce to expansion or contraction of the knowledge base.
- Motivates policy and evaluation audits in [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) and [locally relevant research metrics](../validations/locally_relevant_research_metrics.md).

## Operationalization

- Construct topic assignments for papers and demographic group weights for authors.
- Compute each group's topic share relative to its overall share in the study population.
- Use concentration measures such as coefficient of variation to compare whether groups are broadly distributed or specialized across topics.
- Interpret topic concentration together with field, institution, funding, career age, and prestige networks.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Kozlowski et al. (2022) reports homophily between intersectional identities and research topics in U.S.-affiliated first-author publications.
- In Social Sciences and related fields, the paper reports that Black authors are highly represented in topics about racial discrimination, African American culture, and African studies and communities.
- Kozlowski et al. report that Latinx authors are highly represented in topics related to immigrants, political identity, racial discrimination, language literacy, and Latin-American issues.
- The paper reports that highly feminized topics include gender-based violence, families, learning, LGBT studies, nursing, pregnancy, and education.
- Kozlowski et al. summarize topic concentration with [demographic topic concentration CV](../measures/demographic_topic_concentration_cv.md), finding broader topic ubiquity for White authors and stronger specialization for Asian, Black, and Latinx authors.
- These patterns support the conclusion that workforce diversity changes the scientific topic portfolio rather than only the demographic makeup of authors.

## Caveats

- Topic-identity homophily can reflect agency, community commitment, exclusion, funding constraints, or institutional sorting.
- It should not be interpreted as evidence that groups are naturally suited to particular topics.
- Topic labels and demographic categories can obscure within-group heterogeneity.
