# Reference-age attention diversion

## Summary

Reference-age attention diversion is the mechanism in which deeper searching of older literature reallocates citation attention away from recently published papers.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: reference list, cited-paper age, citation trajectory, paper cohort, or field.
- Typical representation: average reference age trend, time-to-peak shortening, or attention-diversion mechanism.
- Mechanism: when newer papers can reach further back into the literature, some citation attention that might have gone to recent papers is diverted to older work.
- Empirical signature: papers reach peak citation attention sooner while average reference age rises.

## Operationalization

- Measure average or distributional reference age over publication cohorts.
- Estimate time to peak citation attention for papers in the same cohorts.
- Test whether increasing reference age is associated with shorter time to peak for recent papers.
- Compare the mechanism with alternative explanations such as faster indexing, publication growth, and field-topic shifts.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Parolo et al. (2015) reports decreasing mean time to peak attention in recent times.
- The paper links this pattern to prior findings that average reference age increases with time.
- Parolo et al. suggest that more recent papers can dig deeper into the scientific literature, reducing attention available for recently published papers and shortening the time needed to peak.

## Caveats

- Rising reference age can reflect review practices, field maturation, or database coverage, not only search depth.
- The mechanism is difficult to separate from publication growth and changing reference-list lengths.
- Attention diverted to older papers may still support cumulative knowledge rather than only reduce novelty visibility.
