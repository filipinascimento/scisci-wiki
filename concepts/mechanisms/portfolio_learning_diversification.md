# Portfolio learning diversification

## Summary

Diverse research portfolios support learning across alternatives by revealing which options, combinations, and trajectories work under different conditions.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: portfolio option, learning signal, funding allocation, research trajectory, or policy experiment.
- Typical representation: diversified portfolio as an exploration and learning system.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: learning value of maintaining multiple scientific trajectories.
- Empirical signature: parallel support for diverse options produces comparative evidence that can guide later allocation or governance choices.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects research portfolio mechanism to [funding incentives and exploration](funding_incentives_and_exploration.md) and [scientific ecosystem diversity](scientific_ecosystem_diversity.md).

## Operationalization

- Treat research options as parallel learning probes rather than only independent bets.
- Measure information gained from maintaining multiple fields, methods, or technology paths.
- Update portfolios using learning signals without prematurely eliminating low-current-performance options.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Stirling (2007) links diversity appraisal to decision making in science, technology, and society under plural values and uncertainty.
- This supports interpreting diversified portfolios as learning infrastructure.

## Caveats

- Learning benefits may be delayed and hard to credit to any one option.
- Portfolio updates can reduce diversity too early if short-run indicators dominate.
