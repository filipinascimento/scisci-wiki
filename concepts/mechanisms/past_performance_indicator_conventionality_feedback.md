# Past-performance indicator conventionality feedback

## Summary

Past-performance indicator conventionality feedback occurs when grant selection based on prior productivity, citations, and track record steers funding toward safer and more homogeneous research.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: applicant, proposal, review criterion, past-performance indicator, or funded project portfolio.
- Typical representation: feedback loop from prior outputs to funding allocation to future output conventionality.
- Mechanism target: how evaluation indicators can reproduce conventional research directions.
- Empirical signature: stronger reliance on past performance is associated with lower novelty or reduced diversity in funded outputs.

## Operationalization

- Code review criteria or funding formulas for prior productivity, citation impact, and track-record components.
- Measure novelty, conventionality, or topic diversity in funded outputs.
- Compare funding programs with stronger versus weaker past-performance weighting.
- Test whether effects are stronger for low-status applicants or high-uncertainty proposals.
- Use counterfactual simulations or quasi-experimental changes when available.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Lee, and Walsh (2018) discusses concerns that performance indicators such as publications and citations can orient funding systems toward safer research.
- The paper connects these concerns to competitive project funding and novelty outcomes.
- This mechanism helps interpret why selection based on previous success may disadvantage proposals that depart from established lines.
- It also links funding design to broader metric-feedback motifs in science evaluation.

## Caveats

- Past performance can contain legitimate information about capacity and feasibility.
- Conventionality feedback can be confounded with field, institution, and applicant status.
- Indicator use should be distinguished from peer judgment that only informally considers track record.
