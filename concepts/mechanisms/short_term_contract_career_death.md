# Short-Term Contract Career-Death Mechanism

## Summary

Short-term contract career-death mechanism is the pathway by which non-tenure short contracts can generate productivity volatility and sudden exits from scientific careers.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, contract regime, career trajectory, institution, or field.
- Typical representation: career-growth model with contract duration and exit risk.
- Mechanism: short review cycles and insecure appointments amplify productivity shocks and can terminate careers before long-run potential is observed.
- Empirical signature: contract regimes produce sudden career death, productivity fluctuations, and attrition unrelated to stable research quality.

## Operationalization

- Link appointment type, contract duration, funding regime, productivity, and career continuation.
- Compare scientists across tenure-track, short-term, and long-term contract regimes.
- Estimate whether short contracts predict exits after controlling for prior output and field.
- Simulate unintended policy consequences under different contract-review cycles.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) frames science-policy models as tools for detecting unintended consequences.
- The review describes models in which non-tenure short-term contracts produce productivity fluctuations.
- It also states that those fluctuations often result in sudden career death.

## Caveats

- Contract effects can be confounded with institution quality, funding access, immigration status, and field labor markets.
- Sudden exit is not always involuntary or caused by contract policy.
- Observed productivity drops can reflect missing publications or affiliation changes rather than career death.
