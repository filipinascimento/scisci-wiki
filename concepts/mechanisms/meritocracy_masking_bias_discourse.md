# Meritocracy-masking bias discourse

## Summary

Meritocracy-masking bias discourse is the mechanism by which ideals of scientific meritocracy and apolitical evaluation can obscure race-gender bias in evaluation systems.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: evaluation discourse, metric, institution, reviewer practice, or policy claim.
- Typical representation: discourse mechanism linking universalist ideals to hidden bias.
- Mechanism target: explain why unequal outcomes can persist despite formal claims of impartiality.
- Empirical signature: bias remains unaddressed when evaluation systems treat observed success as neutral merit.

## Operationalization

- Analyze policy, review, or assessment texts for meritocracy and neutrality claims.
- Compare discourse with observed demographic disparities in outcomes.
- Test whether metric or prestige proxies reproduce group disparities.
- Use qualitative evidence to interpret quantitative inequality mechanisms.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Kozlowski et al. (2022) argues that scientific ideals can mask systemic race-gender bias.
- The paper connects meritocracy discourse to the need for intersectional analyses of topics and impact.
- This mechanism explains why descriptive inequality measurement must be paired with critique of evaluation assumptions.

## Caveats

- Meritocratic language can coexist with sincere quality goals.
- Discourse analysis should be tied to concrete allocation and evaluation practices.
- The mechanism does not identify a single causal channel by itself.
