# Block-funding novelty buffer

## Summary

Block-funding novelty buffer is the mechanism by which stable internal funds can protect lower-status scientists' ability to pursue novel work without first passing a competitive project filter.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, project, internal block funding, competitive grant, novelty outcome, or status group.
- Typical representation: higher novelty for lower-status scientists under block funding than under competitive project funding.
- Mechanism: flexible internal funds reduce conformity pressure, proposal-screening costs, and deliverable commitments for vulnerable researchers.
- Empirical signature: low-status groups show stronger novelty under block funding, while high-status groups benefit more from competitive funding selection.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Gives the mechanism behind [funding model status contingency](funding_model_status_contingency.md).
- Extends [funding autonomy and exploration](funding_autonomy_exploration.md) from elite investigator programs to routine internal funding.
- Connects funding design to [novelty penalty](novelty_penalty.md), [peer review gatekeeping](peer_review_gatekeeping.md), and status inequality.

## Operationalization

- Identify projects supported primarily by internal block funds through survey or administrative funding-source data.
- Compare novelty outcomes against projects with high competitive-funding shares.
- Stratify by low-status proxies such as junior rank, gender, institution, or prior reputation.
- Test whether block funding reduces the novelty gap for lower-status researchers.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Lee, and Walsh (2018) reports that competitive funding is associated with higher novelty on average, but lower-status groups fare relatively better under flexible block funding.
- The paper argues that low-status scientists may face stronger conformity pressure in competitive peer review and less flexibility after receiving project funds.
- Wang et al. interpret stable internal funds as important for allowing junior, female, and peripheral-institution researchers to pursue novel ideas.
- The paper's selection-versus-treatment split suggests that both funding selection and post-award constraints can matter, especially for junior researchers.
- The same policy discussion points to [proposal-burden research-time crowding](proposal_burden_research_time_crowding.md) as another cost of relying heavily on competitive project funding.

## Caveats

- Block funding may also support less productive or less novel work; the mechanism is not an argument that block funding is always superior.
- Internal funds can be unequally controlled inside departments or chair units.
- Evidence from Japan's funding system may not generalize directly to systems with different tenure, gender, or institutional hierarchies.
