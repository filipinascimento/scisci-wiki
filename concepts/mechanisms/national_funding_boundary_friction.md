# National funding boundary friction

## Summary

National funding boundary friction is the mechanism motif that nationally allocated research funding can favor domestic or regional collaboration over international ties.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: funding program, country pair, collaboration edge, institution, or policy boundary.
- Typical representation: national funding eligibility, cross-border funding barrier, domestic collaboration bias, or policy-friction edge.
- Mechanism target: explain why collaboration flows may remain spatially or nationally clustered.
- Empirical signature: collaboration ties are denser within national funding boundaries than expected from research capacity and distance alone.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Relates to [international policy goal portfolio](../representations/international_policy_goal_portfolio.md) when funding agencies seek cross-border collaboration.

## Operationalization

- Code funding programs by domestic-only, bilateral, multilateral, or open international eligibility.
- Compare domestic and cross-border collaboration rates before and after funding-policy changes.
- Include distance, language, field, institutional prestige, and national research capacity controls.
- Test whether cross-border eligibility reduces national-boundary residuals.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Pan et al. (2012) notes that research funding is mostly allocated at the national level while motivating analysis of international collaboration and citation flows.
- The paper does not directly estimate funding-policy effects, so this motif is a mechanism inference from its framing.

## Caveats

- National clustering can also arise from language, mobility, institutions, and field specialization.
- Funding eligibility is often correlated with geopolitical and economic factors.
- Direct policy tests require grant-level or program-level data.
