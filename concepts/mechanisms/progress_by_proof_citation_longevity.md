# Progress-by-proof citation longevity

## Summary

Progress-by-proof citation longevity is the mechanism motif that proof-based or theoretical contributions can retain citation attention for unusually long periods because their claims remain foundational.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, field, proof-based contribution, citation trajectory, or attention half-life.
- Typical representation: long citation half-life, delayed decay, foundational theorem, proof-based field, or long-lived reference.
- Mechanism target: explain why some fields or contribution types show slower citation obsolescence.
- Empirical signature: theory- or proof-heavy papers retain citations longer than faster-moving empirical literatures.

## Operationalization

- Tag papers or fields by theory, proof, method, empirical, or clinical contribution type.
- Estimate citation half-life, cumulative citation age, or peak-normalized citation decay.
- Compare trajectories after controlling for field size, reference-list length, and publication-year cohort.
- Test whether proof-heavy papers remain in reference cores for longer windows.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Petersen et al. (2014) contrasts long-lived highly cited mathematics and physics papers with shorter-lived biomedical citation trajectories.
- The paper invokes progress by proof as one reason mathematical papers can remain cited over very long windows.
- This motif links contribution form to citation longevity rather than treating half-life as a purely statistical property.

## Caveats

- Field size, citation density, and publication growth also shape citation longevity.
- Proof-based labels can be hard to assign at scale.
- Long citation life can reflect ceremonial citation or textbook incorporation as well as active use.
