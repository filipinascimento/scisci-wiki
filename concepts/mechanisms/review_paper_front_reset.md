# Review-paper front reset

## Summary

Review-paper front reset is the mechanism in which review papers periodically reconnect a fast-moving research front to older work and replace earlier front papers that have fallen behind the active citation band.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: review paper, tight specialty, chronological citation matrix, research-front band, or cited-paper cohort.
- Typical representation: vertical columns in a chronological citation matrix, review timing after front turnover, or reference consolidation event.
- Mechanism: review papers summarize and relink older evidence, making the active front navigable after many recent papers accumulate.
- Empirical signature: dense review-paper reference columns appear after a run of new papers and reach beyond the recent diagonal front.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a document-role mechanism to [tight-field citation matrices](../representations/tight_field_citation_matrix.md).
- Explains one way [research fronts](research_fronts.md) maintain continuity despite rapid obsolescence.
- Connects [citation-index alerting service](../methods/citation_index_alerting_service.md) with archival synthesis needs.
- Helps interpret review articles in citation networks as front reset and consolidation nodes, not just high-reference outliers.

## Operationalization

- Identify review papers by document type, reference-list length, journal section, or citation-matrix vertical structure.
- Measure the number of papers or years since the prior review-like reset in a bounded specialty.
- Compare review references to the recent front band and to older background literature.
- Test whether later papers cite the review instead of many older papers, indicating consolidation.
- Pair matrix evidence with text or citation-context evidence when possible.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Price (1965) reads vertical lines in a chronological citation matrix as review papers.
- Price argues that in a tight specialty, after roughly 30 or 40 papers there is a need for a review paper to replace earlier papers lost behind the research front.
- The same matrix interpretation distinguishes review papers from horizontal-row classics and from off-diagonal background-noise links.
- Price connects review resets to the broader distinction between recent-front alerting needs and archival organization needs.

## Caveats

- Review timing varies by field, journal norms, and publication volume; the 30-40 paper scale is historical and field-specific.
- A high-reference paper is not always a review, and a review is not always a useful reset.
- Review papers can also create citation substitution that hides original sources.
