# Hyperlink consensus acceleration

## Summary

Hyperlink consensus acceleration is the mechanism by which online citation links, related-article links, and search results quickly route researchers toward already visible or broadly referenced work.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: online article, citation path, search session, paper, journal, or field.
- Typical representation: link-following paths that concentrate attention on central prior work.
- Mechanism: visible links and others' citation choices become quality signals, increasing convergence on prevailing opinion.
- Empirical signature: online access is associated with higher citation concentration and faster convergence on canonical references.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Splits the link-following component out of [electronic access citation narrowing](electronic_access_citation_narrowing.md).
- Connects [attention inequality](attention_inequality.md), [cumulative advantage](cumulative_advantage.md), and [preferential attachment](preferential_attachment.md) to user-facing discovery interfaces.
- Helps explain how more accessible archives can still narrow the effective knowledge base.
- Suggests experiments that diversify related-article links, randomize recommendation exposure, or surface lower-consensus prior work.

## Operationalization

- Trace citation-link and related-article paths from online platforms or browser logs.
- Measure how often paths terminate in highly cited, recent, or central papers compared with random or diversity-aware navigation.
- Compare citation concentration before and after link-rich interfaces, recommendation widgets, or archive digitization.
- Model whether hyperlink-mediated exposure increases use of already high-attention sources beyond field and age baselines.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Evans (2008) argues that hyperlinking through online archives puts experts in touch with consensus about the most important prior work.
- Evans links this process to preferential attachment: researchers treat others' choices as information and factor them into reading and citation selection.
- The paper reports that commercial online availability consistently increases citation concentration and that online access, whatever its source, reshapes knowledge discovery and use similarly.
- Evans concludes that electronic journals can hasten scientific consensus while causing findings that do not quickly become consensus to be forgotten quickly.

## Caveats

- Consensus acceleration is not always harmful; it can help researchers identify reliable standards, methods, or canonical evidence.
- Link structures reflect platform design and citation practices, not only researcher preference.
- Current platforms include recommendation systems, preprints, and social-media links beyond the original Evans evidence window.
