# Central-journal gatekeeping obstacle

## Summary

Lower-than-expected JIF placement for novel papers may reflect difficulty entering central, high-prestige journals.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: novel paper, journal, submission path, JIF tier, or journal network position.
- Typical representation: novelty effect on venue placement with journal centrality and prestige controls.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: gatekeeping or access obstacle faced by novel work.
- Empirical signature: novel papers appear in lower-than-expected venue prestige or centrality after accounting for other paper characteristics..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a gatekeeping mechanism to [journal impact factor novelty bias](../validations/journal_impact_factor_novelty_bias.md).
- Connects novelty evaluation to [peer review gatekeeping](peer_review_gatekeeping.md).
- Useful for distinguishing venue bias from citation bias.

## Operationalization

- Model novelty against journal network centrality, JIF tier, editorial acceptance data, or submission-to-publication paths.
- Compare accepted-publication results with submission data when available.
- Test whether author prestige or field position moderates the obstacle.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang et al. (2017) interprets lower expected venue impact for novel papers as an obstacle to acceptance by centrally positioned journals.
- This supports a central-journal gatekeeping mechanism.

## Caveats

- Venue placement alone cannot distinguish rejection from audience fit, author strategy, or scope mismatch.
- JIF and centrality are imperfect proxies for gatekeeping.
