# Marketplace-of-ideas selection fallacy

## Summary

Frontier ideas do not simply rise by merit because evaluation institutions shape which proposals receive resources.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: proposal, review institution, selection rule, resource allocation, or frontier idea.
- Typical representation: institutional filtering model of idea selection rather than spontaneous merit sorting.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: how review systems mediate scientific problem selection and resource allocation.
- Empirical signature: proposal outcomes depend on review design, reviewer assignment, and scoring rules in addition to idea merit.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines science-resource allocation mechanism by linking it to [peer review gatekeeping](peer_review_gatekeeping.md) and [proposal abundance evaluation bottleneck](proposal_abundance_evaluation_bottleneck.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [funding incentives and exploration](funding_incentives_and_exploration.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [embedded grant contest field experiment](../methods/embedded_grant_contest_field_experiment.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Contrast submitted-proposal quality proxies with review outcomes and selection rules.
- Model peer review, crowd aggregation, and contest filters as institutions rather than neutral marketplaces.
- Track how review rules change the funded portfolio's novelty, distance, and topic composition.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Boudreau et al. (2016) rejects a simple marketplace-of-ideas view and frames academic resource allocation as institutionally shaped by peer review and bounded rationality.
- The motif generalizes the paper's framing beyond the specific endocrine-disease contest.

## Caveats

- The paper uses this as a framing mechanism more than a directly measured variable.
- Merit remains partly unobserved, so institutional selection effects require careful design to estimate.
