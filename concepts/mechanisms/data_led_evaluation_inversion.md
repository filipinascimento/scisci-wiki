# Data-led evaluation inversion

## Summary

Data-led evaluation inversion is the shift from metrics informing research judgment to evaluation decisions being led by available data and scores.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: evaluation system, committee, institution, ranking, indicator dashboard, or assessment policy.
- Typical representation: decision workflow in which metric outputs become the default ordering, threshold, or governing evidence.
- Mechanism or measurement target: substitution of available quantitative traces for contextual expert judgment.
- Empirical signature: committees, recruiters, or administrators follow numbers first and ask qualitative questions only after rankings or thresholds are produced.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a mechanism-level failure mode for [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md).
- Explains why [metrics support qualitative assessment](../methods/metrics_support_qualitative_assessment.md) is a design principle rather than a minor reporting preference.
- Links metric proliferation to [indicator systemic effects](indicator_systemic_effects.md), because data-led rules become incentives once they affect careers, funding, or institutional status.
- Helps audit automated and dashboard-based evaluation systems that claim objectivity while embedding source coverage and indicator-choice assumptions.

## Operationalization

- Map the decision workflow and identify whether indicators are advisory, veto points, thresholds, ranking seeds, or final decisions.
- Compare committee narratives with metric rankings to detect when qualitative review merely rationalizes metric order.
- Inspect policy documents for phrases that make metric thresholds mandatory or default.
- Require evaluation reports to state when and why expert judgment overrides or contextualizes indicator values.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hicks et al. (2015) says research evaluations that were once bespoke and peer-performed had become routine and reliant on metrics.
- Hicks et al. identify the core problem as evaluation being led by data rather than judgment.
- The article warns that metrics are often well intentioned but ill applied when organizations use them without enough knowledge of good practice or interpretation.
- The Leiden Manifesto's first principle responds directly by requiring quantitative evaluation to support, not replace, qualitative expert assessment.

## Caveats

- Quantitative data can legitimately challenge biased or incomplete expert judgment.
- A workflow is not data-led merely because it uses metrics; the inversion occurs when decision responsibility moves from accountable evaluators to indicators.
- Detecting inversion often requires access to internal committee practices, not only published assessment rules.
