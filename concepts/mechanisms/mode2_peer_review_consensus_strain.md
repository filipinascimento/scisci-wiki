# Mode-2 peer-review consensus strain

## Summary

Mode-2 peer-review consensus strain is the pressure placed on discipline-bound peer review when proposals are transdisciplinary, institutionally heterogeneous, and difficult for standard expert panels to evaluate.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: grant proposal, review panel, discipline boundary, proposal novelty, or reviewer expertise set.
- Typical representation: mechanism linking transdisciplinarity and institutional heterogeneity to review uncertainty or elite capture.
- Mechanism target: why competitive review may undervalue boundary-crossing work.
- Empirical signature: proposals that require broader expertise or cross-domain consensus receive weaker or more conservative evaluation.

## Operationalization

- Identify proposals requiring expertise across multiple fields, sectors, or institutions.
- Measure reviewer-proposal distance, panel field breadth, and reviewer agreement.
- Compare novelty or funding success for transdisciplinary proposals against comparable disciplinary proposals.
- Test whether broader or more diverse panels reduce consensus strain.
- Separate review uncertainty from true low proposal quality where possible.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Lee, and Walsh (2018) summarizes arguments that Mode-2 or transdisciplinary research strains traditional review because it spans disciplinary expertise.
- The paper links these review difficulties to concerns that competitive funding can disadvantage novel proposals.
- It also notes the risk that high-stakes review can be captured by disciplinary elites or consensus pressures.
- This mechanism complements the paper's empirical status-contingency results.

## Caveats

- Transdisciplinary strain is not the only reason novel proposals receive lower scores.
- Some broad panels can become less expert rather than more inclusive.
- Proposal novelty, feasibility, and reviewer distance should be measured separately.
