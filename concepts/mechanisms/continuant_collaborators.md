# Continuant collaborators

## Summary

Continuant collaborators are repeatedly publishing authors who remain active before and after a focal year and often act as collaboration hubs in scientific networks.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author, career-year window, coauthorship network, subfield, or collaboration role.
- Typical representation: author role category, high-degree hub, persistent contributor, or mediator of newcomer/transient ties.
- Mechanism: persistent productive authors attract collaborators seeking recognition, access, information, or credibility.
- Empirical signature: continuants are disproportionately coauthors, rarely solo authors in some fields, and often occupy high-degree positions in collaboration networks.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a role-based mechanism for [international collaboration preferential attachment](international_collaboration_preferential_attachment.md).
- Links [coauthorship networks](../representations/coauthorship_networks.md), [scientific career impact](scientific_career_impact.md), and [reputation effects](reputation_effects.md).
- Helps explain why collaboration networks have hubs and why newcomers may enter fields through established authors.
- Splits into the more specific [continuant gatekeeping](continuant_gatekeeping.md) mechanism when the focus is mediated access rather than role classification.

## Operationalization

- Define a focal publication year and classify authors by activity before, during, and after that year.
- Identify continuants as authors with publications both before and after the focal year.
- Measure their degree, coauthor composition, mediation of newcomer/transient relations, and position in the collaboration degree distribution.
- Compare continuant behavior across fields, countries, and collaboration regimes.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wagner and Leydesdorff (2005) uses Price and Gersey's author-role categories: continuants, transients, newcomers, and terminators.
- The paper draws on Braun et al. to argue that continuants mediate coauthorship relations among newcomers, transients, and terminators.
- Wagner and Leydesdorff reinterpret continuants as network hubs: highly connected actors who attract collaborators and mediate entry into international collaboration networks.

## Caveats

- Continuant status depends on the observation window and database coverage.
- High degree can reflect field norms, large teams, or name-disambiguation errors, not only social attractiveness.
- Treating continuants as gatekeepers requires evidence about selection, not just network position.
