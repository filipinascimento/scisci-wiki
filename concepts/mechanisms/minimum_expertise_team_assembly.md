# Minimum-expertise team assembly

## Summary

Minimum-expertise team assembly is the mechanism in which teams form by pooling enough specialized knowledge to cover the full expertise required to implement an idea.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: idea, team, expertise segment, application area, inventor cohort, or collaboration event.
- Typical representation: coverage of a knowledge space, minimum teammate count, expertise arcs, or implementation-feasibility indicator.
- Mechanism: as individual breadth narrows, more people are required to span the knowledge needed for implementation.
- Empirical signature: deeper knowledge areas or narrower individual expertise require larger teams even when the idea's origin begins with one innovator.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a theory-layer complement to [task specialization](task_specialization.md), [mean team-size growth](../measures/mean_team_size_growth.md), and [knowledge-depth team-size gradient](../validations/knowledge_depth_team_size_gradient.md).
- Refines [burden of knowledge](burden_of_knowledge.md) by specifying why specialization creates team dependence.
- Links team formation to representations of expertise coverage, role complementarity, and implementation constraints.

## Operationalization

- Define the knowledge, methods, materials, or roles required to implement a project.
- Represent each team member's expertise as a coverage set over that requirement space.
- Estimate the minimum team size needed to cover all required expertise, then compare with observed team size.
- Test whether knowledge-depth proxies predict larger minimum coverage teams after controlling for field, project scale, and institution type.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Jones (2009) models implementation as requiring all types of foundational knowledge in an application area.
- The model assumes that the inspired innovator forms teams within the cohort when possible and assembles the minimum number of people necessary to implement the idea.
- Jones uses this mechanism to show that narrower individual breadth implies larger teams, especially in deeper knowledge areas and over time when knowledge depth rises.

## Caveats

- Real teams can be larger than the minimum because of labor needs, training, hierarchy, credit allocation, funding requirements, or institutional norms.
- Expertise coverage is hard to observe directly and may require contribution statements, skill embeddings, patents, methods, or topic histories.
- Minimum coverage does not guarantee good coordination, creativity, or equitable credit.
