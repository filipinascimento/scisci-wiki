# Lower-Strata Sponsorship Lift

## Summary

Lower-strata sponsorship lift is the mechanism that elite coauthorship has the largest relative career effect for junior researchers who lack early prestige, productivity, or citation advantages.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: junior researcher, early-career stratum, top-coauthor exposure, discipline, or long-run career outcome.
- Typical representation: treatment effect heterogeneity by early-career excellence group.
- Mechanism: elite collaboration supplies visibility and access that are most scarce for lower-baseline researchers.
- Empirical signature: the relative increase in later success is largest outside the top early-career strata.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds heterogeneity to [early-career sponsorship by top scientists](early_career_sponsorship.md).
- Connects [early-career excellence strata](../representations/early_career_excellence_strata.md) with [coauthor prestige exposure](../measures/coauthor_prestige_exposure.md).

## Operationalization

- Classify junior researchers by early institutional prestige, productivity, and citations.
- Estimate the top-coauthor association separately within each stratum.
- Compare relative and absolute gains in later top-scientist probability and citation impact.
- Check whether the effect persists for papers not coauthored with top scientists later.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Li et al. (2019) reports that top-coauthor effects are largest in less exclusive early-career groups.
- For researchers not in the top 10% of any early-career category, early coauthorship with a top scientist nearly doubles the probability of later top-scientist status.
- The discussion states that top-scientist coauthorship truly makes the difference at lower strata of early-career excellence.

## Caveats

- Selection into elite coauthorship remains a serious confound.
- Lower-strata gains may depend on field, institution type, and access to later opportunities.
- Relative gains can be large even when absolute probabilities remain below elite-strata probabilities.
