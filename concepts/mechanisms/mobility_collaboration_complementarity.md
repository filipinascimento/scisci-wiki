# Mobility-collaboration complementarity

## Summary

Mobility-collaboration complementarity is the mechanism by which physical or institutional movement supports scientific collaboration, so border restrictions can harm collaboration even when remote communication remains possible.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: mobile scientist, collaboration tie, international coauthorship, policy restriction, or research system.
- Typical representation: mobility exposure, collaboration initiation, collaboration persistence, coauthorship change, or post-restriction collaboration loss.
- Mechanism or measurement target: whether mobility and collaboration substitute for each other or reinforce each other.
- Empirical signature: collaboration depends partly on in-person or affiliation-mediated contact, making mobility restrictions reduce network formation or maintenance.

## Operationalization

- Track collaboration formation and persistence before and after mobility events.
- Compare mobile and non-mobile researchers with similar prior productivity, fields, and collaboration histories.
- Analyze policy shocks that restrict mobility while leaving digital communication channels available.
- Distinguish short-term coauthorship continuation from long-term creation of new international ties.
- Combine affiliation histories with [international collaboration networks](../representations/international_collaboration_networks.md) to test whether mobility precedes new edges.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Sugimoto et al. (2017) argues that collaboration can continue under mobility restrictions but that collaboration tends to be initiated and sustained through in-person interactions.
- The paper states that limiting mobility is likely to have adverse effects on a scientific system that increasingly depends on international collaboration.
- Sugimoto et al. use this reasoning to connect travel bans, visa restrictions, and Brexit-style barriers to potential damage in global scientific collaboration and impact.
- The mechanism motivates treating mobility restrictions as shocks to collaboration networks, not only to researcher headcounts.

## Caveats

- Coauthorship is an incomplete measure of collaboration and can lag behind the mobility event that helped create it.
- Remote collaboration tools can weaken or change the mechanism over time.
- Mobility can be endogenous to existing collaboration opportunities, so causal claims need event-study, matched, or quasi-experimental designs.
- The mechanism may differ across fields, facilities, and career stages.
