# Cumulative-success birth-rate timescale

## Summary

Cumulative-success birth-rate timescale translates literature growth into a temporal rate at which each existing success generates further successes.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: success event, citation, publication, growth rate, entity age, or time to additional success.
- Typical representation: annual birth rate of new successes per existing success and its reciprocal timescale.
- Mechanism: cumulative advantage can be interpreted as a pure-birth process where accumulated successes spawn additional successes over time.
- Empirical signature: a growth rate implies an average waiting time for each success to produce a further success.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Gives [cumulative advantage](cumulative_advantage.md) an explicit temporal interpretation.
- Connects [citation pull mechanism](citation_pull_mechanism.md) to growth in the literature.
- Helps separate reinforcement from [citation aging and obsolescence](citation_aging_obsolescence.md).

## Operationalization

- Estimate the growth rate of the relevant literature, citation, or publication system.
- Treat each accumulated success as contributing proportionally to future success production.
- Convert the growth rate into a per-year success birth rate and reciprocal waiting time.
- Compare the implied timescale across fields, eras, or databases.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Price (1976) translates exponential literature growth into a per-success birth rate.
- Price gives an example in which each success produces about 0.07 new successes per year, or roughly one further success every 14 years.
- This temporal reading connects cumulative advantage to observable growth rates.

## Caveats

- The timescale is an aggregate interpretation, not an individual-level prediction.
- Real systems add aging, field growth differences, and attention decay.
- Exponential-growth assumptions can break down over long historical periods.
