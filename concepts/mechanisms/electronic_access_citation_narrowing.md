# Electronic access citation narrowing

## Summary

Electronic access citation narrowing is the mechanism that richer online availability can reduce the breadth and historical depth of cited work when researchers search and follow links instead of browsing through archival context.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: journal-year, subfield-year, citation window, reference set, database interface, or archive-access regime.
- Typical representation: online backfile depth joined to citation age, cited-source breadth, and citation concentration.
- Mechanism: search and hyperlink navigation make salient recent and already-central work, replacing slower print browsing that exposed researchers to adjacent or older material.
- Empirical signature: deeper online availability coincides with more recent references, fewer distinct cited articles or journals, and higher concentration of citations.

## Operationalization

- Join citation-index records to journal online-availability histories by ISSN or another stable source identifier.
- Use source-year data structures such as the [CI-FSO online availability panel](../datasets/ci_fso_online_availability_panel.md) when access histories need to be linked to citing and cited records.
- Measure [online backfile depth](../measures/online_backfile_depth.md) separately for commercial archives and free journal-hosted availability.
- Use [commercial-free access channel contrast](../methods/commercial_free_access_channel_contrast.md) to test whether route-specific availability has similar effects.
- Include [title-word age recency control](../methods/title_word_age_recency_control.md) when average reference age could reflect newer article topics rather than interface behavior.
- Estimate within-journal or within-subfield changes in reference age, distinct cited sources, and concentration as online availability changes.
- Distinguish availability from use where possible by adding search logs, clickstream traces, download data, or interface-level interventions.
- Test whether broader discovery interfaces recover older, lower-consensus, or more diverse cited material.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Evans (2008) uses 34 million articles, citations from 1945 to 2005, and online availability from 1998 to 2005 to evaluate how electronic publication reshaped citation behavior.
- Evans reports that as more journal issues came online, cited articles became more recent, fewer journals and articles were cited, and more citations went to fewer journals and articles.
- The paper's mechanism is behavioral: scientists search electronically and follow hyperlinks more than they browse or peruse, putting them in touch with prevailing opinion more quickly.
- Evans also argues that the loss of forced print browsing removes incidental exposure to older and marginally related work, while hyperlink navigation accelerates convergence on prevailing opinion.
- In the Evans full text, an additional 15 years of online availability predicts a fall in average reference age from about 5.6 years to less than 4.5 years.
- Evans also reports that one additional year of free online issues is associated with 14 percent fewer distinct cited articles, and that 10 additional years of commercial availability predicts a Herfindahl concentration increase from 0.088 to 0.105.
- The result is validated through [online availability panel identification](../validations/online_availability_panel_identification.md), comparing journals and subfields to themselves over time as availability changed.
- A further residual pass adds the access-versus-interface blindspot, core-journal scanning as a current-awareness routine, disciplinary discovery-mode heterogeneity, page/reference-count recency controls, multi-archive robustness, and visible-choice signaling as an amplification microfoundation.

## Caveats

- The Evans design observes availability and citation patterns, not every individual search session.
- Online access can broaden discovery when interfaces promote diversity, full-text search exposes obscure material, or older backfiles are made more visible.
- The original evidence is based on conventional journal citation indexes and does not fully cover blogs, wikis, preprints, books, or newer discovery systems; see [new scholarly-media scope caveat](../validations/new_scholarly_media_scope_caveat.md).
- Citation-indexed references can also omit some supplemental-method references; see [supporting-online-material reference omission](../validations/supporting_online_material_reference_omission.md).
- Discovery behavior and archive multiplicity should be treated as moderators rather than assuming one universal online-access effect.
