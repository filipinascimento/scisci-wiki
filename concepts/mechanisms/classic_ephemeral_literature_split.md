# Classic/ephemeral literature split

## Summary

Classic/ephemeral literature split is the idea that scientific literatures contain durable classic components with long citation lives and ephemeral components that lose citation attention quickly.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, paper cohort, citation half-life class, literature component, or citation-age distribution.
- Typical representation: classic versus ephemeral component mixture, field-specific half-life comparison, or age-stratified citation curve.
- Mechanism: fields differ in how much they rely on durable archival work versus rapidly turning recent-front literature.
- Empirical signature: some fields show long-lived citation to older work, while others concentrate attention in recent papers.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [citation aging and obsolescence](citation_aging_obsolescence.md) by treating aging as a mixture of literature components.
- Connects [research-front citation share](../measures/research_front_citation_share.md) to field differences in citation half-lives.
- Helps interpret [sleeping beauty](sleeping_beauty.md), [delayed recognition](delayed_recognition.md), and [low-citation short lifetime](low_citation_short_lifetime.md).
- Gives [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) a reason to avoid uniform citation windows across fields.

## Operationalization

- Estimate field-level citation-age distributions and citation half-lives.
- Fit or compare mixture components for recent-front, classic, and ephemeral citation behavior.
- Compare fields by the relative mass of long-lived and short-lived citation components.
- Test whether document type, review status, database coverage, or field taxonomy explains component differences.
- Report uncertainty before labeling papers or fields as classic or ephemeral.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Price (1965) cites and extends Burton and Kebler's conjecture that periodical literature may contain classic and ephemeral parts with different half-lives.
- Price argues that the evidence confirms the distinction and suggests that the relative proportions vary by field.
- The paper names mathematics, geology, and botany as more classic-oriented examples, while several engineering areas and physics are described as more ephemeral.
- Price also connects classics to citation incidence by discussing papers cited repeatedly in a year and by later using historical citation matrices to identify full-row classic patterns.
- The same matrix discussion distinguishes classic rows from [review-paper front reset](review_paper_front_reset.md) columns.

## Caveats

- Field-level half-life differences can reflect database coverage, publication type, or citation culture rather than epistemic durability alone.
- A paper can be old and rarely cited because it is obsolete, incorporated, inaccessible, or outside the indexed source base.
- The classic/ephemeral split should be paired with citation-context evidence when used for evaluation.
