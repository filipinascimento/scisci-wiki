# Policy-document production cadence lag

## Summary

Policy uptake can lag because policy documents often have slower production cycles than research articles.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: policy source, report cycle, paper, policy mention, or publication year.
- Typical representation: policy mention lag model with source/report-cycle covariates.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: source-production cadence as a mechanism behind policy mention delays.
- Empirical signature: first policy mentions cluster around slow report cycles rather than immediately after article publication..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [policy mention time lag](../measures/policy_mention_time_lag.md) with source production cadence.
- Useful for interpreting slow-moving assessment reports such as IPCC cycles.
- Connects policy timing to [policy-mention time-area normalization](../methods/policy_mention_time_area_normalization.md).

## Operationalization

- Model first policy mention as a function of source/report cycle, policy document date, publication year, and source fixed effects.
- Separate annual web sources from multi-year assessment report cycles.
- Report lag distributions by source type.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Bornmann et al. (2016) contrasts scientific citation peaks with policy documents such as IPCC reports that appear every six to seven years.
- This supports source cadence as a mechanism behind policy mention timing.

## Caveats

- The motif overlaps policy-mention time lag but isolates the production-cycle mechanism.
- Policy influence may occur before formal report citation.
