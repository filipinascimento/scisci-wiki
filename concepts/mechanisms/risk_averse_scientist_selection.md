# Risk-averse scientist selection

## Summary

Risk-averse scientist selection is the mechanism by which evaluation climates favor researchers who adapt to safe, predictable, and measurable research strategies.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, cohort, lab, training environment, evaluation regime, or career-selection process.
- Typical representation: survival, promotion, funding, or hiring patterns under different incentive climates.
- Mechanism or measurement target: scientists adapt to the reward environment, and those whose strategies fit it survive and reproduce training norms.
- Empirical signature: cohorts shift toward safer topic choices, shorter-horizon outputs, lower variance, or reduced support for exploratory paths.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [indicator systemic effects](indicator_systemic_effects.md) from project choice to career selection.
- Connects [predictable-discovery selection pressure](predictable_discovery_selection_pressure.md) with [scientific ecosystem diversity](scientific_ecosystem_diversity.md).
- Helps explain how evaluation systems can shape the future scientific workforce, not just current publication behavior.
- Provides a mechanism for studying whether training and funding regimes select for exploitative rather than exploratory career strategies.

## Operationalization

- Compare career entry, retention, promotion, and funding outcomes across scientists with different novelty, disruption, interdisciplinarity, and failure profiles.
- Track whether evaluation changes alter the distribution of research strategies among surviving or advancing researchers.
- Link scientists to training lineage, grant types, topics, publication sequences, and risk-reward indicators.
- Use cohort or policy-shock designs to separate selection from within-person adaptation.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Clauset, Larremore, and Sinatra (2017) argues that scientific incentives act like selective pressures.
- The essay warns that scientists adapt or retire as the adaptive landscape changes, transmitting survival strategies to students.
- Clauset et al. describe a possible selection process in which emphasis on predictable discoveries breeds more risk-averse scientists.
- This makes scientist selection a mechanism connecting evaluation policy, training environments, and long-run discovery diversity.

## Caveats

- Observed risk aversion can reflect resource constraints, field maturity, labor markets, or mentorship rather than evaluation climate alone.
- Selection and adaptation are difficult to separate without longitudinal career data and policy variation.
- High-risk behavior is not automatically socially valuable; portfolio design must distinguish productive exploration from poorly supported speculation.
