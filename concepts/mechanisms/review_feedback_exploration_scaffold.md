# Review-feedback exploration scaffold

## Summary

Review-feedback exploration scaffold is the mechanism by which rich, recurring expert critique helps scientists redirect exploratory search before uncertainty turns into wasted effort.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, lab, funding program, review cycle, research portfolio, or exploratory project.
- Typical representation: feedback depth, feedback frequency, reviewer expertise, peer-meeting exposure, and subsequent topic or project redirection.
- Mechanism: high-quality feedback lowers the cost of experimentation by helping researchers identify unfruitful paths, refine uncertain ideas, and reallocate effort.
- Empirical signature: funding or evaluation systems with deeper expert feedback show more effective exploration than otherwise similar systems with sparse, delayed, or formulaic feedback.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [funding autonomy and exploration](funding_autonomy_exploration.md) by separating feedback from funding duration and failure tolerance.
- Gives the [HHMI-NIH incentive contrast](../methods/hhmi_nih_incentive_contrast.md) a concrete process channel beyond money and prestige.
- Connects to [high-touch funding scalability boundary](../validations/high_touch_funding_scalability_boundary.md), because elite feedback consumes scarce reviewer time.
- Complements [renewal-sensitivity incentive validation](../validations/renewal_sensitivity_incentive_validation.md), where feedback quality and renewal sensitivity jointly shape incentives.

## Operationalization

- Code review systems by depth of written critique, oral defense, annual meetings, reviewer expertise, and between-review feedback.
- Distinguish feedback that guides exploration from feedback that mainly enforces compliance with pre-specified deliverables.
- Track whether feedback precedes project pivots, keyword movement, new collaborators, or abandoned unproductive lines.
- Compare matched programs that differ in feedback depth while controlling for funding amount, prestige, and selection.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Azoulay, Graff Zivin, and Manso (2009) presents timely feedback as a core ingredient of exploration incentives.
- The paper argues that feedback lets researchers explore more efficiently and avoid spending too much time on unfruitful ideas.
- In the HHMI-NIH comparison, HHMI review includes oral defenses by elite panels, annual science meetings, critique, encouragement, ideas, and possible collaborations.
- The same text contrasts this with NIH critiques that vary in quality and depth and offer little meaningful feedback between review cycles.
- Azoulay et al. also warn that rich feedback is resource-intensive and may degrade if the program is expanded drastically, linking the mechanism to scalability limits.

## Caveats

- Feedback can make exploration more efficient without necessarily increasing the number of exploratory attempts.
- Expert feedback can also steer scientists toward conventional elite tastes if reviewer pools are narrow.
- Feedback effects are difficult to separate from selection, prestige, peer exposure, and the security provided by long-horizon funding.
