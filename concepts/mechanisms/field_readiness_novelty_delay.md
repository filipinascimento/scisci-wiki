# Field-readiness novelty delay

## Summary

Novel papers may diffuse slowly because the surrounding science, technology, tools, or audience are not yet ready to use them.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: novel paper, enabling method, field audience, citation context, or uptake timeline.
- Typical representation: delayed uptake linked to later enabling science, tools, terminology, or audience formation.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: field-readiness mechanism behind delayed recognition of novel work.
- Empirical signature: uptake accelerates after enabling methods, instruments, or audience communities appear..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects [high-JIF novelty delay interaction](../validations/high_jif_novelty_delay_interaction.md) to [delayed recognition](delayed_recognition.md).
- Useful for distinguishing novelty penalties from premature but valuable ideas.
- Links novelty absorption barriers to field emergence and instrumentation timelines.

## Operationalization

- Link novel papers to later enabling methods, instruments, citation-context shifts, terminology adoption, or field growth.
- Test whether uptake accelerates after those enablers appear.
- Compare novel papers with and without identifiable readiness events.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang et al. (2017) explains delayed uptake of high-JIF novel work as possibly requiring supporting science and technology to develop.
- The paper presents this as a plausible mechanism for novelty delay.

## Caveats

- Wang et al. present this as an explanation, not a directly tested mechanism.
- Readiness can be hard to separate from attention and reputation effects.
