# Faculty gender demographic turnover

## Summary

Faculty gender demographic turnover describes how changes in women's representation among faculty can be driven by differences between incoming hires and departing faculty, even when new-hire shares are flat.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: faculty roster, field, domain, annual hiring cohort, attrition cohort, gender category, or career-age distribution.
- Typical representation: representation trend, new-hire gender share, departing-faculty gender share, demographic-turnover contrast, or age-by-gender profile.
- Mechanism: demographic composition changes through cohort replacement, retirement-age departures, hiring rates, and attrition, not just contemporary hiring parity.
- Empirical signature: overall representation rises when incoming cohorts contain more women than departing cohorts, even if new hires remain below parity or do not improve over time.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Splits a faculty-market mechanism from broad [gender and race stratification](gender_race_stratification.md).
- Links [faculty retention and attrition](faculty_retention_attrition.md), [gendered faculty placement gap](gendered_faculty_placement_gap.md), and long-run parity forecasts.
- Provides a caution for policy evaluation: rising representation does not necessarily prove that current hiring has improved.
- Complements [faculty hiring-attrition dynamic equilibrium](faculty_hiring_attrition_dynamic_equilibrium.md) by showing a different flow logic: representation can change because incoming and departing cohorts differ, even when new-hire shares are flat.

## Operationalization

- Build annual faculty rosters with gender annotations and career-age or doctorate-year information.
- Measure women's representation among all faculty, newly hired faculty, and departing faculty.
- Compare inflow and outflow shares by field and domain.
- Separate demographic turnover from changes in new-hire composition, promotion, and attrition rates.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wapman et al. (2022) reports that women's representation among U.S. tenure-track faculty increased in academia overall, all eight domains, and 80 of 107 fields from 2011 to 2020.
- The paper reports that women's representation among newly hired faculty did not significantly change in 100 of 107 fields and decreased in the remaining seven fields.
- Wapman et al. find that newly hired faculty were substantially more likely to be women than departing faculty in academia overall, all eight domains, and 103 of 107 fields.
- The paper attributes much of the observed representation increase to demographic turnover among departing, retirement-age faculty rather than recent improvements in new-hire shares.
- Wapman et al. caution that future gender parity is unlikely without further change because newly hired faculty remain more likely to be men in academia overall, most domains, and most fields.

## Caveats

- Gender annotation can be missing or misclassified, especially when inferred from names.
- Turnover patterns can reflect retirement age, field history, hiring constraints, climate, and retention, so causal interpretation needs care.
- Binary gender categories miss nonbinary identities and can obscure intersectional differences.
