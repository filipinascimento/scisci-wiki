# Cross-Domain Search-Capacity Constraint

## Summary

Cross-domain search-capacity constraint is the mechanism by which expanding knowledge stocks make unfamiliar domains harder to search, limiting scientists' ability to create useful atypical combinations.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, team, field, paper, or research program.
- Typical representation: boundary-spanning search cost attached to a field or knowledge-space frontier.
- Mechanism: growing domain depth narrows individual expertise and raises the cost of finding useful combinations outside familiar areas.
- Empirical signature: recombinant novelty becomes more dependent on teams, intermediaries, search tools, or conventional anchors as field volume rises.

## Operationalization

- Measure the volume and heterogeneity of potentially relevant external domains.
- Compare solo and team papers on tail novelty, domain distance, and subsequent impact.
- Estimate whether cross-domain search declines as field volume or specialization rises.
- Test whether search infrastructure, diverse teams, or interdisciplinary venues reduce the constraint.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Uzzi et al. (2013) frames boundary-spanning idea production as difficult when scientists become narrower as knowledge expands.
- The paper explicitly names the difficulty of searching unfamiliar domains as a barrier to effective boundary crossing.
- Uzzi et al. connect Web of Science growth across 251 fields to specialization and reduced capacity to comprehend new thinking across domains.

## Caveats

- Search capacity is distinct from evaluation bias, which can penalize novelty even after a combination is found.
- Cross-domain search may be reduced by tools, brokerage, institutional support, or collaborative division of labor.
- Bibliometric domain distance may understate tacit or methodological distance.
