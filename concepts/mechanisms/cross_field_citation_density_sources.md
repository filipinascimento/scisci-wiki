# Cross-field citation-density sources

## Summary

Cross-field citation-density sources are the mechanisms that make some fields produce and receive systematically more citations, including reference-list length, citation customs, and asymmetric cross-field citation flows.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, subject category, reference list, citation flow, or citation baseline.
- Typical representation: field-level citation-density differences before normalization.
- Mechanism: fields differ in how much they cite, how quickly they cite, and how often they cite other fields.
- Empirical signature: raw citation counts are not comparable until field citation-density sources are modeled or normalized.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Explains why [field-normalized citation impact](../measures/field_normalized_citation_impact.md) is necessary.
- Connects cited-side field baselines with [citing-side normalization](../methods/citing_side_normalization.md).
- Provides mechanism-level context for [relative citation performance c_f](../measures/relative_citation_performance_cf.md).
- Helps separate field scale from article quality or influence.

## Operationalization

- Measure mean citation rates by field and publication year.
- Inspect field differences in reference-list length and cross-field citation inflow or outflow.
- Normalize article citations by an appropriate field-year baseline.
- Interpret remaining differences as relative performance rather than raw citation density.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Radicchi, Fortunato, and Castellano (2008) describes large field differences in citation density.
- The paper highlights unequal citation practices and cross-disciplinary citation flows as reasons raw citations are misleading.
- Their normalization framework uses field-specific average citations to compare impact across disciplines.

## Caveats

- Field classifications can be noisy or overlapping.
- Citation-density sources can change over time as fields mature or indexing expands.
- Normalization reduces but does not remove all differences in citation meaning across fields.
