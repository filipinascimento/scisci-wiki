# Industry lifecycle disruption sequence

## Summary

Industry lifecycle disruption sequence is the mechanism in which early inventions in emerging sectors tend to destabilize incumbent capabilities, while later inventions more often consolidate or refine maturing standards.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: industry, technology class, patent cohort, lifecycle phase, or capability regime.
- Typical representation: disruption-consolidation profile over industry age.
- Mechanism: early search unsettles prior approaches, while later development builds on and improves established streams.
- Empirical signature: dynamic-network measures shift from more destabilizing to more consolidating as sectors mature.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [disruption consolidation](disruption_consolidation.md) into an industry-lifecycle hypothesis.
- Links dynamic-network measures to [competency-independent technological effects](competency_independent_technological_effects.md).
- Helps interpret [disruption index](../measures/disruption_index.md) values as historically situated rather than fixed technology traits.
- Provides a bridge between innovation studies and science-of-science novelty measures.

## Operationalization

- Define industry or technology cohorts and their age.
- Estimate disruption or consolidation scores for patents by cohort.
- Compare early, growth, mature, and late phases.
- Test whether lifecycle patterns survive controls for class, citation volume, claims, assignee type, and funding.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Funk and Owen-Smith (2017) discusses how invention effects can differ over industry development.
- The paper links dynamic network measures to whether new inventions destabilize or consolidate existing technological streams.
- It suggests that lifecycle position can shape whether technological change redirects or reinforces prior capabilities.

## Caveats

- Industry boundaries and lifecycle stages can be difficult to define.
- Mature sectors can still experience disruptive shocks, and emerging sectors can consolidate quickly.
- Patent-based timing may lag underlying scientific or market change.
