# Prestige-conditioned cohort-flow attenuation

## Summary

Separates prestige gradients among sitting faculty from those among new hires to reveal whether a pattern reflects current hiring or prior cohorts plus attrition.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: group share by prestige rank for new hires versus existing faculty.
- Typical representation: prestige-conditioned regression coefficients by cohort status and domain.
- Mechanism: observed prestige gradients can be attenuated or amplified by cohort replacement and attrition.
- Empirical signature: group-on-prestige slopes differ between sitting faculty and new hires.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds dynamic interpretation to [institutional prestige hierarchy](institutional_prestige_hierarchy.md).
- Useful for distinguishing current hiring patterns from stock effects accumulated over older cohorts.
- Connects attrition dynamics to self-hiring, international doctorate, and gender representation patterns.

## Operationalization

- Estimate group-on-prestige regressions separately for new hires and existing faculty by domain.
- Compare coefficients and interpret differences as cohort-flow attenuation or amplification.
- Pair with attrition models before making causal claims.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wapman et al. (2022) shows prestige gradients for self-hires and non-U.S. doctorates change between existing faculty and new hires.
- The contrast separates current flow patterns from the standing faculty stock.

## Caveats

- Coefficient gaps are descriptive and can reflect unobserved cohort composition.
- Prestige ranks themselves may be partly shaped by historical flows.
