# False-positive literature persistence

## Summary

False-positive literature persistence is the mechanism by which statistically significant but false findings remain influential because failed replications and null results are difficult to publish and rarely decisive.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: published finding, replication attempt, null result, journal incentive, research program, or policy uptake.
- Typical representation: persistence mechanism, replication-incentive gap, null-publication barrier, or false-positive cost map.
- Mechanism: once a false positive enters the literature, ambiguous failures to replicate and low publication incentives for null or exact-replication results allow it to persist.
- Empirical signature: significant claims receive visibility and follow-on investment while negative evidence is treated as inconclusive or remains unpublished.

## Operationalization

- Track a significant claim and subsequent attempted replications, null results, citations, and policy uptake.
- Compare publication outcomes for exact replications, conceptual replications, and null findings.
- Measure whether failed replications reduce citations, funding, or follow-on studies.
- Audit journals and fields for incentives to publish null or exact-replication evidence.
- Treat persistence as a system property, not only an author-level error.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Simmons et al. (2011) argues that false positives are especially costly because they are persistent once published.
- The paper states that failures to replicate are never conclusive because null results have many possible causes.
- Simmons et al. also note that prestigious journals rarely publish null findings or exact replications, leaving researchers little incentive to attempt them.
- The authors argue that false positives waste resources by inspiring fruitless research programs and potentially ineffective policy changes.

## Caveats

- Persistence can also reflect true effects with boundary conditions, measurement differences, or underpowered replications.
- Citation persistence is not always endorsement; some citations may be critical.
- The mechanism depends on journal norms, field incentives, and replication infrastructure.
- Disclosure and preregistration reduce risk but do not automatically correct already-published false positives.
