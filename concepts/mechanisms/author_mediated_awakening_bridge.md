# Author-mediated awakening bridge

## Summary

Author-mediated awakening bridge is a delayed-recognition mechanism in which later work by the same author reconnects a dormant contribution to active literatures that can cite, reinterpret, or reuse it.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: dormant paper, later same-author paper, co-cited bridge, citing community, or awakening interval.
- Typical representation: same-author bridge chain, co-citation map, pre/post-awakening keyword shift, or citation-context trail.
- Mechanism: authorial continuity preserves or reintroduces a dormant idea until a later community recognizes its relevance.
- Empirical signature: post-awakening citations flow through later works by the same author that are themselves cited by active research fronts.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [awakening trigger citation](awakening_trigger_citation.md) by allowing the trigger to be a same-author bridge sequence rather than a single outside citation.
- Extends [awakening co-citation keyword diagnosis](../methods/awakening_cocitation_keyword_diagnosis.md) with an authorship pathway.
- Complements [affiliation-memory awakening bridge](affiliation_memory_awakening_bridge.md), where institutional memory rather than author continuity preserves the dormant contribution.
- Connects delayed recognition to [citation contexts](../representations/citation_contexts.md), self-citation, reputation, and field migration.

## Operationalization

- Identify a sleeping paper and its awakening time.
- Build pre- and post-awakening co-citation networks for papers citing the sleeping paper.
- Flag later same-author papers that are co-cited with the sleeping paper during awakening.
- Read citation contexts to determine whether the same-author bridge explains the dormant paper's relevance.
- Distinguish author-mediated awakening from ordinary self-citation inflation by checking for uptake by external influential communities.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ke et al. (2015) analyzes Garfield's 1955 citation-index paper, which slept for almost 50 years and became suddenly popular around 2000.
- Ke et al. report that the delayed recognition of the Garfield paper was triggered by later articles by the same author.
- Those later Garfield articles were cited by influential works in two different contexts: Kleinberg's HITS algorithm in network science and Seglen's critique of journal impact factor use in research evaluation.
- The paper also shows a post-2000 keyword shift in citing-paper titles, with impact factor becoming a recognizable contextual marker.

## Caveats

- Same-author bridging should not be equated with causal triggering without citation-context evidence.
- Self-citation can preserve visibility without creating broad community uptake.
- The mechanism is easier to observe for authors with long careers and repeated work on related topics.
- Later same-author papers can also rewrite historical memory in ways that overstate the importance of the dormant paper.
