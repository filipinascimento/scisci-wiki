# Local-global knowledge bridge

## Summary

Local-global knowledge bridge is the mechanism by which locally produced or locally needed research connects to a global science network and returns usable knowledge to local, regional, or national systems.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: researcher, institution, city, country, mobility pathway, collaboration tie, or policy program.
- Typical representation: bridge between local/regional/national systems and a global collaboration or knowledge network.
- Mechanism: global collaboration expands knowledge access, but local benefit depends on people, institutions, and policies that connect remote science back to local users.
- Empirical signature: internationally connected researchers or institutions link local systems to remote expertise, while weak bridging can limit local spillovers.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a policy-relevant mechanism to [international collaboration networks](../representations/international_collaboration_networks.md).
- Links place-based collaboration to [scientific mobility](scientific_mobility.md), [brain circulation networks](../representations/brain_circulation_networks.md), and [geographic collaboration distance](../measures/geographic_collaboration_distance.md).
- Helps evaluate whether internationalization produces local capacity, local knowledge use, or only remote collaboration output.

## Operationalization

- Build collaboration, mobility, affiliation, citation, funding, or training networks across local, regional, national, and global levels.
- Identify actors who connect local institutions to internationally dispersed collaborators or knowledge sources.
- Measure whether global links are followed by local citations, patents, policy use, training, hiring, infrastructure, or follow-on collaborations.
- Compare policy instruments that fund direct link-building with those that make local researchers more attractive collaboration partners.
- Pair bridge analysis with [constructed collaboration sustainability limit](../validations/constructed_collaboration_sustainability_limit.md) when collaboration is mandated by a program rather than self-organized.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wagner and Leydesdorff (2005) frames international collaboration as a communications system operating above national subsystems.
- The paper argues that the global network feeds back into national, regional, and local levels and that knowledge created locally can become available within the global system.
- It also argues that research conducted remotely can be useful locally only when bridges are created, often through people moving between places.
- The policy implication is that collaboration programs should support both global networking and local capture of knowledge spillovers.
- Wagner and Leydesdorff further caution that constructed collaboration needs local scientific and technical capability if global links are to remain sustainable and useful.
- A residual Wagner pass separates [knowledge-creation exploitation-location decoupling](knowledge_creation_exploitation_location_decoupling.md) and [permissive collaboration funding signal](permissive_collaboration_funding_signal.md) as narrower policy mechanisms.

## Caveats

- A bridge can move knowledge, people, data, prestige, or funding; these channels should not be collapsed into one metric.
- Local benefit is hard to measure with publication data alone.
- Policies that maximize international connectedness can still fail if domestic capability, absorptive capacity, or institutional links are weak.
