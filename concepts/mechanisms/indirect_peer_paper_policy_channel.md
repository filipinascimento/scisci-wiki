# Indirect peer-paper policy channel

## Summary

Policy mentions can capture an indirect channel where papers written for scholarly peers are later read and cited by policy actors.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scholarly paper, policy actor, policy reference, advisory link, or policy document.
- Typical representation: policy reference with or without observable advisory/co-production evidence.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: indirect scholarly-paper route into policy use.
- Empirical signature: papers are cited in policy documents even without documented direct advisory interaction..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds an indirect channel to [policy document mentions](../measures/policy_document_mentions.md).
- Useful for separating policy references from face-to-face advice or formal committee participation.
- Connects mention interpretation to [research-policy interaction barrier](research_policy_interaction_barrier.md).

## Operationalization

- Separate policy references with no observable advisory link from cases with committee, consultation, or co-production evidence.
- Use acknowledgments, author affiliations, policy-body rosters, and consultation documents to flag direct channels.
- Interpret residual policy references as possible indirect peer-paper channels, not proof of no contact.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Bornmann et al. (2016) distinguishes direct face-to-face advice from indirect interactions through policy actors reading scholarly papers.
- This supports a mechanism for policy mentions that does not require direct advisory involvement.

## Caveats

- Absence of advisory evidence is not evidence of absence.
- Policy documents may cite papers for symbolic or background reasons rather than direct use.
