# Multiple-discovery redundancy function

## Summary

Multiple-discovery redundancy function is the mechanism by which independent rediscovery of similar ideas increases the probability and speed that a contribution enters the shared body of scientific knowledge.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: discovery, independent discovery report, paper set, field, or communication channel.
- Typical representation: event set of equivalent or near-equivalent discoveries with independent publication and uptake histories.
- Mechanism: redundant reports make a signal harder to lose in communication noise and easier for communities to identify, verify, and reuse.
- Empirical signature: discoveries with multiple independent reports diffuse faster, are cited sooner, or enter reviews/textbooks more reliably than single reports.

## Operationalization

- Identify independent discovery sets from priority disputes, review histories, expert-curated discovery lists, or citation-context clusters.
- Measure the number of independent reports, timing gaps, author status, venues, and later uptake.
- Compare diffusion speed, citation breadth, review incorporation, textbook uptake, or field adoption across single and multiple discovery events.
- Model whether redundancy adds visibility beyond author status, venue, language, and field growth.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Merton (1968) argues that when similar discoveries are made independently, the probability of prompt incorporation into current knowledge increases.
- Merton states that if one version is obscured by communication noise, another version can become visible.
- The same full text poses an optimization problem: estimate how much redundancy increases solution probability before additional repeated effort adds little value.
- Merton then connects redundancy to status, arguing that visibility depends on both the number of independent reports and the standing of the scientists who report them.

## Caveats

- Independent discovery equivalence is historically and conceptually difficult to define.
- Redundancy can accelerate validation and diffusion, but it can also duplicate effort when communication systems already work well.
- Later narratives may overstate independence or collapse distinct contributions into a single discovery label.
