# Interdisciplinary citation delay

## Summary

Interdisciplinary citation delay describes the tendency for some forms of interdisciplinary work to receive less short-term citation attention while accumulating higher long-term citation impact.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, reference-list field distribution, citation window, field, journal, or interdisciplinarity component.
- Typical representation: short-window citations, long-window citations, citation-speed score, citation-delay score, or interaction between interdisciplinarity dimensions and time.
- Mechanism: cognitively distant or cross-field work can take longer to diffuse because audiences, terminology, evaluators, and reuse pathways are spread across fields.
- Empirical signature: higher variety or disparity is associated with lower short-term citations but higher long-term citations.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a temporal mechanism to [interdisciplinarity](interdisciplinarity.md) and [interdisciplinarity dimensions](../measures/interdisciplinarity_dimensions.md).
- Connects [citation aging and obsolescence](citation_aging_obsolescence.md), [delayed recognition](delayed_recognition.md), and [novelty penalty](novelty_penalty.md).
- Provides a policy caution for short-window evaluation of interdisciplinary research.

## Operationalization

- Measure interdisciplinarity components for each paper using referenced fields, topics, or science-map regions.
- Estimate short-term and long-term citation outcomes separately, ideally with journal, field, and year controls.
- Compute citation speed or delay from cumulative citation trajectories.
- Test whether variety, balance, and disparity have different short-term and long-term associations.
- Treat [interdisciplinarity citation-window reversal](../validations/interdisciplinarity_citation_window_reversal.md) as a direct validation check when early and long windows are both available.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Thijs, and Glanzel (2015) analyzes 646,669 papers and finds that variety and disparity have positive associations with 13-year citations but negative associations with 3-year citations.
- Wang et al. also estimate citation-delay models and report that papers higher in variety or disparity are more likely to have delayed citation accumulation.
- The same paper finds that balance has a different pattern: it is negatively associated with long-term citations and less likely to create the same delayed-recognition profile.
- Wang et al. operationalize delay with a [citation speed-delay measure](../measures/citation_speed_delay_measure.md), defined from annual cumulative citation ratios over a 13-year window.
- Their robustness checks also use alternative delay definitions, including the ratio of year-3 to year-13 citations and the year when a paper reaches half of its total citations.
- These results support a longer evaluation window for interdisciplinary work, especially work that combines many or distant fields.

## Caveats

- Citation delay is not necessarily evidence of eventual value; some interdisciplinary work may remain uncited because it fails to connect to usable audiences.
- Results depend on field taxonomy, citation-window choice, and the treatment of multidisciplinary categories.
- Short-window underestimation can be confused with novelty penalties, field normalization errors, or journal audience effects.
