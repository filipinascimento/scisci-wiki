# Prestige-Trap Escape Through Elite Ties

## Summary

Prestige-trap escape through elite ties is the mechanism by which junior researchers at less prestigious institutions may gain long-run visibility and impact through early collaboration with top scientists.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: junior researcher, institution prestige stratum, top-coauthor tie, field, or career trajectory.
- Typical representation: institutional-prestige gradient moderated by top-coauthor exposure.
- Mechanism: elite coauthorship can provide visibility and access that partially offsets lower institutional prestige.
- Empirical signature: less-prestigious institutional strata show strong benefits from early top-scientist collaboration.

## Operationalization

- Score early-career institutional prestige for junior researchers.
- Estimate later career outcomes by prestige stratum and top-coauthor exposure.
- Compare whether top-coauthor exposure changes outcomes most among lower-prestige institutions.
- Interpret results alongside selection into elite ties.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Li et al. (2019) states that junior researchers affiliated with less prestigious institutions benefit most from coauthorship with a top scientist.
- The discussion argues that researchers in less prestigious institutions may escape a prestige trap by connecting with a top scientist.
- The authors interpret this as evidence that less prestigious institutions hold unrealized potential.

## Caveats

- Elite ties may be easier to obtain for already exceptional researchers.
- Prestige-trap escape does not imply equal access to elite collaborators.
- Institution-level prestige may obscure department, advisor, and field-level contexts.
