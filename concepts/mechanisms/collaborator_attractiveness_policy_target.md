# Collaborator attractiveness policy target

## Summary

Network entry into international collaboration depends partly on a researcher or country's attractiveness as a scientific partner.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: researcher, country, partner demand, collaboration tie, capacity indicator, reputation, or policy target.
- Typical representation: partner-attractiveness profile combining capacity, visibility, prior ties, and technical skill.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: network-entry mechanism for international collaboration.
- Empirical signature: countries or researchers with stronger attractiveness signals are selected into more international collaboration ties.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines international-collaboration mechanism by linking it to [scientific capacity network entry threshold](scientific_capacity_network_entry_threshold.md) and [selected collaborator pool diversity](selected_collaborator_pool_diversity.md).
- Useful as a reusable check when [collaboration reward virtuous circle](collaboration_reward_virtuous_circle.md) is used in science-of-science inference.
- Creates cross-links to [permissive collaboration funding signal](permissive_collaboration_funding_signal.md) so the motif is not interpreted in isolation.

## Operationalization

- Measure partner demand through invitations, coauthorship growth, or partner-diversity changes.
- Combine capacity, visibility, prior ties, and technical skill indicators.
- Evaluate whether policy changes affect partner selection through attractiveness rather than only funding supply.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified local full text from Wagner and Leydesdorff (2005) argues that joining international collaboration depends on attractiveness as a partner and that policy should support connected, high-quality research capacity.
- The motif reframes policy as improving network-entry attractiveness.

## Caveats

- Attractiveness policies can reinforce existing elites if used without capacity-building safeguards.
- Partner demand is hard to separate from field mix and historical ties.
