# Laboratory-origin attribution anchor

## Summary

Laboratory-origin attribution anchor is the mechanism by which readers remember or credit a scientific result through its laboratory or senior institutional origin rather than through the specific author list.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: laboratory, senior investigator, author list, paper, reader memory, or attribution statement.
- Typical representation: attribution narrative, acknowledgement trace, lab-origin label, or remembered-source comparison.
- Mechanism: a recognizable lab or senior figure becomes the mnemonic anchor for work produced by less familiar contributors.
- Empirical signature: readers or evaluators describe work as coming from a known laboratory even when they cannot name the actual junior authors.

## Operationalization

- Compare citations, reviews, talks, and biographies for references to author names versus lab-origin labels.
- Track whether work from famous laboratories receives more lab-attributed descriptions than comparable work from less visible labs.
- Separate byline credit, acknowledgement credit, and informal memory of origin.
- Use interviews or citation-context analysis to identify lab-origin attribution.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Merton (1968) describes readers attributing work to a known laboratory or senior figure when the actual author list is less memorable.
- The motif captures a recognition mechanism that operates through organizational origin rather than only through formal coauthorship.
- It helps explain how credit and visibility can concentrate around labs even when junior contributors perform key work.
- This is distinct from generic status bias because the attribution anchor is the lab provenance of the communication.

## Caveats

- Lab-origin attribution can sometimes be accurate when a laboratory provides distinctive resources, training, or intellectual style.
- Informal attribution is difficult to measure without interviews or citation-context evidence.
- The mechanism may vary by field depending on laboratory size, naming conventions, and authorship norms.
