# Contribution archetype career progression

## Summary

Contribution archetype career progression describes how scientists' role profiles are associated with persistence, transitions between career stages, productivity, and citation impact.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, career-stage transition, contribution archetype, publication count, citation-impact share, or survival-to-stage indicator.
- Typical representation: archetype transition matrix, Sankey diagram, stage-specific progression rate, productivity distribution, or highly cited paper share.
- Mechanism: access to leadership or specialized roles can structure career persistence and later output opportunities.
- Empirical signature: leader and specialized profiles progress through career stages more often than support-only profiles, with role-specific productivity and impact differences.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a career-dynamics layer to [task specialization](task_specialization.md).
- Connects [leader-specialized-supporting archetypes](../representations/leader_specialized_supporting_archetypes.md) to [scientific career impact](scientific_career_impact.md).
- Complements team-level mechanisms such as [team hierarchy productivity redistribution](team_hierarchy_productivity_redistribution.md), because role concentration can affect individual opportunity over time.
- Helps [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) distinguish career persistence from raw publication or citation totals.
- Splits support-role persistence into [supporting-archetype career attrition](supporting_archetype_career_attrition.md).
- Connects career transitions to [gendered contribution-archetype gap](gendered_contribution_archetype_gap.md), because role profiles are not demographically neutral.

## Operationalization

- Assign or estimate contribution archetypes for each scientist at each career stage.
- Calculate progression rates from junior to early-career, early-career to mid-career, and mid-career to late-career stages.
- Compare publication counts and share of highly cited papers by archetype within each stage.
- Estimate archetype transitions separately by gender, field, cohort, and contribution-statement coverage when sample size permits.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Robinson-Garcia et al. (2020) reports little attrition from junior to early-career stage, with 93% of junior Specialized and 83% of Supporting authors reaching early career.
- At early career, 84% of Leader-profile scientists reach mid-career, compared with 30% of Specialized and 16% of Supporting scientists.
- From mid-career to late-career, 37% of Leader-profile scientists progress, compared with 1% of Specialized and 2% of Supporting authors.
- The paper reports that 98% of scientists reaching late career had a Specialized archetype in junior stage, and 67% displayed a Leader profile in early- and mid-career stages.
- Robinson-Garcia et al. also report that Leader profiles generally have higher productivity after junior stage, while Specialized scientists are less productive in early- and mid-career stages; differences in highly cited publication share are smaller.
- The same full-text evidence is decomposed in [supporting-archetype career attrition](supporting_archetype_career_attrition.md) for the specific transition-rate penalty and [gendered contribution-archetype gap](gendered_contribution_archetype_gap.md) for demographic role allocation.

## Caveats

- Progression is conditional on surviving in the publication record, so selection and cohort effects are central.
- Contribution roles can be outcomes of institutional position, funding, team structure, gendered opportunity, and field norms.
- Longer careers do not necessarily imply better science or fairer contribution allocation.
