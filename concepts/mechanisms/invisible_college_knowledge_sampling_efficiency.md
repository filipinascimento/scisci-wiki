# Invisible-College Knowledge Sampling Efficiency

## Summary

Invisible-college knowledge sampling efficiency is the mechanism by which teams embedded in broad and diverse collaboration networks access more of a field's knowledge reservoir and produce stronger work.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: team, journal, field, collaboration network, or largest component.
- Typical representation: team access to diverse collaborator neighborhoods in an invisible college.
- Mechanism: network embeddedness samples distributed knowledge more efficiently when collaborators connect large but nonredundant pools.
- Empirical signature: higher-performing journals or teams are associated with larger connected components and less closed repeat collaboration.

## Operationalization

- Measure team members' prior collaborator neighborhoods and their overlap.
- Estimate whether teams or journals with broader nonredundant network access have higher performance.
- Compare access measures with repeat-collaboration fractions and largest-component size.
- Separate knowledge-sampling effects from team size, field growth, and prestige selection.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Guimera et al. (2005) describes the collaboration network as a field-level storehouse of knowledge.
- The paper argues that teams whose members have large but disparate collaborator sets can draw from a more diverse reservoir.
- Guimera et al. report that higher-impact journals in ecology and social psychology tend to have larger giant components, interpreted as better sampling of invisible-college resources.

## Caveats

- Network access can reflect prestige or resource concentration rather than knowledge sampling alone.
- Sparse fields may benefit from reach, while dense fields may face coordination and redundancy costs.
- Coauthorship ties only approximate actual knowledge transfer.
