# Reputation selectivity incentive

## Summary

Reputation selectivity incentive is the mechanism motif that established reputation can create incentives to publish more selectively and emphasize quality over volume.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author, career stage, publication portfolio, reputation level, or output decision.
- Typical representation: selective publishing, quality-over-quantity incentive, reputation protection, top-paper share, or output-volume tradeoff.
- Mechanism target: explain how reputation may alter production strategy after visibility is established.
- Empirical signature: higher-reputation authors reduce marginal output or shift toward fewer, higher-impact works compared with matched peers.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Links [reputation citation premium](reputation_citation_premium.md) to behavior, not only citation reception.
- Connects to [elite publication selectivity signal](elite_publication_selectivity_signal.md), [indicator systemic effects](indicator_systemic_effects.md), and [publication volume-impact decoupling](publication_volume_impact_decoupling.md).
- Offers a mechanism for career-stage changes in productivity and impact portfolios.

## Operationalization

- Model output volume, venue selectivity, and field-normalized impact as functions of lagged reputation.
- Compare within-author changes before and after reputation thresholds.
- Separate strategic selectivity from resource access, team expansion, and topic change.
- Test whether selectivity strengthens in fields where reputation signals are highly visible.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Petersen et al. (2014) links reputation effects and rapid publication opportunities to incentives for quality over quantity.
- The paper suggests that established reputation can alter strategic incentives in the scientific reward system.
- This motif captures a behavioral consequence of reputation, distinct from the citation premium itself.

## Caveats

- Citation impact is an imperfect proxy for quality.
- Selectivity can be confounded by seniority, grants, team size, editorial roles, or reduced publication need.
- Some reputational systems may instead incentivize volume and visibility.
