# Federal funding destabilizing patents

## Summary

Federal funding destabilizing patents describes the association between public research support and university patents that redirect later technological attention away from predecessor streams.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: government-interest patent, university-year grant portfolio, public funding agency, or patent disruption outcome.
- Typical representation: positive association between federal support and `CDt`, `mCDt`, or aggregate destabilizing patent measures.
- Mechanism: peer-reviewed public funding can support more fundamental or exploratory research that opens new technological trajectories.
- Empirical signature: federal support predicts more destabilizing patents while commercial support predicts more consolidating portfolios.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects public science funding to [disruption index](../measures/disruption_index.md) and [university patent portfolio disruption](../measures/university_patent_portfolio_disruption.md).
- Provides a patent-based evidence layer for science-policy debates about transformative research.
- Complements [commercial engagement consolidation](commercial_engagement_consolidation.md) in the same empirical setting.
- Links funding-source design to research novelty, technological change, and responsible metrics.

## Operationalization

- Identify government-interest patents or university grant support from agencies such as NSF, NIH, and DoD.
- Compute patent-level or portfolio-level disruption outcomes.
- Compare federal-support associations with impact-only models and industry-support models.
- Control for technology class, grant year, university fixed effects, scientific output, and patenting experience where possible.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Funk and Owen-Smith (2017) reports positive associations between government-interest patents and both `CD5` and `mCD5` in patent-level regressions.
- In university-level models, increases in support from NSF, NIH, and DoD are linked to more destabilizing patent portfolios.
- The paper contrasts these results with commercial-engagement measures, which are associated with consolidating portfolios.
- Funk and Owen-Smith interpret the findings as evidence that publicly funded academic science can help open new technological trajectories.

## Caveats

- These are observational associations, not clean causal estimates of funding effects.
- Agency programs differ by field, mission, and selection process.
- Destabilization is not the same as quality, welfare, or policy success.
- Public funding can also support consolidating work, especially in mission-oriented or applied programs.
