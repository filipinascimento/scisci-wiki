# Core-team Poisson mode

## Summary

Core-team Poisson mode is a team-formation mechanism in which small scientific teams are assembled around a lead author with team size drawn from a low-rate Poisson process.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, lead author, field, journal, or time period.
- Typical representation: team-size distribution for small `k`, Poisson rate for standard core teams, and a related "core +1" component for mentor-like two-person teams.
- Mechanism: a lead author forms a relatively stable core team whose characteristic size changes slowly over time.
- Empirical signature: the small-team portion of a field's team-size distribution is well fit by one or more Poisson components rather than by the large-team tail.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Gives a generative explanation for the small-team component behind [mean team-size growth](../measures/mean_team_size_growth.md).
- Complements [team assembly mechanisms](team_assembly_mechanisms.md), which model newcomer/incumbent composition and repeat collaboration rather than team-size distributions.
- Separates persistent small-team authorship from [extended-team cumulative-advantage mode](extended_team_cumulative_advantage_mode.md).
- Splits the shifted two-author excess into [core+1 mentor component](core_plus_one_mentor_component.md).
- Supplies a component for [team-size distribution decomposition](../methods/team_size_distribution_decomposition.md) and [authorship-mode contribution share](../measures/authorship_mode_contribution_share.md).
- Provides the small-team side of [team core-turnover survival strategy](team_core_turnover_survival_strategy.md), where stable cores support persistence.
- Helps interpret [coauthorship networks](../representations/coauthorship_networks.md) without treating every coauthorship team as generated by one process.

## Operationalization

- Build a field- and period-specific distribution of authors per article.
- Fit the lower-team-size region with a Poisson component for standard core teams.
- If two-author papers are overrepresented relative to single-author papers, include a shifted "core +1" Poisson component.
- Track the fitted Poisson rates over time to distinguish gradual growth in small core teams from the emergence of large-team tails.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Milojevic (2014) identifies small core teams as one of two principal modes of scientific knowledge production.
- The paper models each lead author as associated with a core team whose size is drawn from a Poisson distribution, with the lead author alone when the draw yields one.
- In astronomy, the 1961-1965 team-size distribution is described as close to Poisson, while later distributions retain a low-`k` hook that requires core-team components even after large teams emerge.
- Milojevic adds a "core +1" component to account for the excess of two-author papers, plausibly including mentor-coauthor behavior by early lead authors.
- The fitted decomposition for recent astronomy assigns a majority of papers to standard core teams, with a smaller share to core +1 teams and the rest to extended teams.
- Longitudinal fitted rates are tracked in [authorship-mode parameter trajectories](../measures/authorship_mode_parameter_trajectories.md).

## Caveats

- A Poisson fit is a model of the size distribution, not direct observation of how each team negotiated membership.
- The core +1 interpretation can mix mentor relationships, peer dyads, and field-specific authorship norms.
- Field boundaries and group-author handling can change the apparent small-team distribution.
