# Biomedical output-scale obsolescence

## Summary

Biomedical output-scale obsolescence is the mechanism motif that fast publication growth and discovery pace can shorten citation attention spans in biomedical fields.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, publication cohort, citation trajectory, half-life, or output-growth regime.
- Typical representation: high publication volume, fast discovery frontier, short citation half-life, or biomedical obsolescence.
- Mechanism target: explain why even highly cited papers can lose annual citation attention faster in rapidly expanding literatures.
- Empirical signature: citation half-life declines as field output scale, growth, and replacement pace increase.

## Operationalization

- Estimate field-year publication volume, growth rate, and citation-density change.
- Regress citation half-life or decay parameters on output scale after field and cohort controls.
- Compare biomedical subfields with slower-growing theory or proof-heavy fields.
- Test whether highly cited papers in high-output fields show faster post-peak decay.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Petersen et al. (2014) contrasts short cell-biology citation half-lives with longer mathematical and physical-science trajectories.
- The paper attributes part of the biomedical difference to the large output scale and faster pace of discovery.
- This motif separates field-growth obsolescence from paper-level reputation effects.

## Caveats

- Biomedical citation half-life also reflects clinical relevance, review practices, funding, and database coverage.
- Fast obsolescence can indicate progress or turnover, not necessarily lower value.
- Field definitions need stable boundaries across time.
