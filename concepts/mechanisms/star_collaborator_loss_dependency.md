# Star-Collaborator Loss Dependency

## Summary

Star-collaborator loss dependency is the mechanism by which a scientist's productivity and impact depend on continued collaboration with exceptionally strong partners.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, dyad, coauthor relationship, career trajectory, or collaboration network.
- Typical representation: loss shock or exposure to a high-status/high-impact collaborator.
- Mechanism: star collaborators provide resources, visibility, expertise, and network access whose removal lowers output.
- Empirical signature: productivity drops after losing a star collaborator, especially when the lost collaborator was a regular coauthor.

## Operationalization

- Identify strong collaborators by citation impact, awards, status, or prior output.
- Define loss events such as death, retirement, departure, or permanent coauthor separation.
- Estimate changes in focal productivity, impact, topic breadth, and collaboration structure after the loss.
- Compare effects by regular versus occasional coauthorship.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) states that collaborators can have large effects on scientific careers.
- The review reports that scientists who lose star collaborators experience substantial productivity drops, especially when the lost collaborator was a regular coauthor.
- Fortunato et al. also report that papers involving extremely strong collaborators gain more citations on average.

## Caveats

- Strong collaborators can be proxies for resources, institution, field, or selection into high-quality projects.
- Loss events may coincide with career stage, topic shifts, or institutional moves.
- The mechanism should be separated from mentoring, sponsorship, and ordinary coauthor prestige exposure.
