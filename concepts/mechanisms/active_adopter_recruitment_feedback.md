# Active-adopter recruitment feedback

## Summary

Active-adopter recruitment feedback is the mechanism in which current active participants directly increase further recruitment into a scientific field.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: active author, recruited susceptible author, field-population model, contact process, or recruitment term.
- Typical representation: feedback from infected or active class to recruitment rate, coupled differential equation, or active-class-dependent entry term.
- Mechanism: visible active researchers attract, train, persuade, or mobilize additional participants.
- Empirical signature: growth accelerates because the active population expands the pool or flow of new entrants, not only because susceptible authors convert at a fixed contact rate.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [field population models](../methods/field_population_models.md) and the [scientific SEIR contact model](../methods/scientific_seir_contact_model.md) with recruitment feedback.
- Connects [scientific field recruitment rate](../measures/scientific_field_recruitment_rate.md) to field-level social visibility and institutionalization.
- Gives [field emergence](field_emergence.md) a mechanism for self-reinforcing participant growth.
- Helps distinguish active recruitment from passive conversion of a fixed [scientific susceptible-author pool](../representations/scientific_susceptible_author_pool.md).

## Operationalization

- Add a model term where the active or infected class affects recruitment into the susceptible or exposed pool.
- Test whether active-class feedback improves fit compared with fixed recruitment and fixed contact-rate models.
- Validate with evidence of training programs, workshops, new grants, labs, curricula, or public field-building activity.
- Compare fields where active adopters have high or low ability to recruit new participants.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Bettencourt et al. (2008) proposes adding a feature in which the size of the infected class facilitates further recruitment by directly linking the active class to the recruitment term.
- The authors present this as an extension to the basic SEIR model for scientific fields.
- This makes recruitment feedback a distinct mechanism from contact-based conversion alone.

## Caveats

- Feedback can be difficult to identify from aggregate growth curves because it can mimic high contact rates or expanding susceptible pools.
- Active researchers may recruit through institutions, funding, or visibility; the mechanism should be specified when possible.
- Feedback can saturate or reverse if a field becomes crowded or resource-limited.
