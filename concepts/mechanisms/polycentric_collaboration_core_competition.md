# Polycentric collaboration-core competition

## Summary

Polycentric collaboration-core competition is the mechanism by which multiple advanced scientific countries form a dense collaborative core while competing for ties with smaller or peripheral national systems.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: country, country pair, collaboration core, regional hub, or international coauthorship edge.
- Typical representation: international collaboration network with several high-degree centers rather than one dominant center.
- Mechanism: leading national systems cooperate in dense core ties while competing to attract productive partners and peripheral links.
- Empirical signature: core countries are densely connected, regional hubs grow, and smaller systems link to multiple potential centers.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [international collaboration networks](../representations/international_collaboration_networks.md) beyond single center-periphery accounts.
- Links [country collaboration core](../measures/country_collaboration_core.md) and [regional collaboration hubs](../measures/regional_collaboration_hubs.md) to a competitive partner-choice mechanism.
- Helps interpret [advanced-country collaboration growth check](../validations/advanced_country_collaboration_growth_check.md).

## Operationalization

- Build country-country collaboration networks over time.
- Identify dense cores, regional hubs, and peripheral country attachment patterns.
- Test whether core growth is concentrated around one center or distributed across multiple high-capacity countries.
- Compare raw collaboration volume with normalized bilateral intensity.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wagner and Leydesdorff (2005) argues that international collaboration is not adequately described by a single center-periphery model.
- Their analysis emphasizes multiple centers, regional hubs, and competition among leading countries for partners.
- The paper frames international collaboration as a complex network with dynamics of both cooperation and competition.

## Caveats

- Country-level cores can hide institutional and author-level heterogeneity.
- Core competition can coexist with historical ties, geographic proximity, and policy programs.
- Peripheral attachment should be normalized for country size and publication capacity.
