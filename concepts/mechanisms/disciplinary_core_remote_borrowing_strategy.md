# Disciplinary-core remote-borrowing strategy

## Summary

Disciplinary-core remote-borrowing strategy is the pattern in which high-impact interdisciplinary work keeps a strong disciplinary core while selectively borrowing knowledge from remote disciplines.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, reference list, discipline, subject-category distribution, or interdisciplinary research strategy.
- Typical representation: dominant-core share plus remote-category references, balance score, disparity score, or novelty-conventionality combination.
- Mechanism: a disciplinary core provides recognizable grounding and evaluative audience, while remote borrowing supplies novelty and recombination.
- Empirical signature: high-impact papers combine conventional disciplinary embedding with some distant knowledge rather than distributing attention evenly across many fields.

## Operationalization

- Estimate whether a paper has one dominant subject category or disciplinary core.
- Measure the share of references outside that core and the cognitive distance of those outside categories.
- Compare impact for core-plus-remote patterns against evenly balanced multi-field patterns.
- Control for journal, field, reference-list size, and team characteristics.
- Interpret balance and disparity separately rather than using one composite interdisciplinarity score.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Thijs, and Glanzel (2015) argues that highly cited interdisciplinary papers may be embedded in a disciplinary core while borrowing knowledge from remote disciplines.
- The authors contrast this with balanced interdisciplinary papers without a disciplinary core, which are relatively less cited in their results.
- Their policy discussion states that the most effective impact-generating strategy may be one disciplinary core plus borrowing from other disciplines rather than drawing evenly from multiple disciplines.

## Caveats

- Citation impact may reward recognizable disciplinary cores even when more balanced work is socially valuable.
- The mechanism may differ across fields, journals, and evaluation regimes.
- Core-plus-remote patterns can be hard to distinguish from superficial citation borrowing.
