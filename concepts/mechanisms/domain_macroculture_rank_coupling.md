# Domain macroculture rank coupling

## Summary

Domain macroculture rank coupling is the tendency for institutional prestige ranks to correlate across fields, suggesting shared domain-level cultures, status signals, resources, or cohort histories.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: university, field, domain, faculty hiring network, prestige rank, or rank correlation.
- Typical representation: cross-field rank-correlation matrix or domain-level clustering of prestige orderings.
- Mechanism: fields within a domain may share status signals, elite resources, hiring norms, and institutional histories that align prestige hierarchies.
- Empirical signature: prestige ranks inferred separately by field remain strongly correlated across fields.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a cross-field mechanism to [institutional prestige hierarchy](institutional_prestige_hierarchy.md).
- Helps explain why [faculty prestige centrality-insularity coupling](faculty_prestige_centrality_insularity.md) can recur across related fields.
- Suggests that [prestige-core norm diffusion](prestige_core_norm_diffusion.md) may operate at domain as well as field scale.

## Operationalization

- Infer prestige ranks separately for multiple fields.
- Compute rank correlations across fields and within broad domains.
- Test whether correlations exceed null expectations from shared university size or coverage.
- Interpret cross-field coupling alongside historical, resource, and status-signaling explanations.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wapman et al. (2022) reports strong correlations among field prestige ranks.
- The discussion proposes domain-level macrocultures, status signaling, elite resources, and cohort effects as possible latent drivers.
- The motif treats these cross-field correlations as a mechanism candidate rather than only a descriptive network result.

## Caveats

- Rank coupling does not identify which latent driver is causal.
- Shared data coverage, university size, or historical prestige can produce correlation without active norm diffusion.
- Domain-level patterns may hide field-specific exceptions.
