# Topic success-rate funding mediation

## Summary

Topic success-rate funding mediation is the mechanism by which funding disparities arise partly because minoritized applicants are concentrated in research topics with lower award success rates.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: applicant, proposal topic, funding decision, demographic group, or award success rate.
- Typical representation: topic-choice mediation path between identity and funding outcome.
- Mechanism target: how topic portfolios translate demographic inequality into funding inequality.
- Empirical signature: group differences in award rates shrink after accounting for proposal topic success rates.

## Operationalization

- Infer or code proposal topics.
- Estimate award success rates by topic.
- Model demographic funding gaps before and after topic controls.
- Test mediation while preserving intersectional categories.
- Check whether lower-success topics are underfunded, riskier, or simply evaluated differently.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Kozlowski et al. (2022) cites evidence that Black NIH applicants were more likely to propose topics with lower success rates.
- The paper uses this as motivation for studying identity, topic choice, and scientific outcomes together.
- The motif generalizes the idea that topic portfolios can mediate institutional resource gaps.

## Caveats

- Topic mediation does not prove that applicants freely choose low-success topics.
- Review bias and topic underinvestment can both create lower topic success rates.
- Proposal-topic data are often less available than publication-topic data.
