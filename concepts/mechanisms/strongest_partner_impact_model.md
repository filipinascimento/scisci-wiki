# Strongest-partner impact model

## Summary

Strongest-partner impact model is the mechanism in which the citation impact of a cross-status collaboration tends to be pulled more toward the higher-status partner's baseline than toward the lower-status partner's baseline.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, collaboration pair, institution tier pair, citation outcome, or field-year cell.
- Typical representation: tier-pair citation-impact table, cross-tier premium estimate, or comparison with upper-tier and lower-tier within-school baselines.
- Mechanism: high-status partners contribute resources, visibility, selection, or signaling that can lift lower-status collaborators without being symmetrically diluted.
- Empirical signature: mixed-tier collaborations perform between the two within-tier baselines and closer to the stronger partner's baseline.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [between-school impact premium](between_school_impact_premium.md) by asking which partner drives the premium.
- Links citation returns to [university tier mixing matrices](../representations/university_tier_mixing_matrix.md), not only to the presence of cross-institutional authorship.
- Provides an impact-side mechanism for [status-assortative collaboration](status_assortative_collaboration.md) and [elite-access stratification trend](elite_access_stratification_trend.md).
- Helps test whether collaboration with elite institutions diffuses advantage or further concentrates it among already central institutions.

## Operationalization

- Estimate citation outcomes for within-tier collaborations and cross-tier collaborations in the same field, year, and team-size frame.
- For each mixed-tier pair, compare the observed outcome with both the upper-tier and lower-tier within-school baselines.
- Test whether the mixed-tier outcome is closer to the upper-tier baseline, lower-tier baseline, or a symmetric average.
- Reestimate with alternative status definitions, citation windows, and field controls.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Jones et al. (2008) compares citation impact across university-tier combinations for two-university papers from 1995 to 2005.
- The paper reports that cross-tier collaboration impact generally lies between the upper-tier and lower-tier within-school baselines.
- Jones et al. interpret the pattern as closer to a strongest-partner model than a weakest-partner model: high-tier partners pull lower-tier impact up more than lower-tier partners pull high-tier impact down.
- This mechanism helps explain why access to elite collaboration matters even when lower-tier institutions also increase cross-university collaboration.

## Caveats

- The model can reflect selection into collaboration rather than causal influence of the stronger partner.
- Tier baselines may confound institutional status with field specialization, funding, seniority, journal access, and project quality.
- Citation outcomes do not reveal which collaborator supplied the decisive contribution.
- Cross-tier collaborations with more than two universities require an explicit rule for defining the strongest partner.
