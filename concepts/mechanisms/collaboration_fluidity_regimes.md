# Collaboration Fluidity Regimes

## Summary

Collaboration fluidity regimes describe how fields move between closed repeat-collaboration systems, high-turnover newcomer systems, and intermediate fluid collaboration patterns.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: collaboration network, field, journal, or team-formation regime.
- Typical representation: repeat incumbent-incumbent link fraction interpreted as a dynamical regime.
- Mechanism: repeated incumbent ties stabilize networks, while lower repeat fractions create turnover and entry opportunities.
- Empirical signature: high repeat-link fractions imply slow structural change; low fractions imply fast turnover; intermediate values imply fluid peer collaboration.

## Operationalization

- Compute repeat incumbent-incumbent link fractions within fields or journals.
- Classify regimes as closed, fluid, or high-turnover using empirical or model-derived thresholds.
- Track how regime membership changes over time or across field subcommunities.
- Compare regimes with innovation, entry, stability, and citation outcomes.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Guimera et al. (2005) states that high repeat-link fraction means established collaborations and slow structural change.
- The paper states that low repeat-link values correspond to high turnover and fast dynamics.
- Guimera et al. interpret intermediate values as fluid peer collaboration and describe a balance between incumbents and newcomers with fresh ideas.

## Caveats

- Fluidity is not automatically better; some fields require stable infrastructure, trust, or long-term coordination.
- The same repeat-link fraction can arise from different institutional causes.
- Regime classification depends on clean author disambiguation and consistent team definitions.
