# Affiliation-memory awakening bridge

## Summary

Affiliation-memory awakening bridge is the mechanism where local institutional memory helps reconnect a dormant paper to an active field during a delayed-recognition event.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: sleeping paper, trigger citation, citing author, remembered prior work, affiliation overlap, or local expert network.
- Typical representation: case narrative linking a trigger citation to shared affiliation, colleague recall, or institutional proximity.
- Mechanism: local social memory makes an earlier overlooked contribution findable or citable when the field becomes ready for it.
- Empirical signature: an awakening trigger is accompanied by evidence that someone near the active research front remembered or recognized the dormant work.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [awakening trigger citation](awakening_trigger_citation.md) by naming a social-memory channel rather than only the first post-sleep citation.
- Links [sleeping beauty](sleeping_beauty.md) and [delayed recognition](delayed_recognition.md) to institutional context, co-location, and informal knowledge transmission.
- Complements [Mendel-syndrome prematurity audit](../validations/mendel_syndrome_prematurity_audit.md), which asks whether historical evidence supports an "ahead of time" interpretation.
- Suggests case-level evidence to collect alongside citation counts: affiliations, acknowledgments, author comments, correspondence, and citation contexts.

## Operationalization

- Identify the trigger citation or early post-sleep citing papers.
- Compare affiliations of sleeping-paper authors, trigger-paper authors, and relevant colleagues around the awakening.
- Read citation contexts and historical comments to determine whether local memory or co-location plausibly helped surface the dormant work.
- Treat affiliation memory as a case mechanism, not a population-level proof unless many cases are coded consistently.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from van Raan (2004) describes Romans's 1986 supergravity paper as the most extreme sleeping beauty in the study.
- The paper identifies Polchinski's 1995 paper as the first citing paper after ten years and calls this the "prince" citation.
- van Raan reports that comments from the involved scientists indicated the field later became ready for the dormant contribution.
- The same case notes that a colleague of the trigger-paper author remembered the Romans paper, likely helped by the shared affiliation with the sleeping beauty at the Institute for Theoretical Physics, University of California at Santa Barbara.

## Caveats

- Shared affiliation can make recall plausible but does not prove causality.
- Institutional memory is visible mainly in well-documented cases and may be missing from ordinary citation metadata.
- The mechanism can coexist with field readiness, rediscovery, review attention, or cross-disciplinary reuse.
