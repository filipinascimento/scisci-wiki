# Author-metric resource-competition use case

## Summary

Author-level metrics are often motivated by scarce-resource allocation, where jobs, promotions, grants, or honors require comparisons among many candidates.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: evaluation setting, candidate pool, metric, and resource allocation decision.
- Typical representation: metric-assisted ranking or screening process.
- Mechanism: scarce resources create demand for compact, comparable author indicators.
- Empirical signature: metric use rises where evaluator attention is scarce and candidate pools are large.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Places [h-index](../measures/h_index.md) inside the institutional demand for evaluation shortcuts.
- Connects metric design to [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md) and safeguards against metric-only decisions.
- Useful for coding whether a paper proposes a metric for description, prediction, or formal allocation.

## Operationalization

- Code the resource setting, evaluator, candidate pool, decision stakes, metric role, and presence of qualitative review.
- Separate descriptive reporting from threshold-based screening or ranking.
- Audit whether the metric is used as evidence, a tie-breaker, or a hard gate.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hirsch (2005) motivates author metrics by limited resources and explicitly mentions recruitment, advancement, and grant allocation contexts.
- This use case explains why simple author indicators can become policy instruments beyond their original statistical definition.

## Caveats

- A descriptive motivation is not an endorsement of metric-only allocation.
- High-stakes use can induce gaming, field bias, and systemic effects that the original metric paper may not evaluate.
