# High-impact gatekeeper topic suppression

## Summary

High-impact journal incentives can suppress locally specific topics by rewarding research framed for global English-language gatekeepers.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: research topic, local-language output, high-impact journal, field, or evaluation regime.
- Typical representation: venue-incentive pathway from high-impact publishing pressure to topic portfolio shifts.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: loss of locally relevant research under high-impact publication incentives.
- Empirical signature: local empirical or policy topics decline as researchers reframe work for high-impact international venues..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a topic mechanism to [impact-factor publication pressure](impact_factor_publication_pressure.md).
- Connects [locally relevant research metrics](../validations/locally_relevant_research_metrics.md) to topic portfolios.
- Relevant for societal-impact and policy-attention analyses.

## Operationalization

- Compare local-language topic portfolios with high-impact English portfolios.
- Track shifts from local empirical problems to abstract or foreign-data topics.
- Audit whether local policy or community topics disappear under venue incentives.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Hicks et al. (2015) argues that pluralism and societal relevance can be suppressed when researchers reshape work for high-impact English-language journals.

## Caveats

- The manifesto's examples are diagnostic and need field-specific topic coding for empirical use.
- Some local topics also benefit from international publication.
