# Collaboration-mediated long-range mobility

## Summary

Collaboration-mediated long-range mobility is the mechanism in which scientific collaboration networks help scientists make geographically distant institutional moves that would be less likely under ordinary distance friction.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, career move, collaboration tie, origin institution, destination institution, or city pair.
- Typical representation: long-tailed move-distance distribution linked to collaboration networks.
- Mechanism: collaborators transmit information, credibility, opportunities, and trust across distance, lowering the cost of long-range relocation.
- Empirical signature: scientific career moves have a heavier long-distance tail than general human mobility and resemble long-range collaboration patterns.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a mechanism for [career mobility distance decay](../measures/career_mobility_distance_decay.md).
- Connects career mobility to [geographic collaboration distance](../measures/geographic_collaboration_distance.md).
- Links institutional movement with [international collaboration networks](../representations/international_collaboration_networks.md).
- Helps explain why scientific labor markets can be more spatially extended than everyday mobility.

## Operationalization

- Measure move distances from geocoded affiliation trajectories.
- Construct collaboration ties before each observed move.
- Test whether prior collaborators, collaborator institutions, or collaborator cities predict long-distance destinations.
- Compare move-distance exponents with collaboration-distance and general mobility baselines.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Deville et al. (2014) reports that scientific career moves decay with distance more slowly than general human mobility.
- The paper notes that city-level collaboration networks show similarly long-range patterns, suggesting collaboration as a plausible channel.
- This mechanism links the observed distance distribution to the social structure of science rather than only to geography.

## Caveats

- Collaboration and mobility can both be caused by field prestige, funding, language, or institutional rank.
- Coauthorship captures only part of the collaboration network.
- Long-range moves can be driven by national job-market structures or elite recruitment without direct prior collaboration.
