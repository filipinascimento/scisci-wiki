# Legitimate recent self-citation mechanisms

## Summary

Legitimate recent self-citation mechanisms are non-misconduct reasons why a journal may cite its recent own articles more often than older own articles.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: journal, topic, recent paper, follow-up paper, editorial scope, citation window, or diffusion community.
- Typical representation: qualitative mechanism list, field-context audit, or alternative explanation for high IFBSCP.
- Mechanism: recent related work can be legitimately concentrated within the same journal because of follow-up research, topical fit, or within-community diffusion.
- Empirical signature: recent same-journal citations align with coherent research continuity rather than editorial coercion or metric-targeting.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides the main caveat layer for [impact factor biased self-citation practices](../measures/impact_factor_biased_self_citation_practices.md).
- Prevents overinterpretation of [citation metric gaming](../validations/citation_metric_gaming.md) flags.
- Defines the benign contrast class for [author preemptive journal self-citation](author_preemptive_journal_self_citation.md) and [editorial impact-factor pressure channel](editorial_impact_factor_pressure_channel.md).
- Supports responsible evaluation by requiring qualitative follow-up after high IFBSCP or [IFBSCP threshold-exceedance share](../measures/ifbscp_threshold_exceedance_share.md) signals.
- Acts as a necessary check inside [IFBSCP first-diagnosis workflow](../methods/ifbscp_first_diagnosis_workflow.md).

## Operationalization

- Inspect whether recent self-citations point to substantive follow-up, method validation, special-issue continuity, or recent topic concentration.
- Compare cited-paper topics, article types, editorial interventions, and author explanations where available.
- Treat high IFBSCP as a screening signal, then separate plausible legitimate continuity from coercive or strategic behavior.
- Report uncertainty rather than converting anomaly scores directly into misconduct labels.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Chorus and Waltman (2016) explicitly states that high IFBSCP does not prove unethical behavior by editors or authors.
- They identify legitimate scenarios: readers may conduct follow-up research after seeing a recent article, authors may submit to a journal because their finalized reference list shows recent related work there, and new ideas may initially diffuse inside the community where they were presented.
- The paper argues that these mechanisms can create a stable aggregate baseline IFBSCP around 1.4 before the post-2004 rise.
- Chorus and Waltman therefore recommend using IFBSCP for first diagnosis followed by further scrutiny, not for journal-specific accusations.

## Caveats

- Legitimate mechanisms can coexist with coercive or strategic citation behavior.
- Qualitative follow-up can be labor-intensive and may require editorial correspondence, article-type information, and topic-level evidence.
- The boundary between topical fit and strategic self-citation can be difficult to observe from citation counts alone.
