# Citation pull mechanism

## Summary

Citation pull mechanism is Price's interpretation that future citations are statistically drawn by a cited paper's past citation history more than pushed independently by each new citing paper.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: cited paper, citation history, citing paper, initial citation pulse, field, or citation index window.
- Typical representation: prior-citation-dependent hazard, initial pulse size, cumulative-advantage trajectory, or pull-versus-push explanation.
- Mechanism: past citation success makes a paper more visible or available for later citation, creating a self-reinforcing demand for the cited work.
- Empirical signature: later citation histories depend strongly on early citation pulses and accumulated citations.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Gives [preferential attachment](preferential_attachment.md) a citation-specific interpretation.
- Connects [single-edged success contagion](single_edged_success_contagion.md) to the behavioral question of who or what generates citation events.
- Helps explain [attention inequality](attention_inequality.md) without assuming that every citing author independently evaluates all possible prior work.
- Provides an early conceptual ancestor for [paper fitness](../measures/paper_fitness.md) and [citation trajectory models](../methods/citation_trajectory_models.md), which later separate early uptake, aging, and persistent quality.
- Feeds into [citation-productivity feedback](citation_productivity_feedback.md) when cited-paper reception changes an author's later publication continuation.

## Operationalization

- Reconstruct paper citation histories from publication onward.
- Estimate how later citation rates depend on prior citation counts after controlling for paper age, field, journal, and topic.
- Model the size of the initial citation pulse and its relationship to long-run trajectory.
- Compare pull models based on cited-paper history with push models based on citing-paper reference-list behavior.
- Use citation contexts to distinguish substantive pull from ritualistic or token citations.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Price (1976) argues that immediately after publication a paper receives an initial pulse of citations as peers weigh it at the research front.
- Price states that this pulse probably determines most future citation history, except for delayed discovery, rediscovery, or new applications.
- In the epilogue, Price says future citation successes appear statistically determined by the past history of the cited paper.
- Price therefore suggests citations are generated by a pull mechanism from previous citation rather than a push mechanism from the papers doing the citing.
- The paper also links early citation reception to author productivity, suggesting feedback from cited-paper success into continued publication at the research front.

## Caveats

- Price frames the pull mechanism as an interpretation that needs more empirical testing.
- Citation behavior also depends on citing-paper goals, field norms, review practices, databases, and social networks.
- Strong early pull can reflect quality, visibility, prestige, topic size, or venue effects.
- Delayed recognition and rediscovery are explicit exceptions to a simple initial-pulse model.
