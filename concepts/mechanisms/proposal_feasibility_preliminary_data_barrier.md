# Proposal feasibility preliminary-data barrier

## Summary

Proposal feasibility preliminary-data barrier is the mechanism in which review criteria emphasizing feasibility, preliminary evidence, and track record push competitive grants toward safer projects and away from novel or junior-led work.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: grant proposal, review criterion, applicant, project idea, review panel, or funding decision.
- Typical representation: preliminary-data requirement, feasibility score, applicant track-record measure, novelty score, or review outcome.
- Mechanism: reviewers reduce uncertainty by favoring projects with demonstrated feasibility, which can penalize projects without enough prior evidence.
- Empirical signature: novelty or early-career disadvantage is stronger where preliminary data and past performance carry high weight.

## Operationalization

- Extract proposal review criteria or reviewer comments that mention preliminary results, feasibility, risk, or applicant record.
- Measure project novelty and applicant status before funding decisions.
- Test whether feasibility or track-record criteria mediate novelty penalties, especially for junior, female, peripheral, or low-status applicants.
- Compare submitted proposals with funded projects where proposal-denominator data are available.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Lee, and Walsh (2018) identifies preliminary data and track record as barriers for junior researchers in competitive funding.
- The paper links past-performance funding and feasibility expectations to conventionality pressure.
- Their selection-versus-treatment discussion separates pre-award review barriers from post-award changes in project behavior.

## Caveats

- Proposal texts and review criteria are often unavailable, so publication-output studies infer this mechanism indirectly.
- Feasibility criteria can prevent waste and are not always anti-novelty.
- Barrier strength depends on grant size, review culture, resubmission rules, and field norms.
