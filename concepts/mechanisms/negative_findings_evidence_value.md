# Negative findings evidence value

## Summary

Negative findings evidence value is the motif that null or non-significant results can be useful evidence, especially when they test important claims, refute broad concepts, or reveal the denominator of attempted research.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: negative finding, null result, study, claim family, field, or evidence synthesis.
- Typical representation: null-result record, failed confirmation, registered negative outcome, or field-level evidence denominator.
- Mechanism: visible negative findings reduce selective attention to positives and improve estimation of what is likely to be true.
- Empirical signature: interpretation changes when negative results are available alongside positive claims.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Complements [selective reporting and publication bias](publication_bias_selective_reporting.md) by treating missing negative results as lost evidence.
- Supports [positive predictive value of findings](../measures/positive_predictive_value_findings.md) because PPV depends on the full tested relationship set, not only published positives.
- Connects to [coordinated failure-publication regime](../methods/coordinated_failure_publication_regime.md) and replication workflows.
- Provides a justification for registered reports, complete reporting, and evidence synthesis that includes null results.

## Operationalization

- Record non-significant, null, mixed, and failed-confirmation outcomes in registries or public repositories.
- Include negative findings in systematic reviews and meta-analyses rather than treating them as absence of information.
- Track the ratio of positive to negative claims within a field and by design class.
- Distinguish uninformative underpowered negative results from well-powered refutations of important claims.
- Report whether negative findings test narrow claims or broader conceptual families.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Ioannidis (2005) states that "negative" research is useful and that the term negative is itself a misnomer.
- The essay focuses on claimed positive relationships but explicitly warns that interpreting only formal significant positives is ill-founded.
- Ioannidis argues that large-scale negative evidence can refute not only a specific proposed claim but also a broader field or substantial portion of it.
- The same paper's PPV framework depends on knowing the full testing context, which makes missing negative findings a source of distorted inference.

## Caveats

- A negative result can be uninformative when a study is underpowered, biased, or poorly measured.
- Null findings should not be converted into claims of no effect without uncertainty and minimum-effect interpretation.
- The value of negative evidence depends on design quality and the importance of the tested claim.
