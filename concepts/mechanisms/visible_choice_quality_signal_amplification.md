# Visible Choice Quality-Signal Amplification

## Summary

Visible choice quality-signal amplification is the mechanism by which visible citation, link, or reading choices become perceived quality signals that amplify small attention differences.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, citation, hyperlink, reader choice, or recommendation signal.
- Typical representation: microfoundation for cumulative attention and consensus formation.
- Mechanism: agents observe others' choices, infer quality from those choices, and feed that signal into their own reading and citation decisions.
- Empirical signature: small initial differences can grow when choices are visible and reused as evidence of quality.

## Operationalization

- Identify where users can observe prior choices, such as citation links, download counts, rankings, or recommendation lists.
- Measure whether visible prior attention predicts later reading or citation beyond topical relevance.
- Compare interfaces with and without visibility of prior choices.
- Distinguish quality-signal amplification from direct access or search-index effects.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Evans (2008) links extreme inequality in hyperlinks, citations, and preferential attachment to the amplification of near-random quality differences.
- Evans argues that agents treat others' choices as relevant information and factor them into their own reading and citation selections.
- The paper uses this mechanism to explain how electronic journals can speed convergence with prevailing opinion while causing some ideas to be forgotten quickly.

## Caveats

- Prior choices can contain real quality information as well as popularity bias.
- Visibility effects may depend on ranking design, field norms, and user expertise.
- The mechanism should be tested separately from content availability and topical relevance.
