# Evaluation-pressure sequence misalignment

## Summary

Evaluation-pressure sequence misalignment is the mechanism motif that publish-or-perish pressure and specialization-oriented assessment can conflict with the exploratory phase that may precede high-impact exploitation.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: career phase, evaluation regime, productivity pressure, specialization norm, or entropy sequence.
- Typical representation: publish-or-perish pressure, premature specialization, evaluation window, exploration suppression, or sequence misalignment.
- Mechanism target: explain how evaluation systems can disrupt explore-then-exploit career dynamics.
- Empirical signature: stronger short-term output pressure is associated with reduced exploration before focused high-impact periods.

## Operationalization

- Measure productivity pressure using tenure clocks, grant-review criteria, publication quotas, or evaluation-policy text.
- Link those measures to career entropy sequences and hot-streak onset.
- Compare institutions, fields, or policy periods with different tolerance for exploration.
- Test whether pressure accelerates exploitation before sufficient exploration.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Liu et al. (2021) discusses tensions among pressure to publish, specialization, career exploration, and evaluation systems favoring specialized productivity.
- The paper's empirical result that exploration followed by exploitation is enriched around hot-streak onset motivates the sequence-misalignment mechanism.
- This motif is a discussion-level mechanism requiring direct policy validation.

## Caveats

- Evaluation pressure can also supply resources or deadlines that support focused exploitation.
- Career entropy measures may not capture all forms of intellectual exploration.
- Causal identification requires variation in evaluation regimes or credible quasi-experiments.
