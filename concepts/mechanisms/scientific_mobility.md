# Scientific mobility and brain circulation

## Summary

Scientific mobility studies how researchers move across institutions, regions, countries, and sectors, and how those moves reshape collaboration, productivity, topics, and recognition.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist career, institution, country, sector, or collaboration tie.
- Typical representation: longitudinal affiliation trajectory or mobility network.
- Mechanism: mobility transfers knowledge, social ties, training, prestige, and access to resources.
- Empirical signature: affiliation changes linked to changes in collaboration, impact, topics, or institutional networks.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Used to study global science, brain drain/circulation, institution prestige, and career inequality.
- Connects coauthorship networks to stratification and field diffusion.
- Important for disentangling place effects from researcher selection.

## Operationalization

- Build author-disambiguated affiliation histories and infer moves over time, auditing [homonym and synonym author errors](../validations/homonym_synonym_author_errors.md).
- Measure origin-destination flows, return mobility, collaboration retention, and post-move outcomes.
- Use matched designs when comparing mobile and non-mobile scientists.
- When evaluating policy restrictions, pair mobility-flow measures with [mobility policy shock evaluation](../validations/mobility_policy_shock_evaluation.md).
- Separate current mobility, accumulated [international experience prevalence](../measures/international_experience_prevalence.md), destination concentration, and return intentions when survey data make those distinctions observable.

## Evidence and Validations

- Franzoni, Scellato, and Stephan's verified Nature Biotechnology full text uses a cross-country survey of research scientists in 16 countries to compare immigration and emigration patterns for foreign-born scientists.
- The same GlobSci evidence now supports split-out motifs for [GlobSci survey](../datasets/globsci_survey.md), [core-country scientist panel](../datasets/core_country_scientist_panel.md), [country-at-18 origin proxy](../methods/country_at_18_origin_proxy.md), [origin-destination mobility matrix](../representations/origin_destination_mobility_matrix.md), [foreign-born scientist share](../measures/foreign_born_scientist_share.md), [scientific emigration rate](../measures/scientific_emigration_rate.md), [scientist return mobility rate](../measures/scientist_return_mobility_rate.md), and [scientific mobility motivation taxonomy](../methods/scientific_mobility_motivation_taxonomy.md).
- A later Franzoni pass separates [international experience prevalence](../measures/international_experience_prevalence.md), [scientific destination-hub dominance](../measures/scientific_destination_hub_dominance.md), [job-conditioned return intention](../measures/job_conditioned_return_intention.md), and [attraction-return policy asymmetry](attraction_return_policy_asymmetry.md) from the broader mobility page.
- Verified full-text evidence from Deville et al. (2014) reconstructs institutional career trajectories from Physical Review affiliations, recording 5,915 career movements and showing that moves are common but usually infrequent and concentrated early in careers.
- Deville et al. report stratification in institutional moves: transitions are overrepresented within elite institutions and within lower-ranked institutions, while cross-group moves are underrepresented relative to a null model preserving origin and destination totals.
- Deville et al. also quantify the number of visited institutions, career timing of moves, geographic distance decay of moves, and normalized citation change around moves.
- Sugimoto et al.'s verified Nature full text uses records for 14 million papers and nearly 16 million individuals from 2008 to 2015, classifying about 96% of researchers as non-mobile and about 4% (more than 595,000 researchers) as internationally mobile.
- The same full text supports finer mobility motifs: first-publication country as scientific origin, migrant versus traveler distinctions, country circulation networks, country producer/cultivator/recruiter/incubator roles, and mobile scholar citation premiums.
- A second Sugimoto pass separates mobility entry-cohort windows, non-directional traveler affiliation patterns, regional net balances, pre/post-move citation role decomposition, mobility-collaboration complementarity, and central-country displacement under isolationist policy.
- A later Sugimoto limitation pass separates academic-origin ambiguity, pre-mobile right censoring, journal-article mobility coverage bias, within-country mobility omission, and capacity-contingent benefits of redirected talent.
- A residual Sugimoto pass separates route-specific high-impact corridors, training-mediated return circulation, dynamic exchange indicators, and impact-weighted policy leverage from the broad mobility mechanism page.
- The motif should separate mobility measurement from causal mobility effects: affiliation histories can show flows, but impact changes require controls for selection, career stage, field, and [institutional prestige hierarchy](institutional_prestige_hierarchy.md).

## Caveats

- Affiliation histories are noisy and often missing.
- Mobility effects are confounded by selection, career stage, and institution prestige.
