# Publication-overload attention competition

## Summary

Publication-overload attention competition is the mechanism in which rapid growth of the literature forces papers to compete for notice and makes readers rely more heavily on selection cues.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: reader, paper, field literature, author name, journal, selection cue, or attention budget.
- Typical representation: publication-volume growth paired with readership, citation, or cue-use measures.
- Mechanism: as the volume of publications grows, readers cannot inspect everything and use names, venues, institutions, or topics as attention filters.
- Empirical signature: low average readership or citation visibility combined with stronger effects of author, venue, or institutional cues.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides the overload ecology behind [article readership reach rate](../measures/article_readership_reach_rate.md).
- Explains why [recognition-visibility coupling](recognition_visibility_coupling.md) becomes more consequential as the archive expands.
- Connects [author name visibility signal](author_name_visibility_signal.md) to literature growth rather than treating it as a static bias.
- Links Mertonian recognition dynamics to modern attention inequality and information overload.

## Operationalization

- Measure publication growth and reader or citation attention per paper.
- Estimate whether status cues become stronger predictors of attention as literature volume rises.
- Compare fields with different growth rates, browsing practices, and search infrastructure.
- Use citation contexts, downloads, reference lists, or reading logs to observe cue-mediated attention.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Merton (1968) links exponential publication growth to the need for selective attention cues.
- Merton argues that author identity and recognition can help determine which communications are noticed in an overloaded literature.
- This motif separates the overload mechanism from the downstream visibility advantage of eminent names.
- It also connects readership limits to cumulative advantage in scientific communication.

## Caveats

- Search engines and recommendation systems can change which cues readers use.
- Publication overload can also increase reliance on topic keywords, review articles, or social recommendation rather than author status.
- Attention competition should not be interpreted as proof that ignored work lacks quality.
