# Instrument-scale collaboration pressure

## Summary

Instrument-scale collaboration pressure is the mechanism by which increasingly large, precise, and costly scientific instruments push researchers and institutions toward collaboration as a resource-pooling strategy.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: instrument, facility, team, institution, field, or research problem.
- Typical representation: resource-pooling mechanism, big-science collaboration pressure, or instrument-cost coordination demand.
- Mechanism: tools needed to expand the frontier exceed what individual investigators or institutions can afford alone.
- Empirical signature: larger instruments and facilities are associated with larger teams, multi-institutional participation, and higher reproducibility burdens.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Connects [burden of knowledge](burden_of_knowledge.md) to material and infrastructural cost.

## Operationalization

- Track instrument cost, facility scale, precision requirements, and institutional participation.
- Measure whether team size and country or institution count increase with instrument scale.
- Compare resource-pooling collaboration with collaboration driven by intellectual diversity alone.
- Audit whether replication or reproduction requires duplicating expensive infrastructure.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) says the instruments required to expand the knowledge frontier have increased in scale and precision.
- The review states that such tools become unaffordable to most individual investigators and institutions, making collaboration a critical solution.
- It uses the Large Hadron Collider as an example and links big science to a reproducibility burden when duplication is infeasible.

## Caveats

- Not all collaboration growth is instrument-driven.
- Resource pooling can create governance, credit, and access asymmetries.
- Big-science facilities can enable large problems while narrowing participation to well-resourced groups.
