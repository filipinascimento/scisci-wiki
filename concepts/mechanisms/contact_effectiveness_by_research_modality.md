# Contact effectiveness by research modality

## Summary

Contact effectiveness by research modality is the mechanism by which idea transmission is easier in some research modes than others because the cost of learning, reproducing, or practicing the work differs.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: field, research modality, contact rate, active adopter, susceptible researcher, or transmission event.
- Typical representation: fitted contact-effectiveness parameter, modality strata, theoretical-experimental contrast, or adoption-rate comparison.
- Mechanism: pencil-and-paper, computational, laboratory, clinical, and engineering fields differ in how much infrastructure and training are needed before contact produces active participation.
- Empirical signature: theoretical fields show higher effective contact parameters than experimental or infrastructure-heavy fields, with mixed fields in between.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Adds a modality interpretation to [scientific SEIR contact model](../methods/scientific_seir_contact_model.md) parameters.
- Links field-growth dynamics to [burden of knowledge](burden_of_knowledge.md), training requirements, equipment access, and lab organization.
- Helps compare theoretical, biomedical, experimental, and technological fields without treating all contact events as equivalent.

## Operationalization

- Classify fields by research modality, such as theoretical, computational, experimental, clinical, or mixed.
- Fit field-population models and compare contact-effectiveness parameters across modality classes.
- Validate high or low contact-effectiveness estimates with independent evidence about training duration, infrastructure, lab formation, and entry barriers.
- Avoid interpreting contact parameters without field-boundary, recruitment, and active-period estimates.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Bettencourt et al. (2008) compares fitted contact-effectiveness values across six fields.
- The paper reports that the two purely theoretical fields, cosmological inflation and cosmic strings, show the greatest contact effectiveness.
- Fields mixing theoretical and experimental components, such as quantum computing and H5N1 influenza, have intermediate values, while more purely experimental fields, prions and carbon nanotubes, have the smallest values.
- Bettencourt et al. interpret this pattern through the practical difference between mastering a pencil-and-paper technique and building or overseeing a laboratory group.

## Caveats

- Contact-effectiveness parameters are model-dependent and can trade off with recruitment, active-period, and susceptible-pool assumptions.
- Field modality is not fixed; fields can shift from theory-heavy to experiment-heavy or infrastructure-heavy over time.
- Publication-count data alone cannot reveal the full training, equipment, or institutional costs of adoption.
