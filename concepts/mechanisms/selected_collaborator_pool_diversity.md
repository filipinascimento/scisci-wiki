# Selected collaborator-pool diversity

## Summary

Selected collaborator-pool diversity is the mechanism by which international weak-tie collaboration draws value from a large, reputation-visible pool of potential collaborators already filtered by the scientific system.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: collaborator pool, weak tie, international partner, reputation signal, field network, or scientific system.
- Typical representation: large pool of eligible partners with known reputations and diverse methods or ideas.
- Mechanism: globally visible, system-selected collaborators offer enough diversity for novelty while retaining credibility and findability.
- Empirical signature: weak international ties are easy to make and sever, and partners are chosen from a broad but already reputation-filtered pool.

## Uses in Science of Science

## Operationalization

- Define potential collaborator pools by field, country, visibility, and prior output.
- Measure diversity across countries, methods, topics, institutions, or prior collaborators.
- Estimate whether weak-tie international collaborations draw from visible and selected partners rather than random scientists.
- Compare novelty, productivity, and persistence for selected-pool ties versus local or repeated ties.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wagner and Leydesdorff (2005) argues that weak international ties expose teams to new ideas and methods.
- The paper states that the international pool of potential collaborators is large enough to offer diversity while already selected by the scientific system.
- It also argues that small-world structure makes reputations visible enough for scientists to seek each other out when collaboration is useful.

## Caveats

- System selection can reproduce elite and regional inequality.
- Diversity in the potential pool does not guarantee diversity in actual teams.
- Reputation-visible pools may exclude emerging scientists, non-English venues, and undercovered regions.
