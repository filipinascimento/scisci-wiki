# Economic-to-multidimensional impact shift

## Summary

Societal-impact assessment has moved from economic return metrics toward broader multidimensional qualitative and quantitative evidence.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: assessment framework, impact dimension, evaluation period, or policy context.
- Typical representation: historical coding of impact dimensions in evaluation frameworks.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: shift from economic-return framing to multidimensional societal impact.
- Empirical signature: later frameworks include clinical, policy, cultural, educational, environmental, or institutional dimensions beyond economic returns..

## Uses in Science of Science

- Places [societal impact case studies](../methods/societal_impact_case_studies.md) in historical assessment context.
- Useful for comparing REF-style broader impact with older R&D return studies.
- Connects target-oriented metrics to multidimensional impact evaluation.

## Operationalization

- Code evaluation frameworks over time for economic, clinical, policy, cultural, educational, environmental, and institutional dimensions.
- Track whether evidence types are qualitative, quantitative, or mixed.
- Relate indicator adoption to evaluation policy changes.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Bornmann et al. (2016) contrasts early R&D and economic-return studies with broader REF-style impact assessment.
- The overview motivates a shift mechanism in societal-impact evaluation.

## Caveats

- This is a historical framing, not a single empirical variable in the Bornmann dataset.
- Broader dimensions can increase comprehensiveness while reducing comparability.
