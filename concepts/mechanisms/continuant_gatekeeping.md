# Continuant gatekeeping

## Summary

Continuant gatekeeping is the mechanism by which persistent, highly connected scientists mediate access to collaboration networks, field information, and reputation opportunities for newcomers and transient authors.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: author, coauthor tie, subfield, collaboration opportunity, or career-stage role.
- Typical representation: high-degree continuant hub, mediated tie, gatekeeper role, or newcomer-to-continuant access path.
- Mechanism: established continuants attract collaboration requests, select among potential partners, and transmit credibility, information, and visibility.
- Empirical signature: newcomer, transient, and terminating authors often connect through continuants, while continuants occupy hub positions in the collaboration network.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Splits a social-selection mechanism out of [continuant collaborators](continuant_collaborators.md).

## Operationalization

- Classify authors by publication continuity before, during, and after a focal year.
- Build the coauthorship network and identify whether continuants occupy high-degree or brokerage positions.
- Estimate whether newcomer, transient, or terminating authors connect disproportionately through continuants.
- Test whether coauthoring with continuants changes later visibility, publication access, citation outcomes, or field retention.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wagner and Leydesdorff (2005) draws on continuant, transient, newcomer, and terminator author categories.
- The paper reports that continuants mediate coauthorship relations among other author categories and reinterprets continuants as hubs in active small-world networks.
- Wagner and Leydesdorff argue that newcomers and transients seek access to senior collaborators' social and technical information, recognition, and reflected reputation, while continuants can choose among collaboration opportunities.
- The paper concludes that continuants mediate entry into international collaboration networks and help create subfield-level dynamics.
- A residual Wagner pass separates this mechanism into [continuant collaboration-cost asymmetry](continuant_collaboration_cost_asymmetry.md), [controlled-access group information](controlled_access_group_information.md), and [selected collaborator-pool diversity](selected_collaborator_pool_diversity.md).

## Caveats

- Gatekeeping requires evidence about selection and access, not only high degree.
- Continuant status depends on the observation window, field pace, and database coverage.
- In large-team or facility-based fields, hub positions can reflect infrastructure or role specialization rather than gatekeeping power.
