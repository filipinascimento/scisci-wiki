# Multi-university collaboration stratification

## Summary

Multi-university collaboration stratification is the pattern that research teams increasingly span institutional boundaries while high-impact cross-institutional collaboration remains concentrated among elite institutions.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, team, institution pair, university tier, field, or time cohort.
- Typical representation: [between-school collaboration share](../measures/between_school_collaboration_share.md), within-school versus between-school authorship categories, [university tier mixing matrix](../representations/university_tier_mixing_matrix.md), or institutional collaboration network.
- Mechanism: teams cross university boundaries to access complementary expertise, resources, and search capacity, but prestige and opportunity structures shape who collaborates with whom.
- Empirical signature: rising between-university authorship, citation advantage for high-tier between-school teams, and assortative or stratified collaboration by institutional rank.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Extends [team impact advantage](team_impact_advantage.md) from author count to institutional composition.
- Links [coauthorship networks](../representations/coauthorship_networks.md) to [institutional prestige hierarchy](institutional_prestige_hierarchy.md), because the same team edge can also encode institutional status.
- Provides a mechanism connecting collaboration growth to geographic dispersion and unequal access to high-impact centers.
- Helps evaluate whether broadening collaboration decentralizes science or concentrates high-impact production among already advantaged institutions.

## Operationalization

- Classify each paper by authorship structure: solo, single-university team, or multi-university team, then track the resulting [between-school collaboration share](../measures/between_school_collaboration_share.md).
- Assign institutions to rank tiers or prestige groups, then estimate within-tier and cross-tier collaboration rates.
- Compare field/year-normalized citation impact for within-school and between-school teams, with separate estimates by institutional tier combination and [between-school impact premium](between_school_impact_premium.md).
- Track geography by distance or region between institutions, and stratification by observed-versus-expected tier-pair frequencies.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Jones et al. (2008) analyzes 4.2 million papers over three decades across science and engineering, social science, and arts and humanities.
- The extracted text reports that teamwork increasingly spans university boundaries, that multi-university collaborations are the fastest-growing authorship structure, and that top-tier universities play a dominant role in the shift.
- The paper reports that multi-university teams produce the highest-impact papers when they include a top-tier university and that collaboration becomes increasingly stratified by in-group university rank.
- The same evidence supports split-out motifs for [geographic reach-frequency decoupling](geographic_reach_frequency_decoupling.md), [university tier mixing matrices](../representations/university_tier_mixing_matrix.md), [status-assortative collaboration](status_assortative_collaboration.md), and the [random tier matching baseline](../validations/random_tier_matching_baseline.md).
- The same full-text cluster also supports the [major U.S. university WoS panel](../datasets/major_us_university_wos_panel.md), [authorship structure taxonomy](../measures/authorship_structure_taxonomy.md), [between-school collaboration field-universality check](../validations/between_school_collaboration_field_universality.md), [communication-technology jump test](../validations/communication_technology_jump_test.md), [strongest-partner impact model](strongest_partner_impact_model.md), [elite-access stratification trend](elite_access_stratification_trend.md), and [regional elite remote-partnership maps](../representations/regional_elite_remote_partnership_maps.md).
- Jones et al. (2008) therefore turns a general team-size trend into an institutional mechanism: collaboration growth can expand geographic reach while concentrating high-impact production in fewer elite centers.
- Verified full-text evidence from Wuchty et al. (2007) supplies the broader team-science baseline, while Jones et al. (2008) adds the institutional geography and stratification layer.

## Caveats

- University rank categories can be crude and historically contingent.
- Multi-university collaboration can reflect consortia, facilities, grants, student-advisor ties, or mobility rather than a single partner-selection mechanism.
- Citation advantage may reflect selection into collaboration rather than causal returns to crossing institutional boundaries.
- Institution disambiguation and address parsing are core data-quality risks.
