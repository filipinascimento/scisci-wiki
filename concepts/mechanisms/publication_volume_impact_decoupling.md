# Publication-volume impact decoupling

## Summary

Publication-volume impact decoupling is a career outcome pattern where groups produce similar numbers of papers but diverge in high-impact output.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, cohort, treatment group, publication count, or hit-paper outcome.
- Typical representation: paired productivity and impact time series.
- Mechanism target: separates quantity of output from concentration of high-impact work.
- Empirical signature: publication volume is comparable while hit-paper probability or citation impact differs.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [early-career setback and resilience](early_career_setback_resilience.md) by separating productivity from high-impact success.
- Qualifies [survivor-conditioned setback advantage](../validations/survivor_conditioned_setback_advantage.md).
- Links [hit-paper probability](../measures/hit_paper_probability.md) to broader [citation impact indicators](../measures/citation_impact_indicators.md).

## Operationalization

- Track publication counts and impact outcomes for matched or quasi-experimental groups.
- Compare short and long windows separately.
- Test whether group differences are driven by more papers or by higher impact conditional on publication.
- Report both mean publication output and tail-impact measures.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Jones, and Wang (2019) compares near-miss and narrow-win NIH applicants after early-career funding decisions.
- The paper finds similar publication counts in key windows but higher hit-paper outcomes among persistent near misses.
- This makes the result a quality-or-impact divergence rather than a simple productivity story.

## Caveats

- Citation impact can reflect field, topic, and visibility differences.
- Similar publication volume does not prove equal effort or equal research resources.
- Attrition means the pattern is conditional on scientists who remain active.
