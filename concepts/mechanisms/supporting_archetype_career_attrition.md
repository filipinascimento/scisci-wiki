# Supporting-archetype career attrition

## Summary

Supporting-archetype career attrition is the career-dynamics motif in which scientists assigned to support-heavy contribution profiles progress through publication-career stages less often than scientists in leader or specialized profiles.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, career-stage transition, contribution archetype, survival-to-stage indicator, or publication-career history.
- Typical representation: archetype transition rates, stage-to-stage Sankey diagrams, attrition curves, or conditional progression probabilities.
- Mechanism: support roles can provide fewer visible leadership signals, fewer independent opportunities, or less evaluable credit, reducing persistence in the publication record.
- Empirical signature: support-profile scientists are less likely to reach later career stages after conditioning on the same prior career stage.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Makes [contribution archetype career progression](contribution_archetype_career_progression.md) more explicit about attrition.
- Connects [task specialization](task_specialization.md) to [scientific career impact](scientific_career_impact.md) and [responsible metrics](../measures/responsible_metrics.md).
- Provides a role-based counterpart to [early-career setback and resilience](early_career_setback_resilience.md), where career persistence is also conditioned on early trajectories.
- Helps evaluation systems distinguish support labor from low productivity.

## Operationalization

- Assign contribution archetypes at each career stage.
- Estimate the probability of moving from junior to early-career, early-career to mid-career, and mid-career to late-career stages.
- Compare transition probabilities by archetype, gender, cohort, and field.
- Pair attrition rates with productivity and citation-impact distributions to avoid interpreting survival as the only valuable outcome.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Robinson-Garcia et al. (2020) reports that scientists in Leader and Specialized archetypes tend to have longer careers than scientists in the Supporting archetype.
- In the junior-to-early-career transition, 93% of Specialized authors and 83% of Supporting authors reach early career.
- In the early-to-mid-career transition, 84% of Leader-profile scientists reach mid-career, compared with 30% of Specialized and 16% of Supporting scientists.
- In the mid-to-late-career transition, 37% of Leader-profile scientists progress, compared with 1% of Specialized and 2% of Supporting scientists.
- The paper argues that a uniform leadership-oriented career model can disadvantage scientists whose labor is specialized or supporting, especially when those roles are demographically patterned.

## Caveats

- Attrition is measured through publication histories, so departures from the indexed publication record may include career moves, field moves, name-disambiguation errors, and cohort censoring.
- Support roles may include essential labor that contribution statements underdescribe.
- A lower transition rate does not establish an individual-level causal penalty without additional design.
