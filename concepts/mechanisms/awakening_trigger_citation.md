# Awakening trigger citation

## Summary

Awakening trigger citations are early post-sleep citations that reconnect a neglected paper to an active research community and start a delayed-recognition cascade.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: sleeping paper, first post-sleep citing paper, citing author, affiliation tie, or citation-context bridge.
- Typical representation: first-citation-after-sleep event, trigger paper metadata, citation context, and subsequent citation trajectory.
- Mechanism: a later work makes a dormant contribution visible, usable, or memorable to a now-ready field.
- Empirical signature: a low-cited paper receives a pivotal citation after a long sleep, followed by a sharp increase in attention.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Provides a case-level mechanism for [sleeping beauty](sleeping_beauty.md) and [delayed recognition](delayed_recognition.md).
- Complements aggregate [awakening probability function](../measures/awakening_probability_function.md) estimates with citation-context evidence.
- Can operate through [affiliation-memory awakening bridge](affiliation_memory_awakening_bridge.md) when local institutional memory helps surface the dormant paper.
- Helps distinguish field readiness from pure database artifacts or random citation noise.
- Can be studied with citing-paper metadata, citation contexts, co-citation shifts, and affiliation histories.
- Has bridge variants such as [author-mediated awakening bridge](author_mediated_awakening_bridge.md), where the trigger pathway runs through later work by the same author.

## Operationalization

- Identify the first or earliest citing papers after a defined sleep period.
- Read citation contexts and compare them with the sleeping paper's original contribution.
- Track whether later citations flow through the trigger paper, topic, field, or author network.
- Record whether the trigger paper is a rediscovery, application, review, methodological extension, or cross-field bridge.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from van Raan (2004) names the first citing paper after ten years as the "prince" for the most extreme sleeping beauty in the study.
- The paper identifies Polchinski's 1995 paper as the trigger citation for Romans's 1986 supergravity paper.
- van Raan reports author and expert comments indicating that the field later became ready for the dormant contribution and that a colleague remembered the earlier paper.
- The same case notes that shared affiliation at the Institute for Theoretical Physics, University of California at Santa Barbara, may have helped make the remembered link possible.
- This case connects a citation-history awakening to historical and social evidence about rediscovery and field readiness.
- Verified full-text evidence from Ke et al. (2015) broadens trigger analysis with Garfield's 1955 citation-index paper: later same-author articles bridged the dormant paper into HITS and impact-factor-debate literatures around 2000.

## Caveats

- The first post-sleep citation is not always the causal trigger; it may only be the first indexed trace of a broader shift.
- Trigger attribution requires full-text citation contexts and historical evidence, not only citation timing.
- Some awakenings are gradual or distributed across many citing papers.
