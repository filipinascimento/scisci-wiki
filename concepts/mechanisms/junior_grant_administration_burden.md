# Junior grant-administration burden

## Summary

Junior grant-administration burden is the post-award management, reporting, and rigid-fund-use load that competitive grants can impose more heavily on early-career researchers.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: funded project, junior PI, grant rules, management task, or research time.
- Typical representation: mechanism linking competitive grants to reduced creative time or flexibility for low-status researchers.
- Mechanism target: why funding receipt can still constrain novelty after selection.
- Empirical signature: administrative and compliance demands crowd out exploration, especially for researchers with less staff, authority, or experience.

## Operationalization

- Measure grant reporting, budgeting, hiring, and fund-use restrictions by PI career stage.
- Compare time allocation and project changes for junior and senior funded researchers.
- Test whether administrative burden mediates the relationship between competitive funding and novelty.
- Separate application burden from post-award administration.
- Compare programs with flexible versus restrictive spending rules.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Lee, and Walsh (2018) discusses post-award management and rigid grant-use demands as possible burdens for junior researchers.
- The paper treats these burdens as mechanisms that can reduce the novelty of competitively funded work for lower-status scientists.
- The discussion complements the main finding that competitive funding's positive association with novelty is concentrated among high-status researchers.
- It points to administrative capacity as part of funding inequality, not only proposal evaluation.

## Caveats

- Administrative burden may be hard to separate from project complexity.
- Junior researchers can also gain resources and visibility from grants.
- Burden effects are likely moderated by institutional grant support.
