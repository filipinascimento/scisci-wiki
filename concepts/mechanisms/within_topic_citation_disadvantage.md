# Within-topic citation disadvantage

## Summary

Within-topic citation disadvantage is the pattern that papers by some demographic groups receive fewer citations than comparison groups even when compared inside the same topic distribution.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: topic, demographic group, paper, first author, citation outcome, or field-year cell.
- Typical representation: topic-sorted group citation curves, topic fixed-effect models, within-topic citation ratios, or group-by-topic citation residuals.
- Mechanism: prestige networks, recognition bias, institutional placement, collaboration access, evaluation systems, and visibility differences affect attention within the same topic.
- Empirical signature: group citation gaps remain after comparing authors or papers within topics.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Complements [between-topic citation disadvantage](between_topic_citation_disadvantage.md) by separating topic placement from recognition within topics.
- Provides a topic-aware extension of [attention inequality](attention_inequality.md) and [gender and race stratification](gender_race_stratification.md).
- Helps evaluate whether topic diversification alone is enough, or whether high-prestige networks and recognition systems also need intervention.
- Supplies a fairness audit for [citation impact indicators](../measures/citation_impact_indicators.md).
- Supports [topic-network equity intervention portfolio](../methods/topic_network_equity_intervention_portfolio.md), which pairs topic investment with prestige-network access.

## Operationalization

- Estimate topic assignments and demographic group weights for papers.
- Compute citation outcomes within each topic and compare groups across the topic citation distribution.
- Use topic fixed effects, topic-specific curves, or weighted group-topic cells to isolate within-topic differences.
- Report whether gaps persist after field, year, topic, author role, institution, and career-stage adjustments where available.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Kozlowski et al. (2022) reports both between-topic and within-topic citation disadvantages for minoritized scientists.
- In Social Sciences, the paper finds that Asian men have higher citations overall, while Black, White, and Latinx women form a block with systematically fewer citations than all other groups across the topic distribution.
- In Health, the paper reports a stronger gender split: men are significantly more cited along the distribution of topics, while women from all racial groups have lower citations.
- Kozlowski et al. conclude that minoritized groups' work is less cited within and across topics, especially where they are underrepresented.

## Caveats

- Within-topic comparisons still may not control for institution, team size, funding, seniority, venue, or citation-network position.
- Topic models may group substantively different work under one topic.
- Citation disadvantage should not be interpreted as lower quality without stronger causal evidence.
- Network-access interventions should be evaluated separately from topic-support interventions.
