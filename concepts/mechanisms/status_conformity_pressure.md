# Status conformity pressure

## Summary

Status conformity pressure is the mechanism by which lower-status scientists face stronger incentives to propose, fund, and publish work that appears conventional to evaluators.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: scientist, applicant, project, paper, reviewer, institution, or status group.
- Typical representation: interactions between low-status indicators and novelty, funding, review, hiring, or publication outcomes.
- Mechanism: vulnerable career positions, weak reputation buffers, institutional peripherality, and demographic underrepresentation make deviation from accepted topics or methods riskier.
- Empirical signature: novelty penalties or conservative choices are stronger for lower-status groups than for higher-status groups.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Generalizes the mechanism behind [funding model status contingency](funding_model_status_contingency.md) and [block-funding novelty buffer](block_funding_novelty_buffer.md).
- Connects [peer review and gatekeeping](peer_review_gatekeeping.md), [status-authority evaluation bias](status_authority_evaluation_bias.md), [gender and race stratification](gender_race_stratification.md), and [institutional prestige hierarchy](institutional_prestige_hierarchy.md).
- Provides a bridge between project-choice models, proposal-review models, and evaluation-policy design.
- Helps explain why average novelty effects can hide unequal consequences across career stages and social positions.

## Operationalization

- Define status proxies such as rank, gender, institutional prestige, prior reputation, elite affiliation, or career stage.
- Measure novelty, atypicality, exploration, or topic deviation at the project, proposal, or paper level.
- Estimate whether low-status groups have lower novelty under evaluative regimes with stronger gatekeeping or deliverable pressure.
- Distinguish self-screening from evaluator selection when proposal-submission data are available.
- Compare flexible-resource settings with competitive or tightly specified funding settings.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Lee, and Walsh (2018) argues that junior researchers, female researchers, and researchers outside core universities are more vulnerable to conformity pressure in competitive project funding.
- The paper reports negative interactions between competitive funding and low-status indicators for novelty, with statistically significant main specifications for junior and female respondents.
- Wang et al. interpret the results as evidence that competitive selection is less receptive to novel ideas from low-status researchers and may also constrain their post-award flexibility.
- The paper's alternative-explanation section treats low-status self-screening of less novel proposals as another expression of the same conformity-pressure mechanism.

## Caveats

- Status measures are proxies and can mix identity, rank, institutional resources, reputation, and autonomy.
- Observed lower novelty can arise before application, during review, after award, or at publication selection.
- The mechanism should be tested separately across fields and national systems because status hierarchies differ.
