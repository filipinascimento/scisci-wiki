# Peer review and gatekeeping

## Summary

Peer review and gatekeeping describe formal evaluation systems that filter manuscripts, grants, hiring, and awards, shaping which ideas and people receive resources and visibility.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: manuscript, grant proposal, reviewer, editor, panel, applicant, or decision.
- Typical representation: review-score/outcome table or reviewer-author-editor network.
- Mechanism: expert judgment, social filtering, bias, noise, conservatism, and strategic signaling affect selection.
- Empirical signature: acceptance/funding decisions vary with reviewer assignment, author identity, field fit, novelty, and prestige.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Used to study evaluation bias, novelty penalties, reproducibility, funding inequality, and publication selection.
- Connects institutional stratification and demographic disparities to mechanisms of resource allocation.
- Provides validation targets for policy experiments such as blinding, lotteries, and reviewer calibration.

- A residual Li and Agha pass adds peer-review process details around [scientific-merit criteria vectors](../measures/scientific_merit_criteria_vector.md), [reviewer budget-blinding rules](../methods/reviewer_budget_blinding_rule.md), [initial-reviewer panel-score aggregation](../methods/initial_reviewer_panel_score_aggregation.md), [proposal-triage discussion denominators](../methods/proposal_triage_discussion_denominator.md), [unsolicited-topic proposal channels](unsolicited_topic_proposal_channel.md), [new-renewal application signal splits](../validations/new_renewal_application_signal_split.md), [prior-NIH PI experience splits](../validations/prior_nih_pi_experience_split.md), [peer-review regime-change continuity caveats](../validations/peer_review_regime_change_continuity_caveat.md), [bibliometric social-welfare boundaries](../validations/bibliometric_social_welfare_boundary.md), and [peer-review cost-accounting omissions](../validations/peer_review_cost_accounting_omission.md).
## Operationalization

- Analyze review scores, decisions, reviewer identities, author metadata, manuscript content, and post-decision outcomes.
- Use randomized reviewer assignment, blinding experiments, or natural experiments where available.
- Compare selected and rejected work when rejected manuscripts can be tracked.

## Evidence and Validations

- Classic sociology anchors the institutional role of review; modern studies use experimental or quasi-experimental review data.
- Verified full-text evidence from Fortunato et al. (2018) links peer review and funding panels to novelty penalties, bias, and alternative resource-allocation designs.
- Verified full-text evidence from Clauset et al. (2017) warns that automated prediction systems for proposals, manuscripts, and young scholars can amplify inequality and suppress unexpected discoveries if used as gatekeeping tools.
- Verified full-text evidence from Wu et al. (2019) adds a funding-portfolio signal: acknowledged agency funding is associated with more developmental, less disruptive work in their team-size analysis.
- Verified full-text evidence from Li and Agha (2015) studies more than 130,000 NIH R01 grants and shows that peer-review scores among funded grants predict later citations, publications, high-impact publications, and follow-on patents after detailed controls. This makes grant review a measurable gatekeeping signal rather than only an institutional description.
- The narrower validation motif is [grant peer-review value-added](../validations/grant_peer_review_value_added.md), which asks whether review scores add predictive information beyond applicant observables.
- The Li and Agha full text also supports lower-level process motifs: [NIH study-section review process](../methods/nih_study_section_review_process.md) captures reviewer assignment, triage, discussion, priority scoring, and percentile normalization, while [NIH peer-review percentile score](../measures/nih_peer_review_percentile_score.md) captures the normalized administrative review signal.
- Verified full-text evidence from Ginther et al. (2011) adds a fairness validation target for this same gatekeeping system: [NIH R01 award disparities](../validations/nih_r01_award_disparities.md) tracks demographic differences in R01 award probabilities after applicant and institutional controls.
- The Ginther full text also splits grant gatekeeping into [priority score receipt](../measures/priority_score_receipt.md), [R01 application resubmission sequences](../methods/r01_application_resubmission_sequences.md), [prior NIH experience indicators](../measures/prior_nih_experience_indicators.md), and applicant-resource controls, making review access, repeat attempts, and system experience distinct mechanisms.
- Verified full-text evidence from Wang, Lee, and Walsh (2018) adds a status-contingent funding gatekeeping mechanism: competitive selection is less receptive to novel ideas from junior researchers and is associated with lower novelty for low-status scientists relative to block funding.
- The same verified full text also supports [status conformity pressure](status_conformity_pressure.md) and [proposal-burden research-time crowding](proposal_burden_research_time_crowding.md) as reusable funding-system motifs.
- Verified full-text evidence from Boudreau et al. (2016) adds randomized reviewer-proposal assignment evidence: evaluator-proposal intellectual distance and proposal novelty both shape scores in a frontier medical-research grant process.
- The Boudreau full text splits into lower-level motifs for [biomedical frontier-review experiment](../datasets/biomedical_frontier_review_experiment.md), [MeSH knowledge-frontier space](../representations/mesh_knowledge_frontier_space.md), [reviewer-proposal intellectual distance](../measures/reviewer_proposal_intellectual_distance.md), [proposal MeSH novelty percentile](../measures/proposal_mesh_novelty_percentile.md), [randomized reviewer-proposal assignment](../methods/randomized_reviewer_proposal_assignment.md), [single-author proposal standardization](../methods/single_author_proposal_standardization.md), [triple-blind proposal review](../methods/triple_blind_proposal_review.md), [proposal-evaluator fixed-effect identification](../methods/proposal_evaluator_fixed_effect_identification.md), [noisy-signal peer-review null](../validations/noisy_signal_peer_review_null.md), [private-interest evaluator bias](private_interest_evaluator_bias.md), [expert cue-sampling bias](expert_cue_sampling_bias.md), [closest-expert review penalty](closest_expert_review_penalty.md), [right-tail novelty review discount](right_tail_novelty_review_discount.md), [expert review aggregation limit](../validations/expert_review_aggregation_limit.md), [ex ante novelty-variance blind spot](../validations/ex_ante_novelty_variance_blindspot.md), [novelty robustness specification sweep](../validations/novelty_robustness_specification_sweep.md), [novelty-aware review calibration](../methods/novelty_aware_review_calibration.md), and [reviewer-distance rank reshuffling](../validations/reviewer_distance_rank_reshuffling.md).
- The same Boudreau evidence also adds bottleneck and downstream-consequence motifs: [proposal-abundance evaluation bottleneck](proposal_abundance_evaluation_bottleneck.md), [rejected-proposal line attrition](rejected_proposal_line_attrition.md), and [proposal ambiguity-aversion discount](proposal_ambiguity_aversion_discount.md).
- A residual Boudreau pass adds review-instrumentation motifs for [frontier-neutral disease solicitation](../methods/frontier_neutral_disease_solicitation.md), [two-phase hypothesis seed-grant design](../methods/two_phase_hypothesis_seed_grant_design.md), [low-entry-cost proposal capture](../methods/low_entry_cost_proposal_capture.md), [proposal batch randomization](../methods/proposal_batch_randomization.md), [single-item disease-impact score](../measures/single_item_disease_impact_score.md), [professional-librarian proposal MeSH coding](../methods/professional_librarian_proposal_mesh_coding.md), [publication-count MeSH expertise vector](../representations/publication_count_mesh_expertise_vector.md), [proposal-format covariate control vector](../methods/proposal_format_covariate_control_vector.md), [reviewer-type interaction null](../validations/reviewer_type_interaction_null.md), and [reputational-exposure proposal incentive](reputational_exposure_proposal_incentive.md).
- Verified full-text evidence from Merton (1968) adds an older status-bias mechanism: [status-authority evaluation bias](status_authority_evaluation_bias.md) occurs when known eminence changes assessment of otherwise comparable scientific work.
- Verified full-text evidence from Simmons et al. (2011) links reviewers to false-positive control: [reviewer transparency enforcement](../methods/reviewer_transparency_enforcement.md), tolerance for imperfect but transparent results, and [exact-replication flexibility adjudication](../methods/exact_replication_flexibility_adjudication.md) can reduce incentives for undisclosed analytic flexibility.
- Registered reports and other [open science interventions](../validations/open_science_interventions.md) make gatekeeping less dependent on the attractiveness of known results by evaluating research questions and methods before outcomes are observed.
- Verified full-text evidence from Sekara et al. (2018) adds a venue-level access mechanism: in high-impact journals, prior junior-author experience in the same venue can shape the transition to senior authorship, linking author teams to publication gatekeeping.
- The Sekara evidence now supports lower-level motifs for [venue PI experience categories](../measures/venue_pi_experience_categories.md), [chaperone-effect ratio](../measures/chaperone_effect_ratio.md), [nonlast-to-last transition probability](../measures/nonlast_to_last_transition_probability.md), and [chaperoned PI citation-impact comparison](../validations/chaperoned_pi_citation_impact_comparison.md).

## Caveats

- Review data are often confidential and non-randomly observed.
- Post-publication success of rejected work is hard to measure without strong tracking.
