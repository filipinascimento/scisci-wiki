# Ignorance-hedging diversity rationale

## Summary

Diversity is valuable under uncertainty and ignorance because plural portfolios hedge against unknown future states and unanticipated failures.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: research option, funding portfolio, uncertainty state, ignorance condition, or robustness outcome.
- Typical representation: portfolio-hedging rationale for maintaining plural scientific options.
- Mechanism, measurement, or validation target: robustness value of diversity under incomplete knowledge.
- Empirical signature: diversified portfolios retain useful options when dominant trajectories fail or future value is uncertain.

## Operationalization

- Identify uncertainty and ignorance conditions where expected-value optimization is fragile.
- Measure diversity across research options, methods, or fields as an insurance-like property.
- Evaluate whether diversified portfolios preserve options across alternative futures.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Stirling (2007) frames diversity as important in science and technology appraisal when value, uncertainty, and perspective differ.
- The paper's governance context supports a rationale for diversity as a hedge against ignorance.

## Caveats

- Hedging can look inefficient under short-term performance metrics.
- The rationale requires explicit uncertainty, not diversity as a blanket virtue.
