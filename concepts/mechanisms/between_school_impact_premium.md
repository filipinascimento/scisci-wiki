# Between-school impact premium

## Summary

Between-school impact premium is the citation advantage associated with papers produced by authors at multiple universities relative to otherwise comparable within-school collaborations.

## Canonical Form

- Unit of analysis: paper, authorship structure, university tier pair, field-year cell, or citation outcome.
- Typical representation: marginal probability of above-average citation impact, field-normalized citation percentile, regression coefficient, or tier-specific premium.
- Mechanism: crossing institutional boundaries can combine complementary expertise, resources, or status signals, but the premium can depend strongly on institutional tier.
- Empirical signature: between-school collaborations have higher citation-impact probability than within-school collaborations after controlling for field, year, and team size.

## Uses in Science of Science

- Refines [team impact advantage](team_impact_advantage.md) by separating institutional composition from team size.
- Gives an outcome measure for [multi-university collaboration stratification](multi_university_collaboration.md).
- Connects partner-choice structure in [university tier mixing matrices](../representations/university_tier_mixing_matrix.md) to citation consequences.
- Helps test whether broader collaboration decentralizes high-impact science or increases the returns to already elite institutions.

## Operationalization

- Classify collaborations as within-school or between-school from author affiliations.
- Define high-impact papers using above-average citations, citation percentiles, or another field/year-normalized outcome.
- Estimate the difference in high-impact probability between between-school and within-school teams, controlling for subfield, team size, and publication year.
- Reestimate by university tier combination to separate average cross-institution premium from elite-tier premium.

## Evidence and Validations

- Verified full-text evidence from Jones et al. (2008) compares citation impact for papers published from 1995 to 2005.
- The paper reports that between-school collaborations have a citation-impact advantage over within-school collaborations in both science and engineering and social sciences after controlling for subfield, team size, and year.
- Averaged across schools, the marginal probability of above-average citations is 2.9 percentage points higher in science and engineering and 5.8 percentage points higher in social sciences.
- The premium is especially strong for collaborations among tier I universities, and it declines for lower-tier combinations.
- The same tier-specific evidence supports the [strongest-partner impact model](strongest_partner_impact_model.md), where mixed-tier collaboration outcomes sit closer to the stronger partner's baseline than to the weaker partner's baseline.

## Caveats

- The premium is not necessarily causal; high-quality projects and researchers may select into cross-university teams.
- Citation advantage may reflect institutional prestige, team composition, resources, journal placement, field choice, or topic visibility.
- The result depends on the high-impact threshold and citation-window choice.
- Within-school collaboration can have unobserved advantages that are not captured by citation outcomes.
